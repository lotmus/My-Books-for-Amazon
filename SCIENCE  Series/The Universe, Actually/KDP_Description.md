```html
<h2>What does humanity actually know about reality, and how do we know it?</h2>
<p>From the quarks inside a proton to the edge of the observable universe, and from the first self-copying molecules to the mind reading this page, <i>The Universe, Actually</i> sets out what science currently understands, the evidence behind it, and exactly where the understanding stops.</p>
<p><b>Eleven parts, two ladders.</b> The first climbs from the rules of motion and energy through the quantum world, particles and atoms, space, time and gravity, and planets, stars and galaxies to the universe as a whole. The second climbs from the origin of life through the human body and mind, the living Earth and modern technology to the way knowledge itself is made and tested.</p>
<p><b>Inside you will find:</b></p>
<ul>
<li>What the Higgs boson explains, and what it leaves unexplained</li>
<li>How gravitational waves, black-hole images and pulsar timing tested Einstein</li>
<li>Why dark matter has been weighed but not identified, and what the Hubble tension may mean</li>
<li>How CRISPR edits a genome, and what the first approved gene-editing cure costs</li>
<li>What climate measurements show, separated from what the models project</li>
<li>What machine learning can and cannot do, from protein folding to language models</li>
<li>The open questions, from the origin of life to consciousness, stated as precisely as the closed ones</li>
</ul>
<p><b>Current to 2026.</b> The text covers recent results such as the third interstellar object, 3I/ATLAS; the most energetic neutrino yet recorded; the revised odds of a Milky Way–Andromeda collision; Google's below-threshold error correction; and the first personalized base-editing therapy.</p>
<p><b>How it differs from the classic tour.</b> If you loved Bill Bryson's <i>A Short History of Nearly Everything</i>, know what this book is and is not. Bryson tells the story of the scientists, with wit and anecdote. This book is a field guide to the findings themselves: denser, more systematic and more current, with seven teaching diagrams, dozens of worked problems with their solutions, and a clear line between what is measured, what is inferred and what is still argued. It has less comedy and more evidence. Read Bryson for the people; read this for where the knowledge stands today.</p>
<p><b>Written for</b> curious adults, students and lifelong learners who want one honest, carefully checked book on the whole of science, not a hundred headlines.</p>
<p><i>Every claim comes with its evidence. Every uncertainty is named. A guide to what is known. Actually.</i></p>
```
