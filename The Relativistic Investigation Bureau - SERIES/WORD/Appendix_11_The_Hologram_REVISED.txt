Chapter 11 — Appendix — The Hologram

Derek’s filing cabinet was full. This was an established fact, supported by the drawer that no longer shut.

Ordinarily, the remedy would be a larger cabinet. Double its width, height and depth, and there would be eight times as much room for files. Trevor would fill it in a fortnight, but that was a staffing problem.

Black-hole physics suggests a stranger kind of accounting. When physicists calculate a black hole’s entropy, the quantity that matters is the area of its horizon. That unexpected result helped inspire the holographic principle: a theory describing a region with gravity may have an equivalent description on a boundary with one fewer spatial dimension.

The filing cabinet is an analogy. Please do not attempt the experiment with the Bureau’s furniture.

What the black holes tell us

A black hole’s event horizon is the boundary beyond which light cannot escape to distant observers. It is not a solid shell.

Jacob Bekenstein and Stephen Hawking connected the horizon’s area to entropy. Here, entropy measures how many microscopic possibilities are compatible with the black hole’s large-scale properties. More precisely, it depends on the logarithm of that number. It can also be expressed as an amount of information in bits.

For a spherical horizon, area is 4πR². Double the radius, and the area becomes four times as large. The Bekenstein–Hawking entropy therefore becomes four times as large too.

An ordinary ball with twice the radius has eight times the volume. This contrast is the clue: the black hole’s entropy follows an area rule.

Penny would describe this as being charged for storage by the size of the wrapping paper. Derek would ask whether the wrapping paper was included.

What information means here

“Information” does not mean that someone has printed a list of everything that fell in on the outside of the black hole. It concerns the microscopic distinctions needed to specify a physical state.

Think of the difference between knowing that a room contains warm air and specifying the detailed state of that air. Many microscopic arrangements can fit the same broad description. Entropy quantifies that multiplicity.

The area rule helped motivate the idea that gravity limits the independent information needed to describe a region in an unexpectedly economical way. Turning that insight into a general rule requires care about which surfaces and regions are being compared.

It does not give Trevor permission to store the accounts on the office wallpaper.

How to read the picture

The upper panel shows the black-hole clue. The larger horizon has twice the radius and four times the entropy. Its grid is a drawing aid, not a photograph of tiny storage compartments.

The lower panel shows the next idea. The bulk is the higher-dimensional interior, described using gravity. The boundary carries a quantum theory that describes the same system without its own dynamical gravity.

The double arrow means that physical questions can be translated between the descriptions. It is not a beam projecting objects into the interior. The drawn waves suggest field activity; they are not a literal map of individual particles to matching boundary bumps.

The boundary in this lower panel represents the boundary used in a holographic duality. It should not be confused with the black-hole horizon pictured above. Time is omitted from the drawing.

An example that does some work

The best-known framework is AdS/CFT, proposed by Juan Maldacena. AdS means anti-de Sitter spacetime, a particular kind of curved spacetime. CFT means conformal field theory, a particular kind of quantum field theory.

In this correspondence, a gravitational description in the bulk is paired with a quantum description on its lower-dimensional boundary. The proposed equivalence is mathematically specific and supported by extensive checks, although its full generality is not proved.

For a concrete example, suitable black holes in the bulk correspond to thermal states in the boundary theory. A physicist can study the black hole’s temperature and entropy through the corresponding quantum system. The two calculations must agree wherever the correspondence applies.

That is considerably more useful than saying the universe looks a bit like a hologram. It lets physicists turn a difficult problem into a different problem they may be able to solve.

Derek would approve of the method. He would object to being billed for both calculations.

What this does and does not establish

A familiar optical hologram uses a surface recording to reconstruct the appearance of depth. That supplies the name and a useful hint. Holographic duality goes much further: its claim concerns equivalent physics, including interactions and evolution.

It is also more than changing coordinates, as we do in relativity. A coordinate change relabels a description; a holographic duality relates theories with very different ingredients and dimensionality.

None of this establishes that our own universe has the particular holographic description shown here. It does not make Derek imaginary, prove a multiverse, or provide a mechanism for tomorrow’s murder notice to arrive today.

Penny’s cinema trip may help her ask a better question. It cannot supply the experimental evidence.

Neither can Jamie Lee Curtis, despite an impressive ability to survive changes of genre.

Three things to keep

Keep this: Black-hole entropy scales with horizon area. That helped motivate holographic descriptions in which bulk gravity and boundary quantum physics encode the same system.

Common trap: The boundary is not a cinema screen projecting an unreal world. The claim is an equivalence between physical descriptions in particular theoretical settings.

Quick test: If a spherical black hole’s horizon radius doubles, what happens to its Bekenstein–Hawking entropy? It becomes four times as large, because the horizon area becomes four times as large.
