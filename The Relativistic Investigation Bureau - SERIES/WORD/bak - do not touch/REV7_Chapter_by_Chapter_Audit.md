## Minimal remaining story work

1. Give Penny or Derek an on-page discovery of the generated image’s source and shared event label before the epilogue. The present ending supplies an explanation without the discovery that earns it.
2. Decide which displays are ordinary clocks and which are being driven by the anomaly. Reconcile Chapter 10’s collective freeze/jump with Chapter 15’s independently advancing clocks and the epilogue.
3. Decide whether the record is an actual incoming message through fictional anomalous geometry or merely a mislabelled reconstruction. Coordinate relabelling alone cannot supply previously unavailable information. Make Chapter 15 and the epilogue agree.

These are flagged rather than silently resolved by inventing new plot evidence. The explanatory diagrams illustrate ordinary special relativity, not a physical validation of the fictional anomaly.

### Story Chapter 1

Opening: At precisely 9:17 on a Tuesday morning, Derek Gent received an email informing him that he was dead. He was not particularly surprised. Derek had always suspected that death would arrive by email. In this, as in so many other matters, he had been only slightly ahead of the general population — a position he found both comforting and faintly depressing, which was, on balance, better than the alternatives. The alternatives were rarely an improvement, a fact Derek had verified more than once, usually at personal cost and never with a sense of novelty.

Closing: DO NOT TRUST THE CLOCKS.
