# Series seam — The Relativistic Investigation Bureau

Not for publication. Read this before writing any later book. It is the list of things that are already true, so the next manuscript sounds like the same office rather than a reboot.

Book 1 is `Book 1 - The Murder That Hadnt Happened Yet/` (master `FINAL_REV10_The_Murder_That_Hadnt_Happened_Yet_KINDLE_READY.docx`; build text `scripts/manuscript_text.txt`). Since the 2 Oct 2026 audit (`notes/audit_2026-10-02.md`) the docx is the master in both books and the text files are behind it. Looks and rooms in detail live in `CHARACTER_AND_SETTING_BIBLE.md`. Case clocks live in `SERIES_BIBLE_continuity_chart.md`. This file is the seam: tone, habits, places, and what must not be reinvented.

Physics is the plot. Private lives are invented. The physicists they echo are not their names. Julius is not Julian Barbour. The other Julian is Tuppence's, and he makes clothes hangers.

Pen name on the page: Spezala Genara Relavi, she/her. Byline: Lothar J. Musiol, writing as Spezala Genara Relavi (S.G.R.). British spelling. One line of dialogue per paragraph. "Said" sometimes, never as the default. Do not explain a joke in the next sentence. Do not let a character announce the chapter number.

## How a chapter sounds

The room argues. A person starts a lecture. Someone who already lives there cuts it to English. The physics that survives is one or two sentences a reader could repeat, plus a number that can fail. Mrs Marsh, if she passes, files the universe as a lease.

Warmth is teaspoons, a shut door, a phone call that goes on too long. It is not a speech about feelings. Trevor suffers in complete sentences when the feeling is his sister's. Penny does not wait to be invited. Derek's voice does not change register between a murder and a request for tea.

Each new book gets a new physics problem. It does not re-solve the last file, and it does not borrow the last file's answer as a twist.

## People (do not reset them)

**Derek Gent.** Thirty-eight. Seventeen years in the field. No driving licence, and he mentions it without being asked. Walks. Three or four shirts, a day past smart. Under stress he straightens something that is already straight. Stillness when thinking is Sophie's reveal, not a line in his own introduction. Father of Sophie. Cousin of Penny. Ex of Tuppence.

**Penny Gent.** Cousin, not a love interest. Dark coat, handbag, already bored with the morning. Do not call her a striking brunette; that description was taken out. Fast when impatient. She carries the face-down photograph when a case is frightening, and an itinerary when it isn't. Greece is booked as of the end of Book 2. She will send Weinstein one number from the air: how much older the flight makes her. That is their intimacy. It is not a courtship unless a later book earns a further step.

**Trevor Boltzman.** Twenty-eight. Tall enough that doorframes are personal. Sandy hair that lost a fight with a comb. Glasses come off when the problem is winning. One problem at a time. Brilliant at maths, intermittently disastrous at life. Drives an ancient Mini the colour of a bruise. Sister: Tabitha, generally considered the more dangerous. His desk holds a glass Klein bottle that cannot hold tea. It is not a hologram.

**Alfred Weinstein.** Early-to-mid fifties. Compact, faintly stooped, grey-white hair that has stopped taking advice. One jacket, year-round, on principle. Patent attorney in Schwabing. He leaps. Herbert thinks he is very smart and very lazy, and says so. He delights in a problem and leaves on a real train when staying would become a tenancy. He has no door on the London corridor. Visiting is not a tenancy. No vampires, ever.

**Herbert Matkowski.** Smaller and neater than Weinstein. Was Weinstein's mathematics professor. Textbook English, faint Bavarian clip, refuses to soften it. Steps, not leaps. Immaculate handwriting. He hangs up when the order is already obvious.

**Julius Barbarian.** Late sixties or early seventies. Munich, and he does not travel. His house is already fully described: do not describe it again. Plaque: APPOINTMENTS ARE A HUMAN CONVENTION. He collects things people use to pretend time exists. He wants the relation, not a substance, and he enjoys being interrupted more than being agreed with.

**Sophie.** Twelve. Small for twelve. Dark hair scraped back hard enough to argue with. Precise, suspicious of adult explanations, inherits the stillness. Her notebook is hers. Her brass clock at home is four minutes fast, tested seventeen times, and was never the Bureau's official filing. School rings Tuppence first. Derek does not turn up at the gates looking like a man who has just been murdered, or like a railway. As of the end of Book 2 she did not write down a heading she did not read, and her name was struck from a file because she had no act. She is not an unborn sender. Do not ask whose child. Do not put her on the mystery train.

**Tuppence.** Sophie's mother. Left three years before Book 1 for Julian. Committee voice. The line "Relativistic, my arse" is hers; use it when she is refusing a lecture, not as a catchphrase in every scene. By the end of Book 2 she has granted an ordinary Wednesday pickup at the school gate. That trust is new. Do not rewind it, and do not reunite her with Derek.

**Tabitha.** Trevor's sister. Unhurried, dressed for weather, flat dry delivery, correct physics in the tone she would use to order coffee. Dark hair pulled back the way Sophie's is. Dog: Gucci, in the coat collar when he comes. He will not eat dog food. He eats only if it is hand-fed from her plate. He will finish leftover tea, except Earl Grey, which he regards as an affectation. Marmite, once, on a plate, made him sick after a delay. As of the end of Book 1 the shut office door in the evenings was Derek and Tabitha, and Gucci waited in the hall at half past six. In Book 2 Derek said the sentence on the Paddington pavement: the shut evenings were him wanting her in the room. She kissed him once after the case closed. The door stayed open because the platform was in the hinge. He would not have shut her out if the hinge had allowed it. Do not restart them as strangers. Do not file the open door as a choice the platform did not force, and do not hide the next step behind another unexplained shut door unless the plot earns it.

**Sherlock.** Dolphin. Remote. Telepathic. Never physically described. Cover art may wear a deerstalker; the text does not. Accounts: Miscellaneous Marine, mackerel. He may witness. Derek and Penny close the case. He does not become the detective, and he is not explained.

**Mrs Marsh.** Building superintendent. Already fully described. Do not describe her again. Pending Geometry is drawer three. Murder is drawer one. She talks to the landlord. A platform is a lease violation. A metaphor is what the landlord would prefer, and she will not give him one if she can help it.

**Mr Pendleton.** Facilities. Already fully described. Thin, precise moustache, apologetic voice. The form is not his form. It is in another building, or another continuum.

**Mr Haskett.** The landlord. Fifties. Camel coat bought to be looked at. Turns a signet ring on his little finger the way other men jingle change. Carries a leather folder. No smell; do not give him one. Unlike his surveyor, he looks straight at the platform. He thinks in units and postcodes ("Could it be let?"). He comes up in person once in Book 2 (Ch 20, Tuesday 17:00) about the insurance. He leaves with an interval, not a time, and both copies of the tenant's statement. He sent the surveyor, Mr Quayle, on the Tuesday (Ch 19). Quayle measured the opening, 896 mm, and wrote FEATURE BEYOND OPENING: NOT ASSESSED (OUTSIDE DEMISE). Haskett's Sunday instruction to bleed the radiator is in Mrs Marsh's log, and in the file twice: ON THE LANDLORD'S INSTRUCTION. Mrs Marsh still handles him. A later book may bring him back. It may not make him believe in metaphors, and it may not put his name in the empty sender field.

**The insurers: Northgate and Castellan.** The building's cover moved from Northgate (six years, premium went up) to Castellan at 10:00 on the Monday Book 2 opens. Haskett's broker renews everything at ten. Both refused, each quoting half of Pendleton's incident report: Northgate the "10:05", Castellan the "already open". The tenant's statement, signed D. Gent and filed in drawer three, says:
- the door was shut at 09:58:12 by phone time (Trevor's photograph of the Klein bottle, sent to Tabitha);
- it was noticed open at about 10:05 by the corridor clock, which is about half a minute slow and has no second hand;
- it opened between those times, and the time within that interval is unknown and not supplied.

Do not let a later book resolve which side of ten o'clock the door opened. Do not give the interval as a fraction ("two-ninths of a door" is Penny's joke against doing that).

## Places

**London Bureau.** Two rooms, second floor, old office building. Frosted glass: DEREK GENT / RELATIVISTIC INVESTIGATION BUREAU / CAUSALITY, CHRONOLOGY & OTHER INCONVENIENCES. Penny's line on the glass: NO MURDERS BEFORE 10 A.M. WITHOUT AN APPOINTMENT. Locked filing cabinet. Kettle, not necessarily where it ought to be. Window on rain, buses, the kerb. Corridor clock is the ordinary clock. Smell of the office is not established. Do not invent one and then treat it as canon. Brake dust belongs to a platform that should not be there, not to the rooms.

**Open door.** On this corridor, an open door means the person is not accounted for. A shut door means they are in there, working, or otherwise occupied, and nobody knocks. The rule is London's. Weinstein's missing door in Munich is not a shut door and not an open one. He has no door here.

**Munich.** Weinstein's patent office is in Schwabing, cold window, trams, a blotter, a brass clock that skips. Barbarian's house is in the same city. Neither of them is "going back to Munich" from a foreign Barbarian. The S-Bahn stays in Munich.

**Paris.** NOT IN THE CURRENT BOOK 1 DOCX (flagged 2026-10-04) — a fresh full-text audit found no Louvre scene, Paris trip, or recurring guard anywhere in the current manuscript. The description below is kept only as a record of the originally planned scene; do not treat it as established Book 1 canon until it is either written into the book or this note is removed. The Louvre basement is small, stone, too quiet, a table, a clock, a paper asking for tau. The guard is never in the same place twice. The building gets older as they go down. Do not restage it as a new mystery unless the new physics needs that room.

**Trains.** Platform Seven and the London terminus that should not exist are the anomalous railway. Paddington is a real station. Book 2 uses it for a 16:00 airport train that leaves after Weinstein boards it. Do not merge Paddington with the doorway platform. The doorway train's board can say YESTERDAY. That word is a direction. On the Monday Book 2 opens, yesterday the calendar day was Sunday.

**School.** Sophie is in double maths when the books need her safe. The gates are where frightened fathers go, and are told not to.

**Penny's flat.** Top floor of a narrow house, divided in the 1970s by a builder with strong views about where a kitchen should not go. The kitchen is in the hall, so the table is in the hall, and her coat hangs on the back of the kitchen door, which is also in the hall. No smell established. Do not invent one. Book 2, Ch 15: on the Monday night she does the flight sum there with Trevor on the phone. The face-down photograph comes out of the bag and goes back unturned. The itinerary is London Heathrow to Athens, Thursday week, 07:40, landing 13:05 local. Its margin reads "x3", "SIGN UNKNOWN. UNTIL FLOWN.", and "GROUND SPEED. HEIGHT. TIME AT HEIGHT. WHICH WAY. DO NOT SEND BEFORE." Penny's number, sent from the air after Book 2, accounts for the Earth's rotation. Flying east it comes out near zero, and the wind decides the sign. Weinstein's "about ten nanoseconds older" left the rotation out.

## Objects that already have a job

- Brass railway clock, 11:03:17, stamped LGV. The letters mean Ligne à Grande Vitesse. Local Gravitational Vector and Large Goods Vehicle were wrong guesses. Do not re-mystify the letters.
- The murder photograph shows three times and no nouns. Penny's margin guessed death, departure, arrival. The file did not agree until Book 1, Chapter 16. Do not print those nouns as the file's caption.
- Case 1047 is closed. Not a murder. Pending Geometry. LOCALHOST sent the photograph and has not been identified. Do not explain LOCALHOST. A later book may inherit the absence. It may not pretend Book 2 was LOCALHOST.
- Cucumber: lunch that survived as an analogy. Two descriptions, one object. Not a theory of blood.
- Tea, when it is the callback: Yes. Milk? No. Sugar? One. "Two" was the murder morning only, because he thought he might be dead by lunch. After Case 1047, one sugar stands.
- Seventeen is a measurement the books are fond of (seconds, years in the field, a seventeen-minute miss in Book 2). It is not a force. A later book that uses it again must let a character refuse to worship it.
- Book 2's closed record: Coming Next's order holds. The sheet was already in the room, then the door opened, then the voice said not to open it. Both the sheet and the opening are before 10:05, and neither has a clock time. The voice is late for the opening, size unknown. The sheet is earlier than the door, and its front is still early for boarding. The paper was read at 10:22. That seventeen minutes is reading time, not the sheet's arrival and not the lateness of the opening. Sunday's slip is the previous warning the back of the sheet meant: a different seventeen, 18:17 after an 18:00 opening. Boarding stayed early until the 16:00 airport train, which Weinstein boards in the ordinary order. The record written at 16:41 is the refusal. It does not claim to have printed the morning sheet. That sender stays blank. Sophie's name came off because she had no act. The platform does not leave because a form was saved.

## What a new book is allowed to invent

A new case, a new tense or a new geometry, a new room only if the old ones cannot hold the scene, and one further step in a relationship that already exists. It is not allowed a new Derek, a London door for Weinstein, a solved dolphin, a solved LOCALHOST, a child as the unborn author, American spelling, a fabricated citation, or a smell presented as something the series has always known.

Sherlock witnesses. Derek and Penny close. Mrs Marsh remains the landlord's problem. The kettle is for tea and for closed cases, not for a new metaphysics.

## Book 3 — case seed (2 Oct 2026; no manuscript yet)

There is no Book 3 manuscript. This is the seed for one, filed here so the next draft starts from the seam rather than from a blank page. Everything below is a proposal until a manuscript makes it true.

**The case.** Eleven residents of one block of flats give statements about a Thursday-evening fire. They agree that the alarm sounded first and the lights went out after it. Several of them use the same words: "the alarm, then the dark." The fire panel's log and the electricity substation's log both put the power cut first, by forty seconds. Nobody is lying. The statements were taken eight days later, after a residents' meeting, a local radio interview and a week of telling one another what happened.

**The physics.** Reichenbach's common-cause principle (*The Direction of Time*, 1956): when two records agree more often than chance allows, either one caused the other or both share a cause. Eleven agreeing statements do not need eleven sightings. One retelling, heard by everyone, will do. Physics has the same audit in a stricter form: a fact is objective when many fragments of the world record it independently, and copies that compared notes count once. Human memory fails that audit in a well-measured way. It is rebuilt each time it is recalled and takes in what the rememberer has heard since (Bartlett, *Remembering*, 1932; Loftus and Palmer, 1974). The popular name for the same wrong memory in many heads is the Mandela effect. The file does not use the phrase.

**The number that can fail.** The logs could be wrong too; the panel clock has never been checked. Trevor dates the evening a third way. One resident's phone video carries the mains hum, and the grid's frequency wanders a few hundredths of a hertz either side of fifty in a pattern the operator logs. Forensic laboratories have matched recordings to that log since the mid-2000s. If the hum in the video lines up with the grid record and the lights go out on the video before the alarm starts, the residents are wrong. If it does not line up, the Bureau is.

**Who does what.** Trevor finds the hum and takes his glasses off when it starts to win. Penny takes the statements apart by who spoke to whom first; her itinerary habit becomes a contact chart. Barbarian, by telephone from Munich, is delighted: a past, for him, is records consistent with one another, and he has been handed eleven consistent records of a past that did not happen. He enjoys being corrected, so let Penny correct him: consistency is necessary, not sufficient, and the copies must not share a cause. Mrs Marsh files the residents' agreement as a party wall. Sherlock may witness. Derek closes.

**Must not.** Do not use this case to explain LOCALHOST or the empty sender of Book 2. Agreeing witnesses are not a sender. Do not make anyone a liar; the case is that nobody lied. Do not re-solve Case 1047. Do not make the number of witnesses seventeen. Do not give the block a smell.
