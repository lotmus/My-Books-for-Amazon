A MURDER THAT HASN'T HAPPENED YET.
A photograph dated tomorrow.
And his daughter, paused in the doorway.

At 9:17 on a Tuesday morning, investigator Derek Gent receives an email saying that he has been murdered. It is polite, precise, and signed by Derek himself. The attached photograph shows him face-down on his office floor. A brass clock reads 11:03 — tomorrow, according to the email. In the doorway behind him, his twelve-year-old daughter is caught mid-step.

The clocks disagree.
The universe disagrees.
The paperwork, unfortunately, does not.

Derek has until 11:03 the next morning to find out who — or what — filed his death. Special relativity cannot explain the case. General relativity cannot either. With his sharp-tongued cousin Penny, his hyper-literal colleague Trevor, and a physicist who suspects time may not be fundamental, Derek follows the evidence through a mystery built from clocks, frames of reference, and one very ordinary cucumber.

For readers who loved the “wait, WHAT” of Arrival or Tenet, but wanted it funnier, more British, and solvable with an ordinary cucumber.

If you cannot trust time, can you trust what you think has already happened to the people you love?

The Murder That Hadn't Happened Yet is Book 1 of the Relativistic Investigation Bureau: a mystery with real physics, dry jokes, and a case solved by reading the clocks properly. No physics background is needed to follow the story. Sixteen short, optional lessons at the back explain the real relativity behind its clues for readers whose curiosity the story sparks.

Recommended for readers aged 14 and up. Strong readers aged 12 to 13 may also enjoy the mystery, with adult support for the denser physics discussions.

AUTHOR BIOGRAPHY (for Author Central / the KDP author page)

[Written 1 Oct 2026 from the book's own About the Author page, so the facts

Spezala Genara Relavi (S.G.R.) is the alleged author of The Relativistic Investigation Bureau series. She has never been formally accused of understanding General Relativity, though several of her fictitious colleagues maintain that she does. She divides her time between London, Munich, and a small pink filing cabinet labelled PRE-TRANSFORMATION / DO NOT OBSERVE. She has not yet been murdered, although she understands this may be subject to change without further notice.

Lothar J. Musiol is German by birth and American by choice, and he writes in British because he regards it as the most awkward American dialect. For exactly that reason he is a staunch aficionado of British films such as Four Weddings and a Funeral. The preposterous accent, however, was inspired by Love Actually, in which a daft sod named Colin Frissell flies to America and has a conversation in a bar before arriving at the true purpose of his travels. The author found that eye-opening and still hopes to get there one day, if he keeps up his admittedly naff British long enough.

A graduate of Munich University of Applied Sciences, he spent more than forty years in the semiconductor industry, first at Germany's largest company in the field, then at a string of American start-ups. Dual citizenship, put to its most sensible use, lets him split his time between San Clemente, California, and Passau, Bavaria, where Austria begins at the garden fence. The household includes one married daughter, one grumpy Teacup Pomeranian named Gucci, and eleven chickens, listed in order of seniority rather than volume.

The Murder That Hadn't Happened Yet is Book 1.
