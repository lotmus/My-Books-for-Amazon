## Editorial assessment

The book’s strongest choice is that the technical mystery and the family conflict share one question: what happens when a system fills gaps with a confident label? Sophie’s notebook, Derek’s letter, Mrs Marsh’s records, and the camera all give the reader ways to test claims. The climax pays these off with ordinary evidence and a human conversation, so the emotional ending does not depend on understanding the equations.

The main drag risk is the run of explanatory scenes in the middle: the novel pauses repeatedly to articulate what each new physics idea means. The character voices and jokes help, and the optional course is clearly separated, but a cold reader may still feel the investigation stop to teach. If a later reader pass finds that, trim repeated explanations at the scene level while preserving the evidence and the optional lessons. The “Rule” tags also work as a deliberate motif; keep them only where they sharpen the chapter’s turn.

**Recommendation:** Yes for readers who enjoy clever, humane SF mysteries, dry institutional satire, and learning science through a story. The manuscript’s continuity is strong after the canon-note corrections. It remains a release candidate until the Kindle Previewer, cover, metadata, and author decisions in `WORK_QUEUE.md` are complete.
