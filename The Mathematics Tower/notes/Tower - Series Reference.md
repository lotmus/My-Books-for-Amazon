## The four volumes

One continuous book, Floors 1–50, by Lothar J. Musiol. Each volume also stands
on its own. The vocabulary is Floors, Rooms, and the Tower.

| Volume | Floors | Rooms |
|---|---|---|
| 1 The Lower Floors | 1–12 | Floor 1 has Rooms 1.1–1.10. Floors 2–12 have Rooms X.1–X.9. 109 rooms. |
| 2 The Middle Floors | 13–25 | Rooms X.1–X.9, except Floor 18, which has Rooms 18.1–18.10. 118 rooms. |
| 3 The Upper Floors | 26–37 | Rooms X.1–X.9. 108 rooms. |
| 4 The Penthouse | 38–50 | Rooms X.1–X.9. 117 rooms. |

Pictures in the manuscripts: Volume 1 = 97, Volume 2 = 107, Volume 3 = 102,
Volume 4 = 114. The last Word page recount, after the tables of contents were
rebuilt, was 525, 608, 581, and 563.

## House style

The manuscripts follow these. Match them.

- American English (-ize, skeptical, rumor).
- Curly quotes and apostrophes. No straight ones in the prose.
- Em dashes spaced — like this — on both sides.
- A proper minus (−), not a hyphen, in arithmetic. The arrow is →.
- Author: Lothar J. Musiol, alone.
- Floors, Rooms, and the Tower. Never “storeys.”

At a hinge, the style guide bans *clearly, obviously, simply, just, of course,*
and *it is easy to see*. Kept on purpose: a sentence that names the fallacy
(“obviously true is not the same as provably true”), the idiom “as a matter of
course,” and a joke that is not sitting on a calculation. The monad operation
`just(x)` in Floor 38 is notation, not that ban.
