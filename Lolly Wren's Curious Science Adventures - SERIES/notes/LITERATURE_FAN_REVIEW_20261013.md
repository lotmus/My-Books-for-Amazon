## Reader response

Yes: both books have a distinctive literary voice, and many sentences reach that level through exact observation, comic timing, rhythm, and a second meaning beneath the joke. The best images grow from the books’ world: forms, umbrellas, doors, ledgers, the notebook, and the corridor smell. Those objects carry the emotional argument instead of pausing the story to explain it.

Book 1 is the more immediately playful book. Its dry narration gives the impossible premise a grounded human scale, and Mrs Chain’s grief remains present under the comedy. The main craft risk is a run of image-heavy descriptions in the opening, where too many turns can compete for the same laugh. I tightened one sentence there by removing a repeated comparison while preserving its character detail.

Book 2 is more reflective and uses repeated images and parallel sentences to give the case emotional shape. Its strongest passages let a short sentence change the meaning of the longer one before it. The technical dialogue is intentionally polished; readers who prefer rougher, more conversational speech may notice how often the cast speaks in compressed, quotable lines. That is a stylistic choice rather than a consistency fault, and the speakers remain identifiable by what each notices and refuses.

Not every line aims to be quotable. The connective narration and case record language are plainer by design, so the vivid lines have space to land. I would recommend both to readers of literary comic science fiction; Book 2 asks for more patience with its layered case.
