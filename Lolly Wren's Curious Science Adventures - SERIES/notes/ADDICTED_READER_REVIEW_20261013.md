# Addicted Reader Review ? 13 October 2026

## Reader verdict

**Recommendation: yes.** These books have a rare engine: the bureaucracy is funny because its errors are legible, while the damage to actual people remains visible. The jokes usually sharpen the moral point instead of giving the reader permission to dismiss it. The satire targets systems and incentives, not the citizens caught inside them.

Book 1 is the faster, more immediately comic read. Mrs Chain?s complaint gives the physics a human cost, the 4C clock supplies mounting pressure, and Lolly?s refusal to certify a false answer carries through to the ending. The consultants? arguments become plot tools; the shutdown and the 308 recovered records pay off earlier choices without pretending that recovery undoes harm. The epilogue?s institutional self-congratulation is a strong final satirical turn.

Book 2 is quieter and more demanding. Ellen?s card, the fourteen, and the 411-attometre reading create a satisfying chain of evidence and restraint. Its unresolved elements are intentional and have clear boundaries: the ending advances the case without pretending to solve it. The middle chapters spend more time in argument than Book 1, so the reader?s reward is accumulation and character judgment rather than a new crisis every few pages. That is the main tradeoff for a binge reader.

Dialogue and description do their work through distinct habits of attention: Lolly notices what a record can and cannot say; Mrs Chain insists on the person; Gideon builds; Fainrose tests; the consultants defend different limits. The prose is lucid, vivid, and consistent, with memorable images rooted in the Ministry?s objects and rooms. Humour is plentiful in Book 1 and drier in Book 2; the second book lets silences and refusals carry more weight. Story and optional science sections are clearly signposted, and the back matter does not interrupt the narrative payoff.
