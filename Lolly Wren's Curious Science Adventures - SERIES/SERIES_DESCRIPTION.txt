LOLLY WREN'S CURIOUS SCIENCE ADVENTURES - SERIES DESCRIPTION

================================================================================
SERIES TAGLINE
================================================================================

What happens when a government tries to measure reality with paperwork?

A series of satirical science-fiction novels about a bureaucracy that has
learned to weaponize uncertainty—and one woman who refuses to let them
make it stick.

================================================================================
SERIES DESCRIPTION (For KDP, Goodreads, Marketing)
================================================================================

The Ministry of Eventualities exists to solve an impossible problem:
how to keep reality from disagreeing with itself.

Lolly Wren works there. She didn't plan to. She takes the job of noticing
what the Ministry has actually done, and she does not do it alone.

Each book in Lolly Wren's Curious Science Adventures explores a new corner
of a bureaucracy that has confused measurement with authority, and assumed
that if reality can be assigned a category, reality must obey it.

Book 1, *Schrödinger's Paperwork*, begins when the Ministry launches The
Simplification Initiative—a project to measure reality so precisely that
it becomes impossible to predict anything at all. The result: contradictory
states, unstable identities, impossible records, and a black hole that is
evaporating on a municipal timetable.

As Lolly learns to work with an auditor who refuses impossible certificates,
a physicist who is never reassured, and an engineer who has found the actual
switch, she discovers something worse than bureaucratic incompetence:
bureaucratic competence in the service of a fundamentally wrong idea.

What follows is equal parts satire, science, and resistance—a series about
what happens when you measure the wrong thing, how to measure the right thing,
and whether it's ever possible to unmeasure something once it's been pinned
down.

Each novel in the series can be read standalone. Each pairs narrative with
optional lectures on real physics—superposition, measurement, entanglement,
uncertainty, decoherence, Bell's theorem, black-hole information, thermodynamics,
and why there is no lid.

Lolly Wren's Curious Science Adventures is for readers who love:
→ Absurdist bureaucratic satire (think Douglas Adams meets Kafka)
→ Science integrated into storytelling (Brian Greene meets narrative fiction)
→ Characters who learn to act despite uncertainty
→ Worlds where the stakes are both personal and quantum-mechanical
→ Physics education woven so seamlessly you don't realize you're taking a course

Read them as novels. Take the courses. Or do both.

Just don't ask the Ministry where your paperwork has gone.

It may not have happened yet.

SERIES POSITIONING (For marketing/discovery)
================================================================================

Genre: Science Fiction › Satire + Popular Science › Physics
Tone: Witty, absurdist, thoughtful
Audience:
  - Douglas Adams / Terry Pratchett fans
  - Popular science readers (Brian Greene, Carlo Rovelli)
  - Physics educators seeking narrative delivery
  - Literary sci-fi readers who want ideas + humor + heart
  - Anyone who's ever been trapped in bureaucratic nonsense

Series Progression (Thematic):
  Book 1: Measurement forces collapse (what do we do when precision breaks things?)
  Book 2, The Permitted Options: a barrier, fourteen citizens, and what a measurement can still honestly say
  Later books: a new corner of the same Ministry, in the same voices. Not a new tone.

The series explores different aspects of how systems (bureaucratic, physical,
mathematical, social) try to impose order on reality and what breaks when
they succeed too well.

================================================================================
BOOK 1 SPECIFIC (Schrödinger's Paperwork)
================================================================================

This is the entry point. It:
✓ Introduces Lolly Wren (the protagonist who learns to act)
✓ Establishes the Ministry (the world where impossible things are policy)
✓ Shows what happens when measurement becomes weaponized
✓ Demonstrates the book's tone (absurdist + serious)
✓ Teaches quantum mechanics (superposition → measurement → decoherence)
✓ Ends with the coupling cut, 308 of 411 states recovered as records, and Lolly accepting Principal Officer, Relational Integrity. Margaret does not come back. 103 stay unrecovered.

It stands alone: You don't need to read further to get a complete story.
Book 2, The Permitted Options, keeps the same office and the same voices. Fourteen citizens resolve outside the register. The measurement that remains is one a bench can still make.

SERIES ARCHITECTURE (For YOUR planning, not public)
================================================================================

What makes this an open-ended series:

1. WORLD is expandable
   - The Ministry is large; each book can explore new departments/initiatives
   - New characters can enter; old ones can return
   - The physics lectures can explore new concepts each book

2. LOLLY'S ARC is open
   - Book 1: She takes the post. The coupling is cut. 308 are recovered. 103 are not. Margaret does not come back.
   - Book 2: She is already Principal Officer. Fourteen citizens, a barrier, and a measurement a bench can still make.
   - Later books: a new corner of the same Ministry. The series ends when her story ends, not when the Ministry ends.
   - (The Ministry probably never ends—that's the point)

3. THEMATIC SCOPE is flexible
   - Book 1: Quantum measurement and bureaucratic collapse
   - Book 2: Tunnelling, entropy, the Casimir force, and a throat that is not a door
   - Later books may take a physics the two books have not already taught, through the same office
   - Each book is a new corner of that physics, and a new case

4. READER EXPECTATIONS are clear
   - Each book pairs narrative + lectures (format stays consistent)
   - Tone is consistent (absurdist + thought-provoking)
   - Lolly is the anchor (she's in all of them)
   - Each book can be read standalone
   - But reading in order enriches the arc (character development, callbacks)
