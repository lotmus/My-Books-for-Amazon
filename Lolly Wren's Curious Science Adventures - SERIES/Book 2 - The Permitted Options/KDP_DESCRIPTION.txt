THE PERMITTED OPTIONS
Book Two of Lolly Wren's Curious Science Adventures

Fourteen people are no longer where the file says they are.

Lolly Wren has already taken the post. The last crisis was filed. What the Ministry of Eventualities has now is a new case, a new department, and a date.

A travel agent in a good coat wants the thin records made fat by the ninth of February. Give people somewhere real they could still go. One of the fourteen, Ellen Prosper, was meant to be on a course in Newcastle or behind the counter of her mother's shop. She is on a survey boat out of Peterhead. The other thirteen are elsewhere. A card sits on the outside of the file. The instruction is to speak to her, not to the trace.

The consultants come in the usual way. An organ restorer who sings, once, and will not let a minute pretend to be a proof. A geometer who trusts nine and will not be met at the station. A man of ninety-one who grew crystals and cannot see why the building is astonished. A lighting engineer who finds the question ludicrous and builds the bench anyway. Downstairs, the smell of solder is how you know the engineer is in.

On the far side of the lobby line, a man waits with his coat over his arm. He does not come in until someone asks him across.

Nobody is permitted to sign the fourteen away. A force is not an address. A barrier is not a door you failed to open.

THE PERMITTED OPTIONS is a satirical science-fiction novel about what a measurement can still honestly say, after the Ministry has already spent the options it liked.

And then there is the physics.

Fifteen lectures follow the story, one to a chapter. Tunnelling. What empty space costs when you put a wall in it. Why a treaty is not an arithmetic. Entropy, the arrow of time, and a letter from a war whose second half was posted to an office and never opened. No doctorate required. Read the novel. Take the course. Or do both.

You can start here. If you have already survived Schrödinger's Paperwork, the same people are still in the building. They have not become nicer.

Ask for Ellen. The form will not be the one who answers.
