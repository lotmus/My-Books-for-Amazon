# Schrödinger's Paperwork — Character & Place Reference

Compiled directly from the manuscript text: every physical description, voice quality, smell, and
attitude/character note the book actually gives each named character and major setting. Where the
book deliberately gives nothing (many secondary Ministry officials have no physical description at
all — characterised entirely through behaviour and dialogue instead), that's noted rather than
invented. Useful for cover art and illustration. Book 2 facts that are not in this file live in `../SERIES_BIBLE.md`. Do not invent a smell this file marks as undescribed. `CHARACTER_AND_PLACE_GUIDE.voice-pass.md` still says Twenty-Two Coldharrow Rise. ## Protagonists & Allies

### Lolly Wren
- **Looks:** Thin, but not zero — the one direct description comes from Priddy's POV in Ch4: "middling
  height, dark hair escaping its clip, and a pencil, never a pen, worn down to a stub behind one ear.
  Its graphite had left a graphite smudge across the pad of her thumb." Otherwise the book characterises
  her almost entirely through action and voice — genuinely the thinnest physical description of any
  major character, which is why the cover art needed the most invention for her face specifically.
- **Sound:** When she speaks up the chain: already edited, "as if the sentence had spent a night in the green notebook first." (Ch1)
- **Smell:** Not described.
- **Attitude:** Ex-physicist (eleven minutes as one, then ten years in electronics, then Transit
  Reconciliation), dry, observant, self-deprecating. Thinks in bra-ket notation when frightened,
  "which turns out to be often." Keeps a green notebook marked *PRIVATE / UNHELPFUL*. Core trait:
  notices things and writes them down rather than acting — the book's whole arc is her learning that
  "noticing is not nothing... only nothing if you stop there" (Eilstein, Ch11).

### Gideon Wigglesworth
- **Looks:** "A big, rumpled man, built more for a workbench than an office chair, with shirtsleeves
  perpetually pushed back over forearms freckled with old solder burns." (Ch1)
- **Sound:** Speaks as if volume were a thing you could trip over: "carefully, and a little behind the thought, which always arrived intact even when the cup didn’t." (Ch9)
- **Smell:** "Hot solder and pencil shavings — a combination Lolly had come to associate with problems
  that were about to be solved rather than caused." (Ch1)
- **Attitude:** Exceptionally intelligent, remarkably clumsy, and almost impossible not to like.
  The cast list stops at the kettle and the Hamiltonian. The doorframe is the Chapter 1 scene only;
  do not put it back into the cast or the epilogue. Lolly's old bench-engineer friend; quietly
  essential (finds the physical relay that lets them cut 4C's coupling in Ch16). In Book 2 he keeps
  the department's benches and remains her close friend: he does the physical work, she thinks of
  him when he is elsewhere, and he is the person she can be unfinished with.

### Dr Stephanie Fainrose
- **Looks:** "Not tall, and not young, and had a stillness that forced other people to feel
  over-articulated. She wore a dark cardigan, narrow trousers, and spectacles that looked like a
  final administrative judgment upon the human face." (Ch5)
- **Sound:** "Quieter than the doorway suggested it should be, each word set down like an instrument she'd already calibrated. It carried the particular weariness that comes of spending a career making catastrophic news sound like routine paperwork." (Ch5)
- **Smell:** Her house: "paper, tea, solder, and intellectual impatience." Her kitchen, later:
  "scorched flux and Earl Grey — the two ingredients... from which Dr Fainrose was assembled." (Ch5-6)
- **Attitude:** Former Ministry physicist, brilliant, blunt, exacting. "Rarely reassured." Left the
  Ministry after "the Fainrose Unpleasantness" (a disagreement about continuity theory). Runs the
  Institute for Unhelpful Clarity to employ exactly one person: herself. Becomes Lolly's mentor.

### Beatrix Sloan
- **Looks:** First seen from Desk Three: "a file under one arm and the expression of someone who had
  once been patient and had since overcome it. Charcoal; badge clipped with punitive exactness."
  "Narrow and upright, and moved with the same brisk economy she brought to a ledger: nothing wasted,
  nothing repeated." (Ch2)
- **Sound:** "The voice she used for minutes: each clause already a finding." Dialogue stays clipped, declarative, unadorned. (Ch2, Ch5)
- **Smell:** "Faintly of ink and wet wool, a combination Lolly would learn to recognise from three
  departments away." (Ch2)
- **Attitude:** Internal Audit. "Wields certainty the way other people wield a truncheon." Builds the
  legal case that saves the district; carries a fountain pen and eleven boxes of evidence everywhere.
  Loathed, for no established reason, by her neighbour's cat.

### Jago Flint
- **Looks:** "Dark coat, narrow face, stack of sealed envelopes under one arm, the general air of
  someone who belonged everywhere on a provisional basis." (Ch2) No hair colour, build, or age given
  beyond Eilstein calling him "a quick young man."
- **Sound:** "Cheerful, faintly amused, and carrying slightly too much information, as if the sentence were a parcel he was pleased to have got through in one piece." (Ch2)
- **Smell:** "Biro ink and other people's post." (Ch2)
- **Attitude:** Interdepartmental Courier Service, Restricted/Floating Clearance ("authorised where
  things aren't settled"). Knows everyone, rather better than the personnel files admit; owes half of
  them money. Comic relief with a real undercurrent of competence — three years of courier work taught
  him "more about Ministry hardware than any induction course ever could."

### Mrs Elspeth Chain
- **Looks:** "In her late sixties, silver hair pinned back, in a dark coat and sensible shoes... Short
  and compact, built like a woman who had spent decades winning small arguments with tradesmen by
  simply outlasting them, and she led with her chin the way other people lead with an opening
  sentence." Wears spectacles. Carries an umbrella "that has seen things" (Cast of Characters) —
  treated throughout as a weapon/professional instrument. (Ch1)
- **Sound:** "Low and dry, built... to carry the length of a queue without ever once needing to be
  raised for it." Later, after loss: "the same dry instrument it had always been, only smaller, as if
  something had been spent." (Ch1, Ch17)
- **Smell:** Not described.
- **Attitude:** A citizen, not a professional of any kind — and the book's moral centre. Does not
  require the world to make sense before proceeding through it. Formidable, unsentimental, dry to the
  point of weaponised. Her sister Margaret is the reason any of this matters.

## Ministry Officials & Other Named Characters

### Detective Inspector Albert Priddy
- **Looks:** "Compact, tweed-jacketed, and carried an umbrella he plainly considered a professional
  instrument rather than a concession to weather." Carries a black, elastic-banded notebook whose
  corners are "exactly as soft with handling" as Lolly's own. (Ch4, Ch17)
- **Sound:** Built for dictation, not conversation, "as though every sentence to come was already halfway to being a statement." (Ch4)
- **Smell:** "Wet wool and boot polish, the smell of a man permanently between a rained-on beat and a
  report not yet written." (Ch4)
- **Attitude:** CID. Investigates the impossible by entirely reasonable methods, and closes the file
  honestly when they don't hold up. A classical investigator inside a quantum plot; his running
  subplot is built on Rashomon-style contradictory witness statements. Genuinely respects Lolly's
  notebook by the end.

### Alistair Venn
- **Looks:** "A dark coat of almost expensive sobriety, with a narrow tie and a face that looked as
  though it had once tried charm and settled, after a risk assessment, on procedure." Carries a slim
  case of brushed metal (later teal) and a card badge with a silver spiral. (Ch4)
- **Sound:** Polished, procedural — "the polished smile of someone whose job consisted of describing
  intolerable things as procedural necessities." (Ch3-4)
- **Smell:** Not described ("was not damp, which only served to make the rest of him more offensive" —
  i.e., defined by an absence).
- **Attitude:** Senior Review Officer, Continuity. "Believes, sincerely and without malice, that
  reality is a filing problem" (Cast of Characters). The Ch3-4 antagonist; forces premature collapse
  on Mrs Chain's house object by object.

### Mr Voller
- **Looks:** Not described physically. Characterised entirely through gesture: "straightened his
  cuffs, which is what such men do instead of praying, having long ago misfiled the latter as an
  external dependency." (Ch8)
- **Sound / Smell:** Not described.
- **Attitude:** Vault custodian; believes he holds an impossible "reference copy" of pre-simplification
  reality. Not malicious, just credentialed past the point of noticing his own error. Reassigned to
  Backup Standards in the Epilogue, where he becomes (ironically, sincerely) a stickler for stated
  margins of error.

### Crispin Vale
- **Looks / Sound / Smell:** Never described — he is almost entirely offstage, known through the
  directive he signs and the consequences that follow.
- **Attitude:** Signs Directive 11-C "without reading the second page" (Cast of Characters). Later
  testifies for eleven days against the very directive he signed; scapegoated for the disorder the
  recovery causes rather than credited for making it lawful. Goes home to a garden in Woking.

### Miss Adaeze Pike
- **Looks:** A "young woman from Certification," carrying "a laptop, a lanyard, and the specific
  brightness of a person doing well within a system she had not yet examined." (Ch9)
- **Sound / Smell:** Not described.
- **Attitude:** Certification officer. Builds the (impossible) Directive 11-C spreadsheet in good
  faith, then — under Fainrose's questioning — admits in writing that Column B is fabricated, at real
  professional cost. One of the book's clearest moments of individual moral courage.

### Mrs Prosser
- **Looks:** Comes out "wiping her hands on an apron which suggested that she had left something on a
  stove from sheer bureaucratic duty." (Ch10)
- **Sound:** Not separately described.
- **Smell:** "Coal smoke and simmering stew, with the specific damp-wool note of someone who had come
  outside without a coat for the ninth night running." (Ch10)
- **Attitude:** Runs Outer Fenwick's moon-observation rota. Treats an absurd cosmic bureaucracy with
  the flat, put-upon competence of a woman managing the bins. Has written to the Ministry nine times
  and "would like that on record."

### Mr Pilbeam
- **Looks / Sound / Smell:** Not described — purely comic-administrative presence.
- **Attitude:** Chairs the Board of Inquiry "with the weary patience of a man who has chaired several
  universes' worth of them." Keeps an unauthorised terrier, Nelson, who attends anyway and treats
  every object in the room "as a reasonable romantic prospect." Understands perhaps a third of any
  given meeting but has the unerring bureaucratic instinct to know when a decision is forming.

## The Physicist-Consultants

*(Real twentieth-century physicists, lightly disguised — see "A Word on Who Is Who." Names below are
the in-book identities only.)*

### Albrecht Eilstein *(disguised Einstein)*
- **Looks:** "A great disorganised nimbus of white hair, a moustache of magnificent indifference, a
  cardigan that had outlived several arguments, and no shoes. His face was one everyone in the room
  had seen on posters and school laboratory walls." Casts no shadow, has no reflection. (Ch11)
- **Sound:** Ghost of an accent too old for any country still drawing its own borders, "and a courtesy so complete it was faintly alarming." (Ch11)
- **Smell:** "Very faintly, of spent matches and photographic developer." (Ch11)
- **Attitude:** "The oldest consequence" — in the story he is a vampire who feeds on discarded quantum branches, not
  blood ("Blood is a courier"). The cast list does not say vampire and does not say what he feeds on.
  It says he is very old and arrives when invited. Excellent table manners, unsettling honesty. Genuinely alarmed by
  Phase Two despite (because of) his own appetite for collapse. Needs to be invited in. Also the
  subject of a running mundane-explanation joke: Jago claims he already asked, years earlier ("in a
  stairwell in '19"), and got told the pallor is office fluorescents and the Bloody Marys are just a
  vodka preference — nobody who's met him actually believes the deflection. (Ch11)

### The Duc de Broccoli *(disguised Louis de Broglie)*
- **Looks:** "Slight, elderly, and dressed with an exactness that had gone out of fashion so long ago
  it had returned as a moral position: a dark suit of beautiful cut, a stiff collar, a narrow grey
  tie, and gloves. He held a hat." His handshake is "quite cold" and never warms. (Ch12)
- **Sound:** "Slow and exact," which makes Lolly realise how quickly everybody else has been speaking. (Ch12)
- **Smell:** "Something old... beeswax, or cedar, or a wardrobe shut a very long time ago." (Ch12)
- **Attitude:** Explains pilot-wave theory in the language of Mrs Chain's wallpaper. Patient almost to
  the point of stillness; describes his own certainty about unproven physics as "patience," not faith.

### Werner Heisenburger *(disguised Werner Heisenberg)*
- **Looks:** "Tall, hollow-cheeked, charcoal from collar to cuff, his posture that of someone who had spent decades being the cleverest person in dangerous rooms. He carried nothing." (Ch13)
- **Sound:** "The same flat monotone he brought to every sentence, each word given exactly the same
  weight as the last, as though emphasis were a variable he had long ago set to zero." (Ch13)
- **Smell:** Deliberately none: "He carried no scent either — no coffee, no chalk dust, nothing... his
  was the one presence that gave nothing away." (Ch13)
- **Attitude:** "Declines to speculate about anything he cannot measure; formalism, not foundations;
  the difference is the entire difference" (Cast of Characters). Refuses ontology, trusts only the
  dial.

### Max Tengelman *(disguised Max Tegmark)*
- **Looks:** "Younger by decades, sandy-haired, tablet in one hand and coffee in the other... a
  rumpled energy, as though he had dressed mid-argument with himself: collar done up one hole wrong,
  shirt untucked on one side." (Ch13)
- **Sound:** Fast, run-on, enthusiastic — arrives mid-sentence.
- **Smell:** "Burnt coffee and the faint ozone tang of a tablet that had been running hot in his
  pocket all morning." (Ch13)
- **Attitude:** "Cheerfully believes in all of it, all at once" (Cast of Characters) — many-worlds,
  sincerely. Will put a local objection in the minutes when corrected; the only honest courtesy his
  interpretation can offer a grieving person.

### Jack Bellboy *(disguised John Bell)*
- **Looks:** "A smallish man with a red beard going white, in a jumper, with the bright unblinking
  gaze of somebody who has spent thirty years being polite to people he considers sloppy." (Ch13)
- **Sound:** Belfast worn smooth by decades of being right in rooms that resented it. Brings a number, not an interpretation. (Ch13)
- **Smell:** Not described (he is, notably, dead — "deceased, of Belfast, and still the most dangerous
  man in the room," per the Cast of Characters).
- **Attitude:** Brings a number instead of an interpretation. No patience for theories in which nobody
  can ever be wrong. The character least interested in being liked and most interested in being
  checkable.

### Eduard von Wittenberg *(disguised Edward Witten)*
- **Looks:** "A slight man in his seventies, entirely bald... a pleasant, unemphatic face and the
  mildest possible manner." Writes with a pencil "resharpened so many times... it was barely the
  length of a finger." (Ch14)
- **Sound:** Speaks without competing with the room, so that she has to come to the sentence. The size of the claim does the volume. (Ch14)
- **Smell:** "Old paper and cold radiator dust — the kind of smell that settles into a man after
  decades spent among books." (Ch14)
- **Attitude:** "Eleven dimensions, no experimental confirmation, and says so" (Cast of Characters).
  Refuses to oversell his own field; came to physics from history, "as did de Broccoli, as did
  Bellboy, in his fashion."

### Erich Schrottfinger *(disguised Erwin Schrödinger)*
- **Looks:** "Very old and very Viennese, in a bow tie that had been tied by somebody who cared, with
  a great deal of white hair and an air of enormous and slightly disreputable charm." Carries an
  empty wooden box "about the size of a hatbox, with air holes." (Ch18)
- **Sound:** "Still carried Vienna in the vowels, seven decades and an ocean removed, roughened by a
  lifetime of lecture halls into something half gravel, half delight." (Ch18)
- **Smell:** "Faintly and not unpleasantly, of pipe tobacco he no longer smoked and coffee he clearly
  still drank." (Ch18)
- **Attitude:** Delivers the book's cosmological closing argument as a "Prologue" placed at the back
  on principle. Charming, disreputable, allergic to being taken as a mystic about his own famous cat.

## Places

### The Ministry of Eventualities (Coldharrow HQ) / Regional Transit Reconciliation Annex
Coldharrow is a district of west London that kept its own name. The Annex window looks onto that weather. The A40 runs west out of Coldharrow and on out of London, past the Uxbridge road. Do not treat Coldharrow, London, and Uxbridge as three maps.
"A respectable soot-softened stone building in Coldharrow, between a Department of Minor Infrastructure and an
office responsible for the licensing of ceremonial ladders. Its sign was small. Its corridors were
long. Its carpets had the peculiar brown colour adopted by institutions that have survived several
changes of government and are now too embarrassed to ask for anything." (Prologue) Lolly's own
office: "one window, four filing stacks, and the temperament of a damp envelope" — smells of
"photocopier toner and lukewarm tea" on a good day, "wet coats" on a bad one. (Ch1)

### Queue Management (basement hall)
A former atrium "since persuaded to regret its openness: chrome stanchions, retractable belts, lanes
of subtle cruelty under a high ceiling that had given up on grandeur." Smells of "hand sanitiser, hot
plastic from the ticket printer, and the flat, coffee-less anxiety of people who had been standing
there long enough to consider it a personality trait." (Ch2)

### Mrs Chain's house — Twenty-Two Elm Grove (called Chain Terrace only during the crisis), Equivalent Residence Unit 7B
A "modest cream-coloured house with blue trim" among identical semi-detached houses with fractionally
wrong numbers and identical stone birdbaths — "as if someone had described cheerfulness to a machine
and accepted the first draft." (Ch3) Interior: scentlessly immaculate, "the specific scentlessness of
a show home." (Ch3) Later, her actual final home: **Twenty-Two Elm Grove**, restored after the Ministry's Chain Terrace plates came down, which smells of
"instant coffee and furniture polish, applied in that order and recently." (Ch17)

### The Institute for Unhelpful Clarity (Fainrose's home)
Down a narrow lane behind a wrought-iron gate that "appeared where Lolly would have sworn there had
been no gate," reading *Visitors by prior argument only. No refunds for conclusions.* "A long, low
brick house with a slate roof and the defensive air of a property that had spent years discouraging
governments." Brass armillary sphere on the lawn; a boot-scraper stamped CALIBRATED; a faint
continuous electronic whine "pitched just high enough to make visitors feel quietly reprimanded
without knowing why." Interior smells of "paper, tea, solder, and intellectual impatience." (Ch5)

### Regional Compression Annex
Behind two fences and a camera mast, marked *CIVIC INFRASTRUCTURE HARMONISATION SITE — No public
gravitation beyond this point.* "Walls were pale, windowless, and faintly curved in a way that
suggested either modern design or local damage to geometry." Corridors smell of "photocopier toner
and hot electronics, and beneath that... a faint ozone tang." (Ch7)

### Outer Fenwick
A terraced-housing ridge above Sub-District 6 where residents sit out nightly on folding chairs with
flasks and clipboards, "the settled patience of racegoers told that the principal attraction was
running late," waiting for the moon to be sufficiently observed to commit to existing. (Ch10)

### Coldharrow Street library (reading room)
A branch library reading room overlooking a "shut-down carpet showroom" car park — the setting for
both von Wittenberg's (Ch14) and Schrottfinger's (Ch18) scenes. Quiet enough that a car alarm outside
is a notable event.

### Board of Inquiry conference room
Smells of "coffee going bitter in its pot on the sideboard and the flat photocopier-toner air of a
space that had hosted a great many inquiries and concluded almost none of them." (Ch13)

### Sub-District 6
The frozen district under Vale's continuity order, holding the local grid steady while 4C is argued
about elsewhere — described from the ridge above as "a grid of windows all lit the same flat yellow,
not one of them changing." (Ch10) Site of the climactic Bell test and the coupling cut (Ch14-16).

### Singularity Asset 4C
Never physically described as a place one stands in (it is a small engineered black hole in regional
infrastructure) — known entirely through its instrument readings: a descending mass curve, a rising
temperature curve, and Fainrose's line that listening to it via von Wittenberg's team is "an ear held
against a burning wall." (Ch5-6, Ch16)
