Style. Flat, warm, stylized illustration — not photorealistic, not moody chiaroscuro. Think animated-film-poster energy: clean shapes, confident outlines, a limited but vivid palette, expressive faces. Closer in spirit to a Discworld or Hitchhiker's Guide cover, or a Pixar poster, than to literary upmarket art. Warm parchment/cream ground rather than black, with one or two confident accent colors (teal and amber both read well against cream) rather than the previous cover's gold-on-black officialese.

Composition. Lolly Wren (curly dark hair, cardigan, her ever-present green notebook in hand) and Gideon Wigglesworth (glasses, mid-stumble) sharing one clear comic beat: Gideon knocking into a filing cabinet or a leaning tower of forms, arms windmilling, while Lolly — mid-stride, notebook already open — doesn't even glance up, fully unsurprised. That one beat sells both characters and their dynamic without a word of text. Behind them, a glimpse of an ornate doorway with small background signage reading "MINISTRY OF EVENTUALITIES" (kept small and incidental, not a focal point — this is the in-story department name and can stay as set dressing even though the book's own title changed), and one small impossible detail visible through the doorway — a form hanging mid-air, a second faint shadow, a subtly doubled reflection — hinting at the quantum weirdness without dense text.

Title treatment. "SCHRÖDINGER'S PAPERWORK" in large, confident lettering with personality — a slab serif or a serif with real character, not somber gold officialese — sized and weighted to stay legible once compressed to roughly 150px tall. Beneath it, small: "A Novel of Quantum Bureaucracy." Smaller still: "Lolly Wren's Curious Science Adventures — Book 1."

What to leave off. No character-bio paragraphs, no prop-sign jokes, no laurel-wreath badge text — none of it. Everything that isn't the title, the tagline, the series line, and the illustration goes on the back cover instead.
