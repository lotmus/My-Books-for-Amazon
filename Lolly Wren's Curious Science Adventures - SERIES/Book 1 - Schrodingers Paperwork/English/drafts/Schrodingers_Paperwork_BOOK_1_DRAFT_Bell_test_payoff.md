## Draft A — new opening for Chapter 15

> The fault took a day to find and an afternoon to starve.
>
> What the four detectors had been agreeing about, in the end, was the time. Every timing
> rack the Ministry owned drew its clock from the same civic distribution — the annex, the
> compression site, the Continuity Review scanners, and now Fainrose's borrowed rig in the
> primary school hall in Sub-District 6. The two ends of a Bell test are meant to be
> strangers to one another. Hers had been keeping time by the same heartbeat, drawing
> their random setting-choices off the same central feed, and taking mains power from the
> same substation, and between them they had produced a beautiful, stable and entirely
> worthless agreement: the apparatus, measuring the Ministry, and subtracting it from
> itself.
>
> "It is the cleanest result I have ever recorded," Fainrose said, "it means nothing
> whatsoever, and had I taken it to a committee they would have funded three more."
>
> Lolly had the shape of it before the explanation finished, because by then she had the
> shape of it everywhere. "It's Venn's scanner. The instrument was inside the thing it was
> measuring."
>
> "The instrument was inside the thing it was measuring," Fainrose agreed, "and the
> instrument was ours, so nobody thought to look."
>
> The repair was not clever. It was subtraction, which by now Lolly understood to be the
> only move the whole business had ever actually rewarded. They cut the rig loose from
> anything with a crest on it. Two clocks instead of one — a pair of cheap oscillators
> left to run free and deliberately out of step, one at each station. The setting-choices
> now came off two smoke-detector sources Gideon had signed out of the annex stores, on
> the sound principle that a decaying nucleus has never once waited for a central feed to
> tell it when. Jago drove the second detector rack to the far side of the sub-district
> and left it in a van on the ridge with a car battery and a young man from the annex
> whose entire brief was to change nothing. What the rig did, once it was properly deaf,
> was put the same two questions to a pair of houses at opposite ends of Fainrose's
> lattice — houses the Ministry had surveyed as separate cases, on separate afternoons, in
> separate folders — over and over, in an order neither end could predict, and count how
> often their answers agreed.
>
> They agreed far too often.
>
> "That's the one Bell minded about most," said Bellew, who had come out to the hall to
> watch a trace he had no need to watch, being both dead and certain. "Not the detectors.
> The choices. If something you cannot see is deciding what gets measured *and* which
> measurement gets made, the argument folds and you never learn that it folded. He worried
> about it to the end. So should you."
>
> They ran it clean on the Sunday. The number Bellew had promised in the conference room
> came up a little after four.
>
> Fainrose read it off the timing computer with no expression at all, which was how Lolly
> knew it was real. "Two point four one. Plus or minus three in the last place."
>
> "Ceiling's two," said Bellew.
>
> "The ceiling is two. The threshold we put in front of Pilbeam in writing was two point
> three, error bars clear of it."
>
> "Then you're clear of it." He looked at the trace a moment longer. "A change made in one
> place altering what is true in another, and nothing crossing between. Nine thousand
> houses, surveyed one at a time, answering as one thing. Werner cannot call that
> metaphysics — it is an instrument reading, and instrument readings are the only things
> he has ever accepted. Max cannot call it harmless — it is his instrument reading too.
> There is no interpretation anywhere in the figure. That is precisely why it will hold."
>
> Fainrose did not pack the rig away afterwards. She moved one station indoors, retuned it
> from Bellew's cross-lattice correlation to the district's own internal one, and set it
> to log. "That one stays," she said. "Different pair, different number. It will sit flat
> and stupid for as long as the anchor is still listening to these houses. The morning it
> starts to climb is the morning we have actually let go."
>
> Lolly wrote the legal number in the green notebook, and then the sentence beneath it
> that mattered more:
>
> *You cannot test a thing with an instrument that shares its clock. The apparatus was
> part of the Ministry, so of course it agreed with the Ministry.*
>
> And under that:
>
> *They surveyed nine thousand houses separately. The houses answered as one. That is now
> a measured fact rather than an argument, and it is the only sentence in this entire
> affair that a court cannot talk its way around.*
>
> She telephoned Beatrix at six and was told to say the number. She said it. Beatrix hung
> up without another word, which from Beatrix was effusive.

## Chapter 16 follow-through (closes audit finding C2)

With the planted line in Draft A ("Fainrose did not pack the rig away… *the morning it
starts to climb is the morning we have actually let go*"), the Chapter 16 readout stops
being a second, contradictory "S".

**Current (para 2022):**

> Then the display marked S, which had sat at 1.2 for eleven weeks with the patience of
> something that had given up being interesting, climbed to 2.79 in slightly under forty
> minutes, and stopped.

**Suggested:**

> Then the coherence monitor Fainrose had left running since Sub-District 6 — flat at 1.2
> for eleven weeks, with the patience of something that had given up being interesting —
> climbed to 2.79 in slightly under forty minutes, and stopped.

Now the two numbers are doing different jobs and the physics is consistent:

- **Bellew's cross-lattice S = 2.41** (Ch 15): a Bell violation *between* dwellings the
  Ministry surveyed as separate cases → proves the nine thousand surveys were one act.
  This is the legal instrument.
- **The coherence monitor, 1.2 → 2.79** (Ch 16): the district's *own internal* pair
  correlation. While the anchor is still coupled it is decohering that pair, so the pair
  looks classical (1.2, below the local bound). Cut the coupling and the pair stops being
  monitored by the anchor; its coherence recovers toward the quantum ceiling (2.79, just
  under 2√2 ≈ 2.83). A rise on disconnection is now exactly right, and "clean
  disconnection" means what Fainrose says it means.

Fainrose's line in para 2023 ("That's disconnection. Clean disconnection.") then needs no
change.

## Notes

**Voice / craft**
- Leans on established beats: "subtraction" as the only intervention that works (de
  Brévanne's lesson, Ch 12; "the lie required electricity", Ch 4); "the instrument was
  inside the thing it was measuring" (Venn's scanner, Keinschein's "there is no
  outside"); Bellew "being both dead and certain"; "which from Beatrix was effusive"
  (the book's "which in her case meant…" construction).
- Bellew's closing speech deliberately echoes his Ch 13 promise ("Werner will accept it
  because it's an instrument reading… none of the three of us can wriggle"). Trim it if
  it feels too on-the-nose.
- Mrs Chain is deliberately absent — this is a technical beat, and her Chapter 15 scene
  with Fainrose (the "number" vs "distribution" conversation) lands harder for the
  contrast. Add her only if you want her present for the result.
- Gideon and Jago get concrete jobs consistent with what they already do (Gideon +
  requisition stores; Jago + moving kit off the books).

**Physics (sound)**
- The fault is a real, named class of Bell-test loophole: a shared timing/reference and
  setting-choices drawn from a common feed break the independence the test assumes
  ("freedom of choice" / locality). Bell was in fact most exercised about the
  freedom-of-choice loophole late in his life — Bellew's line is accurate.
- The fix — independent free-running clocks, spatially separated stations on isolated
  power, and setting-choices from radioactive-decay hardware sources — is how real
  experiments close it.
- **S = 2.41 ± 0.03** is a realistic real-world violation (cf. the 2015 loophole-free
  results, ≈ 2.4 with wide error bars); it clears the local bound of 2 and stays under
  Tsirelson's 2√2 ≈ 2.83. The stipulated threshold of 2.3 "with error bars clear of it"
  gives Bellew's "no wriggle."

**What I did not change**
- Chapter 14's ending line and its Notes pointers (Draft A leaves them intact).
- The rest of Chapter 15 (the four-plans scene, the week-8/9 material). This scene only
  supplies the missing foundation under "Beatrix Sloan won the injunction application on
  the Monday morning."
- The Monday/Tuesday injunction wording (audit T1) and the wider calendar (T2–T7) — still
  open. This draft does at least give the Chapter 15 side explicit days (fault Friday,
