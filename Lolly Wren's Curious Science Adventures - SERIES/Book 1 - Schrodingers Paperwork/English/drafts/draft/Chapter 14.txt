# Chapter 14

## The Train That Arrived Before It Left

The corridor ended at Platform Seven.

Derek stopped.

"Of course."

Penny looked at the sign.

**PLATFORM 7**

Trevor looked at the timetable.

"There isn't a Platform Seven."

Derek nodded.

"Excellent."

Trevor stared at him.

"Why is that excellent?"

"Because if there were a Platform Seven, this would be considerably less interesting."

Penny looked down the platform.

There was, in fact, a train waiting.

This was unfortunate.

It was an entirely ordinary Underground train.

Grey.

Damp.

Slightly depressing.

The sort of train that looked as though it had been designed by a committee whose principal objective had been to discourage tourism.

Except for one small problem.

There was no driver.

There were no passengers.

There was no conductor.

There was no staff.

And there was no record of the train on the timetable.

Trevor checked the timetable again.

Nothing.

He checked his phone.

Nothing.

He checked the transport app.

Nothing.

He looked at Derek.

"Perhaps it's cancelled."

Derek looked at the train.

"It's here."

"Yes."

"Therefore it isn't cancelled."

Trevor considered this.

"That's not how cancellations work."

"It is if the train exists."

"Does it?"

Derek smiled.

"Excellent question."

Penny walked toward it.

The destination board read:

**LONDON TERMINUS**

She stopped.

"Where is London Terminus?"

Derek looked at the sign.

"Everywhere."

Trevor sighed.

"That isn't helpful."

"It wasn't intended to be."

Penny looked at the train.

"There's no such station."

Derek nodded.

"Correct."

"Then why does the train say it's going there?"

"Perhaps it's optimistic."

The doors opened.

Nobody moved.

Derek stepped inside.

Penny followed.

Trevor remained on the platform.

Derek looked back.

"Coming?"

Trevor looked at the empty carriage.

"Do you know where it goes?"

"No."

"Do you know who is driving it?"

"No."

"Do you know why it exists?"

"No."

Trevor sighed.

"Fine."

He stepped aboard.

The doors closed.

The train moved.

There was no announcement.

No warning.

No reassuring voice telling them to mind the gap.

It simply accelerated.

Derek looked at his watch.

11:17.

The train clock showed 11:17.

Penny's phone showed 11:17.

Trevor checked his.

11:17.

Everything appeared perfectly normal.

This was, naturally, suspicious.

The train accelerated harder.

Derek watched the carriage.

"Acceleration."

Trevor nodded.

"We can feel it."

"Yes."

"And that means this isn't an inertial frame."

"Correct."

Penny looked out of the window.

"The station is accelerating away from us."

Derek smiled.

"Depending on your frame."

Trevor nodded.

"Right."

The train continued into the tunnel.

Derek took out his notebook.

"Let's keep this simple."

Trevor looked at him.

"That's unusual."

"Special Relativity first."

He wrote:

**INERTIAL FRAME**

Then:

**LORENTZ TRANSFORMATION**

Then:

**PROPER TIME**

Penny nodded.

"The train has its own proper time."

"Yes."

"The passengers would have their own worldlines."

"Yes."

"The station has another."

"Yes."

"And the clocks measure proper time along their respective worldlines."

"Exactly."

Trevor nodded.

"Fine."

The train suddenly slowed.

Derek looked at the clock.

11:17.

The train stopped.

The doors opened.

They stepped onto the platform.

Trevor looked around.

His expression changed.

"This is Platform Seven."

Derek nodded.

"Yes."

"But..."

Trevor pointed down the tunnel.

"We just left here."

"Yes."

"And now we're back."

"Yes."

Trevor checked his phone.

11:18.

Derek checked his watch.

11:18.

Penny checked hers.

11:18.

The station clock read:

**11:17**

Trevor stared.

"So the station clock hasn't advanced."

"No."

"But our clocks have."

"Yes."

"And we were moving relative to the station."

"Yes."

Trevor looked at Derek.

"Time dilation."

Derek smiled.

"Possibly."

"Why possibly?"

"Because we've only observed a difference in clock readings. We still need to understand the geometry of the situation."

Trevor nodded.

"Right."

Penny pointed at the tunnel.

"The train is coming back."

Derek looked.

The same train was approaching.

Trevor frowned.

"But we just got off it."

"Yes."

"It hasn't had time to go anywhere."

Derek watched the train approach.

"That depends on what you mean by time."

Trevor sighed.

"I knew you were going to say that."

The train stopped.

The doors opened.

A second Derek stepped out.

Trevor went completely still.

Penny stared.

Derek stared at Derek.

The second Derek looked at them.

Then at his watch.

Then at the station clock.

"You're late."

The first Derek frowned.

"I haven't gone anywhere."

The second Derek smiled.

"I know."

Trevor looked between them.

"Which one of you is Derek?"

Both Dereks turned toward him.

"Me."

Trevor closed his eyes.

"Of course."

Penny looked at the second Derek.

"Where did you come from?"

"The train."

"Where was the train?"

"Here."

"It wasn't."

"It was."

"You just arrived."

"Yes."

"From where?"

"Here."

Penny stared.

Derek smiled.

"He's right."

Trevor opened his eyes.

"Don't encourage him."

The second Derek looked at the first.

"You're still trying to describe the whole thing with one clock."

Derek nodded.

"Yes."

"Stop."

Derek considered this.

"Fair."

The second Derek pointed toward the station.

"Think locally."

Derek looked around.

"Local inertial frames."

"Exactly."

"Special Relativity applies locally."

"Yes."

"But globally the geometry can be curved."

"Yes."

"So the coordinates can become strange."

"Very."

"But the physical laws remain consistent."

"Exactly."

Trevor looked at Penny.

"Are they always like this?"

Penny smiled.

"Unfortunately."

The second Derek turned to the first.

"You've been asking why the train arrives before it leaves."

"Yes."

"Wrong question."

"What is the right one?"

"Which events are actually connected by a causal curve?"

Derek stared.

Trevor frowned.

Penny nodded.

"Causality."

"Exactly."

Derek looked down the tunnel.

"Light cones."

"Yes."

"The causal future and causal past of an event."

"Yes."

"And events outside the light cone are spacelike separated."

"Exactly."

Trevor nodded slowly.

"So two observers can disagree about which of two spacelike-separated events happened first."

"Yes."

"But they cannot use that to send information into their own past."

"Correct."

Derek smiled.

"Well, that's settled."

The second Derek looked at him.

"For now."

Then he disappeared.

Trevor jumped.

"Where did he go?"

Derek looked at the empty platform.

"Virtuality."

Penny nodded.

"Obviously."

Trevor stared at them.

"I am beginning to dislike that word."

The train doors closed.

The train departed.

This time Derek watched carefully.

He took measurements.

Speed.

Acceleration.

Distance.

Clock readings.

Everything appeared ordinary.

Then the train emerged from the tunnel.

They were above ground.

Rain streaked across the windows.

London appeared.

Grey buildings.

Grey sky.

Grey people.

Grey everything.

Trevor looked relieved.

"Back to normal."

A raindrop hit the window.

It travelled downward.

Then stopped.

Then moved sideways.

Then went upward.

Trevor stared.

"No."

Penny leaned closer.

"Interesting."

Derek smiled.

"Very."

The droplet split into two.

One part moved down.

The other moved up.

Trevor looked at Derek.

"That isn't normal."

"No."

"Coordinate effect?"

"Possibly."

"Geometry?"

"Possibly."

"Train?"

"Possibly."

"Rain?"

"Possibly."

Trevor sighed.

"You're enjoying this."

"Immensely."

The train slowed.

Derek checked his watch.

11:18.

The train clock:

11:18.

Penny's phone:

11:18.

The station clock outside:

12:04.

They stopped.

The doors opened.

Everyone stepped out.

The rain was falling normally again.

Trevor looked at the station clock.

12:04.

He looked at his phone.

11:18.

Then back at the clock.

12:04.

"Now what?"

Derek smiled.

"Now we have a problem."

Penny nodded.

"Good."

Trevor stared at her.

"Why is that good?"

"Because we know the discrepancy is not just between two clocks."

Derek nodded.

"The station itself is assigning different temporal coordinates."

Trevor frowned.

"So which time is correct?"

Derek looked at him.

"Both."

Trevor sighed.

"Of course."

Derek pointed toward a bench.

A newspaper lay there.

He picked it up.

The headline read:

**RELATIVITY PROVED WRONG**

Trevor nodded.

"Finally."

Derek turned the page.

Underneath was another headline:

**RELATIVITY PROVED RIGHT**

Penny laughed.

"That's more like it."

Derek turned the page again.

A third headline:

**RELATIVITY PROVED BOTH**

Trevor stared.

"That's impossible."

Derek looked at him.

"No."

Trevor waited.

Derek smiled.

"It's journalism."

Penny laughed.

They sat on the bench.

Derek spread the newspaper across his knees.

"Let's sort this out."

Trevor nodded.

"Please."

Derek drew a spacetime diagram.

"An event is represented as a point in spacetime."

He drew another point.

"Different observers can assign different coordinates to those events."

Penny nodded.

"Because their frames of reference differ."

"Yes."

He drew light cones.

"The speed of light defines the causal structure."

Trevor looked at the diagram.

"If two events are inside one another's light cones..."

"They can be causally connected."

"And everyone agrees on their causal order."

"Yes."

"But if they're outside one another's light cones..."

"They're spacelike separated."

"And different observers can disagree about which happened first."

"Exactly."

Trevor smiled.

"That explains the train."

Derek nodded.

"Potentially."

Trevor frowned.

"Why potentially?"

"Because our train is also moving through curved spacetime."

"So General Relativity matters."

"Yes."

"And then the coordinates can become even stranger."

"Yes."

Penny pointed at the newspaper.

"So the headline could appear before the article that caused it."

Derek looked at her.

"Not quite."

"Why?"

"Because information cannot travel outside the causal structure."

"So no actual message from the future."

"Correct."

"Just a strange coordinate description."

"Exactly."

Trevor nodded.

"That's reassuring."

Derek folded the newspaper.

"Never be reassured too early."

They stood.

The train had disappeared.

There was no record of it on the station board.

No destination.

No arrival.

No departure.

Nothing.

Trevor looked at Derek.

"Where did it go?"

Derek looked at the rails.

"I don't know."

Penny smiled.

"That's a first."

Derek ignored her.

He crouched beside the track.

There was something on the rail.

A small brass plate.

He picked it up.

It was engraved with a single number:

**11:17**

Trevor stared.

"Again."

Derek nodded.

"Again."

Penny looked at the plate.

"Perhaps 11:17 isn't a time."

Derek looked at her.

"What else could it be?"

"An event."

Derek went very still.

Penny continued.

"Something happens at 11:17."

"Yes."

"But perhaps the clocks aren't telling us when it happens."

Derek nodded slowly.

"They're identifying it."

"Exactly."

Trevor looked between them.

"So 11:17 is an event label."

Derek smiled.

"Maybe."

Penny nodded.

"Then when the geometry changes, the labels become misleading."

Derek looked toward the tunnel.

"Which means we've been asking the wrong question."

Trevor sighed.

"Again?"

Derek smiled.

"Again."

They stood silently for a moment.

Then Derek looked at the sky.

The rain had stopped.

That was unusual.

Very unusual.

Penny noticed it too.

"London has stopped raining."

Trevor looked upward.

"That's worse than the train."

Derek nodded.

"Much worse."

A single drop of water fell from the sky.

It stopped halfway down.

Then moved upward.

Then sideways.

Then disappeared.

Derek smiled.

"General Relativity."

Trevor frowned.

"Are you sure?"

Derek looked at him.

"No."

Trevor nodded.

"Well, that's settled."

And somewhere beneath them, the train that had never officially existed began its journey again.

According to one clock, it had already arrived.

According to another, it had not yet departed.

According to the railway timetable, it had never existed.

Which, Derek thought, was probably the most reliable information of all.
