## Premise

The Ministry of Eventualities is a satirical UK civil-service department that handles "events that
have not happened yet." Its "Simplification Initiative" has been forcing ambiguous quantum states
(peoples' memories, houses, routes, identities) into premature, false certainty to make its own
paperwork easier — and dumping the resulting "waste" (suppressed alternatives) into an engineered
black hole, Singularity Asset 4C, which starts evaporating (Hawking radiation) partway through the
book, creating a ticking-clock disclosure problem. Junior officer Lolly Wren investigates a single
citizen's complaint (Mrs Elspeth Chain's sister has been bureaucratically erased) and uncovers the
whole scheme. A sequence of real-physicist-inspired "consultants" (see below) each bring a different
interpretation of quantum mechanics to bear; Lolly refuses to pick one and instead builds a recovery
plan that uses tools from all of them. The anchor is safely decoupled; 308 of 411 pending states are
recovered; the book ends on themes of honest incompleteness ("recoverable, not unharmed") and the
long cosmological future ("There is no lid").

Structure: Prologue + 18 chapters + Epilogue (the story), then an Appendix of 18 physics lectures
(Chapter 14 has two: strings, then entropy and holography), then back
matter (Glossary, Further Reading, Who Is Who, Author's Note, About the Series).

## Style conventions established this project

**Lecture Appendix content (novel-talk limit):** the lectures should stay physics-forward. A good
joke or story reference is fine; pointless character-flavor filler or unfunny bits that don't teach
anything are not. Applies most strictly to the technical subsections — "Going deeper," "Where the
popular version goes wrong," "What sticks" — since those are explicitly the skippable, textbook-voice
parts of each lecture. "What this chapter was actually showing you" is the one subsection where heavy
story-referencing is the whole point (that's its job: connect the plot beat to the physics), so it
gets more latitude. When adding lecture content, prefer a story reference that also teaches the
concept (e.g. Ch1's "sum of amplitudes, not of journeys with umbrellas" line, which explains path
integrals through the image rather than around it) over one that's just continuity flavor or emotional
weight parked in a section meant to be skippable.

**Em-dashes:** reduced throughout as an AI-tell/overused-punctuation pass. KEEP em-dashes only for:
interrupted/cut-off dialogue ("I'm afraid I can't release—"), speech hesitation/self-correction
("That's — yeah."), the Cast of Characters / "A Word on Who Is Who" "Name — role" entries, the
Glossary's "Term — Definition (Chapter N)" entries, and the one protected sentence: "...the other six
must be curled up small — wound so tightly that nothing you or I can do will ever push anything along
them." (restored via forensic archaeology earlier in this project; never re-edit its exact wording).
Convert everything else (narrative asides, ALL-CAPS in-story document headers) to comma/colon/
semicolon/parentheses/period+new-sentence, whichever reads most naturally.

**Dialogue-exchange formatting:** where a quick back-and-forth needs to read as one tight block, put
it in ONE paragraph with a `<w:br/>` line break at every point the speaker changes (not at every
quote boundary — consecutive quotes from the same speaker stay on one line, no break). This removes
the inter-paragraph spacing gap while keeping each statement visually on its own line. Do NOT
space-join into flowing prose. **COMPLETE as of this writing** — applied book-wide across all ~595
candidate paragraphs (originally found via `re.findall('“[^”]*”', text)` count >= 2 on Normal-style
paragraphs); see progress log below for the per-chapter breakdown.

**Dialogue tags:** a tag is only needed where the speaker would not be clear without one (i.e. don't
over-tag once alternation/line-breaks already make it obvious). Where a tag is kept, prefer a good
synonym for "said" over bare "said" — this book's established voice already uses "remarked,"
"observed," "considered," "reflected," "noted," etc. character-appropriately. This is a wording
change (unlike the line-break work), so it is kept in separate, separately reviewable commits, one
chapter at a time. **COMPLETE as of this writing** — 684 total `said` occurrences found book-wide;
per-chapter counts and completion logged below. Working rule established during this pass: leading
tags (start of a turn) and mid-sentence split tags ("X," she said, "Y") are normally kept (just the
verb is swapped) since removing a mid-split requires merging the sentence, which is a bigger edit
than a tag swap; trailing tags at the end of an already firmly alternating exchange, with no adverb
or extra clause attached, are removed outright (with the quote's internal comma changed to a period).
A tag is also removed when the speaker was already named in the sentence immediately before the quote
(true double redundancy), merging the split quote into one sentence. Narrative uses of "said" that
aren't attached to a live quote (e.g. "of what he'd said earlier") are left untouched.

**Headings:** Amazon Ember font, 18pt, bold, color `#0C447C`, centered. Applies to all Heading 1
(chapter/section titles) and Heading 2 (in-appendix "Chapter N: Lecture: Title") paragraphs.

**Body font:** Bookerly.

**In-story documents/announcements** (ALL-CAPS lines like `COMPLAINT TYPE: TRANSIT DISCREPANCY`,
Ministry memo headers, terminal readouts, signage): indented one tab (0.5in = the document's own
default tab stop), Normal style, not bold.

**Physics-lecture template** (each of the 19 lectures in the Appendix must contain, in this order):
`You need first:` → `When you walk out, you should be able to:` → `Start here.` → `The idea, step
by step` (numbered list) → `In one breath.` → `What this chapter was actually showing you.` →
`Where the popular version goes wrong.` → `Going deeper (skip freely).` → `What sticks` (bulleted) →
`One-line summary.` → `Stop. Before you go on —` → `Say it to Mrs Chain. She will not be impressed
by the word 'basis.' She will be impressed if the sentence is true.` → `If you cannot, you do not
have it yet. That is allowed. Reread. Then continue.` All 19 lectures were verified/repaired to this
exact template earlier in this project.

**Stop-line addressee (changed from the original "funny, smart, hot woman"):** per an editorial
review, the stop-check line now addresses Mrs Chain instead — same "explain it in plain language or
you don't have it yet" function, but ties the ritual to the book's actual emotional core instead of
an ungrounded hypothetical. Applied identically across all 19 lectures plus the "How to sit in this
class" intro paragraph in the Appendix header. **COMPLETE.**

**Physics accuracy:** every formula, date, and named-attribution in the lectures has been checked
against real physics history and confirmed accurate (Hawking 1974, Bell 1964, de Broglie 1924, the
2015 loophole-free Bell tests, the 2022 Nobel Prize, the fine-structure constant ≈1/137.036, etc.).
Never fabricate a citation in the Further Reading/Bibliography section — every entry there has been
verified to be a real book with the correct author/publisher/year.
