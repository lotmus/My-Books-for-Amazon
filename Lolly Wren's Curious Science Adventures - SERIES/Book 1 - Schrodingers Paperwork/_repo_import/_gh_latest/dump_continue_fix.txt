===== LESSON 6 2345-2395 =====
2345|• A quantum state needs a law of change as well as a list of possible measurement results.
2346|• The Schrödinger equation gives smooth, deterministic and reversible evolution for an isolated system.
2347|• Measurement is physical interaction, not audience participation.
2348|• The measurement problem concerns definite outcomes, not a gap in ordinary quantum evolution.
2350|One-line summary. Quantum theory is not strange because nothing obeys a law; it is strange because the law is so orderly until a record is made.
2351|Stop. Before you go on — Where does the strangeness of quantum theory enter, if the Schrödinger evolution itself is orderly?
2352|Say it to Mrs Chain. She will not blink at ‘orderly’; she wants to know what happens the moment the order stops.
2353|Struggling is not failing here. It only means: reread, then continue.
2354|Lesson 6: Hawking Radiation
2356|← Back to Chapter 6: The Object Is Not Mislaid
2357|You need first: Lessons 1 and 5. Lesson 3 if you want records.
2358|When you walk out, you should be able to: Link falling mass to rising temperature for an evaporating black hole, and name why information loss is a problem.
2360|Start here. Picture a very large block of ice in a quiet, cold room. It melts so slowly that, for a long while, it looks permanent. A smaller block has less staying power: as it dwindles, the conditions that rendered it seem solid no longer protect it in the same way, and the ending becomes noticeably brisker. A black hole is not ice, and one should resist improving the analogy past its useful life, but the change of pace matters.
2362|The idea, step by step
2363|1. A black hole is a region whose gravity is so strong that, once past its event horizon, light cannot return to a distant observer.
2364|2. In quantum field theory on curved spacetime, a distant observer predicts that a black hole emits faint thermal radiation, called Hawking radiation.
2365|3. Radiation carries energy away, and energy has mass-equivalent, so an isolated black hole loses mass.
2366|4. A smaller black hole has a higher Hawking temperature, so it emits more strongly than a larger one.
2367|5. The loss therefore accelerates as the hole shrinks; large astrophysical holes change absurdly slowly, while a hypothetical small engineered one would be less patient.
2368|6. If the final radiation looks only thermal, we must ask how the detailed quantum information about what formed and entered the hole can still be preserved.
2370|In one breath. A black hole is not perfectly black: quantum physics predicts that it emits thermal radiation and therefore loses mass. The smaller it becomes, the hotter it gets and the faster the loss proceeds, while what happens to the information in everything it swallowed remains a deep problem.
2371|What this chapter was actually showing you. Fainrose’s two curves — Asset 4C’s mass descending and its temperature rising — are the distinctive linked signs of Hawking evaporation. The Ministry has not mislaid a civic object; its black hole is shrinking by law, which is why “within projected envelope” is such a poor comfort. When Lolly notices that thermal radiation does not plainly reveal what fell in, she reaches the black-hole information problem. Mrs Chain’s home, the queue records and the overflowed alternatives give that question human stakes: if a system has stored irreplaceable structure in 4C, it matters fiercely whether that structure is recoverable. In the novel, that makes the black hole a deadline rather than merely a scientific curiosity: when the anchor evaporates, information that has not been recovered may no longer be recoverable by any ordinary intervention.
2372|Where the popular version goes wrong. The usual tale says that a pair of “virtual particles” appears at the horizon, one falls in and one escapes. It can gesture towards the answer, but it is only a heuristic and is widely criticised as a literal account; it suggests little particles popping out at one special place and can get the energy bookkeeping badly wrong. The better framing is about quantum fields in curved spacetime. What counts as an empty vacuum, and even what counts as a particle, is not observer-independent there; comparing the field’s distant past and distant future around a black hole yields radiation for a far-away observer. No little horizon factory is required. A separate, more calculable derivation treats the process as quantum tunnelling — the same barrier-crossing mechanism behind alpha decay and the scanning tunnelling microscope, where a particle has some probability of crossing a region classical physics says it cannot reach — applied to the horizon itself; it reproduces Hawking’s temperature exactly, with no factory and no story about where the pair was born.
2374|Going deeper (skip freely). Hawking’s 1974 calculation applied quantum field theory to a black-hole spacetime and found a thermal spectrum at infinity. In words, the temperature is inversely proportional to the mass: double the mass and halve the temperature.
2375|TH = ℏc3 / (8πGMkB).

2376|Put a number on it: for one solar mass, that formula gives a Hawking temperature of roughly 60 billionths of a kelvin — colder than the cosmic microwave background it sits in, which is why no astrophysical black hole has ever been caught evaporating. In the semiclassical treatment, the evaporation time of an isolated, non-rotating, uncharged black hole scales as tevap ∝ M3. That calculation creates the information problem: quantum evolution normally preserves distinctions between initial states, but exactly thermal final radiation seems not to display them. Most modern work expects information to be preserved, yet the complete mechanism requires quantum gravity and is not settled in a simple textbook picture.
2378|What sticks
2379|• Hawking radiation is a predicted thermal emission from black holes.
2380|• Smaller isolated black holes are hotter and evaporate faster as they lose mass.
2381|• The “particle pair at the horizon” story is a limited heuristic, not the mechanism to take literally.
2382|• Black-hole evaporation raises the question of how quantum information is preserved.
2384|One-line summary. Asset 4C is not going missing; it is becoming hotter, smaller, and harder to forgive.
2385|Stop. Before you go on — Why is “within projected envelope” a useless comfort if Asset 4C is evaporating by law?
2387|Say it to Mrs Chain. Tell her Asset 4C is not ‘within projected envelope’ — it is evaporating, on a schedule, by law.
2389|If that stumped you, you are not behind — you are simply not done. Reread, then go on.

2577|Lesson 12: Pilot-Wave Theory
2579|← Back to Chapter 12: Standing in the Wave
2580|You need first: Lessons 1–11.
2581|When you walk out, you should be able to: State de Broglie’s matter-wave idea and the pilot-wave picture: definite positions guided by a real, nonlocal wave.
2583|Start here. Imagine a small toy boat in a canal. The boat is at one particular place, but ripples arriving from different parts of the water can still steer its next turn; the route depends on a wider pattern than the boat alone. The image is not a literal model of an electron, but it earns the central thought: something can have a definite location while being guided by an extended pattern.
2585|The idea, step by step
2586|1. In 1924 Louis de Broglie proposed that material particles have an associated matter wave, extending the already puzzling wave-and-particle behaviour of light.
2587|2. The de Broglie wavelength is shorter for greater momentum, so the wave aspect of a fast macroscopic object is far too fine to notice in daily life.
2588|3. Electrons and other particles produce diffraction and interference patterns, showing that “a particle is only a tiny hard ball” is not an adequate account.
2589|4. Pilot-wave theory, also called de Broglie-Bohm theory, adds a definite particle position at every moment and a real wavefunction that guides its path.
2590|5. The particle occupies one path, but unoccupied or “empty” parts of the guiding wave can still affect its motion and produce interference; the wave does not collapse in this account.
2591|6. The theory is explicitly nonlocal: the guidance of one particle can depend on the wider configuration, including distant entangled particles. That is not a defect concealed by the theory but the reason Bell’s theorem, next chapter, does not rule it out.
2593|In one breath. Matter can show a wave-like pattern, and one interpretation says a particle is always somewhere definite while a real guiding wave directs its motion. That interpretation is deterministic, but its guidance reaches across distance in a way that is explicitly nonlocal.
2595|What this chapter was actually showing you. The Duc de Broccoli gives Lolly an alternative to Eilstein’s account. When he speaks of one actual wallpaper, a wave spread over other possible configurations, and “empty” alternatives that still steer, he is describing pilot-wave theory in the language of Mrs Chain’s house. On that interpretation Mr Venn’s scanner did not conjure a fact out of conscious observation; it physically changed the guiding conditions, while the actual configuration remained definite. 
2596|Where the popular version goes wrong. Matter waves do not mean that an electron is a miniature ripple of water, nor that standard quantum mechanics has simply been replaced by a classical hidden mechanism. Pilot-wave theory is a serious interpretation that reproduces the ordinary quantum statistics under its usual assumptions, but it pays for definite particle positions with genuine nonlocality. Bell’s theorem rules out local hidden-variable accounts, not every possible hidden-variable account; pilot-wave theory survives precisely because it does not pretend to be local. The guiding wave is a physical part of the theory, not a record waiting for a conscious observer to read.
2598|Going deeper (skip freely). De Broglie’s 1924 doctoral thesis proposed the relation
2599|
λ = h / p
2600|where λ is the matter wavelength, h is Planck’s constant, and p is momentum. Electron diffraction in the late 1920s made the wave aspect impossible to dismiss; de Broglie’s idea earned the recognition that follows being right about something that strange. Bohm later gave pilot-wave theory its modern general form: a deterministic guiding law once the initial positions and wavefunction are fixed, with the usual probabilities arising from an unknown distribution of those positions. In the normal quantum-equilibrium form, it makes the same tested predictions as standard non-relativistic quantum mechanics, so it remains an interpretation rather than an experimentally established replacement.
2602|What sticks
2603|• Matter has a de Broglie wavelength and can produce wave-like diffraction and interference.
2604|• Pilot-wave theory gives particles definite positions guided by a non-collapsing real wave.
2605|• Empty parts of the guiding wave can affect the particle that is actually present.
2606|• Pilot-wave theory is nonlocal, which is why Bell’s theorem does not exclude it.
2608|One-line summary. A pilot-wave particle is always somewhere, but the whole wave, including its empty possibilities, helps decide where it goes next.

2617|When you walk out, you should be able to: State Bell’s theorem: local hidden variables cannot reproduce the observed correlations; name what a CHSH violation means for administration.
2619|Start here. Imagine two friends who take jobs at opposite ends of a city, one at each of two restaurants. Each restaurant asks its server, at random, whether to offer tea or coffee. The friends have agreed in advance exactly how to answer either question, but cannot phone one another after they leave home. Their answers can match with real cleverness, yet there is a maximum cleverness possible when all the agreement was packed into the plan at breakfast. If the matches are better than that maximum, the friends did not merely bring a local plan.
2621|The idea, step by step
2622|1. A pair of particles can be prepared in an entangled state, meaning that separate measurements show a pattern of joint results that cannot be reduced to two independent descriptions.
2623|2. A hidden-variable account says each particle carries additional, pre-existing instructions that determine its result for every measurement that might be chosen.
2624|3. Locality adds that the result here cannot depend on which measurement is freely chosen far away, except through influences no faster than light.
2625|4. Bell showed that local pre-written instructions put a numerical ceiling on the correlations between the two sets of results.
2626|5. The CHSH test uses four alternative settings, chosen after the pair has separated, and experiments find correlations above that ceiling.
2627|6. Each result seen at either end remains unpredictable on its own, so the shared pattern cannot be turned into a faster-than-light telephone.
2630|In one breath. Bell’s theorem says that if distant things carry pre-written local answers, their joint results can only agree up to a fixed limit. Experiments exceed that limit, so nature cannot be explained by local hidden variables, even though it still cannot be used to send a message faster than light.
2631|What this chapter was actually showing you. Jack Bellboy gives Mr Pilbeam a number rather than a favourite account of reality. The Ministry calls its surveys of nine thousand dwellings separate local acts; Lolly sees that a Bell-style test across Fainrose’s correlation lattice can test that claim. Heisenburger, Tengelman and Bellboy disagree about what quantum mechanics means, but all three can be bound in advance by the reading from the timing rack in Sub-District 6. Mrs Chain’s correction matters too: even if Tengelman’s branches are part of an interpretation, harm in her branch is not fashioned cost-free by an account of other branches.
2632|Where the popular version goes wrong. The slogan is not that Bell proved that “nothing is real”, nor that a conscious observer reaches across space and changes a distant particle. A measurement is settled by the apparatus, not by anyone’s gaze. Bell’s result rules out the combination of local influence and pre-existing hidden instructions under the usual assumption that the setting choices are not secretly coordinated with the source. It does not rule out every hidden-variable theory: the pilot-wave theory met in Chapter 12 has definite particle positions but is explicitly nonlocal, and so it survives Bell’s theorem. Nor does a Bell violation settle the competition among Copenhagen-style accounts, many-worlds, and pilot-wave theory; those interpretations are not currently experimentally distinguished.
2634|Going deeper (skip freely). Bell’s theorem was a direct answer to a 1935 challenge from Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen — the “EPR paradox” — who argued that if quantum mechanics were complete, a measurement here should not be able to fix the value of a property over there, and that the theory must therefore be incomplete. In Bell’s 1964 argument, and in the later CHSH form used in most modern tests, each side chooses one of two settings, a, a′ and b, b′. If the outcomes are recorded as +1 or −1, a local-hidden-variable model must obey
2635|
|S| = |E(a,b) + E(a,b′) + E(a′,b) − E(a′,b′)| ≤ 2
