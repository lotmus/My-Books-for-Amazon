It can gesture towards the answer, but it is only a heuristic and is widely criticised as a literal account; it suggests little particles popping out at one special place and can get the energy bookkeeping badly wrong. The better framing is about quantum fields in curved spacetime. What counts as an empty vacuum, and even what counts as a particle, is not observer-independent there; comparing the field’s distant past and distant future around a black hole yields radiation for a far-away observer. No little horizon factory is required. A separate, more calculable derivation treats the process as quantum tunnelling — the same barrier-crossing mechanism behind alpha decay and the scanning tunnelling microscope, where a particle has some probability of crossing a region classical physics says it cannot reach — applied to the horizon itself; it reproduces Hawking’s temperature exactly, with no factory and no story about where the pair was born. Picture a locked London garden square: railings, a key, a view you are not entitled to. Classically the grass on the other side stays other people’s grass. Quantum-mechanically a small amplitude leaks through anyway, which is the barrier-crossing this derivation applies to the horizon.
2375|Going deeper (skip freely). Hawking’s 1974 calculation applied quantum field theory to a black-hole spacetime and found a thermal spectrum at infinity. In words, the temperature is inversely proportional to the mass: double the mass and halve the temperature.
2376|TH = ℏc3 / (8πGMkB).

2377|Put a number on it: for one solar mass, that formula gives a Hawking temperature of roughly 60 billionths of a kelvin — colder than the cosmic microwave background it sits in, which is why no astrophysical black hole has ever been caught evaporating. In the semiclassical treatment, the evaporation time of an isolated, non-rotating, uncharged black hole scales as tevap ∝ M3. That calculation creates the information problem: quantum evolution normally preserves distinctions between initial states, but exactly thermal final radiation seems not to display them. Most modern work expects information to be preserved, yet the complete mechanism requires quantum gravity and is not settled in a simple textbook picture.

2660|The idea, step by step
2661|1. String theory replaces pointlike fundamental particles with extraordinarily small one-dimensional strings whose different vibrations would look like different particles.
2662|2. The mathematics naturally includes a vibration with the properties expected of a graviton, a proposed quantum carrier of gravity, but no experiment has confirmed strings or gravitons.
2663|3. Consistent versions require extra spatial dimensions, usually imagined as compact directions too small to notice, and their several formulations are related by dualities.
2664|4. M-theory is the still-incomplete proposed framework linking those formulations in eleven dimensions and allowing extended objects called branes as well as strings.
2665|In one breath. String theory proposes that the basic ingredients of nature may be tiny vibrating threads rather than points, and that several seemingly different string theories are, mathematically, the same underlying object seen from different angles — a broader picture called M-theory. It is an unconfirmed framework, not an established fact.
2668|What this chapter was actually showing you. Von Wittenberg comes to the Coldharrow Street library not with a fix for 4C but with an honest account of his own field: elegant, internally consistent, and never yet confirmed by a single experiment. When Lolly presses him — she has a woman mid-sentence about her own sister, and he is offering violin strings — he agrees with her completely and still does not leave. 2669|Where the popular version goes wrong. “Everything is made of strings” is not an established fact, and neither string theory nor M-theory has experimental confirmation. The mathematics is elegant and internally disciplined, which is not the same thing as being true of our universe.
2671|Going deeper (skip freely). In the mid-1990s, work associated especially with Witten connected five superstring theories as limits of a broader eleven-dimensional proposal called M-theory — five descriptions of one thing, related by dualities that are provable in the simplest cases and, so far, consistent everywhere else: turn a quantity up high in one description and it becomes a different description with that quantity turned low.

2685|← Back to Chapter 14: Eleven Dimensions and a Whisper
2686|You need first: Lesson 14, Part 1, and Lesson 6.
2687|When you walk out, you should be able to: State the area law for black-hole entropy and the holographic idea that a region’s description may live on its boundary.
2690|Start here. A circle drawn on paper can be fully described by a single number written on its edge: the radius. You do not need to describe the whole disc it encloses to know everything geometrical about it — the boundary already carries that information. Holography proposes something far stranger but structurally similar: a full description of a volume of space, gravity included, might be completely captured by a description living one dimension down, on its boundary alone.
2692|The idea, step by step
2693|1. A black hole has entropy, a measure of how many microscopic arrangements can look the same from outside, and its entropy grows with the area of its horizon rather than the volume it encloses.
2694|2. In the mid-1990s, string theorists tested this directly for a special, mathematically convenient class of black hole: they counted the microscopic string-and-brane arrangements and got exactly the predicted entropy back out — a rare case where string theory produced a checkable number instead of another elegant hope.
2695|3. The holographic principle is the proposal, realised exactly in some special mathematical models, that information about a region with gravity can be represented by a lower-dimensional boundary description.
2697|In one breath. A black hole’s information capacity follows the area of its horizon, not its volume — and this area law motivates the holographic principle, the idea that a region’s full description might live on its boundary. Together they turn evaporation from a simple ending into a slow, scrambled disclosure.
2699|What this chapter was actually showing you. This is von Wittenberg’s useful contribution to 4C: Hawking’s area-law entropy makes the horizon a vast ledger of possible states, and holography makes Fainrose’s phrase “an ear held against a burning wall” intelligible. When the engineered black hole evaporates, the story treats its radiation as a time-limited, badly scrambled disclosure channel. The dialogue is right to call that a working hope, not a certificate of recovery. In the story, the horizon is therefore not a storage cabinet but a boundary whose information capacity matters. The deadline in 4C is not administrative theatre.
2700|The Page curve is the grown-up version of “evaporation as scrambled disclosure.” Early radiation looks thermal; later radiation is correlated with it, so the entropy carried by the escaping radiation itself — not the hole’s — rises early and falls again as those correlations accumulate. Whether our universe follows that curve for a real, messy hole is not a thing von Wittenberg will sign. He will only say the horizon is a ledger, not a cupboard, and that ledgers can be read badly.
2701|Where the popular version goes wrong. Holography does not mean that the universe is literally a flat cinema screen, nor that a horizon is a filing cabinet with readable labels on it. The claim is about an exact equivalence between descriptions in certain carefully defined theories. The old picture of a black hole as a pair of particles torn apart at the horizon is only a heuristic; Hawking radiation is better understood as radiation predicted by quantum fields in curved spacetime. Whether the full information in realistic evaporation can be recovered in practice remains an extraordinarily difficult question.
2703|Going deeper (skip freely). Black-hole entropy was proposed in the early 1970s; Hawking’s 1974 calculation fixed its value:
2704|SBH = kBc3A / (4Gℏ)

2705|The equation says that the entropy SBH is proportional to the horizon area A, with the striking factor of one quarter in suitable natural units. A mid-1990s microscopic count for a special supersymmetric class of black holes reproduced that formula exactly — one of string theory’s few genuine numerical victories. The holographic idea, made precise in certain models soon after, says a gravitational region can be fully described by a lower-dimensional theory on its boundary.
2707|What sticks
2708|• Black-hole entropy scales with horizon area, suggesting that a horizon counts genuine microscopic possibilities.
2709|• That mid-1990s microscopic count is a major string-theory success: for a special class of black holes, the entropy formula came out exactly.
2710|• Holography supports the idea that evaporation may preserve information in correlations, even when recovery is impracticable.
2712|One-line summary. In von Wittenberg’s cautious picture, the black hole’s vanishing is not proof of loss but a brief, terrible chance to read a scrambled account.

2869|That combination fuses electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, and relativity into a single figure, and no theory has ever derived it from more basic principles. It has only ever been measured — currently to about ten significant figures — never explained. Richard Feynman called it “one of the greatest damn mysteries of physics,” and admitted nobody knows where the number comes from. Wolfgang Pauli spent decades obsessed with it and died, by a coincidence nobody has ever explained, in hospital room 137.

2871|On the vacuum that is not empty (from Lectures 6 and 14). What a distant observer calls empty space around a hole is not a cleared desk. Quantum fields have a lowest-energy arrangement, and which arrangement counts as “lowest” depends on the observer’s motion and on the spacetime they inhabit. That is why Hawking radiation does not require a particle factory, and why the Unruh effect can warm an accelerating thermometer in a vacuum another observer still calls cold. The Ministry’s phrase for this would be “legacy emptiness.” It is not recommended.
2872|On the number that is a bound, not a score (from Lecture 13). Tsirelson’s 2√2 is not a target for staff appraisals. It is the most quantum mechanics itself will give you in a CHSH test. Local hidden variables cannot pass 2. Nature passes 2 and stops before 4. Any spreadsheet that prints a 4 has not discovered new physics.
