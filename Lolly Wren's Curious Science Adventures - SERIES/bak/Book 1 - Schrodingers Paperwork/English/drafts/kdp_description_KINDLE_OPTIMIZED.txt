SCHRÖDINGER'S PAPERWORK
A Novel of Quantum Bureaucracy

What happens when a government department tries to measure reality with forms?

Lolly Wren works at the Ministry of Eventualities—a place where your arrival can be a filing error, your identity depends on which office opens your file, and paperwork sometimes gets completed before anyone bothers to fill it in.

Then the Ministry launches The Simplification Initiative.

It's supposed to make reality more manageable. Predictable. Rational. Instead, it makes reality impossible.

People exist in contradiction. Identities flip between states. A black hole appears in regional infrastructure and begins evaporating on a municipal timetable. And somewhere in the wreckage, Mrs Elspeth Chain's house has started disagreeing with itself about whether it exists.

The Ministry's solution is, naturally, more paperwork.

But Lolly—a physicist masquerading as a bureaucrat—begins to suspect the problem isn't faulty administration. The Ministry has confused measurement with authority. They've assumed that if reality can be assigned a category, reality must obey it.

Alongside an auditor who refuses impossible certificates, a physicist who is never reassured, a courier who knows everyone's secrets, and an engineer who actually understands how the machines work, Lolly must decode what the Ministry has done, what can honestly be recovered, and what must simply be named as gone.

SCHRÖDINGER'S PAPERWORK is a satirical science-fiction novel about bureaucracy, uncertainty, quantum mechanics, and the inconvenient habit reality has of refusing to cooperate.

And then there is the physics.

Each of the eighteen chapters pairs with an optional lecture in the Appendix—a real class on what the universe actually does. No PhD required. You can read it as pure comedy about paperwork. Or as popular science wrapped in an absurdist adventure. Or both.

Read it as a novel. Take the course. Or do both.

Just don't ask the Ministry where your paperwork went.

It may not have happened yet.
