816|Heading 1|Chapter 5: Dr Fainrose Is Not Reassured
817|Normal|Forty minutes after the interruption of Mrs Elspeth Chain’s domestic convergence, Beatrix Sloan was in an unlicensed grey car with Lolly Wren, Jago Flint, a confiscated Civic Stability Substrate module, one morally indignant umbrella, and a woman whose house was fluctuating between several unsatisfactory versions of itself. This was, by her own private reckoning, witness selection — the third of the only three responses to institutional catastrophe she considered proper, the first being documentation and the second containment, and reserved, as a rule, for special occasions and senior management.
818|Normal|The car belonged to Jago. Or rather, it belonged to a man in Croydon who believed it was in long-term bonded storage, which was not identical but had, Jago maintained, all the practical advantages of ownership without the correspondence.
819|Normal|Lolly sat in the back holding the grey module in both hands, as if it might otherwise begin revising her. Mrs Chain sat beside her with the umbrella across her knees like a constitutional amendment awaiting use. Beatrix occupied the front passenger seat in a state of concentrated disapproval. Jago drove with one hand and the relaxed fatalism of a man who trusted neither maps nor causality.
820|Normal|“Where exactly,” said Lolly, for the third time, “are we going.”
821|Normal|Beatrix did not turn. “Fainrose.”
822|Normal|“That is not a place.”
823|Normal|“No,” said Jago. “It’s worse. It’s a person.”
824|Normal|Mrs Chain looked up. “The name on the device.”
825|Normal|“Yes,” said Beatrix.
826|Normal|Lolly glanced down at the scratched inscription on the module, though she already knew every stroke of it by heart. If found active in residential field, contact S. Fainrose. She was right. She had been trying, without great success, not to dwell on the final sentence. There is something uniquely disheartening about the discovery that a person one has not yet met has already won an argument with the universe.
827|Normal|“Who is she,” asked Mrs Chain.
828|Normal|Beatrix was silent for a moment, which in her case meant the answer required enough accuracy to be annoying. “Former Ministry physicist,” she said at last. “Systems architecture. Interpretive dynamics. Early transition modelling.”
829|Normal|“I’m sorry,” said Lolly. “Are those real subjects.”
830|Normal|“They were funded as if they could be made to sit still in a briefing paper.”
831|Normal|Jago turned left through a yellow light with the lightness of conscience that comes from never having truly recognised traffic law as binding philosophy. “She used to be very fashionable,” he said. “Brilliant, impossible, terrifying in committee. Left after some unpleasantness involving continuity theory and a minister who thought eigenstates were a minority voting bloc.”
832|Normal|Mrs Chain considered this. “And we trust her.”
833|Normal|“No,” said Beatrix.
834|Normal|“Good.”
835|Normal|“We require her.”
836|Normal|“Better.”
837|Normal|They drove west. The city thinned gradually, as though suburban planning had once set out to continue and then lost conviction. Office blocks gave way to terraces, terraces to detached houses, detached houses to stretches of overmanaged green in which municipal trees stood at intervals suggesting budget approval. The morning had developed into one of those bright English afternoons which seem determined, against all meteorological instinct, to deny the possibility of disaster while helping it along socially.
838|Normal|Lolly watched the route display on the dashboard. It altered twice. The first time, their destination changed from Wetherby Lane to Wetherby Approach. The second time it became Fainrose Cottage / Pending Local Address Reconciliation. She sighed. Jago noticed. “You’ve got to stop taking road systems personally.”
839|Normal|“They’ve started first.”
840|Normal|“Fair.”
841|Normal|At length they turned off the main road and followed a narrow lane bordered by hedges, old brick walls, and an atmosphere of concentrated refusal. A wrought-iron gate appeared where Lolly would have sworn there had been no gate half a second earlier. It bore, in flaking black paint, a sign reading: THE INSTITUTE FOR UNHELPFUL CLARITY Visitors by prior argument only No refunds for conclusions Jago braked.
842|Normal|Mrs Chain leaned forward. “I like her already.”
843|Normal|Beyond the gate stood a long, low brick house with a slate roof, tall narrow windows, and the defensive air of a property that had spent years discouraging governments. An old dish antenna had been bolted to one chimney. Three bicycles leaned against a side wall in ways suggesting neither sport nor leisure. On the lawn stood a brass armillary sphere, two folding chairs, and what appeared at first glance to be a weather vane but, on closer inspection, was almost certainly measuring something less forgivable.
844|Normal|Lolly got out and stared. “This,” she said, “is where the Ministry’s former continuity architect lives.”
845|Normal|“No,” said Beatrix. “This is where she waits for it to fail.”
846|Normal|Before anyone could knock, the front door opened. Stephanie Fainrose was not tall, and not young, and had the sort of stillness that makes other people feel physically over-articulated. She wore a dark cardigan over a pale shirt, narrow trousers, and spectacles that looked less like an accessory than a final administrative judgment upon the human face. Her hair, cut short and streaked with steel, suggested she had long ago discovered vanity to be one of several forces in the universe that could safely be ignored.
847|Normal|She looked first at Beatrix, then at Lolly, then at the module in Lolly’s hands, and then at Mrs Chain’s umbrella.
848|Normal|“Oh dear,” she said. “You’ve brought evidence and emotion.”
849|Normal|Mrs Chain drew herself up. “I beg your pardon.”
850|Normal|“Not criticism,” said Fainrose. “Classification.”
851|Normal|Her eyes moved to the module again. “Come in,” she said. “If that thing is active, the hallway is the worst possible place for it unless you happen to dislike your staircase.”
852|Normal|They entered. The house smelled of paper, tea, solder, and intellectual impatience. Books lined every wall, not decoratively but in layers of active use. Blackboards leaned against shelves already burdened by journals, manuals, and notebooks labelled in a hand so precise it seemed almost vindictive. A long table beneath the window held three microscopes, two mugs, several stacked crockery plates, a brass desk lamp, and what Lolly realised with quiet alarm was half of a dismantled detector array.
853|Normal|Mrs Chain looked around. “She lives properly.”
854|Normal|“I make allowances for entropy,” said Fainrose, closing the door.
855|Normal|Beatrix removed the module from Lolly’s hands and placed it on the table. Fainrose looked at it for perhaps two seconds. Then she said, “Idiots.”
856|Normal|Lolly, who had spent the day surrounded by euphemism, felt an immediate and unreasonable surge of gratitude. “You know what it is,” she said.
857|Normal|“Yes,” said Fainrose. “A domestic projection stabiliser, third-generation civilian adaptation, vulgar casing, incompetent thermal shielding. I warned them not to deploy these into lived environments without local phase damping. They said I was being precious.”
858|Normal|“Were you.”
859|Normal|“No,” said Fainrose. “I was being exact. Precision merely looks emotional to the incompetent, who prefer it in bullet points.”
860|Normal|Jago wandered toward the bookshelves. “Did you scratch the note.”
861|Normal|“Of course.”
862|Normal|Mrs Chain stepped forward. “My house has been replaced.”
863|Normal|Fainrose looked at her then, properly, and the severity of her face altered by a fraction. “Not replaced,” she said. “Compressed. There’s a difference.”
864|Normal|“I don’t care for either.”
865|Normal|“No,” said Fainrose. “Most sane people don’t.”
866|Normal|She picked up the module with thumb and forefinger, carried it to a bench at the far end of the room, and placed it inside a wire mesh box connected to several cables. A screen above the bench lit at once with graphs, matrices, and a pulsing red bar marked LOCAL COERCION FIELD.
867|Normal|Lolly took an involuntary half-step closer. Fainrose noticed without looking up. “You studied physics once.”
868|Normal|She blinked. “How do you know that.”
869|Normal|“You’re standing like a woman being insulted by notation.”
870|Normal|“That seems unfairly accurate.”
871|Normal|“Yes,” said Fainrose. “The world rarely improves under scrutiny.”
872|Normal|She tapped several keys. The red bar lengthened. Then lengthened again. Then, to Lolly’s mounting discomfort, split into several thinner lines that rose and fell like competing heartbeats. Fainrose stared at them. “Oh, that’s worse than I expected.”
873|Normal|Beatrix folded her arms. “How worse.”
874|Normal|Fainrose pointed at the screen. “This device is not merely enforcing one stable domestic reading. It’s selecting from multiple possible home-states and driving the property toward whichever one best satisfies central continuity metrics.”
875|Normal|Mrs Chain said, “In English.”
876|Normal|“It is choosing the cheapest version of your life that still looks enough like your life to survive complaint.”
877|Normal|Mrs Chain inhaled once, slowly, in the dangerous manner of a woman considering whether murder may briefly count as tidying.
878|Normal|Lolly said, “But it isn’t settling cleanly. The clocks, photographs, labels—they contradict one another.”
879|Normal|“Of course they do,” said Fainrose. “Because they’ve confused a state with a solution.”
880|Normal|She turned from the screen and looked directly at her. “When a system evolves naturally,” she said, “it doesn’t jump from richness to paperwork. It moves through lawful change. You can dislike the law, but you may not skip it. They’re imposing an end state without understanding the dynamics that produce one.”
881|Normal|Lolly felt the sentence land in her with peculiar force. “You mean—”
882|Normal|“Yes,” she said. “They’re vandalising the evolution.”
883|Normal|Beatrix frowned. “Evolution of what.”
884|Normal|“Of everything that has not yet become final,” said Fainrose. “Homes. Routes. Identities. Intentions. Any state with multiple live continuations.”
885|Normal|Jago, still scanning titles on the shelves, said, “That sounds broad.”
886|Normal|“It is broad,” said Fainrose. “Which is why one grows tired.”
887|Normal|Lolly had taken out the green notebook without noticing. Now she wrote: Not state alone. Need law of change.
888|Normal|Fainrose saw this and gave the smallest of nods. “Good,” she said. “At least one of you is house-trained.”
889|Normal|Mrs Chain looked from one to the other. “What exactly are they doing to my house.”
890|Normal|Fainrose paused, perhaps deciding how much truth a person with an umbrella ought to receive at once. Then she said, “Imagine your home not as a place, but as a cloud of possible details held together by habit, memory, objects, routes through rooms, repeated actions, shared history, emotional residue, and expectation.”
891|Normal|Mrs Chain stared. “That is a surprisingly poetic start.”
892|Normal|“Don’t interrupt. It won’t happen again.”
893|Normal|Jago smiled openly. Fainrose continued. “Now imagine some administrative imbecile decides this complexity is inefficient. Instead of letting that cloud develop into the version sustained by your actual life, they force it into a reduced pattern selected for stability, compliance, and cost. The trouble is, they don’t understand the equations well enough to know which details support the rest. So they flatten the system before it has finished cohering.”
894|Normal|Mrs Chain considered this. “In other words,” she said, “they have made a bad substitute.”
895|Normal|“Yes.”
896|Normal|“Of something they did not understand.”
897|Normal|“Yes.”
898|Normal|“While being certain.”
899|Normal|“Yes.”
900|Normal|Mrs Chain nodded. “Animals.”
901|Normal|Fainrose almost smiled. It was a quick, dangerous thing, gone before it could become sociable.
902|Normal|Beatrix said, “Can it be reversed.”
903|Normal|“Locally, perhaps. Systemically, not by amateurs.”
904|Normal|“We did not come to you because we enjoy amateurs,” said Beatrix.
905|Normal|“No,” said Fainrose. “You came because someone at last noticed the Ministry has confused measurement with authority.”
906|Normal|Lolly looked up. “That’s what the queue was doing.”
907|Normal|“Obviously.”
908|Normal|“It was selecting the interpretive frame first and forcing the case into it.”
909|Normal|“Yes.”
910|Normal|“Then the house module does the same thing materially.”
911|Normal|“Yes.”
912|Normal|“And the whole Simplification Initiative is—”
913|Normal|“A badly governed experiment in coercive collapse,” said Fainrose. “Congratulations. You’ve almost caught up with your own morning.”
914|Normal|Fainrose’s face changed. Not much. Just enough. “The module,” she said. “Thermal shielding. I told them it was ornamental.” She tapped the descending line as if a domestic fault might still confess.
915|Normal|Beatrix saw the label before Fainrose did. “What.”
916|Normal|Fainrose leaned closer. The line was no longer pretending to be local. “I was wrong,” she said quietly. “That is not domestic.”
917|Normal|Lolly joined her at the bench. A label had appeared beneath the descending line: REMOTE ANCHOR LOSS GRAVITATIONAL CIVIC MASS INDEX BELOW TOLERANCE She read it twice. Then again.
918|Normal|“Gravitational civic mass?” she said.
919|Normal|Fainrose rubbed the bridge of her nose. “There are days,” she said, “when I miss private thought.”
920|Normal|Mrs Chain came round the table. “What does it mean.”
921|Normal|Fainrose did not answer at once. Instead she reached for a second keyboard and called up a satellite map, then a network diagram, then what looked to Lolly like a topological model overlaid with municipal infrastructure and three equations pretending not to be terrified. At the centre of one regional cluster blinked a small black icon. Next to it, in yellow: Singularity Asset 4C Status: Diminishing
922|Normal|Jago put down the book he had been pretending to browse. “Oh,” he said. “That seems bad.”
923|Normal|Fainrose was very still now. “Yes,” she said. “It does.”
924|Normal|Lolly looked from the screen to her face. “What is Singularity Asset 4C.”
925|Normal|She answered without turning. “A black hole.”
926|Normal|No one spoke. This was partly because the statement had arrived with the calm administrative weight of a tea tray and partly because each of them, in different ways, required a moment to resent the universe properly.
927|Normal|Mrs Chain said at last, “Why is there a black hole in a civic cluster.”
928|Normal|Fainrose’s mouth tightened. “Transport curvature, waste density management, long-range compression, several profoundly stupid infrastructure efficiencies from fifteen years ago. The Ministry likes gravity when it can invoice it.”
929|Normal|Beatrix was already back in audit mode. “Why is it diminishing.”
930|Normal|“That,” said Fainrose, “is the question.”
931|Normal|Lolly looked again at the falling line on the screen. “Can black holes diminish.”
932|Normal|Fainrose gave her a sidelong look. “Yes.”
933|Normal|“In a municipal timeframe?”
934|Normal|“That,” she said, “would be unusual enough to ruin my afternoon.”
935|Normal|Jago leaned against the doorframe. “Would it be the sort of thing one ignores until it devours a county.”
936|Normal|“No,” said Fainrose. “Worse.”
937|Normal|He frowned. “Worse than devouring a county.”
938|Normal|“It may be evaporating.”
939|Normal|The room went quite still. Lolly heard the sentence not as sound but as a rearrangement of threat.
940|Normal|“A black hole,” she said carefully, “cannot simply evaporate.”
941|Normal|Fainrose looked at her with almost maternal disappointment. “Oh, Lolly,” she said, and it was the first time she had used her name, which made the correction land harder, “of course it can. The only question is whether someone has been foolish enough to make it do so where the public can object.”
942|Normal|Mrs Chain gripped the umbrella. “I object already,” she said.
943|Normal|“Quite right,” said Fainrose.
944|Normal|She shut down the domestic display and isolated the black-hole trace in a narrow window of its own.
945|Normal|“We will leave Mrs Chain’s house for the moment,” she said. “Not because it is unimportant. Because it is now evidence of a larger stupidity. If they have begun forcing collapse locally and interfering with gravitational anchors centrally, then the system is no longer merely rude. It is unstable.”
946|Normal|Beatrix nodded once. “Next step.”
947|Normal|Fainrose looked at all of them in turn. “Tea,” she said.
948|Normal|Jago brightened. “At last. A response proportionate to the danger.”
949|Normal|“Then,” said Fainrose, “we begin by assuming the worst and trying, with discipline, not to underestimate it.”
950|Normal|Mrs Chain set down the umbrella with ceremony. “At last,” she said, “someone sensible.”
951|Normal|Lolly opened the green notebook and wrote only four words. Fainrose not reassured. Then, beneath it: Neither am I.
952|Normal|Rule: Quantum theory is not strange because nothing obeys a law; it is strange because the law is so orderly until a record is made.
