658|Heading 1|Chapter 4: Continuity Review
659|Normal|The man in Mrs Chain’s doorway was not damp, which only served to make the rest of him more offensive. He was in a dark coat of almost expensive sobriety, with a narrow tie and a face that looked as though it had once tried charm and settled, after a risk assessment, on procedure.
660|Normal|In his right hand he carried a slim case of brushed grey metal. In his left he held a card badge displaying a silver spiral and the words:
661|Normal|MINISTRY OF CIVIC STABILITY CONTINUITY REVIEW Field Resolution
662|Normal|Mrs Elspeth Chain tightened her grip on the umbrella.
663|Normal|Lolly Wren looked down at the warm grey module in her hands, then back at the visitor. “Yes,” she said. “That does seem, in hindsight, to have occurred.”
664|Normal|The man stepped one shoe inside the threshold, then paused as if waiting for the house to endorse him. It did not, exactly, but a lamp in the sitting room brightened by a degree that felt sycophantic.
665|Normal|“My name is Alistair Venn,” he said. “Senior Review Officer, Continuity. If you’d all remain calm, this can be resolved locally.”
666|Normal|Mrs Chain gave a short laugh of such pure disbelief that even the daffodils in the hall seemed to reconsider themselves. “Mr Venn,” she said, “you have entered my house during a metaphysical burglary and used the word locally.”
667|Normal|“I do understand this is a distressing transition.”
668|Normal|“It is not a transition,” said Mrs Chain. “It is vandalism with signage.”
669|Normal|Beatrix Sloan had already moved to one side of the hall, where she stood with her arms folded and the air of an auditor preparing to improve a man’s afternoon beyond recovery. “Show me your authority,” she said.
670|Normal|Venn smiled. “Internal Audit.”
671|Normal|“Yes.”
672|Normal|“How comforting.”
673|Normal|“No,” said Beatrix, “specific.”
674|Normal|Venn set the slim case on the hall table—teal, Lolly noticed with renewed disgust—and removed a folded document stamped with too many approvals to be morally sound.
675|Normal|“Phase One continuity authority,” he said. “Regional domestic stabilisation. Sub-clause twelve permits local correction where unresolved identity adhesion threatens public confidence.”
676|Normal|Mrs Chain pointed the umbrella at him. “Public confidence in what.”
677|Normal|Venn’s smile remained in place with admirable institutional training. “In dwellings, among other things.”
678|Normal|Jago Flint, who had taken up a position against the wall in the vague manner of a man unwilling to commit to being fully in one room, said, “That’s not an answer, is it.”
679|Normal|“It is the one currently issued,” said Venn.
680|Normal|Lolly, still holding the detached module, felt the house shifting minutely around her. The cracked mirror in the hall was attempting to decide whether it had always been cracked, and the clock in the sitting room had, for the moment, become a photograph again. One sensed a large number of small possibilities pressing upward through a badly fitted administrative lid.
681|Normal|Venn noticed the module in Lolly’s hand. “If you’d return that to me,” he said, “I can re-establish coherence.”
682|Normal|Lolly did not move. “What exactly does it do.”
683|Normal|“It supports stable domestic identity.”
684|Normal|“By replacing people’s teacups,” said Mrs Chain, “and misfiling their dead.”
685|Normal|Venn gave her a patient look. “By reducing low-value divergence.”
686|Normal|There are phrases which, once spoken aloud, remove any residual doubt as to whether a person deserves tea.
687|Normal|Beatrix said, “Define low-value.”
688|Normal|“Features whose emotional significance exceeds their civic necessity.”
689|Normal|Mrs Chain stared at him. “You ghastly man.”
690|Normal|Venn ignored this with professional ease. “Now then. If we proceed promptly, most residual variance can be contained.”
691|Normal|He opened the slim case. Inside lay a small handheld scanner, three blank forms, a silver pen, and a set of coloured tabs arranged with a precision that suggested neatness had, for him, stopped being a habit and become a creed.
692|Normal|A small, cold audit opened somewhere under Lolly’s ribs. “What do the tabs do,” she asked.
693|Normal|“Nothing dramatic,” said Venn. “They classify continuity pressure.”
694|Normal|“That is a dramatic sentence,” said Jago.
695|Normal|Venn selected a pale yellow tab and affixed it to the top of one form. “Site unstable,” he murmured. “Occupant resistant. Decorative conflict moderate.”
696|Normal|Mrs Chain made a sound like a kettle about to begin legal proceedings.
697|Normal|Then Venn raised the scanner and pointed it at the mantelpiece. A soft tone sounded. The photograph of the spectacled man flickered and became, for half a second, the clock again.
698|Normal|Lolly stepped forward. “Stop that.”
699|Normal|Venn lowered the device. “Mr—”
700|Normal|“Wren.”
701|Normal|“Ms Wren, your interference is understandable, but not helpful. The property is currently exhibiting unresolved domestic supernumeraries. Until I determine which elements belong to the authorised continuity path, the environment may display minor symbolic agitation.”
702|Normal|“Symbolic agitation,” said Beatrix. “Is that what you call deleting a woman’s life.”
703|Normal|“No,” said Venn. “That would be an escalation.”
704|Normal|The room went still. Even Jago seemed briefly at a loss for an appropriate angle of insolence.
705|Normal|Lolly looked at the scanner. “You’re testing the house object by object.”
706|Normal|“Of course.”
707|Normal|“And every test makes it narrower.”
708|Normal|Venn tilted his head. “That is a somewhat theatrical way of putting it.”
709|Normal|“It is an accurate one.”
710|Normal|“Accuracy,” said Venn, “is not always the same thing as usefulness.”
711|Normal|This was, Lolly felt, a remark that ought to disqualify a man from discussing reality.
712|Normal|Venn turned to Mrs Chain. “I’ll need to confirm a few details to reduce remaining branch pressure.”
713|Normal|“No,” said Mrs Chain.
714|Normal|“I’m afraid yes.”
715|Normal|“No,” she repeated, with the sort of clarity one associates with church bells and artillery.
716|Normal|Venn consulted the form as if the form might lend him courage. “Was your late husband,” he asked, “habitually visible in the front room.”
717|Normal|Mrs Chain blinked. “What.”
718|Normal|“On display,” Venn clarified. “Photographic representation, commemorative object, or emotional proxy.”
719|Normal|Lolly shut her eyes briefly.
720|Normal|Beatrix said, “You cannot possibly think that is an admissible question.”
721|Normal|Venn sighed the sigh of a man burdened by other people’s attachment to language. “We have found that unresolved bereavement often creates object density around obsolete personal states.”
722|Normal|Mrs Chain advanced one step. “My husband,” she said, “was not an obsolete personal state.”
723|Normal|Venn made a small note on the form. “High memorial adhesion,” he murmured.
724|Normal|Jago straightened from the wall. “I wouldn’t write that down if I were you.”
725|Normal|Venn, not yet appreciating the counsel, continued. “Now then. Sister present or absent in core domestic narrative?”
726|Normal|Mrs Chain simply looked at him.
727|Normal|Venn tried again. “Would you say your sister is structurally central to your self-model.”
728|Normal|Mrs Chain turned to Lolly. “Ms Wren.”
729|Normal|“Yes.”
730|Normal|“If I strike him, will the paperwork improve.”
731|Normal|“No,” said Lolly honestly. “But I am beginning to see its appeal.”
732|Normal|Venn raised the scanner again, this time toward the bookshelves. The titles shimmered. Pleasant Soups Through the Year became Regional Stews for Quiet Evenings, then Domestic Warmth: A Practical Guide, then settled once more on soup.
733|Normal|Lolly stared. The scanner was not detecting a stable reality. It was forcing a choice each time it asked. Question. Narrowing. Selection.
734|Normal|She opened the green notebook and wrote quickly:
735|Normal|Repeated inspection. Narrower answers. The form decides what survives.
736|Normal|Underneath that she added:
737|Normal|The house is larger than the form.
738|Normal|She looked up and said, “You’re not restoring anything. You’re compressing it.”
739|Normal|Venn glanced over with mild interest, as one might regard a spaniel that had unexpectedly solved an invoice. “Compression,” he said, “is an inelegant term.”
740|Normal|“It’s exact.”
741|Normal|“It is practical.”
742|Normal|“It’s theft.” This from Mrs Chain, with such force that even Venn chose not to answer at once.
743|Normal|Then, from upstairs, came a crash. Everyone looked up.
744|Normal|Venn frowned. “That shouldn’t happen.”
745|Normal|“Good,” said Jago. “I enjoy it when systems lose confidence.”
746|Normal|Another crash followed, then the sound of a drawer opening in what Lolly would have sworn were at least two contradictory bedrooms.
747|Normal|Venn stepped toward the stairs. “Please remain here.”
748|Normal|Beatrix moved at once to block him. “No.”
749|Normal|“Ms Sloan, this is authorised territory.”
750|Normal|“You’ve already revised the kitchen into herbal defeat. You don’t get the upper floor without witnesses.”
751|Normal|Venn considered objecting, thought better of it, and climbed.
752|Normal|They followed in a compact and deeply unwilling procession: Venn first, Beatrix at his shoulder, Lolly with the notebook and the warm module, Mrs Chain gripping the umbrella like the last practical principle in the cosmos, and Jago behind them, carrying no visible authority at all and somehow appearing the least concerned.
753|Normal|Upstairs, the landing had changed again. The stickers remained on the doors, but they no longer agreed with the rooms.
754|Normal|BEDROOM A hung over what was plainly a bathroom. SANITATION UNIT had attached itself to the airing cupboard. OPTIONAL LINEN SPACE now marked the stairs to the loft.
755|Normal|“That,” said Mrs Chain, “is deranged.”
756|Normal|Venn scanned the corridor, and the labels jumped once more, nervously, as though caught gossiping. “Substrate drift,” he muttered.
757|Normal|He opened the first door. Inside, what had been earlier a bedroom now existed in a state of elaborate compromise.
758|Normal|Half the wallpaper showed faded blue flowers; the other half had become a tasteful cream stripe. On the bed lay two duvets, one floral, one aggressively neutral, occupying the same territory with visible resentment.
759|Normal|A framed photograph on the chest of drawers showed Mrs Chain beside the spectacled man from downstairs; another, half-phased into it, showed her alone on a cruise ship she had almost certainly never taken.
760|Normal|Lolly felt the sudden intellectual terror of recognition. It was not that the room contained random errors. It contained alternatives still trying to survive.
761|Normal|She whispered, almost to herself, “They’re all still here.”
762|Normal|Venn turned. “What.”
763|Normal|“The discarded versions. The house hasn’t lost them. It’s suppressing them.”
764|Normal|Beatrix looked sharply from the room to Lolly. “Meaning.”
765|Normal|“Meaning the imposed version needs constant effort. Every scan and classification keeps pressing the other details out of view.”
766|Normal|Jago nodded as if this were perfectly ordinary conversation to be having beside a bed with contradictory duvets. “So the house is pretending harder than it can manage.”
767|Normal|“Exactly.”
768|Normal|Venn’s expression tightened for the first time. “Ms Wren, you are not qualified to assess continuity architecture.”
769|Normal|“No,” said Lolly. “But I am qualified to notice when a lie requires electricity.”
770|Normal|Venn took a step forward. “Return the substrate module.”
771|Normal|Lolly did not. There followed one of those brief, lucid silences in which every person present understands that the day has crossed from paperwork into decision.
772|Normal|Then Jago said, “I may be wrong, but is this little grey thing wireless.”
773|Normal|Venn’s gaze flicked toward him. “Restricted information.”
774|Normal|“Excellent,” said Jago. “That means yes.”
775|Normal|He was moving almost before the sentence had ended. One moment he was in the doorway; the next he had crossed the room, lifted the floral duvet, and yanked from beneath the bed a thin cable leading into the skirting board.
776|Normal|“Jago,” said Lolly.
777|Normal|“Trust me.”
778|Normal|“Why would I.”
779|Normal|“No time.”
780|Normal|He pulled. The cable came free with a crack and a shower of plaster.
781|Normal|The effect on the house was immediate. Not catastrophic. Worse. Intimate.
782|Normal|The cream stripes on the wall dissolved into blue flowers. The photograph of the cruise ship vanished and was replaced by one of Mrs Chain and a dark-haired woman laughing over some unseen mishap. The neutral duvet slid to the floor.
783|Normal|From downstairs came a rapid sequence of sounds: the kitchen mugs shattering, the herbal sachets dropping like failed policy, a clock giving up halfway through a chime.
784|Normal|Mrs Chain gripped the bedpost. “My sister,” she said softly.
785|Normal|The woman in the restored photograph was, unmistakably, someone she knew.
786|Normal|Venn swore, and the effect was so startling in a man like him that it briefly improved him.
787|Normal|He lunged toward Lolly for the substrate module. Beatrix intercepted him with the clean, efficient brutality of a person who had spent years carrying boxes of evidence and had made peace with leverage.
788|Normal|Venn hit the wardrobe, rebounded, and sat down abruptly on the edge of the bed with all the dignity of a policy meeting its evidence.
789|Normal|Jago looked genuinely impressed. “Oh, splendid.”
790|Normal|Beatrix did not look at him. “Wren.”
791|Normal|Lolly, heart thudding in a most administratively inconvenient way, turned the grey module over in her hands.
792|Normal|On its underside, beneath the Ministry stamp, something had been scratched in tiny letters as if by a previous handler unwilling to trust official channels.
793|Normal|If found active in residential field, contact S. Fainrose.
794|Normal|She was right. Lolly read it twice. Then a third time.
795|Normal|“Beatrix,” she said.
796|Normal|“What.”
797|Normal|She held out the module.
798|Normal|Beatrix read the inscription, and for the first time since Lolly had known her, her expression shifted from contemptuous control to something much closer to recognition.
799|Normal|“Bloody hell,” she said quietly.
800|Normal|Mrs Chain looked up. “What is it.”
801|Normal|Beatrix straightened. “A name.”
802|Normal|“Whose.”
803|Normal|She glanced at Lolly. “Someone who may know how bad this actually is.”
804|Normal|Venn, still recovering both breath and authority, said, “You cannot involve external theoretical parties.”
805|Normal|“No,” said Beatrix, tucking the module into her coat. “But we may.”
806|Normal|Downstairs, the house continued rearranging itself in unhappy little surges, as if relieved to be less wrong but not yet sure how to be right.
807|Normal|Lolly looked once more at the half-restored room: at the wallpaper finding its conviction, at the single duvet, at the photograph of the sister returned from administrative extinction.
808|Normal|Then she opened the notebook and wrote:
809|Normal|Too much observation can make the wrong world stick.
810|Normal|And below it:
811|Normal|Need Fainrose.
812|Normal|She did not wait to see whether the sentence agreed with her. She was already reaching for her coat.
813|Normal|“Bring the module,” said Beatrix, without turning. “If we are going to be wrong, we will be wrong with evidence.”
814|Normal|Rule: Inspect a quantum state often enough in the right physical way, and the inspection itself can help keep it where it was.
