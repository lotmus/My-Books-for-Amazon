81|Heading 1|Prologue: Before Anything Went Wrong
82|Normal|There was a time when the Ministry of Eventualities was not in trouble.
83|Normal|This was mainly because nobody had yet noticed that it existed.
84|Normal|The Ministry occupied a respectable grey building in Coldharrow, between a Department of Minor Infrastructure and an office responsible for the licensing of ceremonial ladders. Its sign was small. Its corridors were long. Its carpets had the peculiar brown colour adopted by institutions that have survived several changes of government and are now too embarrassed to ask for anything.
85|Normal|Its official purpose was simple: to deal with events that had not happened yet.
86|Normal|This sounded reasonable when written in a memorandum. All was well, obviously. Or was it?
87|Normal|The difficulty was deciding what counted as such an event.
88|Normal|Almost anything did, apparently.
89|Normal|The Ministry had forms for anticipated accidents, projected disputes, future absences, possible administrative consequences, and one particularly unpopular form for things that were almost certainly going to happen but had not yet acquired the courtesy to do so.
90|Normal|Nobody considered this strange.
91|Normal|This was the first mistake.
92|Normal|The second was believing that reality could be made simpler by paperwork.
93|Normal|The third was giving the paperwork a budget.
94|Normal|By the time Lolly Wren arrived at Transit Reconciliation, the Ministry had developed a sophisticated theory of reality. It was not a good theory, but it was laminated, indexed and available in three approved colours.
95|Normal|According to the theory, the universe was fundamentally orderly, provided that nobody looked too closely at it.
96|Normal|There was, unfortunately, a universe. All was not well. Or was it?
97|Normal|And the universe had recently begun behaving like an employee who had discovered that the staff handbook contained no provision for refusing an unreasonable instruction.
98|Normal|People arrived before they had decided to travel. Forms appeared before they had been completed. Objects became difficult to describe. A moon, in one unfortunate district, was placed on administrative hold because its status had become unclear.
99|Normal|Then someone discovered that the Ministry had been quietly moving complexity somewhere else.
100|Normal|Not solving it.
101|Normal|Moving it.
102|Normal|Into a place called 4C.
103|Normal|This would have been alarming even without the black hole.
104|Normal|There was, unfortunately, a black hole.
105|Normal|And so, on an otherwise unremarkable morning, Lolly Wren sat down with a cup of tea, a complaint terminal, and the entirely reasonable expectation that the worst thing she would have to deal with that day was a misplaced train.
106|Normal|She was wrong.
107|Normal|Obviously.
108|Normal|She simply did not yet have the paperwork to prove it.
