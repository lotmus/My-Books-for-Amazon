# Commercial book method

Two tracks. Do not mix them, and do not use either track on *Your First Book That Sells* or *Your First YouTube Channel That Sells*. Those are practical guides with their own folders and manuscripts.

Use the textbook track for course and textbook projects (math, electronics, physics courses). Use the popular-science track for narrative science books. If a topic is mature and standardized, use 2 sources. Use a third only when its emphasis is actually different.

## Shared rules

Work in three passes. First pass: rough condensation and merge. Second pass: shared terms and structure. Third pass: only the sections that failed a similarity check or a quality check. Do not polish every page equally.

Write the constraints once (length, tone, notation, what must not stay close to a source) and use that same sheet on every pass.

AI does the volume: condensation, shared terminology, first merge, first alternative explanations, and one similarity audit of the full draft. The human pass is for legal safety, technical correctness, and what the reader actually experiences. Leave the rest at a competent draft.

Run one similarity audit on the merged draft. Fix the flagged passages. Do not audit section by section before the merge exists.

AI may draft replacement examples and analogies. Manually upgrade the weakest 30–40 percent, not every one.

Do not copy distinctive wording. The finished book has to be independent of the sources in examples, explanatory devices, and the order of the argument.

## Textbook track

1. Select the sources.
2. Condense each to about 50 pages of definitions, key results, essential derivations, and the minimum examples. Keep the technical content accurate.
3. Check each condensation for errors and for anything important that was dropped.
4. Build one nomenclature and notation glossary, then rewrite every condensation into that notation.
5. The human finalizes the glossary.
6. Merge into one text. Combine overlaps. Keep a useful second explanation when the sources really differ. One logical flow.
7. The human sets the final structure. Keep a standard order when the field already teaches it that way. Rearrange only where the teaching gets better.
8. Section by section, replace explanations enough that the wording, rhythm, and explanatory moves are not the source's.
9. Replace or redesign examples. Do not keep the source's choice, order, or framing. Standard exercise types may be adapted. Invent or fully redesign the 10–15 exercises that carry the course. Simple diagrams are enough except for the few figures the argument depends on.
10. Audit the full draft against each condensation. Flag close parallels in examples, teaching devices, argumentative flow, and distinctive presentation. Rewrite every flag.
11. Add something the sources do not have: a clearer bridge, updated material, or a diagram that teaches better.
12. Human review for accuracy, teaching quality, and independence. Then editing and formatting.

## Popular science track

Voice, narrative, analogies, and whether the reader stays with the page matter more than exercise sets or formal completeness.

1. Select strong popular-science sources, preferably with different emphases.
2. Before merging, write a one-page voice guide: tone, attitude, how much wit, how directly the book addresses the reader. Every later pass follows it.
3. Condense each book to its argumentative spine, about 40–60 pages. Keep the essential claims, key evidence, central analogies, and major rhetorical moves. Drop secondary anecdotes and repetition. Do not keep distinctive wording.
4. Note which analogies are powerful or famous. Those must be replaced. Ordinary ones may be lightly reworked.
5. Build one shared vocabulary and rewrite the condensations into it.
6. The human sets the narrative arc first, at outline level, so it does not follow any source's chapter order or rhetorical progression. AI fills sections inside that fixed arc.
7. For each major section, generate alternative explanations and analogies, then rewrite them. Replace distinctive analogies with new ones of at least equal clarity.
8. Rebuild the voice. Do not leave personality and persuasive energy to the draft.
9. Add original hooks, transitions, and moments of insight.
10. Audit the full draft against the condensations. Flag parallels in analogy choice and framing, argumentative sequence, distinctive explanatory devices, and narrative rhythm. Remove every flag.
11. Add an original contribution: new framing, updated science, a different emphasis, or a recent example.
12. One dedicated pass for voice, pacing, and engagement after the draft exists. Do not try to make every paragraph lively during the first merge.
13. Human review for voice, pace, accuracy, and independence. Then editing.

## What agents must not do

Do not start either track until the sources are chosen. Do not treat a condensation as the manuscript. Do not restore *How to Publish and Make Good Money* or the misspelled `How  to  Pubish and Make Good Money` folder in order to run this method.
