# Part Five: While You Are Still Alive

*Educational guide, not legal advice. Powers of attorney and healthcare directives have strict formal rules; use the forms recognised where you live.*

The documents people forget are the ones that work before death. If you are ill, they may matter sooner than the will. A will speaks once, at the end. These documents speak every day you cannot speak for yourself.

## Chapter 20. The Financial Power of Attorney

### What it does

You name an agent to handle your money, property, and contracts. The agent can pay bills, deal with banks, file tax returns, manage investments, sell property if the power allows it, and talk to the pension office on your behalf. A "durable" power continues after you lose capacity, which is the whole point; a non-durable power ends exactly when you need it most.

**California.** The Probate Code contains a Uniform Statutory Form Power of Attorney (§ 4401). It covers financial matters only, not healthcare. It must be signed and acknowledged before a notary. Lawyers often draft longer custom powers, but banks are familiar with the statutory form, which is an underrated virtue.

**Germany.** The equivalent is the *Vorsorgevollmacht*, a lasting power of attorney covering finances, health, or both. It must be in writing if the agent is to make certain serious medical decisions, and those decisions must be named expressly (BGB § 1820). For real estate, a notarised or at least officially certified power is usually needed, and banks frequently ask for their own form or a notarised one. If no power exists and you lose capacity, the guardianship court appoints a legal guardian (*Betreuer*), who may or may not be the person you would have chosen. You can also sign a *Betreuungsverfügung*, a statement of whom you would want the court to appoint if a guardian ever became necessary.

Both the *Vorsorgevollmacht* and the *Betreuungsverfügung* can be registered in the Central Register of Lasting Powers of Attorney (*Zentrales Vorsorgeregister*) run by the Federal Chamber of Notaries. Guardianship courts check it. Registration does not replace the document; it only makes sure the court knows it exists.

### Immediate or springing

An immediate power works from the day you sign. A springing power works only when you become incapacitated, usually proven by a doctor's certificate. Springing powers sound safer and work slower (Case 16). The real safeguard is choosing the right agent, naming a backup, and, if you want oversight, requiring the agent to report to a second person.

### Choosing the agent

Choose someone honest, organised, and available, in that order. Proximity helps: an agent who lives near your bank and your doctors can do in an afternoon what takes a distant agent a week. If you name two agents, say whether they must act together (safer, slower) or may act separately (faster, riskier).

Talk to the agent. Tell them where your Family List is (Part Six). Give them a copy of the power, or tell them where the original is kept.

### Spouses are not automatically agents

In California, being married does not give you authority over your spouse's separate accounts or retirement plans. In Germany, since 2023, spouses have a limited emergency right to represent each other in health matters for up to six months (BGB § 1358), but not in financial matters, and not if they live apart (Case 18). Each spouse should sign a power for the other.

## Chapter 21. Healthcare Decisions

### Two jobs, one or two documents

A healthcare directive does two jobs: it names the person who decides for you when you cannot, and it says what you want.

**California.** The advance health care directive combines both jobs in one document. The statutory form lets you name a healthcare agent and alternates, give instructions about end-of-life care, pain relief, and organ donation, and name your primary physician. It must be signed and either notarised or signed by two qualified adult witnesses; there are restrictions on who may witness (your healthcare provider, for example, may not). You can register it voluntarily with the California Secretary of State's Advance Health Care Directive Registry, which, according to the Secretary of State, costs ten dollars for a new registration; amendments and revocations are free. Registration does not affect validity.

**Germany.** The *Patientenverfügung* (patient's directive) states in writing which examinations, treatments, or procedures you consent to or refuse in situations not yet imminent (BGB § 1827). It can be revoked informally at any time. The person who carries it out is your agent under a *Vorsorgevollmacht* or, if none exists, a court-appointed guardian. The directive binds them if it fits the actual situation. If it does not fit, they must decide according to your treatment wishes or presumed will, which is where vague directives fail.

### Write in situations, not slogans

"No heroic measures" is a slogan (Case 19). A useful directive describes situations and decisions:

- If I am terminally ill and unable to communicate, I do / do not want resuscitation, a ventilator, artificial nutrition, dialysis.
- If I have permanent severe brain damage with no reasonable prospect of recovery, I want / do not want life-prolonging treatment.
- If I have pneumonia or another treatable infection on top of a serious illness, I do / do not want antibiotics.
- I want pain relief even if it may shorten my life.
- I would prefer to die at home / in a hospice / in hospital if possible.
- People I want with me; people I want informed; religious or spiritual wishes.

Appendix F turns these into a worksheet in ordinary language, with a filled example. Fill it in first. Then take it to the official form, or to your doctor, and make the formal document match.

### Who may talk about you

Privacy rules can keep relatives away from basic medical information. A healthcare agent named in a valid directive can usually receive it. If there are other people you want informed (an adult child who is not the agent, a partner), say so in writing and give the hospital a copy.

### Give it to the people who need it

A directive in a drawer at home is not in the emergency file. Give copies to your agent, your doctor, and your hospital if you have a regular one. Keep a note in your wallet and on your phone's emergency screen saying where it is. Put it on the Family List.

### Guardianship, the outcome you are avoiding

If you cannot decide and no valid power exists, someone may have to ask a court to appoint a conservator (California) or a legal guardian (Germany). It is public in California, involves court supervision in both places, and the court might not choose the person you would have chosen. A power of attorney and a healthcare directive, signed while you are well, avoid most of it. They take an afternoon. The court process can take months.
