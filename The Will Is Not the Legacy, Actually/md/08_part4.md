# Part Four: The Alternatives, With the Failure Modes Filled In

*Educational guide, not legal advice. Each tool below can be exactly right or quietly disastrous depending on facts this book cannot see.*

A will is the default tool, not always the main one. Much of a modern estate already moves outside the will. The useful question is not "will or no will?" It is "which asset should pass by which path, and what is left over for the will to catch?" This part takes each path in turn: when it works, the surprise it can spring, and a case where it overrode the will.

## Chapter 12. Beneficiary Designations

### When it works

Life insurance, retirement plans, annuities, and many bank and brokerage accounts let you name who receives the money at death. The institution pays that person directly, usually quickly, without probate in California. In Germany, a contract in which payment is promised to a third party on someone's death gives the third party the right to the payment at the death (BGB § 331), which is how named beneficiaries of life insurance receive it outside the estate.

### The surprise

The form ignores your will, your divorce (sometimes), your later intentions, and your family's sense of fairness. It does exactly what it says.

- **Divorce.** California automatically cancels many nonprobate transfers to a former spouse (Probate Code § 5040), but federal law governs most employer retirement plans, and the U.S. Supreme Court held in *Egelhoff v. Egelhoff* that federal law displaces a state rule of that kind for those plans (Case 1).
- **Death of the beneficiary.** If the only named beneficiary dies first and there is no contingent, the plan's default rules apply. Often that means the estate, which brings back the probate you were trying to avoid (Case 4).
- **Minors.** A child cannot receive a large sum directly; someone has to be appointed to manage it (Case 5).
- **Benefits.** An heir with a disability who relies on means-tested benefits may lose them if they receive money outright (Chapter 19).
- **The estate as beneficiary.** Naming your estate drags the asset into probate and, for retirement accounts, can shorten the time over which the money can be taken out and taxed. Ask a tax adviser before doing it deliberately.

### What to do

Make a beneficiary audit part of your Family List (Appendix D has the worksheet). For each policy and account, record the primary and contingent beneficiaries, the date you last checked, and how to contact the institution. Check after every marriage, divorce, birth, adoption, and death, and otherwise every few years. Ask the institution for written confirmation of what is on file. People are regularly surprised by what is.

## Chapter 13. Payable-on-Death and Transfer-on-Death

### When it works

In California, bank accounts can carry a payable-on-death (POD) designation and securities accounts a transfer-on-death (TOD) registration. Real estate can pass by a revocable transfer-on-death deed (Probate Code §§ 5600 and following). You keep full ownership and control while alive; you can change or revoke the designation; and at death the named person takes the asset, usually without probate.

California's revocable TOD deed must be signed, notarised, and recorded with the county recorder within the time the statute allows. The statute authorising these deeds is set to be repealed on 1 January 2032 unless extended; deeds executed before then remain valid.

Germany has no direct equivalent to a TOD deed for real estate. Property transfers at death through the heirs (or by a legacy they must fulfil), and the land register is changed on proof of succession. For bank accounts, German banks offer arrangements that pay a named person on death, built on the contract-for-a-third-party rule; ask the bank what its form does, and remember that such a payment can still count when compulsory shares are calculated.

### The surprise

A POD or TOD designation ignores the rest of your plan. One child on the deed and three children in the will is a plan to disinherit two of them (Case 6). Designations to minors create the guardianship problem. And a TOD deed done wrong (wrong form, not recorded in time, wrong legal description) can cloud the title for years.

### What to do

Use POD and TOD as coordinated pieces of a plan, not as shortcuts you keep secret from your will. Name all the people you want to benefit, in the shares you want, with contingents. Write each designation on your Family List.

## Chapter 14. Joint Ownership

### When it works

Married couples often own their home and accounts jointly, and the survivor takes everything without fuss. In California, spouses can hold property as community property with right of survivorship, combining the survivorship feature with community property's tax treatment; your lawyer or accountant can say whether that suits you.

### The surprise

Adding someone who is not your spouse to a title or account is usually a present gift (Case 7). Their creditors may reach it. Their divorce may involve it. You may need their signature to sell or refinance. It may carry worse tax consequences than an inheritance would. And it overrides what your will says (Case 3).

Tenancy in common is different: no survivorship, so your share passes through your estate, possibly to people your co-owners would never have chosen (Case 8). In California, a married person's will controls only their half of community property (Case 9).

In Germany, spouses who own a house together each typically own a fractional share, which passes through their estate to their heirs. Joint bank accounts are common, but whether the balance belongs to the survivor as a matter of inheritance law is a separate question from whether the survivor can access it; the bank's contract and the couple's arrangements decide. The compulsory share rules can still reach the deceased's portion.

### What to do

Do not add a name to a title "to make it easier" without advice. If the goal is convenience, use a power of attorney. If the goal is inheritance, use a beneficiary designation, a TOD deed, a trust, or a will.

## Chapter 15. Gifts While Alive

### When it works

Giving now lets you see the gift used, and can shrink what has to be administered later. In the United States, the annual gift tax exclusion for 2026 is 19,000 dollars per recipient, according to the IRS; gifts within that amount generally need no gift tax return. Larger gifts usually need a return but rarely result in tax, because they count against the lifetime basic exclusion amount, 15 million dollars per person in 2026.

In Germany, gifts are taxed under the same law as inheritances (the Inheritance and Gift Tax Act, *ErbStG*), with the same tax-free allowances: 500,000 euros for a spouse or registered partner, 400,000 euros for each child, 200,000 euros for a grandchild, and 20,000 euros for many other people, depending on tax class (ErbStG § 16). Gifts from the same person within ten years are added together (§ 14). Families who start early can use the allowances more than once over a long life.

### The surprise

- **You may need the money.** Long illnesses are expensive. A gift you cannot take back is gone.
- **Tax basis (United States).** Inherited property usually gets a new basis at death; a gift generally carries over the giver's basis, which can mean a larger capital gains tax later for the recipient (Case 7).
- **Compulsory shares (Germany).** Gifts made within ten years before death can be counted when calculating a disinherited relative's compulsory share, at a value that falls by one tenth each year (BGB § 2325, Case 10). Gifts to a spouse do not start that clock until the marriage ends.
- **Fairness.** Unrecorded gifts to one child become disputes after death. "I helped Alex with the deposit on the flat" needs to be on paper if you want it counted.

### What to do

Document large gifts. Say in writing whether a gift is meant as an advance on an inheritance, and make sure your will or trust says the same. In Germany, ask a notary about compulsory-share effects before giving away real estate.

## Chapter 16. Dying Without a Will

### When it works

Intestacy is the plan you already have if you do nothing. For a family that matches the statute exactly, it can work, slowly.

**California.** A surviving spouse receives the deceased's share of community property. For separate property, the spouse receives one half if there is one child and one third if there is more than one child, with the rest to the children (Probate Code § 6401). Without descendants, parents and siblings come into the picture. The court appoints an administrator.

**Germany.** Descendants inherit first, in equal shares, with the children of a deceased child taking that child's place (BGB § 1924). A spouse inherits one quarter alongside descendants, one half alongside parents, siblings, or grandparents (§ 1931). In the default matrimonial property regime, the spouse's share is increased by another quarter (§ 1371), so a spouse with children typically inherits one half. If the spouses had chosen separate property (*Gütertrennung*) and there are one or two children, the spouse and each child inherit equally (§ 1931(4)).

### The surprise

Statutes do not know your life. Unmarried partners usually receive nothing. Stepchildren you raised but never adopted usually receive nothing. Friends receive nothing. A separated but not divorced spouse may receive a great deal (Case 8). In Germany, the heirs form a community of heirs (*Erbengemeinschaft*) that must agree on what to do with each asset, which is a fine arrangement for siblings who like each other and a slow-motion catastrophe for those who do not.

### What to do

If the statute's answer is not your answer, make a will.

## Chapter 17. Small-Estate Procedures

### When it works

**California.** If the estate is small, heirs can avoid full probate. For deaths on or after 1 April 2025, the California courts list these limits:

| Procedure | Limit |
|---|---|
| Small estate affidavit for personal property (Probate Code §§ 13100–13101), usable 40 days after death | 208,850 dollars gross value |
| Court petition for the decedent's primary residence (§§ 13151–13154) | 750,000 dollars |
| Affidavit for real property of small value (§ 13200) | 69,625 dollars |
| Spouse collecting unpaid salary (§§ 13600–13601) | 20,875 dollars |

Many assets are left out when calculating the value, including cars, property in a trust, joint tenancy property, assets passing by beneficiary designation, and property passing to a surviving spouse. Debts are not subtracted. The limits are adjusted every three years; the next adjustment is due on 1 April 2028 (Probate Code § 890).

**Germany.** There is no direct equivalent, because there is no probate to avoid. Heirs still need to prove they are heirs. Banks may not insist on a certificate of inheritance as a matter of course: the Federal Court of Justice held in 2013 (XI ZR 401/12) that a bank's standard term demanding one in every case was invalid, and in 2016 (XI ZR 440/15) that an opened handwritten will can be enough proof if it states the succession clearly.

### The surprise

A small-estate affidavit is a procedure, not a plan. It does not choose your heirs. It only collects for the people the will, or the statute, has already chosen.

## Chapter 18. Contracts That Override Everything

Prenuptial and postnuptial agreements, divorce decrees, business buy-sell agreements, partnership and shareholder agreements, and company articles can all dictate where an asset goes at death. A later will that forgets them does not win.

- **Marital agreements.** A prenuptial agreement can waive a spouse's rights in the other's estate. In Germany, a marriage contract (*Ehevertrag*, notarised) can change the matrimonial property regime, which changes the spouse's statutory share (Chapter 16), and a separate notarial contract can waive inheritance or compulsory-share rights (BGB §§ 2346, 2348).
- **Business agreements.** A buy-sell agreement may require your heirs to sell your shares to your partners at a formula price. The articles of a German GmbH may restrict who can hold shares after a death. Your will cannot override either. Your heirs may receive money instead of the business, or a business they cannot run.
- **Divorce decrees.** A decree may require you to keep life insurance for a former spouse or children. A will cannot cancel that obligation.

Bring every one of these documents to the drafting meeting. A will written without them is a will written about an imaginary estate.

## Chapter 19. Trusts

Trusts are American and English inventions. German law has no trust in the common-law sense, and German courts and tax authorities treat foreign trusts in their own way; anyone with a German connection and an American trust should get advice in both countries (Case 21). This chapter is about trusts in California.

### The revocable living trust

You create it while alive, usually naming yourself as trustee. You transfer ("fund") assets into it. You keep complete control and can change or revoke it while you have capacity. At your death, or your incapacity, a successor trustee takes over and manages or distributes the assets according to the trust's terms, usually without court involvement for the assets the trust owns.

**It is useful when:** you own real estate (especially in more than one state); you want privacy; you expect incapacity; you want managed distributions to children or grandchildren; or your estate is large enough that probate fees would be significant.

**It does not:** save income tax; protect assets from your own creditors while you control it; or control anything it does not own.

### Funding, asset by asset

"Funding" means changing the ownership of each asset so the trust owns it. This is where trusts fail.

| Asset | How it is funded | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| House | New deed from you to yourself as trustee, recorded | Deed never recorded; or refinancing lender takes it out of the trust and nobody puts it back |
| Out-of-state real estate | Deed recorded in that state | Forgotten, creating a second probate |
| Bank and brokerage accounts | Retitled to the trust, or POD/TOD naming the trust | New accounts opened in your own name |
| Retirement accounts | Not retitled; beneficiary form may name the trust or individuals, after tax advice | Retitling (which can be a taxable distribution), or naming the trust without advice |
| Life insurance | Beneficiary form names the trust or individuals | Old form still names someone else |
| Cars | Often left out, because small-estate procedures can handle them | Assuming everything is in the trust |
| Business interests | Assignment, with partners' consent if required | Buy-sell agreement forbids transfer |
| Personal belongings | General assignment to the trust | No assignment at all |
| Digital assets and intellectual property | Assignment of copyrights and domain names, where possible | Forgotten |

A pour-over will catches anything left outside and sends it to the trust, but those assets may still have to go through probate on the way.

### The testamentary trust

A trust created by your will. It does not avoid probate, because the will must go through probate to create it. Its job is management: holding a young person's inheritance until a chosen age (Article 8 in Chapter 9), or managing money for someone who should not receive a lump sum.

### The special-needs trust

For a person with a disability who receives, or may need, means-tested public benefits. If such a person inherits money outright, they may lose eligibility until the money is spent down. A properly drafted third-party special-needs trust, funded by your will, your living trust, or your beneficiary designations, can hold the inheritance for their benefit without counting as their own resource. Drafting rules are technical. Do not improvise this with a sentence in a home-made will.

### Pet trusts

California allows a trust for the care of a domestic or pet animal (Probate Code § 15212). It lasts until no animal alive at your death remains; then any leftover money goes where the trust directs, or to the residue of your estate. Name a caretaker who has agreed, a trustee who will pay them, and an amount that bears some relation to what a pet actually costs. Leaving the entire estate to a cat is not illegal in California, but it will make the evening news.

### Irrevocable trusts

You generally cannot take back what you put into an irrevocable trust. They are used for tax planning, benefit planning, and asset protection, and they are not a weekend project. If someone suggests one, ask what problem it solves, what it costs, and what you give up.
