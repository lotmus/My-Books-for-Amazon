# PART X — What the Future Is Allowed to Be

## 43. Five Ways It Can End

The leftover glow does not say how the story closes. The shove does most of the talking, and it talks in a low, accelerating voice. Here are five closings, with their temperatures.

A kitchen loaf can finish in more than one way. It can dry until it is a brick. It can be pulled apart while it is still warm. It can be shoved back into a hotter oven and collapse. It can spoil from the inside, a bubble of wrong chemistry that eats the crumb at the speed of the room. It can be sliced so thin and glued to tomorrow’s dough that you call the end a beginning. Only one of those is on the receipt you were handed with the ingredients. The receipt, in this cosmos, is the bombs, the leftover glow, and a shove that is winning. The receipt says: it dries. The other four are allowed as other kitchens, other valleys, other mistakes in how we read *w*. They are not on this ticket. Katie Mack wrote the tour; Barrow would have walked you to the door of each and then asked which one had a measurement. This chapter does that, with a clock.

**Heat death.** The working default. The stretch keeps winning. Bound islands last a long time and then do not: stars burn out, remnants cool, black holes leak (Chapter 23), the islands thin toward a sameness in which no heat can run a trick. Time, as a useful word for change, gets bored. This is Kelvin’s old gloom, updated with dark energy. It is not a crunch. It is a fade. Hot as the track the bombs picked. Warm only in the details no one has photographed.

William Thomson — Kelvin — said it in the nineteenth century, when the leftover glow was not yet a fact and the shove was not yet a bomb. Stars would burn out. The universe would reach a sameness of temperature. No heat engine, no trick, no kitchen. The gloom was thermodynamics applied to a cosmos he could not yet weigh. The update is the shove. Without it, you still had a fight: pull versus stretch, a possible recollapse, a possible tired forever. With it, the fight is over unless the shove is a liar. Isolated lumps stay bound — the Local Group, a while; a galaxy, longer. Between them the dough wins and keeps winning. New stars, after a long career, stop being made. Red dwarfs, the stingy ones, spend on the order of 10¹³ to 10¹⁴ years and then sit as cinders. White dwarfs, neutron stars, brown leftovers: a degenerate era, dimming. Then the holes. A stellar-mass hole leaks on a timescale of order 10⁶⁷ years. A giant, a billion Suns, can last toward 10¹⁰⁰. Chapter 23 already wrote the leak. After the last leak, the islands are a thin of particles and a leftover stretch, too even to run a trick. That is the fade. Hot as a default given the bombs. Warm in the details: we have not photographed year 10¹⁰⁰. We have the equations that got the leftover glow right, pointed forward. Mara, if the potatoes prosper, still lives inside one bound island. The island can go dark without a recollapse. The afternoon is long enough to grow basil.

**The rip.** If the shove is not a constant but a growing rudeness — *w* less than −1, phantom, a word that should not have survived the committee — then bound things unbind. Galaxies, then stars, then worlds, then you. Finite time. No evidence requires this. The bombs permit a constant. Cold, unless *w* misbehaves in a future survey.

*w* is the ratio of pressure to energy density. A cosmological constant sits at *w* = −1. Acceleration needs *w* less than −1/3. A rip needs the phantom: *w* less than −1, and no decay of the field that would let it sit down. Then the shove grows. Clusters unbind. Galaxies unbind. The Solar System unbinds. Atoms, in the rude versions, unbind. There is a last day, and it is not a metaphor. The working surveys, as of this kitchen, are consistent with *w* = −1 to a few percent. That is not a proof it cannot wander later. It is a proof it has not been required to. The working surveys, as of this kitchen, have not been required to make *w* phantom. If a future survey finds it, this paragraph gets a promotion.

**The crunch.** Pull wins. The web falls back. The last three minutes are a hot workshop in reverse, then a scramble, then a singularity or a bounce. The bombs and the leftover glow say we are not on this track unless the shove is a liar. Cold as destiny; allowed as a different valley.

A crunch was the textbook future for a long time, the one that made forever-minds look like a calculation. Chapter 19 already broke the calculation. The shove is winning. Recollapse needs the pull to take the budget back. It has not. Even in a universe that *did* crunch, the mixmaster of Chapter 16 is a scramble, not a control panel. BKL chaos is not a computer. You may still write a crunch as a different bubble’s fate. You may not write it as ours.

**Vacuum decay.** A jitter somewhere turns the vacuum into a ruder vacuum. A bubble of the new one grows at light, rewriting the laws inside it. No warning: the warning would arrive with the bubble. The leftover glow’s vacuum looks stable enough that we are still here. “Stable enough” is not “stable.” Cold-to-warm; do not lose sleep; do not pretend the risk is zero.

The Higgs vacuum, given the measured masses of the Higgs and the top quark, may be a metastable sit: not the lowest valley, a ledge. A ledge can tunnel. Coleman and De Luccia wrote the bubbles; the wall, once nucleated, runs at light. You would not see it coming because seeing is a light-speed fact, and the wall is that fact. The estimated lifetime, in the working Standard Model, is vastly longer than the present age — longer, often, than the fade. That is why you should not lose sleep. The estimate depends on numbers we still sharpen and on physics above the energies we have stacked. That is why you should not pretend the risk is zero. If a bubble arrives, there is no project that outruns it. Mara’s greenhouse, the melt, the library — all of them are inside the same vacuum.

**The bounce.** The end is a middle. A crunch becomes a bang, or a dark sea of extra-dimensional branes slaps, or Penrose’s conformal trick glues a thin future to a next beginning. Cyclic, ekpyrotic, conformal: names, papers, no leftover-glow signature that has stuck. Cold.

A bounce needs the pull to win first, or a different high-curvature theory that turns a bang into a rebound, or a pair of branes that collide in a room we have not photographed. Loop-quantum sketches bounce. Ekpyrotic sketches slap. Penrose’s conformal cyclic cosmology glues a thinned, far future to a next fog by a rescaling that makes infinity look like a start. Each of these is an idea for ideas with equations. None of them has a leftover-glow signature that has survived the surveys as a detection.

![Figure 43. Five rows: Freeze, Rip, Crunch, Decay, Bounce. One word each.](Figures/figs/fig43.png)

Notice what is not on the list: a required forever-mind. Notice what *is* on the list, quietly, as a project rather than a fate: a greenhouse, a dark sea, a machine that does not sleep. Those do not close the cosmos. They occupy a few more worldlines before the fade. That is the only closing this book will bless.

The Omega Point wanted a sixth row: crunch, then mind, then God. Chapter 19 deleted the row. The Final Anthropic Principle wanted the row as a law. The data did not countersign. You may still want mind to last. Wanting is a project. Put it in the greenhouse, in the probe, in the library. Do not put it in the Friedmann equation.

Appendix A43 writes *w*, decay rates, bounce conditions. Here, pick the default without romance: a long accelerating stretch, islands going dark, the leftover glow thinning until even the holes begin to leak. If you want a prettier last page, write it yourself, in a room, while there is still a room.

## 44. Does Time Exist?

We have used “time” as if we knew.

In the kitchen, time is the clock. In relativity, time is a direction in the loaf, mixed with space, sliced differently by walkers. Proper time is a length of thread. The leftover glow picks out a useful slicing — cosmic time — because the glow is almost the same everywhere and we can watch it cool. That slicing is a convenience the universe approximately offers, not a throne.

The wall clock is still the right tool for a casserole. It is a terrible cosmology. GPS is the grown-up version of the same split. The satellites carry clocks. The clocks run fast, relative to the ground, because they sit higher in Earth’s well, and they run slow because they are moving. Both corrections are geometry. The well’s gift is the larger one: about forty-five microseconds a day of speeding-up. The motion’s tax is about seven microseconds a day of slowing-down. The net, about thirty-eight microseconds a day, would be a joke if light were slow. Light is not slow. Thirty-eight millionths of a second, times the speed of light, is on the order of ten kilometers of lie per day if you skip the metric. That is why the phone in the kitchen can find the street. You do not need a philosophy of time to land a plane. You need the interval. That is the first honest answer, and it is hot.

Mara and Eli are still on that argument, years apart. He is allowed to use the wall clock. Her radio is the late relation the clock cannot annex. Chapter 1 already gave them Andromeda as the thing the clock cannot settle. This chapter gives them GPS as the thing the geometry can. The clock is a local tool. The satellites are the same tool, corrected. Neither is a river. Both are relations among events: a tick here, a tick there, a radio that is late on purpose. If “does time exist?” is a way of asking whether those relations are real, the answer is yes, and your dinner depends on it.

Does time *exist*? The honest answers come in flavors.

**Yes, as geometry.** The loaf has a metric. Intervals are real. This is the hot answer, and it is enough to run GPS.

Enough, also, to run the twin who comes home younger, the particle that lasts longer in the lab, the radio that answers twenty minutes late. Chapters 1 through 6 spent this money. An event is a happening. The interval between happenings is the thing the geometry cares about. Proper time is how much of that interval a particular thread accumulates. “Does time exist?” in this flavor is like asking whether length exists. Length is a relation among events along a ruler. Time is a relation among events along a worldline. The relation is as real as the casserole’s doneness. The throne — a universal now the relations are painted on — is what the kitchen clock invented and relativity retired.

**Yes, as an arrow, but the arrow is a boundary.** The laws do not prefer a direction. The low-entropy past does. Eggs smash because the beginning was smooth. Carroll’s Past Hypothesis is this sentence in a better suit. If the past had been a scramble, we would not have a “we.”

The microscopic rules run as well backward as forward. That is not a rumor. It is a feature of the equations that got the three minutes right. The arrow you feel — memory, cause, the way a cup does not unshatter — is not a second law hiding in the metric. It is a fact about the region we call the past: it was special, smooth, low in the kind of mess that would have been a heat death at the start. From a special past, the typical futures are messier. That is the arrow. It is hot as a thermodynamic fact in kitchens and stars. It is warm as a cosmological claim: we do not have a derivation of *why* the past was special. We have the leftover glow as a photograph of a smoothness, and a name for the postulate that says the smoothness was there. The Past Hypothesis is that name. Without it you do not get observers who remember. With it you do not need a river.

Memory is the arrow made intimate. You remember the special past because the special past left records, and records are a kind of order: a photograph, a scar, a leftover glow, a notebook of beaks. You do not remember the fade because the fade has not left you a fossil of itself in your head. That is not a failure of the brain. It is what a boundary condition looks like from inside a thread. An egg smashes and does not un-smash for the same reason. The laws would allow the un-smash. The past would not. The kitchen already knew this before it had a name. Cosmology’s job was to notice that the leftover glow is the same kind of fact, written on the sky.

**Not as a river.** There is no glowing NOW moving down the loaf. Chapters 1 through 6 were that retirement.

A river would be a privileged slice, lit, advancing, making the future into the present and the present into the past. The loaf does not have that furniture. Different walkers slice differently. Andromeda’s navy is not waiting for your kitchen instant. The leftover glow offers a convenient slicing because it is almost the same everywhere, a last-scatter surface you can watch cool. Convenience is not a throne. You may still say “now” to take the casserole out. You should. Just do not promote the kitchen to a cosmology.

**Maybe not as a fundamental word.** In some attempts to quantize gravity, the wave of the universe does not mention time. The Wheeler–DeWitt equation is a constraint, a “this must add up,” not a “this happens next.” Julian Barbour takes the hint all the way: the inventory is configurations, Nows, a heap. Dynamics is what a heap looks like when it contains records of other heaps. Carlo Rovelli takes a different hint: time is what a system with heat says about itself. Thermal time. A useful word for a useful state.

Wheeler and DeWitt wrote a wavefunction of geometry that sits still: Ĥ Ψ = 0, no external *t* on the page. That is the problem of time in quantum gravity, and it is not a slogan. If the whole loaf is the system, there is no clock outside it to say “next.” Time, if it is to be recovered, has to be recovered from inside: a subsystem that can serve as a clock, a semiclassical limit where a geometry becomes classical enough to have a *t*, a relational fact among degrees of freedom. One family of recoveries says: treat part of the loaf as a clock and ask how the rest correlates with it. Another says: when the geometry is large and gentle, a WKB limit hands you something that looks like the Schrödinger equation in a *t* that was not fundamental. Warm-to-cold as a research program. No experiment has selected the recovery. The GPS clock does not adjudicate this fight. GPS lives in the limit where the recovery has already succeeded, or was never needed, because the metric is classical enough to land a plane.

Barbour’s heap is the austere version. The world is a pile of Nows — instantaneous configurations — and what we call motion is a correlation: this Now contains records that match that Now, the way a brain contains a memory that matches a room. There is no moving spotlight. There is a heap with internal likenesses. It is a picture that takes “no river” all the way to “no primitive duration.” It is cold. It is the right kind of cold: it does not add a someone, and it does not contradict GPS, which lives in the classical limit where the heap looks like a loaf.

A fossil, in the heap, is a Now that contains a record of another Now: a glow with a temperature, a notebook with a beak, a scar. Dynamics is the pattern of those containments. You do not need a spotlight moving down a hallway of rooms. You need the rooms to include pictures of other rooms. That is either a deep truth or a way of talking. We do not yet have a leftover-glow test that picks. What we have is a warning: do not smuggle a river back in just because English has a tense.

A tempting merger waits here, and Chapter 37 already taught the habit for refusing it. Barbour has a heap of Nows. Everett has a sum of branches. Both say the inventory is bigger than the slice you remember. Put them in one coat and you get a slogan: every possible Now exists, so the multiverse is just the heap. Two drawers, one bench.

Open them one at a time. The heap is configurations: every arrangement the stuff could have, one point per arrangement, whether or not anything in it remembers anything. That is configuration space, a map of the possible. Everett’s branches are not points on that map. A branch is a term in the wave that has decohered from the other terms, a pattern of records that agree with each other about which face the coin shows. Most points in the heap belong to no branch worth the name. They are scrambles with no records at all. A branch is a thin, well-behaved thread through the heap, picked out by decoherence. Not by time. Not by being possible.

Barbour did build a bridge. In his picture the wave of the universe piles its weight on the Nows that hold consistent records, which he calls time capsules. That is a bridge between two drawers, not a fusion of them. It is a proposal about where the weight sits, and nobody has derived it from anything we can test. The GPS clock is no help here either. It lives where the heap already looks like a loaf.

Hot: decoherence is a measured rate. Warm: Everett as a reading of the rule that builds chemistry. Cold: the heap as the fundamental inventory, and the time capsules as its weighting. Colder than any of them: the fused slogan. Possible is not the same word as realized. A map of every arrangement is not a census of worlds.

Rovelli’s thermal time is a different recovery. A system with enough heat, enough statistical mix, can have a flow defined by that mix: time as what equilibrium says about itself. In ordinary kitchens this is overkill. In a quantum gravity where no *t* is on the page, it is a candidate for where the word comes back. Cold-to-warm. Allowed. Not a photograph.

![Figure 44. A clock face dissolving into a pile of marks.](Figures/figs/fig44.png)

Hartle and Hawking’s no-boundary idea tries to replace “before the bang” with a rounded-off geometry, time crystallizing only when the universe is large enough to have it. Vilenkin tunnels from nothing, which is a technical nothing, not a kitchen nothing. These are cold, and they are the right kind of cold: they are attempts to stop using a word where the word may not apply.

“Before the fog” may not be a kitchen word. Empty, in physics, is already a something: a vacuum, a rule, a jitter. A technical nothing is a path integral without a prior boundary, or a tunneling from a state that is not a waiting room. Neither is an empty oven with the lights off. Chapter 7 already refused the waiting room. This chapter refuses to staff it with a *t*. Cold: we do not have a leftover-glow fossil of the rounding-off. Allowed: the attempt is what you do when you take the problem of time seriously instead of drawing a throne and calling it God.

This book’s working answer, the one that can carry a greenhouse and a dark sea: time exists as relations among events. It does not exist as a stage the events are put down on. It may not exist, in the quantum-gravity limit, as a primitive. The arrow exists because the past was special. You exist as a thread that remembers the special past and cannot remember the fade. That is enough to hit an airlock and call it morning.

The wall clock still useful, not a cosmology. That is the kitchen sentence. Use it to take the casserole out. Use GPS to know where the greenhouse is. Use proper time to know how old the woman is when the window opens. Use cosmic time as a convenience when you want to say how long after the leftover glow’s last scatter a galaxy formed. Do not use any of them as a river the loaf is floating on. Do not use “before” as a room. Do not use a required forever-mind as a clock that runs after the holes have leaked. Relations among events are enough. They were always enough. The rest was a throne we built because kitchens have clocks and clocks have a way of looking like laws.

Hot: intervals, GPS, the arrow as a fact about a smooth past. Warm: the Past Hypothesis as the name of that smoothness; cosmic time as a convenience. Cold: a primitive *t* in the quantum-gravity wave; a waiting room labeled BEFORE; a river. The working answer sits in the hot and the warm and lets the cold stay cold. That is how this book has used temperatures since the front. It will not stop at the last chapter but one.

## 45. The Honest Ending

Let us cash the recognitions, the way a book of this family is supposed to. Cashing is what you can *do* once the recognition has arrived and stayed. A second reading of the same list would be a choir.

An event is a happening: so you stop asking where the casserole “is” in time and ask which happenings it is between. “Now” is a slice you draw: so you keep the word for the kitchen and retire it for Andromeda. The past we can rebuild is a region: so the three minutes are not a myth you are being asked to respect, they are a workshop whose tools you still own. Most of what pulls does not shine: so you weigh by lean, not by glow. Most of the budget shoves: so the future is a fade unless the shove is a liar, and a required last mind is a story. A horizon is a one-way fact: so you do not book a door. A handle is a bill: so you do not staff a subway. Copies are four kinds: so you do not fuse them and then sell a ticket. Arrangement is a filter: so you do not crown the fish. Time is a relation: so the wall clock stays a tool. Each of those is a refusal that leaves a project standing. The project is the point of the cash.

And then the part the leftover glow does not owe us:

A dead world can hold a greenhouse if someone bullies the ice and the hours. A frozen moon can hold a sea if a giant squeezes it, and that sea can hold a second origin or an empty kitchen, and the only way to know is to melt and look, without poisoning the answer. A crew that does not sleep can carry a library farther than flesh. The library is a physical object. The project can fail. Failure is allowed. Destiny was the thing that was not allowed.

That paragraph is the second trunk of the book, and it is not a schedule of downtowns. It is a permit list. Water, carbon, a little quiet, a launch window, tact. No downtown assigned. No recovery of this world promised by leaving it. No helium for a body that wants a thousand centuries. Those rooms, if they are rooms, are later kitchens. This cosmology signs a smaller invoice: a greenhouse that may die in month four, a melt that may taste an empty, a library that may miss. The invoice is interesting *because* it can fail. Destiny cannot fail. That is why destiny was the thing that was not allowed.

![Figure 45. Rover tracks toward a near horizon. Meaning is local, on one worldline.](Figures/figs/fig45.jpg)

If every allowed outcome is on the inventory, this outcome is still the one with your hands in it. If the block is real, the basil you log on a cold morning is as real as the leftover helium. If the block is not quite real, if time is a heap of Nows, the heap still includes the smell of a lie in a room of LEDs. You do not need the universe to have a purpose in order to have a purpose in it. You need a permit — water, carbon, a little quiet — and a refusal to wait for the ending to compute you back.

Local, on one worldline. That is the moral the field equation will not write and does not have to. Everett, if you buy the reading, puts other outcomes on the inventory. The cheap copies, if space is that large, put other kitchens out of reach. Neither of them cooks this basil. Grief is still a fact about one thread. So is a project. So is the decision not to plague a dark sea. Meaning that had to be scheduled at crunch was a way of not having to decide. Meaning that is local is a way of having to.

Is there a path to ultimate knowledge?

There is a path. There is no chapel at the end of it, and the word *ultimate* is a temptation the loaf itself refuses.

Knowledge, in this book, is a record you could have missed. A prediction that can fail. A fossil that does not need your agreement: the glow, the helium, the stretch, the ring around a hole, a leftover in a navy file that still has no name. The path is the widening of that kind of record — not a feeling, not a collapse of the ocean when you look, not a framework that can survive every outcome and therefore explains none.

Darwin’s notebook was already the whole method. A pile of beaks. A sentence that could die. Wallace’s letter. The leftover glow is that habit aimed at a fog. Weinberg’s three minutes are that habit aimed at a workshop. The bombs of 1998 are that habit aimed at a future that did not want to recollapse. A melt that tastes a sea is that habit aimed at a lid of ice. The path does not change species when the subject changes. It does not become a chapel when the subject is the loaf. It stays a record you could have missed. Lengthen it. Do not crown it. Do not build a last room and call the room God.

The steps are few, and they are severe. They are also old. A notebook, a pile of beaks, a sentence that can die: that is already the whole method. The sky is a larger shed of pigeons.

Learn the bills. Special relativity: no shared now, no local dash past light, mail that is late on purpose. General relativity: gravity is the loaf; a handle and a bubble are sentences that still owe a stuff we have not stacked. Quantum mechanics: amplitudes, decoherence, pairings without a telegram. The seam where the two great theories do not yet share a kitchen is the largest honest hole we have. Sit in it. Do not furnish it with a visitor or a god of the gaps.

The bills are not homework for a degree. They are the price of not being fooled. Special relativity is why Andromeda’s navy is a parable and not a paradox. General relativity is why a horizon is a fact about events, why GPS needs a metric, why a handle has an invoice. Quantum mechanics is why a pairing is not a radio and why a branch, if Everett is right, is not a ticket. The seam is the hole where those two great theories do not yet agree on a kitchen: a geometry that is also a quantum object, a time that may not be a primitive, a bang that may not have a before. Sitting in the seam is the opposite of filling it. Filling it is how you hire a craftsman. Sitting in it is how you keep the next record honest. **String theory cannot currently be tested.** It is one of the attempts to furnish the seam. It has not named a kill-shot. Until it does, it is a large, ingenious maybe. Maybes are allowed. Maybes are not results.

Learn the walls. An event horizon is a fact about influence. A cosmic event horizon is a fact about which galaxies will never get your letter. A branch, if Everett is right, is a fact about a term you cannot enter. “Before the fog” may not be a word. Those are not insults. They are the shape of what a worldline is allowed to know.

Walls are not dares. A dare is a poster that says the wall is a door if you are brave. Chapter 24 already fired the poster. You cannot fetch a letter from inside a hole. You cannot fetch a letter from a galaxy past the cosmic event horizon. You cannot hop a branch. You cannot sit in a waiting room labeled BEFORE and interview the fog. Knowing the walls is part of the path. It keeps you from spending a life on a ticket that was never for sale. It also keeps you from despair: the rooms that *are* in your future cone — a dead world with ice in its bones, a moon with a sea, a dish that can hear a shout — are not small because they are not the whole loaf. They are the part of the loaf a thread can still work.

Refuse what cannot die. A story that fits every leftover glow is not a story. String theory, as it stands, has not named a kill-shot. A craftsman who leaves no channel nature does not use has not clocked in. An observation-creates-life that cannot say what counted as the first observer is a regress, not a path.

The refusal is a temperature rule. If a claim cannot be wrong, it is not a claim in this kitchen. The leftover glow could have been a different temperature; it was not. The helium could have been a different fraction; it was not. The stretch could have been slowing; it is not. Those are sentences that could have died. Keep them. Fire the ones that survive every outcome.

Build the next record. A telescope that can smell an oxygen-methane pair and be wrong. A dish that can hear a shout and know the shout was not the ionosphere. A melt that tastes a dark sea without writing over it. A library that is a box, not a sermon. A legislature, even, when it is doing its only smart job: asking whether the leftover in its own air is an adversary, a secret of its own, or a file that still says unidentified. That last is not ultimate knowledge. It is hygiene.

Melt, dish, library: three beaks. The melt is the only way a second origin, or a clean empty, leaves this solar system as a measurement instead of a mood. The dish is the only way a shout that is not us becomes a fossil. The library is the only way a trick of carbon and water travels farther than a body that has to eat. None of them is a destiny. Each of them can fail. Each of them is a widening of the record. Planetary protection is the tact that keeps the melt from becoming a flood. Chapter 32 already wrote the rule: look first, seed later, never confuse the two. Tact is not a chapel. Tact is how a local craftsman does not become a plague.

What would *ultimate* have to mean, if we kept the word? Not a God’s-eye inventory of every branch, every before, every event outside our cone. That inventory, if it exists, is not a destination. Ultimate, for a thread, is asymptotic: more of the loaf brought into a record that can fail, fewer sermons in the gaps, a closing of the QM–GR seam if the seam can be closed, a second dictionary under ice or a clean empty, a leftover named or honestly unnamed. You will not finish. The fade, if the fade is coming, will not wait until you finish. The point of the path is not to arrive at a last fact. It is to be the kind of worldline that can tell a last fact from a mood.

Lengthen without a chapel. That is the whole close, if you want it in four words. A chapel is a last room that cannot be wrong. This cosmology has no such room. It has a kitchen clock that is still useful, a leftover glow that is still late, a shove that is still winning, a seam that is still a hole, and a handful of projects that still might fail. That is a richer inventory than a throne. It is also a ruder one. Rudeness is the courtesy of a universe that does not need us in order to have had a first three minutes, and does not owe us a last mind in order for a morning to be worth hitting an airlock.

The clock on the kitchen wall is still lying. Let it. Take the casserole out. Then, if you have the years and the machines and the tact not to plague a dark sea, send a library. Not because the loaf requires it. Because you can, and because the fade, if the fade is coming, will not be hurried or helped by your waiting.

That is the honest ending. It is not a door. It is a door you build, on a world that did not ask for one, under a sky that is late on purpose. It is also the only path this cosmology can sign: **widen the record, pay the bills, stop at the walls, and do not hire a someone to finish your homework.**

Meaning, if you still want the word after forty-five chapters, is local, expensive, and optional. It lives on one worldline, in a room that smells like a lie, under a clock that was never a cosmology. Grow the basil. Log the pH. Melt if you can do it without writing over the answer. Send the box if you have the tact. Admit that the dirt can vote no. That admission is not despair. It is the difference between a project and a destiny, and it is the last recognition this book will cash.
