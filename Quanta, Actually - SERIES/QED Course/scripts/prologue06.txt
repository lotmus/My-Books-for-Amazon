# How to use this prologue

The symbols | ⟩ and ⟨ | look like a secret handshake. They are not. A ket is a name for a complete physical situation. A bra is a question you ask that situation. The answer is one of Prologue 1's arrows, i.e. a complex number. Lesson 10 puts this on Hilbert space with integrals. This prologue is the version for someone who has never seen the notation. Expect three to five hours.

## Learning objectives

- Read |ψ⟩ as "the situation called ψ" and ⟨φ| as "the question: how much of φ is in this?"

- Compute P = |⟨φ|ψ⟩|² as the probability that situation ψ is found as φ.

- Use ⟨ψ|ψ⟩=1 as the statement that some outcome happens.

- Expand a ket in a basis, |ψ⟩=Σ c_n |n⟩, and read c_n as arrows.

- Translate a sentence of ordinary English into a bra-ket sandwich.

## Notation and conventions

| Symbol | Meaning |
| |ψ⟩ | A ket: the whole situation, Prologue 1's "what is going on" |
| ⟨φ| | A bra: the question that picks out one possibility |
| ⟨φ|ψ⟩ | The arrow (complex number) from ψ to φ |
| ⟨ψ|ψ⟩=1 | The situation is normalized: probabilities will add to 1 |

# 1 A ket is not a vector until Lesson 1 says so

Write |electron here⟩, |photon going left⟩, |click in the detector⟩. The vertical bar and the angle bracket are just a frame around a name. You can call the name anything. Physicists use Greek letters because they run out of ordinary ones, not because the idea got harder.

The whole of quantum mechanics is: situations combine like Prologue 1's arrows. So kets add, and they can be stretched by a complex number. That is Lesson 1's vector space, postponed until the name is not scary.

# 2 A bra is the question

⟨click| is the question "did the detector click?" asked of whatever ket you sandwich it onto.

$$ ⟨ click | ψ ⟩

is the arrow for "situation ψ produces a click." The probability is the square of that arrow's length, exactly Prologue 1.

### Worked example 1

English: "If the photon left the source, how likely is a click in patch A?" Bra-ket: P=|⟨A|source⟩|². The ket |source⟩ is the situation after emission. The bra ⟨A| is the question "in patch A?" You do not add P(A)+P(B) when A and B are two undistinguished paths to the same click; you add ⟨A_path1|source⟩+⟨A_path2|source⟩, then square. That is Prologue 2 in this notation.

# 3 Normalization

⟨ψ|ψ⟩=1 means: if you ask "is it this situation?", and it is this situation, the arrow has length 1, P=1. If a ket is not normalized, divide by its length. Lesson 10 will write this as ∫|ψ(x)|² d³x=1. Same sentence, more furniture.

# 4 A basis is a complete list of questions

If the detector can only click in patch A or patch B, the two kets |A⟩ and |B⟩ are a basis. Any situation is

$$ |ψ⟩ = ⟨A|ψ⟩ |A⟩ + ⟨B|ψ⟩ |B⟩

The coefficients are the arrows. If you know all the arrows onto a complete list of exclusive outcomes, you know the ket.

# 5 What this is not

A ket is not a little particle. A bra is not a machine in the lab. ⟨φ|ψ⟩ is not "φ times ψ" in the sense of multiplying two waves on a blackboard until Lesson 10 says it is an integral. For this opening, sandwich = arrow.

# Summary

1. A ket names a situation.

2. A bra asks a yes-amount question.

3. The sandwich is Prologue 1's arrow.

4. Its length squared is the probability.

5. Lesson 10 is this, with integrals.

# Solutions

**1.** |ground⟩, or |g⟩.

**2.** |⟨excited|atom⟩|².

**3.** |i/2|²=1/4.

**4.** So that the probabilities of a complete list of exclusive outcomes add to 1.

**5.** |ψ⟩=⟨A|ψ⟩|A⟩+⟨B|ψ⟩|B⟩.

**6.** Prologue 8 and Lesson 51: in-state to out-state.

**7.** No. It is the situation that produces clicks with certain arrows.

**8.** Reversing "from ψ to φ" reverses the arrow's direction, which is complex conjugation.
