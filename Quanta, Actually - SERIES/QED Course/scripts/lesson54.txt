# How to use this lesson

Every tool this course has built is now put to use at once. Electron-muon scattering, e⁻(p₁)+μ⁻(p₂)→e⁻(p₃)+μ⁻(p₄), is the simplest process with a genuine, nontrivial QED cross section: two distinguishable charged particles, exchanging a single photon, with no second diagram to worry about, since the electron and muon cannot be swapped for one another the way two identical particles could. Because the two fermion lines are independent, the calculation factors cleanly into an "electron tensor" and a "muon tensor," each built from Lesson 52's master formula and Lesson 21's trace identities, contracted together. The result, converted to Mandelstam variables with Lesson 53's tools, is a clean, famous, and completely explicit formula. Expect eight to ten hours; this is the longest calculation in the course so far.

## Learning objectives

- Derive the second-order S-matrix element for electron-muon scattering by identifying the specific Wick contraction that connects the electron and muon currents through a single virtual photon.

- Show that the internal photon's momentum-squared is exactly t, the Mandelstam variable, confirming this is a t-channel process with no competing diagram.

- Derive the amplitude iℳ=(ie²/t)[ū(p₃)γ^μu(p₁)][ū(p₄)γ_μu(p₂)].

- Show that spin-averaging and summing |ℳ|² factors into a product of an electron trace and a muon trace, using Lesson 52's master formula on each fermion line independently.

- Carry out the full tensor contraction, converting every dot product to Mandelstam variables, to obtain the general (massive) result and its massless limit, 2e⁴(s²+u²)/t².

- State, as a tool with its derivation deferred to Lesson 59, the two-body center-of-momentum cross-section formula, and combine it with this lesson's |ℳ|² to give an explicit dσ/dΩ.

- Reproduce the full calculation, from diagram to final formula, from memory.

# 1 The diagram and amplitude

IMG: L54_emu.png | 3.8 | Figure 54.1. Electron–muon scattering at lowest order: one diagram, one photon in the t-channel, q = p₁ − p₃.

## 1.1 The process

Electron-muon scattering proceeds, at leading order, by a single virtual photon exchanged between the two particles: the electron emits (or absorbs) the photon at one vertex, and the muon absorbs (or emits) it at the other. Since the electron and the muon are distinguishable particles, there is no second diagram obtained by relabeling which particle does what, unlike Lesson 50's Compton scattering.

## 1.2 The Wick contraction

The interaction Hamiltonian has one term for each charged field, ℋ_int=−e[ψ̄_eγ^μψ_eA_μ+ψ̄_μγ^μψ_μA_μ] (the same coupling e for both, by the universality of Lesson 44). At second order, S^(2)=(−i)²/2!∫d⁴x₁d⁴x₂ T{ℋ_int(x₁)ℋ_int(x₂)} contains a cross term, 2e²[ψ̄_eγ^μψ_eA_μ](x₁)[ψ̄_μγ^νψ_μA_ν](x₂) (the factor of 2 from the two ways of assigning the electron- and muon-current pieces to x₁ versus x₂), in which ψ̄_e(x₁), ψ_e(x₁) contract with the external electron legs, ψ̄_μ(x₂), ψ_μ(x₂) contract with the external muon legs, and A_μ(x₁), A_ν(x₂) contract with each other, forming the internal photon line, using ⟨0|T{A_μ(x₁)A_ν(x₂)}|0⟩=D_Fμν(x₁−x₂) (Lesson 48, Section 4.1, the covariant propagator this course uses in practice).

## 1.3 Assembling the amplitude

Substituting D_Fμν(x₁−x₂)=∫d⁴q/(2π)⁴[−ig_μν/(q²+iε)]e^(−iq(x₁−x₂)) and every external factor (Lesson 49), the x₁ and x₂ integrals give (2π)⁴δ⁴(p₃−p₁−q) and (2π)⁴δ⁴(p₄−p₂+q) respectively, forcing q=p₁−p₃ and overall momentum conservation p₁+p₂=p₃+p₄, as in Lesson 50's mechanism. The (−i)²/2!=−1/2 prefactor combines with the combinatorial factor of 2 to give an overall −1 (Exercise 2), and

$$ i ℳ = ( i e / t ) e , [ ū(p₃)γ^μu(p₁) ] , [ ū(p₄)γ_μu(p₂) ] = (i e²/t) , [ ū(p₃)γ^μu(p₁) ] , [ ū(p₄)γ_μu(p₂) ]

using q²=(p₁−p₃)²=t (Lesson 53, Section 1.1) for the internal photon's momentum-squared.

### Worked example 1

Explain, in one sentence, why electron-muon scattering has exactly one diagram, in contrast with Compton scattering's two. Wick's theorem produces one term for every way of assigning the field operators to the given external states consistent with which field annihilates or creates which particle; since ψ_e can only contract with electron states and ψ_μ only with muon states, there is only one way to complete the contraction of Section 1.2, unlike Compton scattering, where both photon fields could equally well be paired with either the incoming or the outgoing photon.

# 2 Squaring and averaging

## 2.1 The spin-averaged, spin-summed square

Neither experiment prepares nor measures the spins of these particles, so the physical quantity is (using Lesson 52, Section 1.2, with two unmeasured initial fermions)

$$ (1/4) Σ_(spins) |ℳ|² = (1/4)(e⁴/t²) Σ_(s₁,s₃) Σ_(s₂,s₄) [ū(p₃)γ^μu(p₁)][ū(p₃)γ^νu(p₁)]^* [ū(p₄)γ_μu(p₂)][ū(p₄)γ_νu(p₂)]^*

## 2.2 Factorization

Because the electron spinors (p₁, p₃) and the muon spinors (p₂, p₄) never mix in the amplitude of Section 1.3, the double sum factors exactly into a sum over electron spins and a sum over muon spins, each evaluated independently by Lesson 52's master formula:

$$ (1/4) Σ|ℳ|² = (1/4)(e⁴/t²) , L_e^(μν) , L_(μ,μν)

$$ L_e^(μν) := Σ_(s₁,s₃) [ū(p₃)γ^μu(p₁)][ū(p₃)γ^νu(p₁)]^* = Tr[γ^μ(p̸₁+m_e)γ^ν(p̸₃+m_e)]

$$ L_(μ,μν) := Σ_(s₂,s₄) [ū(p₄)γ_μu(p₂)][ū(p₄)γ_νu(p₂)]^* = Tr[γ_μ(p̸₂+m_μ)γ_ν(p̸₄+m_μ)]

### Worked example 2

Explain why this factorization is a genuine simplification specific to electron-muon scattering, not a generic feature of every QED process. The two fermion lines here belong to two independent particle species with no shared field, so the spin sum over one line's spinors has no effect on the other line's factors; a process such as Compton scattering, whose amplitude is a sum of two diagrams involving the same electron line throughout, does not factor this cleanly, since interference terms between the two diagrams mix the electron spinors in a way that cannot be separated into two independent sums.

# 3 The electron and muon tensors

## 3.1 The electron tensor

Using Lesson 21, Section 5's result directly, with p₁, p₃ the electron's momenta,

$$ L_e^(μν) = 4 [ p₁^μp₃^ν + p₁^νp₃^μ − g^(μν)(p₁·p₃ − m_e²) ]

## 3.2 The muon tensor

By the identical formula, applied to the muon's momenta and mass,

$$ L_(μ,μν) = 4 [ p_(2μ)p_(4ν) + p_(2ν)p_(4μ) − g_(μν)(p₂·p₄ − m_μ²) ]

### Worked example 3

Explain why no new trace calculation was needed for Section 3.2, beyond relabeling Section 3.1's result. Lesson 21's trace identity Tr[(p̸_a+m)γ^μ(p̸_b+m)γ^ν]=4[p_a^μp_b^ν+p_a^νp_b^μ−g^μν(p_a·p_b−m²)] was derived for arbitrary momenta p_a, p_b and mass m, with no reference to which specific particle they belong to; substituting the muon's own momenta and mass reproduces the identical formula with no new algebra.

# 4 Contracting the two tensors

## 4.1 The full contraction

Multiplying L_e^μν by L_μ,μν, the nine resulting terms (Exercise 10 works through each) simplify remarkably: the cross term proportional to (p₁·p₃)(p₂·p₄) cancels exactly between the three contributions that produce it, and the term proportional to m_e²m_μ² cancels exactly as well, leaving

$$ L_e^(μν) L_(μ,μν) = 32 (p₁·p₂)(p₃·p₄) + 32 (p₁·p₄)(p₂·p₃) − 32 m_μ²(p₁·p₃) − 32 m_e²(p₂·p₄) + 64 m_e²m_μ²

## 4.2 Converting to Mandelstam variables

Using Lesson 53, Section 5.2's table, with p₁·p₂=p₃·p₄=(s−m_e²−m_μ²)/2, p₁·p₄=p₂·p₃=(m_e²+m_μ²−u)/2, p₁·p₃=m_e²−t/2, p₂·p₄=m_μ²−t/2 (Exercise 13 substitutes every term explicitly),

$$ L_e^(μν) L_(μ,μν) = 8 (s−m_e²−m_μ²)² + 8 (u−m_e²−m_μ²)² + 16 t (m_e²+m_μ²)

so

$$ (1/4) Σ |ℳ|² = (2e⁴/t²) [ (s−m_e²−m_μ²)² + (u−m_e²−m_μ²)² + 2t(m_e²+m_μ²) ]

the complete, exact result for arbitrary electron and muon masses.

## 4.3 The massless limit

Neglecting both masses (valid whenever √s is much larger than either m_e or m_μ),

$$ (1/4) Σ |ℳ|² = 2 e⁴ (s²+u²) / t²

a clean, famous, and completely explicit result, symmetric in s and u, singular only at t=0 (forward scattering, where the exchanged photon becomes soft, Lesson 75's later subject).

### Worked example 4

Explain why the exact cancellation of the (p₁·p₃)(p₂·p₄) term in Section 4.1 is a meaningful check on the calculation, not merely a convenient simplification. Nothing in the structure of the tensor product obviously requires this specific combination to vanish; that it does, exactly, rather than leaving some small leftover piece, is the kind of clean cancellation that signals a calculation has been carried out correctly, in the same spirit as the exact cancellations already verified in Lessons 42 and 46–47.

## 5.1 The two-body formula

In the center-of-momentum frame, for elastic 2→2 scattering, the general cross-section formula of Lesson 51 reduces (a phase-space simplification derived in full in Lesson 59) to

$$ dσ/dΩ = (1/(64π²s)) |ℳ|²

## 5.2 The explicit result

Combining Section 4.3 with Section 5.1, in the massless limit,

$$ dσ/dΩ = (2e⁴/(64π²s)) (s²+u²)/t² = (e⁴/(32π²s)) (s²+u²)/t²

with s, t, u further related to the center-of-momentum scattering angle θ by Lesson 53, Section 4.3 (t=−2p²(1−cosθ), with u fixed by the sum rule once s and t are known), giving a complete, explicit prediction for the angular distribution of scattered electrons, the first such prediction this course has produced from first principles.

# 7 Summary

1. Electron-muon scattering proceeds through a single t-channel photon-exchange diagram, with no competing diagram, since the two particles are distinguishable.

2. The amplitude is iℳ=(ie²/t)[ū(p₃)γ^μu(p₁)][ū(p₄)γ_μu(p₂)], with the internal photon's momentum-squared exactly equal to t.

3. Spin-averaging and summing factors into an electron tensor and a muon tensor, each given directly by Lesson 21's trace formula.

4. Contracting the two tensors, several terms cancel exactly, leaving a clean general result in terms of s, t, u, and both masses.

5. In the massless limit, this reduces to the famous result (1/4)Σ|ℳ|²=2e⁴(s²+u²)/t².

6. Combined with the two-body cross-section formula (stated here, derived in Lesson 59), this gives a complete, explicit prediction for the scattering angular distribution.

# Solutions

*Attempt each exercise before reading its solution. If you are stuck, read only the first sentence of the solution, then try again.*

**1.** This term would connect two electron-current insertions to each other, requiring the external states to include four electron-related particles (some combination of incoming/outgoing electrons or positrons) at the two vertices, with no muon involved at all; it contributes instead to processes such as electron-electron scattering (Lesson 55) or electron self-energy loops (Lesson 64), not to a process with an external muon.

**2.** T{ℋ_int(x₁)ℋ_int(x₂)} is symmetric under x₁↔x₂; the specific contraction of Section 1.2 (electron legs at x₁, muon legs at x₂) has an exact mirror image with the labels swapped (electron legs at x₂, muon legs at x₁), and since the integral runs over both x₁ and x₂ independently, both labelings contribute identically, giving a factor of 2, as in Lesson 50, Exercise 8.

**3.** Combining all exponential factors as in Lesson 50's method: at x₁, e^(ip₃x₁)e^(−ip₁x₁)e^(−iqx₁)=e^(i(p₃−p₁−q)x₁), integrating to (2π)⁴δ⁴(p₃−p₁−q); at x₂, e^(ip₄x₂)e^(−ip₂x₂)e^(iqx₂)=e^(i(p₄−p₂+q)x₂), integrating to (2π)⁴δ⁴(p₄−p₂+q). The first delta function forces q=p₃−p₁, equivalently −q=p₁−p₃; since the propagator depends only on q², either sign convention gives the same physical result, and taking q=p₁−p₃ (electron momentum transfer) is the standard choice.

**4.** By definition, t=(p₁−p₃)² (Lesson 53, Section 1.1); since q=p₁−p₃ (Exercise 3), q²=(p₁−p₃)²=t directly.

**5.** Following Lesson 50's method: the prefactor (−1/2)×2=−1 (Exercise 2), combined with e² (from expanding the two vertex terms) and the propagator's explicit −i (Lesson 48, Section 4.1), gives (−1)(e²)(−i)=ie². Comparing with the momentum-independent numerical factors of (ie)(ie)(−i/t): (ie)²(−i)=i²e²(−i)=(−e²)(−i)=ie², matching exactly.

**6.** Lesson 52, Section 1.2, gives a factor of 1/2 for each unmeasured initial fermion spin; electron-muon scattering has two unmeasured initial spins (the electron's and the muon's), so the combined averaging factor is (1/2)(1/2)=1/4, not the 1/2 appropriate to a process with only one unmeasured initial spin.

**7.** With two electrons, both incoming legs and both outgoing legs would be electron states, drawn from the same field ψ_e; Wick's theorem would then produce an additional diagram from the alternative way of pairing the external electron states with the field operators (the crossed, or exchange, diagram), which mixes the electron spinors between what would otherwise be two separate lines, preventing the clean factorization of Section 2.2.

**8.** Tr[(p̸_a+m)γ^μ(p̸_b+m)γ^ν]=4[p_a^μp_b^ν+p_a^νp_b^μ−g^μν(p_a·p_b−m²)] (Lesson 21, Section 5). Setting p_a=p₁, p_b=p₃, m=m_e: L_e^μν=4[p₁^μp₃^ν+p₁^νp₃^μ−g^μν(p₁·p₃−m_e²)], exactly Section 3.1.

**9.** Setting p_a=p₂, p_b=p₄, m=m_μ in the same formula: L_μ,μν=4[p₂_μp₄_ν+p₂_νp₄_μ−g_μν(p₂·p₄−m_μ²)], exactly Section 3.2.

**10.** Writing L_e^μν=A+B+C with A=p₁^μp₃^ν, B=p₁^νp₃^μ, C=−g^μν(p₁·p₃−m_e²), and L_μ,μν=A′+B′+C′ analogously: AA′=(p₁·p₂)(p₃·p₄), AB′=(p₁·p₄)(p₂·p₃), CC′=4(p₁·p₃−m_e²)(p₂·p₄−m_μ²) (verifying three of the nine terms as requested; the remaining six follow by the same direct index contraction, as tabulated in Section 4.1's derivation).

**11.** The three (p₁·p₃)(p₂·p₄)-proportional contributions come from AC′+BC′ (each giving −(p₂·p₄−m_μ²)(p₁·p₃), contributing −2(p₁·p₃)(p₂·p₄) net after also expanding the m_μ² piece separately) and CA′+CB′ (giving −2(p₁·p₃)(p₂·p₄) similarly) and CC′ (giving +4(p₁·p₃)(p₂·p₄) from its leading term); summing the coefficients −2−2+4=0, confirming exact cancellation.

**12.** The m_e²m_μ² terms arise only from CC′=4(p₁·p₃−m_e²)(p₂·p₄−m_μ²), whose expansion contains a single +4m_e²m_μ² piece; tracking this single contribution through Section 4.1's bookkeeping (no other of the nine terms produces an m_e²m_μ² piece at all, since A, B, A′, B′ contain no mass dependence), it survives unmodified, matching the final +64m_e²m_μ² term in Section 4.1's result (4×16=64, restoring the overall factor of 16 from L_e^μνL_μ,μν=16×[the bracketed sum]).

**13.** Substituting p₁·p₂=(s−m_e²−m_μ²)/2 (giving (p₁·p₂)(p₃·p₄)=(s−m_e²−m_μ²)²/4, using p₃·p₄=p₁·p₂ from Lesson 53, Exercise 13), p₁·p₄=(m_e²+m_μ²−u)/2 (giving (p₁·p₄)(p₂·p₃)=(u−m_e²−m_μ²)²/4, using the squared value's sign-independence), p₁·p₃=m_e²−t/2, and p₂·p₄=m_μ²−t/2 into Section 4.1's expression, and simplifying (the m_e²m_μ² cross terms cancelling as in Exercise 12), gives exactly Section 4.2's result.

**14.** Setting m_e=m_μ=0 in Section 4.2's result: (2e⁴/t²)[(s−0)²+(u−0)²+2t(0)]=(2e⁴/t²)[s²+u²]=2e⁴(s²+u²)/t², confirming Section 4.3.

**15.** With s+t+u=0 (massless limit), u=−s−t; substituting into 2e⁴(s²+u²)/t² gives 2e⁴[s²+(s+t)²]/t², a form manifestly built from s and t alone with no separate u; this is algebraically equivalent to the original s↔u-symmetric expression, but the symmetry is only manifest in the original s,t,u form, since after eliminating u in favor of s and t the expression no longer looks symmetric under any simple relabeling of s and t alone, confirming that the s↔u symmetry is a genuine, physically meaningful property of the underlying amplitude (reflecting the s-channel-like and u-channel-like roles the two variables play relative to a fixed t) rather than an accident of how the formula happens to be written.

**16.** A −1 sign or combinatorial doubling of the kind Lesson 37 associated with fermion loops, or that Lesson 55 will associate with identical-particle exchange, arises specifically when two field operators of the same type could be contracted with either of two indistinguishable external states, producing genuinely distinct Wick-contraction terms that must be added (with a relative sign, for fermions) or when a closed loop requires a cyclic reordering; here, Worked example 1 already established that only one contraction is possible at all, since ψ_e can never be confused with ψ_μ, so neither phenomenon has any opportunity to arise, and the calculation proceeds with a single term and no exchange-related sign.
