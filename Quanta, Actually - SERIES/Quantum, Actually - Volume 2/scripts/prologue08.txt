# How to use this prologue

The symbols | ⟩ and ⟨ | look like a secret handshake. They are not. A ket is a name for a complete physical situation; a bra is a question you ask that situation; the answer is one of the arrows of Prologues 1–6, a complex number. The second half of this prologue writes the law that turns those arrows as time passes, the Schrödinger equation: iℏ times how fast the ket changes equals a machine Ĥ, the energy, acting on the ket. Lessons 10 and 12 do both properly. This is the version for someone who has never seen the notation. Expect five to eight hours.

## Learning objectives

- Read |ψ⟩ as "the situation called ψ" and ⟨φ| as "the question: how much of φ is in this?"

- Compute P = |⟨φ|ψ⟩|² as the probability that situation ψ is found as φ.

- Use ⟨ψ|ψ⟩=1 as the statement that some outcome happens.

- Expand a ket in a basis, |ψ⟩=Σ c_n |n⟩, and read c_n as arrows.

- Translate a sentence of ordinary English into a bra-ket sandwich.

- Write iℏ d|ψ⟩/dt = Ĥ|ψ⟩ and say every symbol in English.

- Explain a stationary state: the ket rotates, the probabilities do not.

- Connect the rotation rate of Prologue 1 to E/ℏ.

- State that Ĥ is the energy, and that the Schrödinger equation cannot survive relativity unchanged (Lesson 19).

# 1 A ket is not a vector until Lesson 1 says so

Write |electron here⟩, |photon going left⟩, |click in the detector⟩. The vertical bar and the angle bracket are just a frame around a name. You can call the name anything. Physicists use Greek letters because they run out of ordinary ones, not because the idea got harder.

The whole of quantum mechanics is: situations combine like Prologue 1's arrows. So kets add, and they can be stretched by a complex number. That is Lesson 1's vector space, postponed until the name is not scary.

# 2 A bra is the question

⟨click| is the question "did the detector click?" asked of whatever ket you sandwich it onto.

$$ ⟨ click | ψ ⟩

is the arrow for "situation ψ produces a click." The probability is the square of that arrow's length, exactly Prologue 1.

IMG: P8_braket.png | 5.6 | Figure P8.1. A ket names the situation; a bra is the question; their sandwich ⟨φ|ψ⟩ is an arrow; the detector reports only its squared length.

### Worked example 1

English: "If the photon left the source, how likely is a click in patch A?" Bra-ket: P=|⟨A|source⟩|². The ket |source⟩ is the situation after emission. The bra ⟨A| is the question "in patch A?" You do not add P(A)+P(B) when A and B are two undistinguished paths to the same click; you add ⟨A_path1|source⟩+⟨A_path2|source⟩, then square. That is Prologue 2 in this notation.

# 3 Normalization

⟨ψ|ψ⟩=1 means: if you ask "is it this situation?", and it is this situation, the arrow has length 1, P=1. If a ket is not normalized, divide by its length. Lesson 10 will write this as ∫|ψ(x)|² d³x=1. Same sentence, more furniture.

# 4 A basis is a complete list of questions

If the detector can only click in patch A or patch B, the two kets |A⟩ and |B⟩ are a basis. Any situation is

$$ |ψ⟩ = ⟨A|ψ⟩ |A⟩ + ⟨B|ψ⟩ |B⟩

The coefficients are the arrows. If you know all the arrows onto a complete list of exclusive outcomes, you know the ket.

# 5 What this is not

A ket is not a little particle. A bra is not a machine in the lab. ⟨φ|ψ⟩ is not "φ times ψ" in the sense of multiplying two waves on a blackboard until Lesson 10 says it is an integral. For this opening, sandwich = arrow.

# 6 The law in one line

$$ i ℏ  d|ψ⟩ / dt  =  Ĥ |ψ⟩

English, left to right:

- i: rotate the arrow by 90°.
- ℏ: a conversion between energy and rotation rate. In this course, often 1.
- d|ψ⟩/dt: how the situation is changing right now.
- Ĥ|ψ⟩: take the situation and ask "what is its energy mix?" Ĥ is a machine, not a number, until the situation is an energy eigenstate.

If Ĥ|ψ⟩=E|ψ⟩, a single energy, the solution is |ψ(t)⟩=e^{−iEt/ℏ}|ψ(0)⟩. The whole ket multiplies by a rotating arrow of length 1. Every sandwich ⟨φ|ψ(t)⟩ picks up that rotation. Probabilities, being lengths squared, do not notice a global rotation. That is a stationary state.

### Worked example 3

Prologue 1 said blue light's arrow rotates faster than red. Where is that in Schrödinger? A photon of energy E=hf has rotation rate E/ℏ=2πf. Blue has larger f, hence larger E, hence faster rotation. The easy QED clock hand and the Schrödinger phase are the same object.

# 7 Superposition moves

If |ψ⟩=|A⟩+|B⟩, two different energies, each piece rotates at its own rate. The relative arrow between A and B swings. Then |⟨click|ψ⟩|² can tick. That is every interference experiment that changes in time, and every electron that moves.

# 8 What Schrödinger cannot do

The equation treats time as a driver and space as a label on the wave function. Relativity will not allow that split. Lesson 18 tries Klein–Gordon, Lesson 20 succeeds with Dirac. This prologue is the nonrelativistic machine, and it is still the right machine for atoms when v≪c, including the hydrogen that Lesson 77 will shift by a loop.

# Summary

1. A ket names a situation.

2. A bra asks a yes-amount question.

3. The sandwich is Prologue 1's arrow.

4. Its length squared is the probability.

5. Lesson 10 is this, with integrals.

6. The Schrödinger equation says how a ket changes.

7. Ĥ is the energy machine.

8. One energy: rotate, probabilities sit still.

9. Several energies: relative arrows move, interference ticks.

10. Relativity will replace the equation; the idea "energy rotates arrows" stays.

# Solutions

*Attempt each exercise before reading its solution. If you are stuck, read only the first sentence of the solution, then try again.*

**1.** |ground⟩, or |g⟩.

**2.** |⟨excited|atom⟩|².

**3.** |i/2|²=1/4.

**4.** So that the probabilities of a complete list of exclusive outcomes add to 1.

**5.** |ψ⟩=⟨A|ψ⟩|A⟩+⟨B|ψ⟩|B⟩.

**6.** Prologue 9 and Lesson 51: in-state to out-state.

**7.** No. It is the situation that produces clicks with certain arrows.

**8.** Reversing "from ψ to φ" reverses the arrow's direction, which is complex conjugation.

**9.** Energy rotates the situation's arrows.

**10.** e^{−iEt/ℏ}|ψ(0)⟩.

**11.** A common rotating arrow of length 1 cancels in |⟨φ|ψ⟩|².

**12.** E=hf, rate E/ℏ=2πf; blue has larger f.

**13.** The relative phase moves; P can tick.

**14.** Time and space must enter symmetrically; Schrödinger does not.

**15.** Lesson 12.

**16.** T=2π/E.
