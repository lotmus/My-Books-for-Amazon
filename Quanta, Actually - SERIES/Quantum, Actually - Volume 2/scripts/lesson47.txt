# How to use this lesson

Lesson 46 built the scalar Feynman propagator by time-ordering the two-point function D(x−y) of Lesson 35. This lesson repeats the construction for the electron, starting from the un-time-ordered fermionic two-point function S(x−y)=⟨0|ψ(x)ψ̄(y)|0⟩ already defined in Lesson 37. One new subtlety appears: fermionic time-ordering carries a minus sign between the two orderings, a consequence of the same anticommutation structure responsible for Lesson 37's sign rules. Handling that minus sign correctly leads to a genuinely elegant result: the electron propagator is obtained from the scalar one, S_F(x−y)=(i∂̸_x+m)Δ_F(x−y), by applying the exact same differential operator that already related S(x−y) to D(x−y) in Lesson 37. Expect five to seven hours.

## Learning objectives

- State the fermionic time-ordering rule T{ψ(x)ψ̄(y)}=θ(x⁰−y⁰)ψ(x)ψ̄(y)−θ(y⁰−x⁰)ψ̄(y)ψ(x) and explain the origin of its minus sign.

- Show that differentiating Δ_F(x−y) with ∂̸_x produces no extra delta-function term, because the would-be term is proportional to D(x−y)−D(y−x) at equal times, which vanishes.

- Derive S_F(x−y)=(i∂̸_x+m)Δ_F(x−y) from the fermionic time-ordered definition.

- Derive (i∂̸_x−m)S_F(x−y)=iδ⁴(x−y), confirming S_F is a Green's function of the free Dirac operator.

- Derive the momentum-space form S_F(p)=i(p̸+m)/(p²−m²+iε) by applying (i∂̸_x+m) to Δ_F's momentum integral.

- Identify the residue of S_F(p) at its pole with the spin-sum completeness relation Σ_su_s(p)ū_s(p)=p̸+m.

- State both the position-space relation to Δ_F and the momentum-space form of S_F from memory.

# 1 Time-ordering the Dirac field

## 1.1 The fermionic minus sign

IMG: L47_electron.png | 4.2 | Figure 47.1. The electron propagator i(p̸ + m)/(p² − m² + iε): a solid line with an arrow that follows the flow of negative charge.

Lesson 37, Section 6.2, established that every reordering of fermionic operators carries a sign, and Lesson 46, Exercise 16, anticipated the consequence for time-ordering: because :ψ̄(y)ψ(x):=−:ψ(x)ψ̄(y): (Lesson 37, Section 5.2), building a time-ordered product from the two orderings of a fermion bilinear must include a relative minus sign, not the plain sum used for the bosonic field in Lesson 46. The definition is fixed accordingly:

$$ T { ψ(x) ψ̄(y) } = θ(x⁰−y⁰) ψ(x)ψ̄(y) − θ(y⁰−x⁰) ψ̄(y)ψ(x)

## 1.2 The electron propagator

$$ S_F(x−y) := ⟨0| T{ψ(x)ψ̄(y)} |0⟩ = θ(x⁰−y⁰) S(x−y) − θ(y⁰−x⁰) ⟨0|ψ̄(y)ψ(x)|0⟩

Using Lesson 37, Section 5.2, ⟨0|ψ̄(y)ψ(x)|0⟩=−(i∂̸_x+m)D(y−x), the second term becomes −θ(y⁰−x⁰)·[−(i∂̸_x+m)D(y−x)]=+θ(y⁰−x⁰)(i∂̸_x+m)D(y−x), so

$$ S_F(x−y) = θ(x⁰−y⁰) S(x−y) + θ(y⁰−x⁰) (i∂̸_x+m) D(y−x)

with a plain sum of the two theta-weighted terms, the minus sign of Section 1.1 having already been absorbed into Lesson 37's own minus sign for the reversed ordering.

### Worked example 1

Explain why Section 1.2's final expression looks like a plain sum despite Section 1.1's minus sign, without any contradiction. Two separate minus signs are involved: the one fixed in the definition of T{ψψ̄} (Section 1.1), and the one already present in Lesson 37's formula for ⟨0|ψ̄(y)ψ(x)|0⟩ itself; the two cancel against each other in this particular combination, leaving a plain sum in the final line, but both signs were used, not dropped.

# 2 S_F from Δ_F directly

## 2.1 A key cancellation

Using S(x−y)=(i∂̸_x+m)D(x−y) (Lesson 37, Section 5.1), Section 1.2's result is S_F(x−y)=θ(x⁰−y⁰)(i∂̸_x+m)D(x−y)+θ(y⁰−x⁰)(i∂̸_x+m)D(y−x). Compare this with directly applying (i∂̸_x+m) to Δ_F(x−y)=θ(x⁰−y⁰)D(x−y)+θ(y⁰−x⁰)D(y−x): since ∂̸_x=γ⁰∂_t+γ^i∂_i and only the γ⁰∂_t piece can strike the theta functions (which depend on x⁰ alone), differentiating produces

$$ ∂̸_xΔ_F = γ⁰δ(τ) [ D(x−y) − D(y−x) ] + θ(τ) ∂̸_xD(x−y) + θ(−τ) ∂̸_xD(y−x)

exactly parallel to Lesson 46, Section 3.1's calculation. The delta-function term vanishes because D(x−y)=D(y−x) at equal times (Lesson 33, Worked example 4), the same fact used throughout Lesson 46, leaving ∂̸_xΔ_F=θ(τ)∂̸_xD(x−y)+θ(−τ)∂̸_xD(y−x) with no extra term.

## 2.2 The result

Multiplying by i and adding mΔ_F,

$$ (i∂̸_x+m) Δ_F(x−y) = θ(τ) (i∂̸_x+m) D(x−y) + θ(−τ) (i∂̸_x+m) D(y−x)

exactly matching Section 2.1's expression for S_F term by term. So

$$ S_F(x−y) = ( i∂̸_x + m ) Δ_F(x−y)

the electron propagator is obtained from the scalar propagator by applying the same differential operator that Lesson 37 used to build S(x−y) from D(x−y), with no additional complication introduced by time-ordering.

### Worked example 2

State exactly why the cancellation of Section 2.1 was not automatic, and what would have happened if D(x−y)≠D(y−x) at equal times. If D did not agree with its own reverse ordering at equal times, the term γ⁰δ(τ)[D(x−y)−D(y−x)] would survive as an extra, unwanted contact term in ∂̸_xΔ_F, and the clean relation S_F=(i∂̸_x+m)Δ_F of Section 2.2 would fail, requiring an extra correction term; the elegance of this lesson's result rests on the same equal-time fact already used twice in Lesson 46.

# 3 S_F is a Green's function of the Dirac operator

## 3.1 The calculation

Using (i∂̸−m)(i∂̸+m)=−∂̸∂̸−m²=−□−m² (Lesson 37, Worked example 5, citing ∂̸∂̸=□),

$$ (i∂̸_x−m) S_F(x−y) = (i∂̸_x−m)(i∂̸_x+m) Δ_F(x−y) = − (□+m²) Δ_F(x−y)

Lesson 46, Section 3.2, established (□+m²)Δ_F(x−y)=−iδ⁴(x−y), so

$$ (i∂̸_x−m) S_F(x−y) = − (−iδ⁴(x−y)) = i δ⁴(x−y)

confirming S_F is a genuine Green's function of the free Dirac operator (i∂̸−m), with the source term on the right-hand side, the property S(x−y) alone lacks (Exercise 9).

## 3.2 Consistency

This result was obtained entirely from Section 2.2's relation and Lesson 46's already-verified scalar result; no new independent calculation of the Dirac case was needed, illustrating how directly this lesson's construction builds on Lesson 46's.

### Worked example 3

Explain in what sense Section 3.1's derivation is a genuine test of Section 2.2's claim S_F=(i∂̸_x+m)Δ_F, rather than an automatic consequence with nothing left to check. Section 2.2's relation was itself derived by direct differentiation of the time-ordered definitions; Section 3.1 uses that relation together with a purely algebraic gamma-matrix identity to predict a specific Green's function property, one that could, in principle, have failed to hold if an error had been made in Section 2's derivation. That it succeeds, reproducing exactly the expected iδ⁴(x−y) source, is a meaningful consistency check on the whole chain of reasoning.

# 4 The momentum-space propagator

## 4.1 Applying the operator to the momentum integral

Starting from Lesson 46, Section 4.1's Δ_F(x−y)=∫d⁴p/(2π)⁴ [i/(p²−m²+iε)] e^(−ip(x−y)), and using i∂_μe^(−ipx)=p_μe^(−ipx) (so that i∂̸_xe^(−ipx)=p̸e^(−ipx)),

$$ (i∂̸_x+m) e^(−ip(x−y)) = (p̸+m) e^(−ip(x−y))

## 4.2 The result

$$ S_F(x−y) = ( i∂̸_x+m ) Δ_F(x−y) = ∫ d⁴p/(2π)⁴ , i(p̸+m) / (p²−m²+iε) , e^(−ip(x−y))

so the momentum-space electron propagator is

$$ S_F(p) = i (p̸+m) / (p²−m²+iε)

exactly the formula previewed without derivation in Lesson 37, Section 7's connections table, now obtained directly from this lesson's construction.

### Worked example 4

Verify Section 4.2's momentum-space result independently, by checking that (p̸−m)S_F(p)=i (up to the iε limiting procedure), paralleling Lesson 46's own momentum-space check. Using p̸p̸=p² (the gamma-matrix identity of Lesson 21), (p̸−m)(p̸+m)=p̸p̸−m²=p²−m², so (p̸−m)·i(p̸+m)/(p²−m²+iε)=i(p²−m²)/(p²−m²+iε)→i as ε→0⁺ away from the pole, exactly the momentum-space statement of Section 3.1's Green's function property (i∂̸_x−m)S_F=iδ⁴(x−y), with i∂̸_x becoming p̸ under the Fourier transform.

# 5 The numerator p̸+m and the spin sum

## 5.1 The residue at the pole

Near the pole p⁰≈E_p, p²−m²=(p⁰−E_p)(p⁰+E_p)≈2E_p(p⁰−E_p), so S_F(p) behaves as i(p̸+m)/(2E_p(p⁰−E_p+iε′)), with p̸+m evaluated at the on-shell point p⁰=E_p.

## 5.2 Connection to completeness

At this on-shell point, p̸+m is exactly the object appearing in the spin-sum completeness relation Σ_su_s(p)ū_s(p)=p̸+m, already fixed as a convention of this course. The propagator's numerator, evaluated at its pole, is therefore literally the sum over the two physical spin states of the on-shell electron; the propagator, in this sense, carries the full spin content of the particle it describes, resolved into a specific spin state only once an external spinor is attached to one of its ends, as happened explicitly in Lesson 45's vertex calculation.

### Worked example 5

Explain briefly why this connection is physically sensible, given that an internal electron line in a Feynman diagram is not associated with any specific, measured spin. Since the internal line's spin is never measured (only the external, incoming and outgoing particles are), a calculation must implicitly account for every spin state the internal particle could have been in; the propagator's numerator, containing the full completeness sum p̸+m at its pole, is exactly what performs this implicit summation, without requiring the intermediate spin to be tracked or specified anywhere in the calculation.

# 7 Summary

1. Fermionic time-ordering carries a minus sign, T{ψ(x)ψ̄(y)}=θ(x⁰−y⁰)ψ(x)ψ̄(y)−θ(y⁰−x⁰)ψ̄(y)ψ(x), tracing to Lesson 37's general fermionic sign rule.

2. Substituting Lesson 37's formulas for S(x−y) and its reverse ordering gives S_F(x−y)=θ(x⁰−y⁰)S(x−y)+θ(y⁰−x⁰)(i∂̸_x+m)D(y−x).

3. Direct differentiation shows this equals (i∂̸_x+m)Δ_F(x−y), with no extra delta-function term, because D(x−y)=D(y−x) at equal times.

4. Combined with Lesson 46's (□+m²)Δ_F=−iδ⁴(x−y) and the identity (i∂̸−m)(i∂̸+m)=−□−m², this gives (i∂̸_x−m)S_F(x−y)=iδ⁴(x−y): S_F is a Green's function of the Dirac operator.

5. In momentum space, S_F(p)=i(p̸+m)/(p²−m²+iε), confirmed both by direct construction and by an independent check paralleling Lesson 46's.

6. The residue of S_F(p) at its pole equals the spin-sum completeness relation Σ_su_s(p)ū_s(p)=p̸+m, evaluated on shell.

7. This construction directly mirrors Lesson 46's, with the one new ingredient being the fermionic minus sign of time-ordering.

# Solutions

*Attempt each exercise before reading its solution. If you are stuck, read only the first sentence of the solution, then try again.*

**1.** T{ψ(x)ψ̄(y)}=θ(x⁰−y⁰)ψ(x)ψ̄(y)−θ(y⁰−x⁰)ψ̄(y)ψ(x). Lesson 37, Section 6.2, established that every fermionic reordering carries a factor (−1)^P, with P the number of transpositions needed; swapping the order of ψ(x) and ψ̄(y) is a single transposition, so the term with the reversed ordering must carry an extra minus sign relative to the un-reversed term, exactly as written, in contrast with Lesson 46's bosonic definition, where no such sign appears because bosonic operators commute past each other freely.

**2.** S_F(x−y)=θ(x⁰−y⁰)⟨0|ψ(x)ψ̄(y)|0⟩−θ(y⁰−x⁰)⟨0|ψ̄(y)ψ(x)|0⟩=θ(x⁰−y⁰)S(x−y)−θ(y⁰−x⁰)[−(i∂̸_x+m)D(y−x)]=θ(x⁰−y⁰)S(x−y)+θ(y⁰−x⁰)(i∂̸_x+m)D(y−x), the two minus signs (the one in the time-ordering definition and the one already in Lesson 37's formula) cancelling.

**3.** ∂̸_x[θ(τ)D(x−y)]=γ⁰∂_t[θ(τ)D(x−y)]+γ^i∂_i[θ(τ)D(x−y)]. The γ^i∂_i piece passes through θ(τ) untouched (τ depends only on x⁰), giving θ(τ)γ^i∂_iD(x−y). The γ⁰∂_t piece gives γ⁰[δ(τ)D(x−y)+θ(τ)∂_tD(x−y)] by the product rule. Combining, the delta-function term is γ⁰δ(τ)D(x−y), and the remainder is θ(τ)∂̸_xD(x−y).

**4.** By the identical method, using ∂_tθ(−τ)=−δ(τ), ∂̸_x[θ(−τ)D(y−x)]=γ⁰[−δ(τ)D(y−x)+θ(−τ)∂_tD(y−x)]+θ(−τ)γ^i∂_iD(y−x), giving a delta-function term −γ⁰δ(τ)D(y−x) and remainder θ(−τ)∂̸_xD(y−x).

**5.** Adding the two delta-function terms from Exercises 3 and 4: γ⁰δ(τ)D(x−y)−γ⁰δ(τ)D(y−x)=γ⁰δ(τ)[D(x−y)−D(y−x)]. At τ=0, D(x−y)=D(y−x) (Lesson 33, Worked example 4), so the bracket vanishes, and the combined delta-function term is zero.

**6.** Adding the two remainders from Exercises 3 and 4, with the delta-function terms cancelled per Exercise 5, ∂̸_xΔ_F(x−y)=θ(τ)∂̸_xD(x−y)+θ(−τ)∂̸_xD(y−x), exactly as claimed, with no additional term.

**7.** Multiplying Exercise 6 by i and adding mΔ_F=m[θ(τ)D(x−y)+θ(−τ)D(y−x)]: (i∂̸_x+m)Δ_F=θ(τ)[i∂̸_xD(x−y)+mD(x−y)]+θ(−τ)[i∂̸_xD(y−x)+mD(y−x)]=θ(τ)(i∂̸_x+m)D(x−y)+θ(−τ)(i∂̸_x+m)D(y−x). Using S(x−y)=(i∂̸_x+m)D(x−y) (Lesson 37, Section 5.1) for the first term, this is θ(τ)S(x−y)+θ(−τ)(i∂̸_x+m)D(y−x), exactly Exercise 2's expression for S_F(x−y), so S_F(x−y)=(i∂̸_x+m)Δ_F(x−y).

**8.** (i∂̸_x−m)S_F(x−y)=(i∂̸_x−m)(i∂̸_x+m)Δ_F(x−y)=(−∂̸_x∂̸_x−m²)Δ_F(x−y)=−(□+m²)Δ_F(x−y) (using ∂̸∂̸=□). Substituting Lesson 46's (□+m²)Δ_F(x−y)=−iδ⁴(x−y), this is −(−iδ⁴(x−y))=iδ⁴(x−y).

**9.** Lesson 37, Worked example 5, showed (i∂̸_x−m)S(x−y)=0 directly, the homogeneous Dirac equation with no source term; a Green's function must instead satisfy the inhomogeneous equation with a delta-function source on the right, the property Exercise 8 verified for S_F but not for S(x−y) alone.

**10.** ∂_μe^(−ipx)=∂_μe^(−ip_νx^ν)=−ip_μe^(−ipx), so i∂_μe^(−ipx)=i(−ip_μ)e^(−ipx)=p_μe^(−ipx). Contracting with γ^μ, i∂̸_xe^(−ip(x−y))=iγ^μ∂_(x^μ)e^(−ip(x−y))=γ^μp_μe^(−ip(x−y))=p̸e^(−ip(x−y)).

**11.** (i∂̸_x+m)e^(−ip(x−y))=(p̸+m)e^(−ip(x−y)) (Exercise 10, plus the mΔ_F term contributing m unchanged). Substituting into (i∂̸_x+m)Δ_F(x−y)=(i∂̸_x+m)∫d⁴p/(2π)⁴ [i/(p²−m²+iε)]e^(−ip(x−y)) and passing the operator through the integral (which acts only on the exponential), S_F(x−y)=∫d⁴p/(2π)⁴ [i(p̸+m)/(p²−m²+iε)]e^(−ip(x−y)), giving S_F(p)=i(p̸+m)/(p²−m²+iε).

**12.** (p̸−m)(p̸+m)=p̸p̸+p̸m−mp̸−m²=p̸p̸−m² (the middle terms cancel since p̸ and the scalar m commute) =p²−m² (using p̸p̸=p², the standard gamma-matrix identity from Lesson 21). So (p̸−m)S_F(p)=(p̸−m)·i(p̸+m)/(p²−m²+iε)=i(p²−m²)/(p²−m²+iε)→i as ε→0⁺ for p²≠m², matching (i∂̸_x−m)S_F(x−y)=iδ⁴(x−y) under the Fourier correspondence i∂̸_x↔p̸.

**13.** p²−m²=(p⁰)²−E_p²=(p⁰−E_p)(p⁰+E_p); near p⁰≈E_p, the second factor is approximately 2E_p, so p²−m²≈2E_p(p⁰−E_p). Substituting into S_F(p)=i(p̸+m)/(p²−m²+iε), and absorbing the iε into a shift of the pole as in Lesson 46, Section 5.1, S_F(p)≈i(p̸+m)/(2E_p(p⁰−E_p+iε′)), with p̸+m evaluated at p⁰=E_p (on shell) as the coefficient of the simple pole in p⁰.

**14.** At p⁰=E_p, the electron four-momentum p is on its mass shell, and the completeness relation Σ_su_s(p)ū_s(p)=p̸+m applies directly, so Exercise 13's residue coefficient, p̸+m evaluated on shell, equals exactly Σ_su_s(p)ū_s(p), the sum over both physical spin states of the on-shell electron.

**15.** An external line in a diagram is attached to a specific, measured (or prepared) particle with a definite spin, contributing its own specific u_s(p) or ū_s(p′) to the amplitude (Lesson 45, Section 2); an internal line, by contrast, connects two vertices with no measurement in between, so any spin state consistent with the particle's momentum must be included, and the propagator's pole residue, containing the full completeness sum, is exactly what accomplishes this without requiring the calculation to track or choose a specific internal spin.

**16.** By analogy, Lesson 48 will likely define a time-ordered photon two-point function ⟨0|T{A^i(x)A^j(y)}|0⟩ built from the mode expansion of Lesson 38 and derive a Green's function equation for it, paralleling Sections 2–4 of this lesson. One expected complication is that Lesson 38's Coulomb-gauge conditions A⁰=0 and ∇·A=0 make the photon field manifestly non-covariant (only two of its four would-be components are independent, and the gauge choice itself is not preserved under a general Lorentz boost), so building a propagator that both matches this specific gauge's mode expansion and can be used consistently in covariant, Lorentz-invariant diagram calculations is expected to require additional care beyond the direct differential-operator method that sufficed for the scalar and Dirac cases, which had no analogous gauge redundancy to contend with.
