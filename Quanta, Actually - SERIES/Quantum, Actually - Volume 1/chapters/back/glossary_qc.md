## Glossary entries from Part Two

Short meanings, in the sense this book uses them, in one alphabet. The chapter is where the term is first put to work. Letters, recurring numbers, and the distinctions that are easy to fuse live in “Symbols, Numbers, and Distinctions”.

**Absorber theory.** Wheeler and Feynman's mid-century account of classical radiation reaction as a direct interaction among charges, built from advanced and retarded solutions together. It does not replace QED. Chapter 18.

**Action.** A single number built from an entire history. Each history contributes a phase factor *exp(iS/ħ)*. Chapters 15 and 21.

**Advanced and retarded.** Two pieces of one radiation solution. Retarded propagates forward in time, the ordinary way. Advanced propagates backward in time as mathematics, not as a wave a charge sends into the past. In absorber theory the backward pieces cancel, and what remains is the forward radiation and the recoil. Chapter 18.

**Aharonov–Bohm effect.** An interference shift set by magnetic flux in a region the electron never enters. Chapters 11, 12, and 23.

**Amplitude.** The complex number quantum mechanics assigns to one way a story might unfold. Its length, squared, is the probability. Its angle is the phase. Chapter 11.

**Anomalous magnetic moment.** *a_e = (g − 2)/2*. Dirac's theory gives *g = 2*. QED predicts *a_e* as a series in *α*, beginning with *α/(2π)*. Chapter 26.

**Bandgap.** The energy a photon must clear to free an electron inside a semiconductor. A metal's threshold is a work function instead. Chapter 15.

**Canonical momentum.** The momentum tied to the phase gradient. With a vector potential present it splits from the mechanical momentum, *p − qA*. Chapters 14 and 23.

**Coherence.** A shared phase, stable enough that contributions add instead of canceling at random. Chapters 13 and 24.

**Compton scattering.** An X-ray photon bouncing off an electron and leaving with a longer wavelength. A straight-back bounce lengthens it by about five picometers. Chapter 19.

**Connection.** The rule, supplied by the potential, for comparing phase at neighboring points. Chapter 20.

**Cooper pair.** Two electrons bound into one superconducting state, opposite spin and opposite momentum, charge *q* = 2*e*. In ordinary low-temperature superconductors the attraction is carried by lattice vibrations. The pair is why the flux step is *h*/2*e*. Chapters 12 and 28.

**Covariant derivative.** An ordinary derivative with the potential built into it. Equation (6). Set *c = 1* and it is the same coupling as Equation (8). Chapter 22.

**Crossing.** Reading an outgoing positron as an incoming electron with time reversed on the diagram, so one calculation covers several related processes. Chapter 19.

**Curvature.** The failure of a phase comparison to come back independent of path. In this book, that failure is the electromagnetic field. Chapters 20 and 31.

**Decoherence.** Environmental entanglement that scrambles the phase relationships large-scale interference needs. Chapter 24.

**Effective theory.** A description that keeps the variables relevant at one scale and absorbs the rest into its parameters. Chapters 20 and 27.

**Entanglement.** One joint state of two systems that cannot be pulled apart into two independent states. Chapter 25.

**Feynman diagram.** A picture of one term in a perturbative amplitude. Not a photograph of a process. Chapter 23.

**Fine-structure constant.** *α = e²/(4πε₀ħc) ≈ 1/137.036* at everyday energies, and slightly different at others. Chapter 27.

**Flux quantum.** *Φ₀ = h/2e*, the flux step for a coherent pair of electrons in the thick-loop picture. Chapter 12.

**Fluxoid.** The closed-loop combination of enclosed flux and the loop's own current. It reduces to plain flux in the thick, well-screened loops this book uses. Chapter 12.

**Four-potential.** The scalar potential and the vector potential packaged as one relativistic object. Chapters 12 and 16.

**Gauge transformation.** A rewrite of the potentials that leaves the fields, and every measurement, unchanged. Chapter 12.

**Green's function.** The response at one event to a pointlike source at another. It carries the theory's causal structure. Chapter 14.

**Interference.** Addition of amplitudes. Routes whose phases agree reinforce; routes whose phases disagree cancel. Chapter 11.

**Josephson junction.** A thin barrier between two superconductors. The supercurrent depends on the phase difference across it. Chapter 28.

**Lagrangian.** The density integrated over spacetime to make the action. In Chapter 22 the interaction term is the one the substitution checks. The field term beside it, −(1/4μ₀)*F_uvF^uv*, is the standard SI density for the free electromagnetic field, left unexpanded into **E** and **B**. Chapter 22.

**Lamb shift.** The splitting, about 1058 megahertz, between the hydrogen 2S and 2P levels. Chapter 19.

**Magnetic monopole.** An isolated magnetic charge. None has been detected. Dirac showed that one, anywhere, would force electric charge to come in steps. Chapter 22.

**Order parameter.** The collective variable that describes an organized many-body state. Here, the superconducting phase and its amplitude. Chapters 24 and 28.

**Path integral.** The sum over histories, each weighted by a phase built from the action. The classical path is where neighboring phases agree. Chapter 15.

**Phase.** The angle of an amplitude. Unobservable alone. Decisive when two routes to the same outcome are added. Chapters 11–12.

**Photon.** A quantum excitation of the electromagnetic field, carrying energy *ħω* and momentum *ħω/c*. Chapters 15 and 19.

**Potential.** The scalar potential *φ* and the vector potential **A**. They enter a charged particle's phase directly. The fields are built from them. Chapters 11–12 and 16.

**Poynting vector.** *S* = (1/μ₀)*E*×*B*, the classical flow of electromagnetic energy. Equation (3). Chapters 18 and 19.

**Propagator.** The contribution, inside an amplitude, of a disturbance at one event to another event. Chapters 14 and 23.

**Qubit.** Two unevenly spaced energy levels of a Josephson circuit, addressed like an artificial atom. Chapter 28.

**Renormalization.** The bookkeeping that keeps measurable predictions unchanged when the resolution scale changes. Chapters 26–27.

**Running coupling.** The effective strength of the electromagnetic interaction as the energy of the probe changes. Chapters 26 and 27.

**SQUID.** A superconducting quantum interference device: a superconducting loop interrupted by one or two thin barriers. Its current swings as the enclosed flux changes by single flux quanta, which is sensitive enough to map the magnetic fields of the brain. Chapter 13.

**Stokes' theorem.** The step that turns a loop integral of **A** into the magnetic flux through the surface the loop encloses. Chapters 12, 20, and 24.

**Supercurrent.** The current of a coherent condensate, tied to *ħ∇θ − qA*. Chapter 24.

**Vacuum polarization.** The correction to one photon's propagation from a charged-particle loop. A different diagram from light scattering off light. Chapter 26.

**Virtual photon.** An internal line in a Feynman diagram: a term in a calculation, not a particle caught in a detector. Chapters 15 and 23.

**Wilson loop.** The phase factor from the potential integrated around a closed curve. Equation (9). Chapter 25.
