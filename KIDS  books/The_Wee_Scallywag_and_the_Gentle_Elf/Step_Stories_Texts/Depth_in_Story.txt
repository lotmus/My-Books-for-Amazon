Wilf cleared the stone because he wanted to see the water run true. He set the slate down afterward, already knowing it would be marked later and that the marking would make the next path clearer. Boot shifted on his foot; he waited until the baby settled again before he moved.

Titch broke the crust into even pieces and set them out. On sharper mornings she still did this. She kept the cloth folded and ready. When a soft voice had come before, she had gone still so the others could see the stillness; she did the same now with the quiet, and it was enough.

Sparefunk put on the walking boots and left the spare pair standing where a hurry could find them. He stacked the newest tyre so it would not roll. He did not ask where it had come from. When the boots felt like a short run he took it, only as far as the bend, then stopped. He shut his mouth without being told.

Hopsi ate two hops, stitched the small tear in the crust cloth, and put the needle through her sleeve where morning would find it. She offered the bowl. Boot declined. She accepted the refusal and did not speak. The day did not grow longer.

Boot, once thin enough to sleep inside the spare boot, lay across Wilf’s foot and took its piece of crust. It ran when they ran and returned when they sat. It did not choose the path. It stayed where the warmth and the crust remained reliable.

They sat with these things done. The light that had left the sun eight minutes earlier lay across the flat place. The way back was open. The way ahead was open. No speech was added.

The stream continued.
