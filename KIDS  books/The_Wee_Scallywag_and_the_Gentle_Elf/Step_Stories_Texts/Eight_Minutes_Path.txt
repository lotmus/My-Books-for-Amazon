The light that reached them had left the sun while they were still deciding nothing. Wilf set the slate where it would stay dry. Titch shook the crust cloth and folded it. Sparefunk tried the spare boots on for a moment — still too big for Boot, still right for him — and took them off again. Hopsi finished the hops. Boot drank from the stream and returned to the foot.

They walked until the mill was a smaller shape. The path stayed kind. No soft voice came from the road. The rectangular eggs were long behind. At the next flat place they sat. The way back had not closed. The wetter coast had not arrived. The eight-minute light lay across both.

No speech was needed. Tomorrow the same light would leave the sun again, and they would still be able to choose.

The stream continued under it.
