The Wee Scallywag and the Gentle Elf
Book 1 of The Step Stories
by Otis Umberlight

Wilf came up the path with the slate and the wolf baby. The baby trotted as if the step had been its idea all along. Titch was already turning the hem that had started the day in a bad mood and was now merely determined. Sparefunk arrived with one pair of boots on, the spare pair in hand, and three tyres he had collected on the way; he stacked them with the satisfaction of a person whose universe was behaving politely. Hopsi set down her bowl of hops and began on a seam.

“Finished?” said Sparefunk.
“Yes.”
“Still annoying?”
“Yes. But done. And the baby likes the walk.”

The wolf baby accepted the crust, refused a hop with dignity, and sat on Wilf’s foot. A moonbeam cancelled its wrinkles in plain sight and arrived straight. The rectangular eggs kept their corners. No one reached.

They settled to the small work. Wilf moved a stone the water had nudged back. Titch made the hem lie flat. Sparefunk arranged the tyres so they would not roll and left the spare boots where they could be found in a hurry. Hopsi stitched and ate hops in turns. The axle bird sang four notes, mentioned worms, and forgot.

Then the soft voice came from the road. Sweets. A quicker way. Hopsi looked once at the face and shut her mouth. Wilf said, “Run.” They ran — slate, hops, spare boots, wolf baby and all — only to the bend where the mill step could still be seen. The stranger stayed on the road. They sat in the grass, shared the rest of the crust, and watched the light stay ordinary. Behind them the channel ran clear.

Later they walked on. The path curved with the stream. The mill grew smaller. Sparefunk carried the spare boots without complaint. Hopsi offered the last hops; the wolf baby declined again. Titch kept the empty crust cloth. At the next rise they stopped. The way back was still open. The way forward was simply there.

They sat until the light thinned. The wolf baby claimed the boot. No speech was required. Tomorrow the slate would be marked again, the hops would be found, and the step — or the path — would still be ready.

The stream continued.

The End
