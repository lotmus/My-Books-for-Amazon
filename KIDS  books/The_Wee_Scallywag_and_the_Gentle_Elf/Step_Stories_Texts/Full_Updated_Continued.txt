The next morning the light arrived eight minutes after leaving the sun. Boot was already waiting on the foot. Titch set out the crust in two even pieces. Wilf opened the slate; school would want it marked. Sparefunk put on the walking boots and left the spare pair standing — still too large for Boot to sleep in, still right for a hurry. Hopsi ate three hops and began the small tear she had noticed in the cloth.

They walked to the bend and sat. The mill was visible when they turned. The path ahead was damper. Boot drank from the stream and came back. No soft voice appeared. The rectangular eggs were far behind and still cornered, as far as anyone knew.

Wilf cleared a stone the night water had shifted. Titch kept the cloth ready. Sparefunk ran only as far as the next rise because the boots felt like it, then stopped. Hopsi offered the bowl; only she took from it. They did not speak of the coast.

By afternoon the choice was still open. They shared the last of the crust. The light lay across both ways. Boot settled. No one lengthened the day with talk.

Tomorrow the same light would leave the sun again. The slate, the hops, the boots, and the stream would still be there.

The stream continued.
