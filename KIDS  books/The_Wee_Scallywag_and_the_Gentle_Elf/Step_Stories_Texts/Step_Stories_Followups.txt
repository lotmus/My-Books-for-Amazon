The Step Stories
Content drafts of follow-up books
by Otis Umberlight

Book 2: A New Path

They leave the step at dawn. Wilf carries the slate. Titch carries the folded crust cloth. Sparefunk wears one pair and carries the spare. Hopsi carries the needle and a small bowl of hops. Boot trots between them.

The path follows the stream. The mill grows smaller. At the first bend they can still see it. At the second they cannot, unless they turn and wait. The light that reaches them left the sun eight minutes earlier. No soft voice comes. They share the crust. Boot declines hops.

They reach a flatter place where the ground is damper. The coast is still further. They sit. The way back has not closed. No one chooses before the light thins. In the morning the same choice waits.

Possible quiet incidents: a tyre found on the path, a hem that needs turning far from home, Boot drinking and returning, the latch of the mill remembered but not needed.

Book 3: The Latch

They return. The mill door is checked first. It holds. Boot sniffs the threshold and stays out. Days of ordinary work: channel cleared, crusts even, tyres stacked, seams closed. One dusk the door opens a hand’s width by itself. They wait. Wilf shuts it. Next morning the latch is checked again. Nothing further happens. The rectangular eggs keep their corners. The path remains open if wanted.

Book 4: Corners

One rectangular egg cracks. What comes out is wet, ordinary in shape, and not interested in rolling. It dries into a bird that sings four notes and forgets to advertise. The other eggs stay whole. No one turns them. Boot watches and does not chase. The children leave the new bird to the axle and the reeds. The stream continues.

All books keep the same voice: deadpan, kind, small true things, way back open, ages 6–9.
