The light arrived gently, having cancelled its wrinkles, and lay across the step like a blanket someone had remembered to leave.

Wilf set the slate down more carefully than usual. Titch put out the crust and, after a moment, broke a second small piece for the wolf baby without being asked. Sparefunk moved the spare boots a little so they would not be in anyone’s way, then sat in the pair he was wearing as if the step had room for all of them. Hopsi ate her hops slowly, finished the seam, and left the needle where Wilf’s hand would find it if a button came loose.

The wolf baby took the extra crust, looked at each of them in turn, and settled with its nose on Wilf’s boot, closer than yesterday. No one reached for the moonbeam. The rectangular eggs kept their corners. The stream continued, softer in the evening light.

They stayed until the brightness thinned. Sparefunk’s spare pair stood dry and ready. Hopsi’s bowl was empty but not lonely. Titch’s hem was done. Wilf’s slate was finished. The wolf baby slept. It was enough, and it was quiet, and it was theirs for as long as the step held them.

Tomorrow the same small kindnesses would still be possible.
