The light arrived as it always did. It had tried the wrinkled routes, cancelled them, and left the least-time path on the step. No dice had been required. QED had kept the account open until the account closed; what remained was ordinary brightness. The rectangular eggs kept their corners. The grass stood taller where the sun had actually reached. The blind sorting had already happened; Wilf’s cleared channel only made the next water less selective.

Titch set out the crust. The wolf baby took it and arranged itself on Wilf’s boot, which had proved warm enough to keep. Sparefunk counted the tyres — still one more than explanation covered — and left the spare boots empty. Hopsi ate hops, finished the seam, and did not reach for the beam. The axle bird sang, mentioned nothing, and left.

No one spoke of watchmakers or dice. The stream continued. People talked too much and did too little elsewhere; on the step the small true things were simply done. By evening the slate was set aside, the hops were fewer, the spare pair stood dry, and the wolf baby slept. What had not cancelled remained.

Tomorrow would bring whatever fitted. The rest would thin out, as it always had.
