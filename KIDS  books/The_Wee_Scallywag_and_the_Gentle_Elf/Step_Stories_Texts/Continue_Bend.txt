Morning found them still at the bend. Boot was already awake, nose on Wilf’s boot, waiting for the crust that Titch would set out. Sparefunk checked the spare pair — still empty, still too small for sleeping in — and put on the ones he would walk in. Hopsi ate the first hops of the day. Wilf looked at the slate; it would need marking later.

They walked a little further. The stream grew wider by a hand’s breadth. The mill slipped behind a rise and then reappeared when they turned. No stranger came. The rectangular eggs were far behind. Boot trotted, declined a hop, and returned to the foot whenever they sat.

By afternoon they had chosen nothing final. The way back was open. The wetter coast was still ahead. They shared what crust remained and let the light arrive straight. Sparefunk left the spare boots within reach. Hopsi shut her mouth because nothing needed saying.

The stream continued. Tomorrow the same choice would wait, and Boot would still be larger than the boot it once used.
