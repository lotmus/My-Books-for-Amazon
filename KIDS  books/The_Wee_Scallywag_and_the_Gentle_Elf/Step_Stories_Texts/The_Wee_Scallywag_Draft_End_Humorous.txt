The Wee Scallywag and the Gentle Elf
(with Wilf, Titch, Sparefunk, Hopsi, and the wolf baby)

The light thinned for the last time that evening, having first tried several ridiculous wrinkles and cancelled them all with the air of someone tidying a drawer.

Wilf set the slate aside. It was finished, which was more than could be said for most things. Titch left the hem turned and the crust place empty; the wolf baby looked at the empty place, sighed in a small wolf way, and decided not to make a speech. Sparefunk counted the tyres, discovered he had one more than yesterday, and left the spare boots standing dry like a pair of guests who had already said goodbye. Hopsi ate the final hops, folded the cloth, and put the needle where morning would find it — assuming morning was better organised than the rest of the universe.

The rectangular eggs kept their corners in the dark, which was more than the moonbeam had managed. The stream continued, unimpressed. No stranger came, which everyone quietly considered a win. No one ran. No one spoke more than the situation strictly required, and the situation required nothing.

People talked too much and did too little. They knew love and forgot its aim. On the step the four of them — Wilf, Titch, Sparefunk, Hopsi — and the wolf baby simply stayed until sleep took what was left, the wolf baby claiming Wilf’s boot as usual and the spare boots claiming nothing at all.

Tomorrow would bring whatever it brought, probably including another tyre and at least one hop. The boots, the slate, the needle, and the habit of meeting the day without unnecessary drama would still be waiting.

And the stream continued, as streams do, whether anyone wrote it down or not.

The End

Cast
- Wilf Millersen: miller’s son, human boy, slate, wolf baby
- Titch Mitchel: neighbour’s daughter, crusts, occasional sharpness
- Sparefunk: pointed-ear boy, boots (two pairs), spare tyres
- Hopsi (Elfie Didlhop): pointed-ear girl, hop salad, needle
- Wolf baby: domesticated, opinionated about boots

Ages roughly 6–9. Deadpan. Quietly ridiculous.
