The Wee Scallywag and the Gentle Elf
Book 1 of The Step Stories
by Otis Umberlight

The mill stood by the stream. Wilf sat on the step with his slate. Boot the wolf baby lay across his foot; once small enough to sleep inside a spare boot, now larger and choosing the foot. Titch turned a hem. Sparefunk wore one pair of boots and left the spare pair standing ready. Hopsi ate hops and stitched. Tabitha Smoke sat with them, curly hair in a bun, having just met the step properly.

“Finished?” said Sparefunk.
“Yes,” said Wilf.
“Still annoying?”
“Yes. But done.”

Boot took the crust Titch set out evenly and declined a hop. A moonbeam cancelled its wrinkles and arrived straight. No one reached. The rectangular eggs kept their corners in the grass.

They did the small work. Wilf moved the stone so the water ran true. Titch finished the hem. Sparefunk stacked a tyre and left the spare boots where a hurry could find them. Hopsi closed the seam. Tabitha Smoke watched and stayed.

A soft voice came from the road. Sweets. A quicker way. Hopsi shut her mouth. Wilf said, “Run.” They ran — all five and Boot — only as far as the bend where the step could still be seen. The stranger did not follow. They sat, shared what remained, and let the light stay ordinary.

Later they walked the path. The mill grew smaller. The eight-minute light lay across the flat place. The way back stayed open.

They sat until the brightness thinned. Tomorrow the slate, the hops, the boots, and both directions would still be possible.

The stream continued.

The End
