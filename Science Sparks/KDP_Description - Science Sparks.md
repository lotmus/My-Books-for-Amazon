# KDP listing — *Science Sparks*

**Title:** Science Sparks
**Subtitle:** Physics, Life, and Mathematics — The Same Few Rules, Told in Highlights
**Author:** Lothar J. Musiol

## Product description

Energy is never created and never lost. It only changes form, like money moving between accounts — except that no central bank can print more of it and no decree can change the rule. The universe keeps the books. This volume is about what those books record.

Science Sparks is a science atlas for curious general readers. No specialist background is needed: each idea is introduced in plain language, with technical details kept optional. Its 389 short, numbered chapters span eight sections. It is not a textbook or an encyclopedia; it is built to be explored. Mathematics comes first, because it is the language everything else is written in: counting, algebra, trigonometry, logarithms, complex numbers, calculus, Fourier series and matrices, in plain sentences. Physics and the Cosmos uses that language on motion, energy, time and its arrow, relativity, the expanding universe, black holes and the semiconductors that taught matter to think, then holds claims about living longer and settling the Moon and Mars to the same standard of evidence. Quantum Physics brings the foundational lessons, quantum theory, phase and potentials, and quantum electrodynamics together in one section. Biology brings life, evolution and genetics together, including the fact that biological copies are never exact. Science in the Novels turns relativity into a comic murder mystery. Information, Computation and Intelligence follows bits and statistics into AI. Earth, Climate and the Living Planet explains the evidence for human-caused warming and the choices available. When Science Can Harm examines weapons, medical research, pollution, surveillance and the safeguards that keep powerful knowledge answerable.

Every section also stands on its own, and eight reader routes at the front lead through the big picture, quantum physics, science for science fiction, mathematics and physics, life and evolution, artificial intelligence, climate, and the risks of science. Every chapter carries two labels: a status line that tells you how sure science is (settled, strange but solid, serious but unconfirmed, or speculative) and a level that tells you what to read first, from "start here" to "optional deep dive". Every chapter ends with a one-sentence takeaway, every section ends with a short check on where the same few rules turned up again, and a glossary at the back sends you to the chapter that teaches each term.

Updated through October 2026, including the Artemis II flight around the Moon, the latest on the Hubble tension and dark energy, and the first CRISPR medicines.

Written by a semiconductor engineer who studied science deeply enough to know exactly where he simplifies, and where he does not.
