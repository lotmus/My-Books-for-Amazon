*A working guide for creators who want a channel, not a hobby*

# Six Weeks to a Working Channel

*A calendar for getting the eight videos in front of strangers and reading the first counts. Move the dates. Do not skip the path.*

*Layer: Start now. Workbook: the video-job sheet for each video, the week sheet from week five, and the hours on the money sheet.*

This is a frame (author recommendation), not a platform requirement and not a forecast. If a step is not true yet — the description does not say who the channel is for, the example in the video is fake, the license on the footage is unread — move the date. A channel that publishes eight unrelated videos in six weeks has followed a schedule and missed the point.

The pace below suits one person using the free tools in chapter 3. With less time, stretch a week into two. Do not cut the deep dive to save an evening. Write the hours each video took on the money sheet; week six needs them.

## I. Weeks one and two: Start this week

The one-page plan after Start here is weeks one and two: the viewer and twenty ideas, the switches, video one (“who it is for”), then the deep dive and three honest viewers. Two more steps belong in week one. Pick the other six of the eight from your twenty ideas, one for each job in chapter 5. And in Studio, open Earn and choose to be notified when the channel is eligible; YouTube’s eligibility page says it will email you when you meet a threshold.

## II. Week three: their words, then the method

Film the problem video in the viewer’s own phrase, then the method video. Show the steps you would be embarrassed to hide. End each one by naming the next video, and point the deep dive’s end screen at the method video.

The problem video is the one most people replace with a clever title. The method video is the one most people water down, because giving the method away feels like giving the channel away. It is the opposite. The viewer who can finish a small version alone comes back for the next one.

## III. Week four: proof and one objection

Film proof you actually have. If the proof is small, say it is small. Film one objection video: the reason a sensible viewer hesitates, answered without insulting them.

Make a playlist of the five videos that exist, in watch order. If the account is verified, mark it as a series playlist (chapter 2, §VI).

## IV. Week five: comparison, the path, then the count

Film the comparison: your method versus the usual alternative, including doing nothing. Then film the video whose only job is to name which of the other seven to watch next, in order, by title. Publish both. Then stop making new videos for several days and fill your first week sheet (chapter 7, §VI): the Earn tab’s counts, the deep dive’s watch time and view duration, the views your own videos sent each other, and the question people actually asked in the comments.

Do not change the title, the thumbnail, and the opening of the same video in the same week. You would not know which change mattered.

## V. Week six: one decision

Read the money sheet first, including the hours of every video. Then choose one of these, and write it in the decision log with a reason:

- **Keep the subject and fill any gap in the eight,** because strangers watched more than one video.
- **Rewrite the openings and the thumbnails,** because people clicked and left in the first thirty seconds.
- **Rewrite the titles,** because the few who find the videos stay, and almost nobody finds them.
- **Narrow the viewer,** because the comments ask for three different things. Pick one of those people.

Then schedule the next four weeks: any of the eight still unpublished, then answers to the questions in the comments. Not a new niche. A second niche in week six is how a channel that might have reached the gate becomes two hobbies, each with half the hours.

*In short:* Six weeks to eight videos on a path. Move a date if a step is not true. Read the first counts in week five. Change one thing in week six.

## VI. Claims that do not survive week six

- That six weeks is a guarantee of the gate. Six weeks is a deadline for learning whether a stranger stays.
- That you should wait until all eight are perfect. Two honest videos in public teach more than eight private drafts.
- That ads can replace weeks three to five. Official rule: promoted views do not count toward the Partner Program (chapter 8, §VI).
- That changing everything in week six is decisive. One change, then another quiet measurement, is decisive.
