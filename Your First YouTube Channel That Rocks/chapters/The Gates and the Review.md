*A working guide for creators who want a channel, not a hobby*

# The Gates and the Review

*YouTube pays through two gates with published numbers. Meeting a number starts a review. It does not start the payment.*

Every chapter before this one moves three counts. This chapter is what YouTube does with them. The rules are public, they are specific, and in 2026 they changed: YouTube announced in August that the ad gate doubles for new applicants from 1 February 2027. A channel planning a year ahead has to plan against the new number.

Every figure below was checked on YouTube’s own pages in October 2026. These terms move faster than tool prices and faster than a content-safety policy. Confirm each one in Studio’s Earn tab before you plan around it.

## I. Before either gate

YouTube’s own ads on your videos, before you are accepted, are not your revenue. [YouTube’s eligibility page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851) says the platform may serve ads on any content, and that a channel which has not met the higher criteria is not yet eligible to receive a share. You will see ads on your videos. You are not paid for them.

That does not make the early views worthless. Every public long-form hour and every qualified Shorts view in the window counts toward the gate. The early months are the deposit.

## II. The first gate: fan funding and Shopping

In countries where YouTube has opened the expanded program, the first gate opens fan funding and Shopping: channel memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks, and the Shopping affiliate program. [YouTube’s monetization overview](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/94522) sets that gate at:

- 500 subscribers,
- three public uploads in the last 90 days, and
- either 3,000 qualified watch hours on long-form videos in the last 365 days, or 3 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days.

It does not include a share of watch-page ads. The August 2026 announcement says the entry thresholds for fan funding and shopping remain unchanged in 2027. In countries without the expanded program, those features open at the second gate. The overview page lists the countries. Chapter 12 covers what each feature pays.

## III. The second gate: ads, Shorts, and Premium

The second gate adds a share of watch-page ads, Shorts Feed ads, and YouTube Premium revenue, on top of the fan-funding features.

| Applying | Subscribers | Long-form route | Shorts route |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Through 31 January 2027 | 1,000 | 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months | 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days |
| On or after 1 February 2027 | 1,000 | 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days | 20 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days |

YouTube announced that change on [its blog on 10 August 2026](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/). It applies to new applicants. It does not move channels already in the program, and it does not move the first gate.

What qualifies is narrow. Watch hours from Shorts in the Shorts feed do not count toward the hour bar. Hours from private, unlisted, and deleted videos do not count. Views from ad campaigns do not count. Live streams that are unlisted, deleted, or not converted to video on demand do not count. Image posts that appear in the Shorts feed do not count as Shorts views.

For a channel well short of 4,000 hours in October 2026, the practical reading is plain: plan for 8,000. A channel that reaches 4,000 hours and 1,000 subscribers before 1 February 2027 can apply under the old numbers. A channel that does not will meet the new ones.

## IV. What else you need

Both gates sit on the same checklist. The eligibility page lists:

- following the channel monetization policies, which chapter 10 covers;
- living in a country or region where the program is available;
- no active Community Guidelines strikes;
- 2-Step Verification on the Google Account;
- advanced features access on YouTube; and
- one active AdSense for YouTube account linked to the channel, or being ready to set one up in Studio.

The overview page adds an age rule for each feature. Fan-funding features need you to be at least 18. The ad gate accepts a younger creator only with a legal guardian over 18 who handles payments through AdSense for YouTube.

Chapter 1 told you to switch on 2-Step Verification and phone verification in the first week. This is why.

## V. Apply, then wait for the review

Meeting the number does not accept you. In Studio, open Earn and choose Apply Now. Accept the base terms. Set up an AdSense for YouTube account, or link an active one. Studio then shows the review step as in progress.

YouTube’s automated systems and human reviewers look at the channel as a whole. The eligibility page says a decision typically takes about a month, longer when volumes are high or reviewers disagree. If the counts drop while you wait, the review still proceeds. Making videos private or deleting them can affect the application.

If the review says no, you can appeal within 21 days. You can apply again after 30 days, or after 90 days if this was not the first rejection. A rejection usually means reviewers found a significant part of the channel outside the policies. Read chapter 10 against your own uploads before you reapply. More uploads of the same kind will not change the answer.

## VI. Inside: modules, payments, and staying active

Acceptance opens a menu, not a payment. In Earn, each way to earn is a module with its own terms:

- **Watch Page Monetization Module** for ads and Premium revenue on long-form and live videos;
- **Shorts Monetization Module** for Shorts Feed ads and Premium revenue on Shorts, accruing from the date you accept it; and
- **Commerce Product Module** for fan funding: memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, and Super Thanks.

Accept the modules you will use, then switch on monetization per video. Chapter 11 covers the ad settings.

Payment comes through AdSense for YouTube. Earnings accrue there, and AdSense pays out once the balance passes the payment threshold for your currency. Check the threshold and the tax information AdSense asks for in your own account. Tax forms are the step most new partners leave until a payment is held.

Starting 1 February 2027, creators who want to keep full monetization must accept updated terms in Studio by 31 January 2027. That date is on the eligibility page. Do it when the notice appears.

Staying in also takes activity. Today the eligibility page says YouTube may turn off monetization on a channel that has not uploaded a video or posted to the Posts tab for six months or more. Search Engine Journal’s report of the 2027 help pages describes a new definition of “active”: 1,000 qualified watch hours in the past year, or 1 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days, or two long videos or five Shorts uploaded every 90 days. That definition comes from trade reporting, not from a page this book could open. Check the live help page before you rely on it.

## VII. A second channel starts at zero

Running a second channel is allowed: to protect a name, to redirect traffic, or to branch later into Shorts or another language. A second channel does not inherit the first one’s monetized status. It clears the thresholds on its own: its own subscribers, its own watch hours, and its own review. A placeholder with one video and a pinned link is not a monetized channel by association. If it later becomes a real show, it starts the clock at zero, under whatever thresholds apply on the day it applies. Chapter 14 covers when a second channel is worth the split.

> **Key takeaway:** Before the gate, YouTube keeps the ad money. The first gate opens fan funding at 500 subscribers. The second gate opens ads at 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 hours, or 8,000 hours for channels applying from 1 February 2027. Meeting the number starts a review.

## VIII. Claims that do not survive the eligibility page

- That hitting the number is the same as being approved. On YouTube the number only starts the review. It does not start the payment.
- That the 2027 change affects every channel. It applies to new applicants for the ad gate. Channels already in the program, and the fan-funding gate, do not move.
- That ads on your videos before the gate are paying you. YouTube says a channel below the higher criteria does not receive a share.
- That a rejection is final. You can appeal within 21 days, and reapply after 30 or 90 days. The fix is the policy, not the volume.
- That a second channel inherits the first one’s status. Every channel clears its own thresholds from zero.
- That acceptance turns the money on. You accept modules, set up AdSense, and switch on monetization per video.

A last limit. Every number in this chapter is a snapshot, and platform monetization terms are the fastest-moving fact category in this book. Confirm each threshold in Studio’s Earn tab and on YouTube’s eligibility page before planning a channel around it.
