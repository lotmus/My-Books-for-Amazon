*A working guide for creators who want a channel, not a hobby*

# Name the Viewer

*A channel that rocks is one a stranger chooses, finishes, and comes back to. The money follows that, through gates YouTube publishes.*

The title of this book is a job description. A channel that rocks is not a channel with one lucky video, a round subscriber number, or a monetization badge you hope will arrive by itself. It is a channel a specific person recognizes as theirs, watches to the end, and returns to next week. YouTube pays for that behavior, not for effort. Its programs count subscribers, public watch hours, and qualified Shorts views, and they open in a published order. Everything in this book — the click, the cheap production, the recording, the counts — exists to move those numbers honestly and then to turn them into income.

If you cannot name the viewer in one sentence, you do not have a channel yet. You have a place to post. Posting is allowed. It is not the goal.

## I. Name the viewer before the topic

A topic is not a viewer. “Fitness,” “personal finance,” and “home repair” describe shelves. A viewer is a person with a situation, a question they would type into search, and a reason to come back when the next video arrives.

Finish this sentence before you design a banner: “This channel helps [specific person] who wants [specific result], one video at a time.” “People who like cooking” is not a person. “Adults who cook for one on a weeknight and are tired of throwing food away” is.

Write a second sentence: “This channel is a poor fit for people who want [different result].” A retirement-planning channel that also tries to serve day traders will teach neither well enough to be watched to the end. Narrowing is not a branding exercise. A viewer who leaves at second ten adds almost nothing to the watch-hour bar, and YouTube’s recommendation system learns from that exit.

## II. A subject you can make fifty videos about

The gates in chapter 9 count a year of long-form watch hours, or ninety days of Shorts views. Neither arrives from three videos. Choose a subject with enough questions in it that you can still name the next video after the fortieth.

Test it on paper. Write twenty video titles for your viewer in one sitting, each one a question they would type. If you stall at eight, the subject is too narrow or you do not know it well enough yet. If the twenty titles serve five different people, it is too wide.

Two more checks belong here, because they decide what the channel can earn later.

- **Advertiser-friendly by default.** Ads are the first stream most channels qualify for. YouTube’s advertiser-friendly guidelines treat some subjects with caution, and a video in a sensitive category can run with limited ads or none. Chapter 10 has the rules. A channel can cover a hard subject. It should know, before the first upload, that the subject has a cost.
- **What the audience is worth to an advertiser.** YouTube’s ad revenue page says advertisers pay different amounts depending on the time of year, where the viewers are, and the ad formats available. That is why two channels with the same views can earn different amounts. Chapter 11 explains the numbers. Do not pick a subject only because someone online said it pays well. A well-paid subject you cannot make fifty videos about pays nothing.

## III. The money map

YouTube pays in a fixed order. Learn the order now, so you do not plan for a stream the channel cannot reach yet.

| Stage | What opens | Where in this book |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before any gate | Nothing from YouTube. It may run ads on your videos and keep the revenue. | Chapter 9 |
| Expanded program, where offered | Channel memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks, and the Shopping affiliate program | Chapters 9, 12, and 13 |
| Full Partner Program | A share of watch-page ads, Shorts Feed ads, and YouTube Premium revenue | Chapters 9 and 11 |
| Outside the gates | Sponsorships and affiliate links, from companies that pay you directly | Chapter 13 |

[YouTube’s eligibility page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851) is plain about the first row. YouTube can run ads on any video, and a channel that has not met the higher criteria does not receive a share of that revenue. The views before the gate still matter. They are the hours and the subscribers the gate counts.

The last row is the exception, and it has limits. A company can pay you for one video before YouTube does. An affiliate program can pay a commission when a viewer buys a product you recommended. Both need an audience that trusts you, and both need a disclosure next to the link. Chapter 13 sets the rules. Neither replaces the channel. A sponsor pays for attention you already earned.

## IV. Set up the channel once, properly

Set up only what a stranger sees, and the switches the gates will ask for later.

- **A name the viewer understands.** If the name needs explaining, the description has to do that work, and most people never read it. A name that says the subject beats a clever word.
- **A handle and a profile picture** that are readable at the size of a thumbnail on a phone.
- **A description whose first lines say who the channel helps and what arrives each week.** Chapter 15 has a template.
- **A banner you can read on a phone.** Shrink it before you upload. If the words vanish, cut words.
- **2-Step Verification on the Google Account.** The Partner Program requires it. Turning it on now costs a minute.
- **Phone verification, then advanced features.** [YouTube’s sharing-links page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639) says a long-form description and a long-form comment become clickable only after the channel has advanced features. Studio shows the status under Settings, Channel, Feature eligibility. Advanced features ask for phone verification first, then channel history or an identity check where YouTube offers one. Affiliate links in chapter 13 depend on it, and the Partner Program lists it as a requirement.
- **The country in your channel settings.** The Partner Program, and each fan-funding feature, are offered by country. Set it truthfully.
- **The audience setting.** Made for kids or not, at the channel level and on each video. Chapter 10 explains why this is a legal statement and why it switches off memberships, Supers, and more.

Skip the logo exploration. A banner is a sign. The videos are the channel.

## V. Posts keep the channel visible between videos

YouTube lets a channel publish posts: a question, a poll, an image, a line about the next video. [YouTube’s posts page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9409631) describes them as a way to reach viewers. It says posts are unavailable on a supervised account and on a channel set as made for kids, and that they are not currently available in Germany. Confirm that page, because a country limit moves.

Posts matter for money in one specific way. The eligibility page says YouTube may turn off monetization on a channel that has not uploaded a video or posted to the Posts tab for six months or more. A post is not a substitute for videos. It is a way to stay in touch in a slow month, and a poll is a cheap way to ask the viewer which question to answer next.

## VI. Do this before you film

Write the viewer sentence and the poor-fit sentence. Write twenty titles. Fill in the money map with the stage your channel is at today, which is the first row. Set up the channel name, description, banner, 2-Step Verification, and phone verification. Set the audience setting honestly. If you cannot finish that list, the next chapters will help you make videos and will not help them add up.

> **Key takeaway:** Name one viewer, a subject with fifty videos in it, and the order in which YouTube pays. The views before the gate are the hours the gate counts.

## VII. Claims that do not survive the eligibility page

- That a channel earns once it has views. YouTube can run ads on any video. A channel that has not met the Partner Program’s criteria does not receive a share.
- That a high-paying subject is the plan. Advertisers pay by season, geography, and format, and a subject you cannot sustain earns nothing. The viewer comes first, then the arithmetic.
- That subscribers are the scoreboard. Subscribers are one of the counts. Public watch hours or qualified Shorts views are the other half of every gate.
- That sponsors and affiliate links are a shortcut past the gates. They can start earlier. They still need an audience that trusts you, and a disclosure every time.
- That set-up can wait until the channel is bigger. 2-Step Verification, advanced features, and the audience setting all sit on the Partner Program checklist. Do them while they are easy.
- That a post can stand in for a video. A post keeps the channel visible. The watch hours come from videos.

A last limit. This chapter will not choose your subject for you, and it will not make a subject you dislike sustainable. It will refuse the version of a channel that posts for months with no named viewer and then blames the algorithm.
