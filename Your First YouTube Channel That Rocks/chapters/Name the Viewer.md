*A working guide for creators who want a channel, not a hobby*

# Name the Viewer

*Layer: Start now. Workbook: the viewer sheet and the set-up sheet (chapter 15, §I–II).*

The title of this book is a job description. A channel that rocks is not a channel with one lucky video, a round subscriber number, or a monetization badge you hope will arrive by itself. It is a channel a specific person recognizes as theirs, watches to the end, and returns to next week. YouTube’s programs count subscribers, public watch hours, and qualified Shorts views, and they open in a published order. Everything in this book exists to move those numbers honestly and then to turn them into income.

If you cannot name the viewer in one sentence, you do not have a channel yet. You have a place to post.

## I. Name the viewer before the topic

A topic is not a viewer. “Fitness,” “personal finance,” and “home repair” describe shelves. A viewer is a person with a situation, a question they would type into search, and a reason to come back when the next video arrives.

Finish this sentence before you design a banner: “This channel helps [specific person] who wants [specific result], one video at a time.” “People who like cooking” is not a person. “Adults who cook for one on a weeknight and are tired of throwing food away” is.

Write a second sentence: “This channel is a poor fit for people who want [different result].” A retirement-planning channel that also tries to serve day traders will teach neither well enough to be watched to the end. The arithmetic is plain: a viewer who leaves at second ten adds ten seconds to the watch-hour bar. YouTube’s recommendation pages say the system learns from how much of a video people watch (documented platform guidance). They do not say how much one early exit weighs, so do not read more into a single exit than the arithmetic.

## II. A subject you can make fifty videos about

The gates in chapter 9 count a year of long-form watch hours, or ninety days of Shorts views. Neither arrives from three videos. Choose a subject with enough questions in it that you can still name the next video after the fortieth.

Test it on paper. Write twenty video titles for your viewer in one sitting, each one a question they would type. If you stall at eight, the subject is too narrow or you do not know it well enough yet. If the twenty titles serve five different people, it is too wide.

Two more checks decide what the channel can earn later.

- **Advertiser-friendly by default.** YouTube’s advertiser-friendly guidelines treat some subjects with caution, and a video in a sensitive category can run with limited ads or none. Chapter 10 has the rules. A channel can cover a hard subject. It should know, before the first upload, that the subject has a cost.
- **What the audience is worth to an advertiser.** YouTube’s ad revenue page says advertisers pay different amounts by time of year, viewer location, and available ad formats. Chapter 11 explains the numbers. A well-paid subject you cannot make fifty videos about pays nothing.

## III. The money map

YouTube and its partners pay through six streams, and each opens at a different point. This table is the map for the rest of the book. The detailed rules are in the chapter named in the first column.

| Income stream | Can it begin before YPP? | Eligibility dependency | Key caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Affiliate links (chapter 13) | Yes | Outside programs set their own terms. A link in a long-form description or comment is clickable only after advanced features are on. YouTube’s own Shopping affiliate program needs the Partner Program and 500 subscribers, where offered. | Disclose next to every link. A Short’s description and comments are not clickable. |
| Sponsors (chapter 13) | Yes | A direct deal needs no gate. YouTube Creator Partnerships needs the Partner Program, age 18, a supported country, and no active strikes. | Select paid promotion in Studio and say it in the video. Local law can require more. |
| Fan funding: memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks (chapter 12) | No | Partner Program. Where the expanded program is offered: 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days, and 3,000 long-form hours in 365 days or 3 million Shorts views in 90 days. Age 18, the Commerce Product Module, and a country where each feature is available. | Off on made-for-kids videos. Claimed videos lose Super Thanks. Not a donation: income for tax purposes. |
| Watch-page ads (chapter 11) | No | 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 long-form hours in 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. New applicants from 1 February 2027: 8,000 hours or 20 million Shorts views. Watch Page Monetization Module. | YouTube may run ads on your videos before you are accepted. You receive no share of them. |
| Shorts revenue share (chapter 11) | No | The same gate, plus the Shorts Monetization Module. From 1 February 2027, a month pays only if the channel had 10 million qualified Shorts views in the previous 90 days. | Shorts-feed hours never fill the long-form hour bar. |
| YouTube Premium (chapter 11) | No | Comes with the Watch Page module for long videos and the Shorts module for Shorts. Paid from a subscription pool by watch time and views. | The Shorts part falls under the same 10-million floor from February 2027. |

> **Thresholds change.** Before planning around any monetization target, check the live Earn tab in YouTube Studio and YouTube’s official Partner Program eligibility page for your country. This book reflects information checked in October 2026.

Official rule: [YouTube’s eligibility page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851) says YouTube can run ads on any video, and a channel that has not met the higher criteria does not receive a share. The views before the gate still matter. They are the hours and the subscribers the gate counts.

The first two rows are the exception, and they have limits. Both need an audience that trusts you, and both need a disclosure every time. A sponsor pays for attention you already earned.

## IV. Set up the channel once, properly

Set up only what a stranger sees, and the switches the gates will ask for later. Tick each line on the set-up sheet.

- **A name the viewer understands.** A name that says the subject beats a clever word.
- **A handle and a profile picture** readable at thumbnail size on a phone.
- **A description whose first lines say who the channel helps and what arrives each week.** Chapter 15 has a template.
- **A banner you can read on a phone.** Shrink it before you upload. If the words vanish, cut words.
- **2-Step Verification on the Google Account.** Official rule: the Partner Program requires it.
- **Phone verification, then advanced features.** Official rule: [YouTube’s sharing-links page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639) says a link in a long-form description or comment is clickable only after the channel has advanced features. Studio shows the status under Settings, Channel, Feature eligibility. The Partner Program lists advanced features as a requirement.
- **The country in your channel settings.** The Partner Program and each fan-funding feature are offered by country. Set it truthfully.
- **The audience setting.** Made for kids or not, at the channel level and on each video. Chapter 10, §IX explains why this is a legal statement and what it switches off.

Skip the logo exploration. A banner is a sign. The videos are the channel.

## V. Posts keep the channel visible between videos

YouTube lets a channel publish posts: a question, a poll, an image, a line about the next video. Official rule: [YouTube’s posts page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9409631) says posts are unavailable on a supervised account and on a channel set as made for kids, and that they are not currently available in Germany.

Posts matter for money in one way. The eligibility page says YouTube may turn off monetization on a channel that has not uploaded a video or posted to the Posts tab for six months or more. Chapter 9 has the definition of “active” that applies from February 2027. A post is not a substitute for videos. A poll is a cheap way to ask the viewer which question to answer next.

*In short:* Name one viewer, a subject with fifty videos in it, and the order in which the money opens. The views before the gate are the hours the gate counts. The one-page plan in Start this week turns this chapter into Day 1 and Day 2.

## VI. Claims that do not survive the eligibility page

- That a channel earns once it has views. A channel below the Partner Program’s criteria receives no share of the ads YouTube runs on it.
- That a high-paying subject is the plan. A subject you cannot sustain earns nothing.
- That subscribers are the scoreboard. Public watch hours or qualified Shorts views are the other half of every gate.
- That set-up can wait until the channel is bigger. 2-Step Verification, advanced features, and the audience setting are on the Partner Program checklist. Do them while they are easy.
