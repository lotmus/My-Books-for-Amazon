## Numerical/accounting correction applied in REV23

In the Chapter 15 filled example in REV22, the manuscript calculated $200 as 40,000 long-form views × an invented $5 RPM per 1,000, labels that amount “Ads and Premium,” and then adds about $42 from memberships. The arithmetic for $200 is correct, and 12 × $4.99 × 70% = $41.92 (about $42 before applicable taxes and fees). The revenue categories are not additive as written: YouTube defines RPM as total revenue per 1,000 views and includes ads, channel memberships, YouTube Premium, Super Chat, and Super Stickers. The $200 RPM-based amount already includes the membership revenue, so adding another $42 can double-count it.

Primary reference: [Understand ad revenue analytics — YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314357?hl=en).

## RPM denominator correction applied in REV23

REV22 defined RPM in Chapter 11 and the glossary as total revenue per 1,000 “engaged views.” YouTube’s current Help page distinguishes formats: RPM uses all views for videos and engaged views for Shorts. REV23 updates both definitions with the correct format-specific denominator.

Primary reference: [Understand ad revenue analytics — YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314357?hl=en), RPM comparison table.

### Applied correction in REV23

REV23 keeps the invented $200 total and describes it as total YouTube Analytics revenue at a $5 RPM across 40,000 views. It states that the $42 membership estimate is part of that total, not additional to it. The example identifies approximately $158 as ads and Premium when memberships and ads/Premium are the only YouTube income lines. Keep the outside affiliate commission separate because it is not YouTube Analytics revenue. This preserves the teaching point without counting the same membership revenue twice.
