# Sensitivity and Representation Review — 12 October 2026

## Score: A−

The manuscript avoids using nationality, race, gender, religion, disability, class, or age as a punchline or shorthand for ability, credibility, income, or behavior. Most teaching examples are deliberately generic and evaluate a channel’s viewer, topic, evidence, and choices rather than assigning traits to people. No passage-level stereotype or identity-based harm was found that warrants a rewrite.

## Findings

- **National and regional context:** YouTube’s geographic ad-market differences are described as advertiser competition and viewer mix, not as a judgment about viewers’ worth. Country availability is repeatedly tied to the platform’s own country lists. U.S.-specific prices, FTC guidance, and taxes are labeled as U.S.-specific or directed back to the reader’s own jurisdiction.
- **Institutions:** YouTube, AdSense, the FTC, and the U.S. Copyright Office are named as institutions with particular policies or records. Their guidance is attributed rather than generalized into claims about all countries or creators.
- **Children and family channels:** The family-vlog example distinguishes the child’s privacy and choice from the household’s content plan. It tells creators not to expose school names or routes home, and advises keeping children out of thumbnails when the intended audience is adults. This is specific, practical, and protective.
- **Economic identity:** Examples about retirement planning, day trading, homeowners, and sponsors distinguish audience needs or business models; they do not characterize social groups as inherently irresponsible or less capable.
- **Limit:** The manuscript has few individually portrayed creators and is mainly framed around one U.S.-based platform. That limits the range of lived experiences readers encounter, but the book does not claim to represent every national market. Adding unsourced creator stories solely to signal diversity would weaken its evidence standard.

## Recommendation

Keep the current identity-neutral examples and jurisdiction labels. For the listing, position the book primarily for adult beginners; older teens around 16+ may use it with a parent or guardian. The under-18 payment rule belongs beside this recommendation because platform monetization requires an adult’s approved AdSense for YouTube account. Test older-teen usability separately before making stronger claims about that audience.
