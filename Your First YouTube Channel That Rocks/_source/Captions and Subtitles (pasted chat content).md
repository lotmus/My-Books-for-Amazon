**Yes, viewers can freely turn closed captions (CC) / subtitles on or off.**

### How viewers turn CC/subtitles on or off
- **Desktop/web**: Hover over the video → click the **CC** button in the bottom-right controls (or press **C** on the keyboard). Click it again to turn off.
  You can also go to the gear icon (Settings) → Subtitles/CC.
- **Mobile app**: Tap the video to show controls → tap the **CC** icon.
- **Always on by default**:
  - Desktop: Profile icon → Settings → Playback and performance → check "Always show captions" (and optionally "Include auto-generated captions").
  - Mobile: Profile → Settings → Captions → toggle "Show captions".

Viewers can also change language (including auto-translate), font size, color, background, etc., in the settings.

### How to add CC / subtitles as a creator (YouTube Studio)
YouTube supports both **closed captions** (toggleable by the viewer) and automatic captions. Here's the current process:

1. Go to [YouTube Studio](https://studio.youtube.com) and sign in.
2. In the left menu, go to **Subtitles** (sometimes under **Languages** depending on the interface).
3. Select the video.
4. Click **Add language** (or **Add**) next to the desired language.
5. Choose one of these methods:
   - **Upload file** (best for accuracy) — Supported formats include `.srt`, `.vtt`, `.sbv`, and others. Choose "With timing" if your file has timestamps, or "Without timing" so YouTube auto-syncs it.
   - **Auto-sync** — Paste or upload a plain transcript; YouTube times it to the audio.
   - **Type manually** — Watch the video and type captions as you go (useful keyboard shortcuts and "pause while typing" available).
   - **Edit automatic captions** — YouTube often generates auto-captions. Click **Duplicate and edit** (or the three-dot menu → Edit) next to the automatic track, fix errors, then publish.

You can also add subtitles during the video upload process under Video elements → Add subtitles.

**Tips**:
- Always review and edit automatic captions — they can have mistakes with accents, names, background noise, etc.
- Publish the captions when ready. Once published, they appear as a track viewers can toggle.
- You can add multiple languages (translated subtitles).
- Burned-in (open) captions are different — those are permanently part of the video image and can't be turned off.

That's the full process. Adding accurate captions improves accessibility, SEO, and watch time.
