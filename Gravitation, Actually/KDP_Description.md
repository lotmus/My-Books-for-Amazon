## Description (HTML)

```html
<p><b>Let go of an apple and nothing pulls it down.</b> It is already on the straightest path there is. The floor is what gets in the way.</p>
<p>So why do astronauts float in a station where gravity is still 89 percent as strong as on the ground? Why would your phone’s map be kilometers off within a day if nobody corrected the satellite clocks for Einstein? Why does the sea rise twice a day, not once? And why is the edge of a black hole a door and not a wall?</p>
<p><i>Gravitation, Actually</i> answers in ordinary words, and brings the story right up to what has actually been measured:</p>
<ul>
<li>Clocks in orbit that run fast by 38 microseconds a day, and the GPS correction that keeps them honest</li>
<li>Tides as the fingerprint of curved spacetime, and why a small black hole would tear you apart before you reached its edge</li>
<li>Stars with a last size: white dwarfs, neutron stars, and what happens past the limit</li>
<li>The first images of black-hole shadows, M87* in 2019 and Sgr A* in 2022</li>
<li>Ripples in spacetime caught in 2015, which changed the difference between two 4-kilometer arms by a few thousandths of a proton’s width</li>
</ul>
<p><b>Two equations, said in words. No tensor algebra.</b> The book retells the best teaching pictures in the subject for a general reader: the ants on the apple, the two surveyors who disagree on north but agree on distance, the pole in the barn, the tunnel through the Earth, the observers on shells around a black hole, and a map on which the singularity turns out to be a moment, not a place.</p>
<p><b>Read it your way:</b></p>
<ul>
<li>Every section is marked Core or Extension, so you can take the short road or the long one</li>
<li>A short "In this chapter" box and a recap for each of the eight chapters</li>
<li>Optional exercises (68, with worked solutions and the usual mistake) for readers who like to check the numbers. Skip them and the story still holds.</li>
<li>Diagrams drawn for the book, plus real images from NASA and the Event Horizon Telescope</li>
<li>A glossary, review questions with answers, and a reading list with the papers behind each claim</li>
</ul>
<p>For curious readers who want the real idea, not a slogan.</p>
<p><i>Part of the Actually books by Lothar J. Musiol.</i></p>
```
