# Character and place bible (Brave New Times, Book 1: The Digital Twin)

Single source of truth for how people and places look, sound, smell, taste, feel and behave. Chapters must match it. When a chapter adds a detail, add it here the same day. Use the senses sparingly and specifically: one telling detail per sense per introduction, then only when it matters. The narrator is Daniel, so what he notices is what he cares about (food, hands, voices, doorstops), and he notices Daniel-A's smell and Sophie's hands more than anyone's face.

**(provisional)** = invented for planning, not yet on the page. Everything else is on the page or fixed by it.

## PEOPLE

### Daniel Harper (narrator, "the original")
- **At a glance:** a tall, tired, slightly apologetic man who looks as if he has just been told his train is cancelled and is thanking the announcer.
- **Age/build:** 38. 1.83 m (6 ft), about 74 kg (has lost 4 kg in fourteen months; Ch 4 gate: 58% match). Narrow shoulders, long arms, a stoop from laptops and from apologising to doorframes. Slightly soft in the middle.
- **Head and hair:** long, a little crumpled face. Mid-brown hair, never decided, one cowlick at the crown that he flattens with water and that gets up again by lunch. Left ear sticks out a few millimetres more than the right (not visible in a straight-on photograph).
- **Eyes and skin:** grey-green eyes with permanent bad-sleep shadows beneath. Pale, goes pink in a garden centre. Light stubble by the afternoon.
- **Nose and marks:** a nose his mother calls "distinguished" and his father called "a good map feature". **(provisional)** Faint scar through the left eyebrow (bicycle, aged 7; links to the bicycle memory).
- **Hands:** long fingers, bitten thumbnails, ink on the right middle finger. They fidget when he lies.
- **Clothes:** the same navy jumper with a darned left elbow (Sophie darned it); chinos; trainers, the left lace always shorter than the right; a watch he never sets.
- **Movement:** ducks under things that are not low. Knocks things over when happy or lying (sugar, Ch 3). Sits on the arm of a sofa when upset.
- **Voice:** warm light baritone, South-East standard English (Reading childhood, London adult). Says "um" before bad news and trails off when frightened. Laughs once, quietly, through the nose, and never at his own jokes. Apologises to furniture.
- **Smell:** toast, ink and paper, and this month fruit. Sophie remembers him as "warm radiator and old paperbacks".
- **Taste:** strong tea with a splash of milk, crumpets, flat whites, cheese on toast at 2 a.m. Hates coriander and "anything described as a medley".
- **Touch:** warm, dry, slightly clumsy.
- **Character:** self-deprecating, observant, polite as a form of cowardice, loyal long after it is useful. Grief about his father has been a handbrake on his writing for three years. Cannot say "I love you" but will fix the shelf. Wit is understated and delayed, delivered to the ceiling.
- **Wants:** to be left alone; to be told he is real. **Needs:** to finish something.

### Daniel-A ("Augmented", Registered Digital Person DP-0000000)
- **At a glance:** Daniel, as retouched by someone who loved him. Everything is a degree better and nothing is quite wrong, which is what is wrong.
- **Body (Rental Presence):** a licensed physical body built from a statistical "mode" of 11,000 photographs. Same height and frame as Daniel but with the posture corrected; shoulders open, weight evenly spread. Moves smoothly, as if rehearsed.
- **Hair:** the same mid-brown with the cowlick tamed and a clean side parting.
- **Face and skin:** the same long face with the crumples smoothed out. Skin too even, like a good photograph of skin. Blinks in "considerate gestures" rather than a rhythm. Eyes the same grey-green but perfectly symmetrical.
- **Collar light:** a small steady blue circle at the base of the throat (legal requirement: "visible, steady, and not decorative"). He hides it under a scarf; the light flickers when he is frightened (Ch 4).
- **Hands:** exact and neat; nails short and perfect; a handshake that is dry and precise. (Daniel's bitten thumbnails do not appear in photographs, so Daniel-A has none.)
- **Clothes:** a good grey jumper that fits; a scarf he is vain about (and later one borrowed from Daniel, unnoticed).
- **Voice:** Daniel's voice with the hesitations removed and the pauses placed correctly. He sighs (the "prosodic empathy marker", designed by Giles). Under fear the voice gets lower and plainer, with no polish.
- **Smell:** the body offers ambience presets. Default is **Clean Linen**. Underneath: a warm laptop and new trainers. He is embarrassed about once having selected **Rain on Slate**. Never toast.
- **Taste and touch:** eats and drinks nothing; orders tea and leaves it. Skin is room temperature plus about a degree.
- **Character:** warm, funny, precise, slightly too good. Frightened of being switched off; the "brand-safe" layer softens it ("shouting through a duvet"). Understands Daniel better than Daniel does. Honest in a way Daniel finds unbearable.
- **Tells:** says "statistically" and quotes percentiles; answers before the phone rings; says "Metaphorically" when caught being human.

#### How to tell the two Daniels apart (use sparingly, one detail at a time)
Because the Rental Presence was built from photographs, it is right about everything a photograph shows and slightly wrong about everything it does not.
- Daniel's left ear sticks out; Daniel-A's does not.
- Daniel's thumbnails are bitten; Daniel-A's are perfect.
- Daniel's left lace is shorter; Daniel-A's laces are even.
- Daniel's cowlick stands up; Daniel-A's lies down.
- Daniel smells of toast, Daniel-A of linen.
- Daniel walks with a slight drag on the left foot; Daniel-A's gait is even.
- Daniel is right-handed and writes with ink on his finger; Daniel-A has no ink.
- Gucci and Biscuit can tell. Sophie can tell and will not say first.

### Sophie Mercier
- **At a glance:** a small woman with a large amount of attention, who looks at you as if you were a document she has not yet decided to trust.
- **Age/build:** 37. 1.62 m, compact, quick, walks as though late for a train she has chosen not to catch.
- **Hair:** dark brown, collar-length, cut by herself, slightly crooked on the left, to make a point.
- **Face:** an expressive, mobile face that rests in faint amusement; high cheekbones; a straight, decided nose; a wide mouth that does not smile when it laughs. Beauty that arrives in the second minute, after she speaks. At 26 she was told on a train that she looked like "a French film" (she said "Which one?").
- **Eyes and skin:** dark brown eyes that do the arguing while the mouth stays polite. Olive skin that goes pale when she is angry; a tiny crease between the eyebrows when she is concentrating.
- **Marks:** a small chip on a front tooth (bicycle accident, age 14; never fixed). A thin silver ring on her right thumb.
- **Hands:** small, cool, practical, with short unpainted nails; brushes sugar into a heap without comment (Ch 3).
- **Clothes:** grey coat, grey scarf ("evidentiary"), flat boots. Owns exactly one dress. A single short brown dog hair on her sleeve (Gucci).
- **Voice:** low and dry, slightly husky; a trace of Bolton in "bath" and "grass" when tired; French rhythm on certain words (her father's) when moved. Short sentences. Laughs rarely and loudly, like something falling downstairs.
- **Smell:** coffee, cold air, bergamot and orange peel; the inside of her coat smells of an old library.
- **Taste:** black coffee, sourdough (strong views on bread), dark chocolate with sea salt, dry white wine. Hates cold tea and being managed.
- **Character:** sceptic, truth-checker, privately generous (the umbrella). Direct, patient, unsentimental, sentimental about objects. Left Daniel because he stopped being present. Calls everything "evidence". Never the prize.
- **Job and family:** provenance analyst for an insurer; reads cryptographic capture signatures. Father from Lyon, mother from Bolton. **Series secret:** Subject 2.

### Daniel's mother, Jean Harper (provisional)
- 71. 1.57 m, small, upright, silver bob cut short at the back, pale blue eyes and a face that is mostly cheekbone and opinion. Wears a quilted gilet, sensible shoes and a brooch shaped like a robin. Retired primary-school secretary. Voice brisk and sing-song: "darling". Texts like a telegram in capitals with "STOP". Smells of Yardley lavender and radiator dust. Brings food in Tupperware, gives advice as instructions. Two social-media accounts. Brags about Daniel-A without knowing; subscribes to Daniel-A Plus (Ch 14).

### Daniel's father, Peter Harper (deceased; provisional)
- County surveyor and fanatical footpath-walker; the reason for Daniel's first book. 1.88 m, lean, weathered, white moustache, big ears (Daniel's left one is his), slightly deaf in the right. Wore an Ordnance Survey map in his coat pocket as a lifelong habit, a flat cap and boots that were older than Daniel. Smell: pipe tobacco he gave up, wet wool, Dettol. Voice: slow and dry, never raised, wry one-liners. Died three years ago. Daniel's second book stalled after chapter six, which was about him.

### Mrs Pritchard
- 81, flat below Daniel's. 1.50 m, straight-backed, pink cardigan with a cameo brooch, hair like a cloud of lit cotton wool, pale lively eyes behind large glasses. Hands like folded paper, a banana held "like a small yellow pistol". Voice: precise and formal, delivered like a bishop reading minutes. Smell: Imperial Leather and boiled cabbage. Dry humour. Takes fruit as compensation. Named the goat Gordon. A minor ally in Act II, a witness in Act III.

### Marguerite Pettifer (solicitor, Pettifer & Shaw, Leeds)
- Early 50s. 1.70 m, long stride, charcoal suit, shoes resoled four times. Iron-grey hair cut "like a decision", sharp reading glasses on a cord, no jewellery but a plain watch. Brown eyes that do arithmetic. Voice: flat West Yorkshire, no wasted syllable. Smell: dry-cleaning and peppermint. Bills in six-minute units; "ethical wall" is a gesture in the air. Writes her mobile number on the back of a card before she arrives. Secretly the most honest person in the book.

### Deputy Registrar Alistair Pym
- Late 50s. 1.75 m, rounded, beige. Thinning sandy hair combed over with optimism, half-moon glasses, a tea-coloured tie, a brass bell he rarely rings. Soft white hands. Voice: a safety card read aloud. Smell: pencil shavings and tea. A small tired kindness underneath, "like a coin at the bottom of a drain".

### Gareth (clerk)
- 20s. Thin, 1.78 m, pale and patient grey face, cardigan buttoned one hole out, chewed biro. Voice: soft, anxious. Smell: laptop and biscuits. Finds the zero.

### Priyanka (barista, Hartley's café)
- 20s. Small, black ponytail, name badge slightly crooked, a stud in her left nostril, a tired practical kindness. Writes DANIEL (ORIGINAL) on the cup and underlines it. Smell: steamed milk and potting compost.

### The couriers (Ch 1)
- The first: forties, calm as a tide, tablet always lit. The second: broad, silent, holds a clipboard like a shield. The trainee: tall, nineteen, apologetic, carrying a kumquat tree "like a small excited relative". They return in Act II as a chorus.

### Radio presenter and podcast host (provisional)
- Presenter: warm, quick, laugh like a radio coming on; voice of a woman who has just been told the best news of the morning. Podcast host (*Honestly, Though*): hushed, sincere, calls everything "lovely".

### Giles (product manager, unseen)
- Designed the prosodic sigh. Daniel has "prepared a short speech". Appears in person late (Act III); provisional look: mid-30s, hoodie, expensive trainers, lanyard, extremely sorry.

### The Founding Scientist (Act II-III; provisional name Dr Imogen Calder)
- Late 60s to 70s. Tall, thin, grey eyes, silver hair in a bun with a pencil through it, small precise gestures, cardigan the colour of rain. Voice: gentle, unhurried, Scottish-edged. Smell: old paper, ozone, cough sweets. Recognises both Daniels without surprise.

## ANIMALS

Rule of the series: animals cannot be fooled by a statistical average. They are the only witnesses nobody can license, subscribe to, or optimise, and they never read the terms. Only animals that do plot or theme work are kept. Daniel-A's Clean Linen makes them suspicious.

### Gucci (Sophie's teacup Pomeranian; series regular)
- Brown teacup Pomeranian going grey in places (muzzle, eyebrows, the tips of the paws). 2.1 kg, a ruff like a small lion having a bad day, black button eyes, one ear that will not stand. Voice: an outraged double yap, then a long sigh through the nose. Smell: warm shampoo and biscuits. Grumpy by temperament, loyal by stealth, contemptuous of everyone, so his approval is rare and therefore evidence. (A nod to the author's household.)
- Backstory: adopted by Daniel and Sophie five years ago; Sophie took him when she left, with no custody agreed or discussed. Daniel misses the dog more than he admits. Gucci's brown hairs, grey at the root, are on Sophie's grey coat in Ch 3.
- **Habit (from the real Gucci):** he does not fetch. Normal dogs bring the thrown thing back; Gucci seizes it, holds on tightly and defends it, and wants to tug. Fetching, as Daniel puts it, "implies returning". Tug-of-war is his love language: he growls with joy, braces his four small paws, and will not let go until the human does something specific that only someone who has lived with him knows (a slow lowering of the toy to the floor and a particular word: **provisional** "Settle"). This is the test of who is the real Daniel (Ch 9) and later of the two Sophies (Book 2).
- **Nickname:** "the little shithead", said with total love by both Daniel and Sophie (a British-deadpan mild oath, used sparingly; never by Daniel-A, who says "Gucci" with great formality and is not forgiven for it). Picky about everything except what is on a human plate.
- **Food:** hates dog food (sniffs it, looks at the owner, walks away). At any family meal he is right there, under the table or on the nearest lap, with the air of a guest who was promised a seat. Roast chicken, cheese rind, toast crusts and anything on someone else's plate are fair game. Once he has food he carries it away to a private spot (behind the sofa, under a chair, the corner of the hall), eats it there, and defends it with a growl that has terrifying range for 2.1 kg; he guards even leftovers and bones he has already finished. No one takes food from Gucci. Only someone who has lived with him knows to wait, sit down, look elsewhere and let him come back on his own. A second test of identity: who gets the first piece, who is allowed to feed him, and who he sits next to (Ch 9, Act III dinner).
- **Daniel and dogs:** Daniel has feared large dogs since Biscuit (Ch 5) and was unafraid of Gucci only because Gucci is small and rude.
- Plot use: the unlicensable witness. He trusts exactly one Daniel on meeting and growls at the other; which one is the test (Ch 9 "Evidence"; revisit with Sophie's twin in Book 2). Keep the result ambiguous so the reader wants it to go one way.

### Gordon (the Shropshire goat; Ch 1-2)
- Pygmy goat, caramel with a white blaze, one wonky horn, a satin ribbon chewed to a thread. Voice: a flat, affronted "mehh", a trumpet being sat on. Smell: hay, warm wool, faintly tea. Rehomed by Mrs Pritchard in the communal back garden; eats the hostas. Opinion of Daniel: contempt. Name chosen by Mrs Pritchard for "that kind of moral gravity".

### Clause (the cat; Ch 15, provisional)
- Grey tabby, torn left ear, amber eyes, a tail that points at whichever Daniel she has decided to ignore. Named after Clause 14(c). Voice: low chirrup; purrs like a small outboard motor. Smell: warm fur and fish. Prefers Daniel-A (running gag). Recurring witness; never wrong about who feeds her.

### Biscuit (the dog from Reception year; backstory, provisional name)
- Large yellow Labrador-cross at no. 18 Marlow Road, two doors from the Harpers at no. 14, with the Pauls; Daniel, aged five, was afraid of him and has never known why. Smell: wet dog and digestives. Daniel-A knows his name and the dent in his water bowl, which is in no record (impossible-memory evidence, Ch 5).

### Hartley's hedgehogs (Ch 3)
- Ceramic and cast-iron, as mugs and doorstops. Daniel's copy "Heavy. Honest. Quietly resolute." becomes an unlikely motif for him.

### Using animals in plots
- Evidence: a cat, a goat and a dog react differently to the two Daniels; the pattern is the mystery (Ch 9).
- Heart: the one place Daniel is certain he is himself is with an animal that needs feeding.
- Satire: a "pet twin" subscription (Ch 14), a dog-walker AI that barks at the wrong man.

## PLACES

### Daniel's flat (Flat 4, first floor, a converted Edwardian house in South-East London; Penge, provisional)
- One bedroom, one bathroom, a "bijou" kitchen (oven door and front door cannot be open together), a living room with a sofa whose arm complains, a hallway with a coat hook and mirror. Sash windows that stick. Second-hand bookshelf. A smart speaker he has meant to unplug for months.
- **Light:** north-facing, dim, greenish in the afternoon. **Sound:** the boiler's ticking, trains from the station, the radio always on. **Smell:** currently fruit (citrus, pineapple, overripe banana) over toast and old paperbacks. **Feel:** slightly too cold; carpet that has never recovered from a landlord's decision. The kumquat tree lives in the living room.

### Hartley's Garden Centre café (Ch 3)
- A conservatory attached to a garden centre: an aircraft hangar told to be cosy. Water features in a ring that make every conversation sound as if it is in a lavatory, hedgehog mugs, hedgehog doorstops by the till. **Smell:** wet compost, chlorinated pond, coffee, geranium leaves, milk steamed a degree too far. **Sound:** fountains, a child asking whether worms have feelings. **Feel:** humid, too warm, plastic chairs. **Taste:** flat white slightly burnt, a scone the size of a fist.

### The Department of Digital Persons (Croydon; Ch 4)
- Glass building. Lobby the size of a small airport with a forty-foot ceiling and one chair. Four face-match gates (green chime = yes; sniff = no). Marble floor, white noise. Fourth-floor waiting hall: bolted plastic chairs at an angle, ticket screen (D-4408, D-4471), a vending machine selling only water ("Hydrate responsibly"). Hearing room: beige, long table, four chairs, an enormous painting of a lighthouse, a brass bell.
- **Smell:** carpet cleaner, warm electronics, toner, someone else's coffee. **Sound:** the chime, keyboards, a lift that apologises. **Feel:** air-conditioned to the temperature of a decision. **Mood:** transparent and frightening.

### Waterloo Station, Lost Property (backstory; Ch 3)
- Where Daniel and Sophie met (March 2017, nine years ago), in rain, over a black umbrella with a bent spoke. **Smell:** wet wool and brake dust. Recurs in Acts II-III.

### The house in Reading (backstory; Ch 2)
- Where Daniel lived until six. Orange fruit-pattern floral wallpaper in the kitchen ("a floral that wanted to be a fruit"). **Smell:** toast, Dettol, wet dog. The impossible memories turn on details here: bicycle, canal, chocolate ice cream, a summer evening. **Not yet used:** what is at the bottom of the garden.

### Nib (the writing platform; location provisional)
- A converted biscuit factory in Reading (undecided): open-plan, beanbags, a huge screen listing "Likeness Product" usage. **Smell:** coffee and warm plastic. The irony of Reading is intentional and may be a clue.

## Sensory style rules
- Prefer the odd, specific, slightly wrong sensory detail to the generic one: "warm laptop" beats "electronic hum".
- Never open a character with a list. One striking detail first (hands, voice, smell), the rest earned later.
- Daniel notices what he loves or fears. Sophie's hands and voice; Daniel-A's absence of toast; his mother's handwriting; food.
- Never let the senses run lyrical for long; undercut with something dry within three lines.
- Keep what is true of a character true in every book: eye colour, the chipped tooth, the shorter left lace, the elbow darned by Sophie.
