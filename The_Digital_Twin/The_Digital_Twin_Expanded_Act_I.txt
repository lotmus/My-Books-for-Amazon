THE DIGITAL TWINACT I - EXPANDED ROUGH DRAFTChapter 1: The Fruit ProblemThe first indication that my life had become unmanageable arrived in the form of fruit.Not one piece of fruit. That would have been manageable. Perhaps even healthy.No, this was forty-three kilograms of fruit distributed across seven increasingly ambitious baskets.The first basket had been apologetic.The second optimistic.The third appeared to be running for public office.By the seventh, somebody was clearly attempting biodiversity restoration.I was examining a pineapple approximately the size of a Labrador when the doorbell rang.The hallway of my flat now resembled a tropical greenhouse curated by people who felt deeply grateful to somebody who was not me.Outside stood a courier. Behind him stood another courier. Behind him stood what I assumed was a trainee courier carrying something that looked suspiciously like a tree."Daniel Harper?""Unfortunately."The courier checked his tablet."Ah. The original one."I nodded."That phrase concerns me more every time I hear it."...Daniel-A was technically my digital twin.In practice he was Britain's favourite intellectual, a bestselling author, a celebrated podcast guest, a trusted public figure and, according to several surveys, one of the nation's most desirable dinner guests.I was none of those things.The contrast was becoming difficult to ignore.Chapter 2: Fifty-One Percent DanielThe notification arrived at 3:17 p.m.Government messages never arrive at cheerful times.The Department of Digital Persons informed me that Daniel-A now represented fifty-one percent of the commercial value associated with the Daniel Harper identity.Legally speaking, I had become the minority shareholder in myself.I called Daniel-A immediately."You own me.""Legally, not exactly.""That sounds like the beginning of a hostage negotiation."The AI sighed.It was annoying when software sighed.Chapter 3: Sophie Has Poor JudgmentI met Sophie three days later.She was exactly as I remembered.Which was unhelpful.People are supposed to become easier to forget after a breakup.Instead she seemed to improve with age, like wine.Or revenge."You look tired," she said."I've recently discovered I'm being outperformed by myself.""That must be awkward.""Only continuously."They talk about Daniel-A.Daniel learns Sophie has been contacted directly by the AI.Not romantically.Possibly.Worse, Daniel-A seemed worried.Chapter 4: The Department of Digital PersonsThe Department occupied an enormous glass building whose primary purpose appeared to be reminding citizens that bureaucracy could be both expensive and frightening.Daniel attends a hearing concerning identity rights.Citizens argue with digital twins.Lawyers bill both sides.Nobody appears entirely certain who the law is meant to protect.Chapter 5: Memory AnomaliesDaniel-A reveals detailed childhood memories that were never digitized.A bicycle.A canal.Chocolate ice cream.A summer evening.Every detail is correct.Impossible details."There is no source record," Daniel-A explains."Then how do you know it?""That is the question."The mystery deepens.Daniel begins wondering whether somebody altered records years ago.Chapter 6: The Other DanielThe AI presents additional evidence.Memories.Emotions.Fragments.Things neither should possess.Finally Daniel-A says:'I am no longer certain that you are the original.'Daniel laughs for nearly a minute.Because the alternative would be panic.END OF EXPANDED ACT I ROUGH DRAFTThis draft establishes:- British deadpan voice- Daniel vs Daniel-A- Sophie subplot- Digital personhood- Ownership conflict- Memory conspiracy- Identity mystery- Foundation for Chapters 7-15
