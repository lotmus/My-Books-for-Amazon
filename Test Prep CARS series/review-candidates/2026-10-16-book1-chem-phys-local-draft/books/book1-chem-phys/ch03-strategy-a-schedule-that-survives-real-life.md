# Strategy: A Schedule That Survives Real Life

**What you will be able to do**

- Apply the planning method from the companion Core Test Skills book to this section.
- Lay out a 20-week plan for the 25 lessons, 6 practice sets and 2 full-length exams in this book.
- Build the daily flashcard decks this section calls for, and size the review load.

**Why it matters**

The method for building a schedule is general, and it lives in the companion book ([Core Test Skills, Chapters 3, 5 and 6]): choose a target, plan backward from the test date, block fixed times, put review first, keep a buffer and a day off, and expect to lose a week now and then. There is no reason to say it all again here. This chapter answers a more specific question: *what does that method look like for the Chemical and Physical Foundations section?*

Praxis Tip: If you have not yet read those three Core chapters, read them first. This chapter assumes them, and will make more sense with them fresh.

**What this section asks of a schedule**

Three features of this section shape the plan.

1. **It is wide.** Physics, general chemistry, organic chemistry and the chemistry of biomolecules each have their own vocabulary and habits of thought. A schedule that treats it as one subject will leave a gap somewhere.
2. **It is applied.** Roughly two out of three questions ask you to apply or interpret something ([Strategy: Know the Exam and Plan Your Attack](chapter:1)). You need practice sets in the calendar from the start, not saved for the end.
3. **It rewards retention of formulas, trends and reaction logic** that fade without review. That makes spaced repetition ([Core Test Skills, Chapter 6]) especially useful here.

**The hours**

The Core example used 12 hours a week, a 20-week calendar, 2 buffer weeks, and 1 final light-review week, which leaves 17 study weeks and 204 hours. This chapter uses those numbers. If your numbers differ, redo the arithmetic with your own using the worksheet in [Core Test Skills, Chapter 34]; the proportions below survive any reasonable change.

The 50/30/20 starting split means each full week of 12 hours is: content learning 6 hours, practice questions and simulations 3.6 hours, review and error log 2.4 hours. (6 + 3.6 + 2.4 = 12.) Adjust after a month, using your own error log.

**Worked Example: A 20-week plan for this book**

The book has 6 opening strategy chapters, 25 lessons, 6 practice sets (chapters 10, 16, 22, 27, 33 and 37), and 6 chapters of test-day planning and full-length exam sections (chapters 38-43), plus three appendices (the source and license ledger, the formula, constant and fact sheet, and the glossary). | S2-S3 | Mechanics | Lessons 1-3, Practice (ch. 10) | 4A |

| S6-S7 | Atoms, bonding, stoichiometry, gases, solutions | Lessons 9-13, Practice (ch. 22) | 4E, 5B, 4B, 5A |
| S8 | Thermodynamics, kinetics | Lessons 14-15 | 5E |

| S11-S12 | Organic | Lessons 18-22, Practice (ch. 33) | 5B, 5C, 5D |

Count: S1 through S17 is 17 study weeks, plus 2 buffer weeks and 1 final week is 20 weeks. ✓

*Reading the plan.* Physics gets four weeks (S2-S5) because it is a quarter of the section and is new for many learners. General chemistry gets five (S6-S10). Organic and biochemistry get three (S11-S13). Practice sets arrive about every second week, and the first full-length simulation lands in S14, about two-thirds of the way through. If your baseline showed weakness in one area, shift a few days from a strong area into it, and write the change down.

*Skill used:* distributing a fixed hour budget across a fixed syllabus, in proportion to the section's weights.

Praxis Tip: The calendar is a draft, and the buffer weeks are not decoration. When a week collapses, use the triage order from [Core Test Skills, Chapter 5]: keep reviews, postpone new content, do not double up.

**A weekly template for 12 hours**

| Day | Hours | Plan |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | 1.5 | 15 min flashcards; lesson content |
| Tue | 1.5 | 15 min flashcards; lesson content |
| Wed | 1.5 | 15 min flashcards; checkpoint questions from Mon-Tue |
| Thu | 1.5 | 15 min flashcards; lesson content |
| Fri | 1.0 | Error log and flashcards |
| Sat | 4.0 | Timed practice (mixed or a practice set), then full review |
| Sun | 1.0 | Plan the week; light flashcards |

(1.5 × 4 + 1.0 + 4.0 + 1.0 = 12 hours ✓.)

Praxis Tip: Do the lesson's *Concept Review* on Monday and Tuesday and its *Checkpoint Questions* on Wednesday. A day's gap between learning and testing is spacing you get for free.

**The five flashcard decks**

This section makes spaced repetition unusually rewarding, because much of it is compact facts that you must recall without thinking. Build five decks, adding cards as you finish each lesson ([Core Test Skills, Chapter 6] on card design):

1. **Formulas and what each variable means.** One idea per card: "Write the equation for centripetal acceleration in terms of v and r." Do not put the whole formula sheet on one card.
2. **Constants and units.** g ≈ 10 m/s² for estimation, Avogadro's number, R, Faraday's constant, standard conversions. Check each value against the fact sheet in the appendix.
3. **Trends and rules.** Periodic trends, acid strength trends, boiling-point trends, "what increases when ___."
4. **Mechanisms and reactions.** Reagent in front, product and why behind.
5. **Concepts and traps.** The "why" cards, including the traps you meet in the error log.

Cap new cards at about 10 per day. Using the ladder (1, 3, 7, 21 days), the steady-state load is about 50 cards a day, or 8 to 12 minutes, as computed in [Core Test Skills, Chapter 6].

Praxis Tip: Add cards from the *error log* first, new lesson material second. Cards that came from your own mistakes are the ones you are most likely to need.

**More Praxis Tips**

Praxis Tip: Put the first official full-length simulation on the calendar now. Everything else can flex around it.

Praxis Tip: Do physics before 9 p.m. if you can. It is the topic that most punishes tired arithmetic.

Praxis Tip: Rotate the first fifteen minutes of each session among the five decks so that no deck goes stale.

Praxis Tip: When you finish a lesson, schedule its day-3 and day-7 review right away, in the calendar. If it is not on the calendar it is not happening.

**Common mistakes**

- Putting all of physics first and never returning to it.
- Leaving practice sets for the last month.
- Skipping the buffer weeks.
- Making flashcards from lesson text instead of from your own errors.
- Treating the 20-week plan as a contract rather than a plan.

**Do this today**

1. Copy the 20-week table into your calendar or notebook and add real dates.
2. Block next week's sessions at fixed times.
3. Create the five decks, and add your first ten cards from the diagnostic.
4. Write your bad-day minimum on a sticky note.

**Recap**

Use the Core method with this section's proportions: four weeks of physics, five of general chemistry, three of organic and biochemistry, practice every second week, a simulation two-thirds in, buffers where life will need them, and five decks reviewed daily.
