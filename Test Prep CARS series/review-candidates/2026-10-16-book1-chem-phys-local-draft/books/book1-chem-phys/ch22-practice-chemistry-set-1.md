# Practice: Chemistry Set 1

**Timing and conditions**

Fourteen original questions on general chemistry from [Lessons 9 to 13](chapter:17): electronic structure and periodic trends, stoichiometry, bonding and molecular shape, gases, and solutions (categories 4B, 4E, 5A and 5B). Two passage sets are followed by four independent questions. Allow about 22 minutes, in one sitting, with scratch paper and no notes or calculator. A periodic table is available on screen in the real exam, so look up atomic masses rather than memorizing them. Use R = 0.0821 L·atm/(mol·K) where needed. Write your answer letters, with a confidence level (high, medium or low) for each, on scratch paper or in your notebook; an ebook cannot be written in.

Praxis Tip: Chemistry questions reward a consistent first move: write the balanced equation or the formula, then the givens with units. Do it even when the question looks easy; the point is to build the habit for when it is not.

Praxis Tip: Round as you go. Use 100 for the molar mass of calcium carbonate and 0.05 for 5.00/100 when you are estimating, and check your exact answer against the estimate.

**Questions**

**Passage 1 (questions 1-5)**

A student places 5.00 g of calcium carbonate (molar mass 100.1 g/mol) in a sealed, rigid, initially evacuated 2.00 L flask and heats it until the reaction is complete, then brings the contents to 300 K (Questions 1 to 3). The only reaction is

CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g)

Assume that the volume of the solids is negligible, that CO₂ behaves as an ideal gas, and that the decomposition is complete.

1. How many moles of CO₂ are produced?
   A. 0.0500 mol
   B. 0.100 mol
   C. 0.0250 mol
   D. 5.00 mol

2. What is the pressure of CO₂ in the flask at 300 K?
   A. 0.31 atm
   B. 1.2 atm
   C. 0.062 atm
   D. 0.62 atm

3. What mass of solid CaO remains in the flask? (Molar mass of CaO = 56.1 g/mol.)
   A. 5.00 g
   B. 2.20 g
   C. 2.80 g
   D. 1.40 g

4. After the reaction is complete, the flask is heated from 300 K to 600 K. Assuming nothing else changes, the pressure becomes closest to
   A. 0.31 atm
   B. 1.2 atm
   C. 0.62 atm
   D. 2.5 atm

5. In a separate experiment at a lower temperature, the decomposition is incomplete and an equilibrium CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g) is established in a closed container. At constant temperature, adding more solid CaCO₃ will
   A. increase the equilibrium pressure of CO₂
   B. not change the equilibrium pressure of CO₂
   C. decrease the equilibrium pressure of CO₂
   D. increase the equilibrium constant

**Passage 2 (questions 6-10)**

Road crews spread calcium chloride on ice to lower its freezing point. A student investigates dissolved-salt effects using aqueous solutions at 298 K. For water, K_f = 1.86 °C/m. Assume ideal behavior and complete dissociation of the ionic compounds.

6. What is the freezing point of a 0.50 m aqueous CaCl₂ solution?
   A. −0.93 °C
   B. −1.9 °C
   C. −5.6 °C
   D. −2.8 °C

7. What is the osmotic pressure of a 0.10 M NaCl solution at 298 K?
   A. 4.9 atm
   B. 2.4 atm
   C. 9.8 atm
   D. 0.49 atm

8. Compared with a 0.10 M glucose solution at the same temperature, a 0.10 M NaCl solution has
   A. about twice the osmotic pressure
   B. the same osmotic pressure
   C. about half the osmotic pressure
   D. no osmotic pressure

9. Red blood cells are isotonic with a solution of about 0.15 M NaCl. A red blood cell placed in 0.10 M NaCl will
   A. shrink, because the solution is hypertonic
   B. remain unchanged, because both solutions contain NaCl
   C. swell, because the solution is hypotonic
   D. shrink, because salt moves into the cell

10. A sparingly soluble salt MX₂ has K_sp = 3.2 × 10⁻¹¹. Its molar solubility in pure water is closest to
   A. 5.7 × 10⁻⁶ M
   B. 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ M
   C. 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ M
   D. 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ M

**Questions 11-14**

These four questions are independent of the passages.

11. How many unpaired electrons does the ground-state Cr³⁺ ion have? (Chromium has atomic number 24.)
   A. 0
   B. 1
   C. 6
   D. 3

12. Which of the following molecules has a bent molecular shape?
   A. CO₂
   B. BF₃
   C. SO₂
   D. CH₄

13. Which of the following has the highest boiling point?
   A. CH₃CH₂OH
   B. CH₃OCH₃
   C. CH₃CHO
   D. CH₃CH₃

14. A gas mixture contains 2.0 mol of O₂ and 6.0 mol of N₂ at a total pressure of 4.0 atm. The partial pressure of O₂ is
   A. 2.0 atm
   B. 3.0 atm
   C. 0.25 atm
   D. 1.0 atm

**Stop here.** Finish every question and write down your answers (on scratch paper) before you continue to the explanations.

**Answers and explanations**

1. **A.** Moles of CaCO₃ = 5.00 / 100.1 = 0.0500 mol. The equation gives one mole of CO₂ per mole of CaCO₃. [4E · Skill 2] (B doubles the correct value; C halves the correct value; D treats the mass as moles.)
2. **D.** P = n R T / V = (0.0500)(0.0821)(300) / 2.00 = 0.62 atm. [4B · Skill 2] (A divides by the volume of 2.00 L twice; B forgets to divide by the volume; C misplaces a power of ten.)
3. **C.** Moles of CaO = 0.0500 mol, so the mass is 0.0500 × 56.1 = 2.80 g. (Check: 5.00 g − 2.20 g of CO₂ = 2.80 g.) [4E · Skill 2] (A assumes no mass is lost as gas; B is the mass of CO₂ that escaped into the gas phase; D halves the correct value.)
4. **B.** At constant volume and amount, pressure is proportional to the absolute temperature, so doubling T doubles P: 2 × 0.62 = 1.2 atm. [4B · Skill 2] (A halves the pressure; C assumes the pressure does not depend on temperature; D quadruples the pressure.)
5. **B.** The equilibrium constant is K_p = P(CO₂); pure solids do not appear in it. At a fixed temperature, P(CO₂) is therefore fixed regardless of the amounts of solid present. [5E · Skill 2] (A treats the solid as if it appeared in the equilibrium expression; C reverses the direction of any shift; D K depends only on temperature.)
6. **D.** CaCl₂ gives three particles (Ca²⁺ + 2 Cl⁻), so i = 3. ΔT_f = i K_f m = 3 × 1.86 × 0.50 = 2.8 °C below the freezing point of pure water. [5A · Skill 2] (A treats CaCl₂ as a single particle; B treats CaCl₂ as producing two particles; C uses the wrong molality.)
7. **A.** Π = i M R T = 2 × 0.10 × 0.0821 × 298 = 4.9 atm. [5A · Skill 2] (B uses i = 1 (treats NaCl as a single particle); C uses i = 4; D misplaces a power of ten.)
8. **A.** Osmotic pressure depends on the number of dissolved particles. Glucose does not dissociate (i = 1), while NaCl gives two ions (i ≈ 2). [5A · Skill 2] (B ignores the dissociation of NaCl into two ions; C inverts the relationship; D a solute of any kind produces osmotic pressure.)
9. **C.** 0.10 M NaCl has fewer particles than the cell contents (equivalent to 0.15 M NaCl), so it is hypotonic. Water enters the cell by osmosis and the cell swells. [5A · Skill 2] (A reverses the direction of water movement; B ignores the concentration difference; D salt does not cross freely; water moves.)
10. **B.** K_sp = [M²⁺][X⁻]² = s(2s)² = 4s³. So s³ = 8.0 × 10⁻¹² and s = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ M. [5A · Skill 2] (A takes the square root, as if the salt were MX; C is the concentration of X⁻, not the solubility of the salt; D halves the correct value.)
11. **D.** Chromium is [Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵. Cr³⁺ loses the 4s electron and two 3d electrons, leaving [Ar] 3d³: three singly occupied d orbitals. [4E · Skill 1] (A assumes all the electrons are paired; B counts the 4s electron of the neutral atom; C counts the d electrons of neutral chromium.)
12. **C.** SO₂ has three regions of electron density (two bonds and one lone pair on sulfur), giving a bent shape. [5B · Skill 1] (A has two electron regions and is linear; B has three bonding regions and no lone pair, so it is trigonal planar; D has four bonding regions and is tetrahedral.)
13. **A.** Ethanol has an O–H group and forms hydrogen bonds, the strongest intermolecular force of the choices. [5B · Skill 1] (B is polar but cannot form hydrogen bonds with itself; C has dipole-dipole forces but no hydrogen bonding; D has only dispersion forces.)
14. **D.** The mole fraction of O₂ is 2.0 / 8.0 = 0.25, so P(O₂) = 0.25 × 4.0 = 1.0 atm. [4B · Skill 2] (A is the mole count of O₂ expressed in atmospheres; B is the partial pressure of N₂; C inverts the mole fraction.)

**Score and review**

Count your correct answers and compare with your confidence notes. Log every miss in your error log ([Strategy: The Question Method](chapter:4)) with a cause, the lesson number, and the category tag in brackets.

How to read your result:

- **12 to 14 correct:** strong. Continue to Lessons 14 to 17 and Chemistry Set 2.
- **9 to 11 correct:** solid but uneven. Redo the misses cold in three days.
- **8 or fewer:** return to the lessons that match your misses: Lesson 9 for Question 11, Lesson 10 for Questions 1 and 3, Lesson 11 for Questions 12 and 13, Lesson 12 for Questions 2, 4 and 14, and Lesson 13 for Questions 6 to 10. Question 5 draws on Lesson 15. Retake the set after a week.

Praxis Tip: Notice how many of these questions turned on a single multiplier: i in a colligative problem, the factor of 4 in K_sp = 4s³, the mole fraction in Dalton's law. Keep a list of "the multiplier I forgot" in your error log. It is short, and it recurs.

Praxis Tip: Passage 1 ties moles, gas laws and equilibrium to one reaction. If you missed Question 5, reread the rule about solids in K. It reappears constantly in solubility and acid-base problems.

Praxis Tip: Questions 11 to 14 are the cheapest points on the page. Did you spend more than 90 seconds on any of them? If so, practice electron configurations and VSEPR shapes as flash drills.
