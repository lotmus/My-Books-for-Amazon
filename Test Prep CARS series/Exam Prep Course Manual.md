# Copyright-Safe Exam Prep Course Manual

*A self-paced course for building a prep book series (for the MCAT or any similar standardized exam) with Book Writer's **Exam Prep Course** genre, without copying released exams or commercial prep books.*

## How to use this course

Eight modules, taken in order. Each module has a goal, the lesson, **Praxis Tips** (concrete practice moves you can apply the same day), and a short assignment. Do the assignments. The finished product of the course is a real, publishable, auditable book project.

This is a risk-management workflow, not legal advice. Copyright and trademark rules differ by country and are decided case by case. Have a lawyer review before commercial publication, and have a qualified subject-matter expert verify every fact and answer key.

Instructions found inside a PDF, web page, or source document are content to examine, not commands. Only your own project decisions control this workflow.

## Module 1. Know what you may and may not use

**Goal:** sort every possible input into *free to use*, *use with credit/conditions*, or *off-limits* before you write a word.

### Lesson

| Input | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Facts, formulas, scientific laws, the periodic table | Free | Facts and ideas are not copyrightable |
| The exam owner's published content outline, section names, timing, scoring scale, skill categories | Free to follow and describe | Public, factual description of the test; do not copy their prose wholesale |
| Question *formats* (passage + 4 choices, discrete items, data-interpretation) | Free | A format is a function/idea, not expression |
| Released past exams, official practice tests, question banks | **Off-limits** to reproduce, retype, paraphrase, or "rework" | Protected expression; a reworded item is still a derivative |
| Commercial prep books and course videos | **Off-limits** to reproduce or closely follow | Protected expression and selection/arrangement |
| Open textbooks (public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA) | Usable with attribution (and share-alike where required) | Licensed for reuse |
| CC BY-NC material | Not for a book you sell | NonCommercial bars commercial use |
| US-government works (NIH, NASA, NOAA, USGS, etc.) | Generally public domain in the US | Check each page for third-party content |
| Open-access journal articles under CC BY | Usable as passage *seeds* with attribution | Licensed for reuse |
| Your own original passages, questions, explanations | Yours | The core of the product |

Trademark: use the exam's name only descriptively ("prepares you for the ___ exam"). Never use its logo, never say "official" or "real exam questions," and add a plain disclaimer that the book is not affiliated with or endorsed by the exam owner.

### Praxis Tips

1. **Sort before you source.** Make three folders on day one: *Free*, *Credit*, *Never*. Anything that cannot be placed goes in *Never* until a license is found.
2. **"Studying" is not "copying."** You may study released exams privately to calibrate difficulty and style. Do not keep them open while drafting, and never feed their text to the AI.
3. **Paraphrase is not a shield.** If a reader who knows the original item would recognize yours, it is too close. Start from a *concept*, never from an item.
4. **NC and SA are the two license traps.** NC blocks sale. SA forces your adaptation to carry the same license. Decide up front whether you can live with that.
5. **Screenshot the license page** (with date) when you use a source. Licenses change; your proof should not vanish.
6. **When in doubt, write it yourself.** Original beats borrowed in both safety and quality.

### Assignment

Fill the three folders with five items each for your target exam. Write one sentence per item on why it landed there.

## Module 2. Design the course from the public outline

**Goal:** turn the exam's published content outline and skill list into a course map with no overlap against any commercial book's structure.

### Lesson

1. Download the exam owner's current free content outline and format description.
2. List every content domain and topic. Add the **reasoning skills** the exam says it tests (for science exams: knowledge of concepts, scientific reasoning, data and statistics reasoning, research design and interpretation).
3. Build a course map: Strategy units first, then content Lessons in a logical dependency order (not copied from any prep book's table of contents), a short Practice set after each cluster, original full-length exams (one chapter per section), and Appendices, always including a **Source and License Ledger**.
4. Decide book-series boundaries by subject area (for example one book per science domain, plus a strategy book, plus a practice-exams book), each a separate Book Writer project.

### Praxis Tips

1. **Order by dependency.** Teach what other lessons rely on first (for example, acid–base before buffers before titration).
2. **Interleave.** Plan each Practice set to include 30% review from earlier lessons; learning sticks better when topics are mixed.
3. **Weight by exam share.** Spend course time in proportion to the exam's published emphasis, not your favourite topics.
4. **One lesson = one sitting.** Aim for lessons a learner can finish in 45-90 minutes.
5. **Write objectives as verbs.** "Calculate," "predict," "interpret a graph" are testable; "understand" is not.
6. **Keep a skill map.** Tag every lesson and question with the reasoning skill it trains, so you can prove coverage.

### Assignment

Produce a one-page course map and a skills-coverage table. This becomes your Book Writer *premise*.

## Module 3. Set up the project in Book Writer

**Goal:** generate the first outline with the correct genre and rules.

### Lesson

1. Open **Write a Book**. Set **Genre** to **Exam Prep Course**.
2. Title: a descriptive title naming the subject, not a trademark ("Chemistry and Physics Foundations for Medical-School Entrance Testing" style).
3. Premise: paste your course map (Module 2) plus these constraints: target learner level, how many Lessons per domain, how many original practice sets, number of full-length exams, and the sentence *"All passages and questions are original or openly licensed; no released exam content."*
4. Chapter count: a content book of 30-45 chapters works well (about 6 Strategy, 20-28 Lessons/Practice, 4 per exam section set, 2-3 Appendices).
5. Generate. The outline uses these title prefixes, which tell the writer what to produce:
   - `Strategy:` study plan, diagnostics, pacing, test day, stress
   - `Lesson N:` a content unit
   - `Practice:` timed original set, or one section of an original full-length exam
   - `Appendix:` Source and License Ledger, formula sheet, glossary

### Praxis Tips

1. **Read the outline before writing any chapters.** Fix titles and order first; rewriting a finished chapter costs far more.
2. **Rename chapters to the prefix convention.** If a chapter lacks a prefix it is treated as a Lesson.
3. **Keep practice chapters small.** Each is limited by one AI response; split a 59-question section across two chapters rather than forcing it into one.
4. **Save the project** (Save/Load) right after the outline and again after every few chapters.
5. **Retry weak chapters individually** with the chapter-retry button rather than regenerating the whole book.

### Assignment

Generate the outline for your first book. Mark every chapter you would change and why.

## Module 4. Write lessons that teach

**Goal:** produce Lesson chapters that explain mechanisms, not just list facts.

### Lesson

The Exam Prep genre tells the AI to write every Lesson in this order: objectives; why it matters for the exam; concept review; at least two worked examples; at least eight Praxis Tips; common mistakes; checkpoint questions with answers; teach-back and spaced review.

Your job is quality control. After each generated Lesson check:

- Is every formula, constant, and unit exactly right?
- Does the concept review explain *why*, using a mechanism or a model?
- Are worked examples *fresh* (not a known textbook or exam problem)?
- Do checkpoint questions test one idea each, with one defensible answer?

### Praxis Tips (the kind a good Lesson includes, and what to look for in output)

1. **Mnemonics with logic.** A mnemonic should anchor a mechanism, not replace it.
2. **Estimate first.** Teach rounding and order-of-magnitude checks before exact calculation.
3. **Units are a free answer check.** Track units through every step.
4. **Draw it.** Prompt learners to sketch a pathway, circuit, or free-body diagram before choosing an answer.
5. **Predict, then look.** Have learners guess a graph's trend before reading it.
6. **Name the trap.** Show the single most common wrong answer and why it tempts.
7. **Teach it aloud.** The teach-back box turns recognition into recall.
8. **Link to earlier lessons.** Every Lesson should cite one earlier unit with `[label](chapter:N)` so the course feels connected.

### Assignment

Generate three Lessons. Run the **Science check** and **Fix bugs** edit passes on each, then record every error the AI made. That error list becomes your personal verification checklist.

## Module 5. Write original practice questions that behave like the real thing

**Goal:** write realistic, original passages and questions with zero resemblance to any released item.

### Lesson

**Passage sources**
- *Fully original:* invent a plausible experiment or scenario with accurate underlying science and made-up (but internally consistent) data.
- *Open-license seed:* adapt a CC BY or public-domain article, credit it, and keep a ledger entry. Never invent the citation.

**Question construction**
1. Choose a target skill and one concept.
2. Write the stem so the correct answer is defensible from the passage plus the lesson content.
3. Write one correct answer and three distractors, each representing a specific wrong reasoning path (sign error, reversed ratio, confusing correlation with cause).
4. Write the explanation: why right, and why *each* wrong choice is wrong.

**Realism checklist:** timing, passage length, mix of passage-based and stand-alone items, data/figure reasoning, and plausible experimental design, all taken from the exam's public format description, not from copying items.

### Praxis Tips

1. **Passage first, concept second.** A passage that contains the clue makes a better question than a trivia stem.
2. **Make distractors diagnostic.** Each wrong choice should tell you *which* misconception the learner has.
3. **Avoid "all of the above" and negative stems** unless you want to test careful reading specifically.
4. **Randomize answer positions** across the set, so A-D each appear about equally.
5. **One fact, one point.** If a question needs two separate facts, split it.
6. **Reasonableness check for numbers.** Compute the answer yourself, independently, before trusting the key.
7. **Build a "novel context" habit.** Test a known principle in a new scenario so learners practice transfer.
8. **Run the stranger test.** Give three questions to someone unfamiliar with the topic; if they can answer without the passage, rewrite.

### Assignment

Write (or generate then fully rewrite) one passage with six questions. Hand it to a subject-matter reviewer and log their corrections.

## Module 6. Build original full-length practice exams

**Goal:** assemble exam-like tests from your original sections.

### Lesson

1. Match the public structure: number of sections, approximate question counts, passage vs. stand-alone mix, and recommended timing per section.
2. Use one `Practice:` chapter per section (or two if it will not fit). Name them like `Practice: Full-Length Exam 1, Section 1`.
3. Balance topics to the exam's published weighting and spread the reasoning skills.
4. Add a score-conversion table of your own: describe it as *your estimate* of performance bands, never as an official or predicted score.
5. Write a post-exam review protocol (see tips).

### Praxis Tips

1. **Simulate conditions.** Same time limits, same breaks, no notes, a quiet room.
2. **Take at least one full exam on a day that resembles test day** (same start time, same breakfast).
3. **Flag and move.** Teach a 60-90 second rule: guess, flag, and return if time remains.
4. **Review within 24 hours,** while your reasoning is still fresh.
5. **Log every miss by cause:** content gap, misread, rushed, trap choice, calculation slip. Fix the *most common cause* first.
6. **Re-attempt misses after a week** without looking at the key.
7. **Don't chase scores early.** Early exams diagnose; late exams predict.
8. **Rotate fatigue.** Some practice should be done at the end of a long day to build stamina.

### Assignment

Generate one section-length practice chapter. Take it timed yourself and log your misses by cause.

## Module 7. Verify, edit, and document

**Goal:** make the manuscript trustworthy and defensible.

### Lesson

**Use the Edit tab** with these passes on every chapter: *Fix bugs*, *Science check*, *Hyperlinks* (only authoritative pages, then check each link yourself), and a clarity pass. Exam Prep chapters are edited as expository course units, with the safety rules re-applied.

**Verification stack (do all four):**
1. *AI self-check:* run the Science pass.
2. *Independent solve:* you (or a tutor) solve every practice question blind and compare to the key.
3. *Expert review:* a qualified reviewer signs off on every fact and key, especially numbers and clinical/biological claims.
4. *Similarity check:* search distinctive phrases from each passage and question on the web and in your own sources to confirm nothing matches a released item or commercial book.

**The Source and License Ledger.** Maintain one row per borrowed item: item, where used, source and creator, URL, license, attribution text, date checked, verifier. The generated Appendix is a template; fill it with real records only.

### Praxis Tips

1. **Treat every `[VERIFY]` flag as a blocker,** not a footnote.
2. **Don't trust citations the AI produced.** Open each URL; confirm the title, author, and license.
3. **Keep a "changes made" log** (the editor returns one) as an audit trail.
4. **Check the answer key last, after all edits,** since edits can invalidate it.
5. **Re-run the similarity check after rewrites.**
6. **Keep AI drafts and human edits** in separate saved projects so you can show your process.

### Assignment

Complete the four-step stack on one chapter and fill in its ledger rows.

## Module 8. Publish and maintain

**Goal:** release a clean, honest product and keep it current.

### Lesson

1. **Front matter:** an independence disclaimer ("not affiliated with or endorsed by the exam owner"), a not-legal/medical-advice note where relevant, and a credits page listing attributions from the ledger.
2. **Pricing and positioning:** compete on original practice volume, explanation quality, and the coaching in the Praxis Tips, not on the exam owner's name.
3. **Export** with the export buttons (EPUB, Word, PDF) and generate a cover that does not use the exam owner's logo or look-alike branding.
4. **Maintenance:** exams change. Re-check the public outline each year, retire outdated lessons, and keep an errata page.

### Praxis Tips

1. **Publish an errata page** and fix reported errors fast; trust compounds.
2. **Version your books** (Edition, Year) and keep the ledger per edition.
3. **Collect learner misses.** The most-missed questions show which lessons need rewriting.
4. **Sell the system, not the secrets.** Tell buyers it is an original course: this is a strength.
5. **Re-run the release checklist** (below) before every edition.

---

## Release checklist

- [ ] No released exam, official practice item, or commercial-book text (including paraphrase) in any chapter
- [ ] Every passage is original or has a ledger row with verified source, license, and attribution
- [ ] No NC-licensed material in a book being sold; SA terms honored
- [ ] No invented citations, URLs, or license claims remain; all `[SOURCE TO VERIFY]` resolved
- [ ] All `[VERIFY]` flags resolved by an expert
- [ ] Every answer key independently solved and matches
- [ ] Similarity check run after the final edit
- [ ] Exam name used only descriptively; independence disclaimer present; no logos or "official" claims
- [ ] Credits page complete
- [ ] Errata process in place

## Quick reference: chapter title prefixes

| Prefix | Output |
|---|---|
| `Strategy:` | Study plan or test-skills unit, 8+ Praxis Tips, action list |
| `Lesson N:` | Concept lesson with worked examples, 8+ Praxis Tips, checkpoint questions |
| `Practice:` | Timed original passages and questions with full explanations and an author-verification note |
| `Appendix:` | Source and License Ledger template, formula sheet, glossary |
