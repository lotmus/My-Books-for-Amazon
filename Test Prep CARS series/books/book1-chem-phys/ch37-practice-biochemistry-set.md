# Practice: Biochemistry Set

**Timing and conditions**

Fourteen original questions on the biochemistry of the section from [Lessons 23 to 25](chapter:34): amino acids and proteins, enzymes, and carbohydrates, lipids and nucleic acids (categories 5C, 5D and 5E). Two passage sets are followed by four independent questions. Allow about 22 minutes, in one sitting, with scratch paper and no notes or calculator. Write your answer letters, with a confidence level (high, medium or low) for each, on scratch paper or in your notebook; an ebook cannot be written in. Check only when you have finished.

This is the last practice set before the full-length exam sections. Treat it as a dress rehearsal: start the clock, hold to the time checks in the last Praxis Tip below, and use the flag-and-move rule on any question that runs past two minutes.

Praxis Tip: In enzyme passages, find Km and Vmax from the data first, before you read the questions. Nearly every question that follows is a statement about one of those two numbers.

Praxis Tip: In purification passages, write the property each method uses (size, charge, or specific binding) beside its name on your scratch paper. The method's name will not help you; the property will.

**Questions**

**Passage 1 (questions 1-5)**

A biochemist measures the initial rate v of an enzyme-catalyzed reaction at several substrate concentrations, with and without a fixed concentration of an inhibitor, I. The total enzyme concentration in each assay is 0.40 μM.

| [S] (μM) | v without I (μM/s) | v with I (μM/s) |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 13.3 | 7.3 |
| 2.5 | 26.7 | 16.0 |
| 5.0 | 40.0 | 26.7 |
| 10 | 53.3 | 40.0 |
| 25 | 66.7 | 57.1 |
| 50 | 72.7 | 66.7 |

1. Based on the data without inhibitor, what is the value of Km?
   A. 2.5 μM
   B. 10 μM
   C. 5.0 μM
   D. 40 μM

2. What is the value of Vmax without inhibitor?
   A. About 40 μM/s
   B. About 73 μM/s
   C. About 160 μM/s
   D. About 80 μM/s

3. The data with inhibitor are best explained by an inhibitor that is
   A. noncompetitive: Km is unchanged and Vmax decreases
   B. competitive: Km increases and Vmax is unchanged
   C. uncompetitive: both Km and Vmax decrease
   D. an activator: Vmax increases

4. If the substrate concentration were increased to a very large value, the rate with the inhibitor present would approach
   A. about 80 μM/s
   B. about 40 μM/s
   C. about 67 μM/s
   D. zero

5. What is the turnover number, k_cat, of the enzyme?
   A. 200 s⁻¹
   B. 32 s⁻¹
   C. 0.005 s⁻¹
   D. 80 s⁻¹

**Passage 2 (questions 6-10)**

A mixture of three proteins is to be separated. Their properties are listed below.

| Protein | Mass (kDa) | Isoelectric point (pI) |
|---|---|---|
| P | 30 | 4.5 |
| Q | 60 | 8.5 |
| R | 120 | 6.5 |

Protein R is a dimer of two identical 60 kDa subunits joined by a disulfide bond.

6. In which order do the proteins elute from a size-exclusion (gel filtration) column?
   A. P, then Q, then R
   B. R, then Q, then P
   C. Q, then R, then P
   D. All three elute together

7. In SDS-PAGE run without any reducing agent, which protein travels farthest?
   A. Q
   B. R
   C. They all travel the same distance
   D. P

8. At pH 7.0, the mixture is applied to an anion-exchange column (positively charged resin). Which proteins bind?
   A. Q only
   B. P only
   C. P and R
   D. None of the proteins

9. In isoelectric focusing across a pH gradient from 3 to 10, which protein comes to rest at the highest pH?
   A. P, at about pH 4.5
   B. Q, at about pH 8.5
   C. R, at about pH 6.5
   D. All stop at pH 7

10. Protein R is treated with SDS, a reducing agent (β-mercaptoethanol) and heat, and then run on an SDS-PAGE gel. Compared with protein Q, protein R will appear
   A. at the same position as a 120 kDa protein
   B. farther than protein P
   C. at the same position as protein Q
   D. not at all, because R is destroyed

**Questions 11-14**

These four questions are independent of the passages.

11. A sample of double-stranded DNA contains 24 percent cytosine. What percentage of the bases is adenine?
   A. 26 percent
   B. 24 percent
   C. 52 percent
   D. 48 percent

12. D-Glucose and D-mannose differ only in the configuration at carbon 2. They are
   A. anomers
   B. enantiomers
   C. structural isomers
   D. epimers

13. An unfavorable reaction A → B has ΔG°′ = +20 kJ/mol. It is coupled to the hydrolysis of ATP (ΔG°′ ≈ −30.5 kJ/mol). The ΔG°′ for the coupled process is
   A. +50.5 kJ/mol
   B. −30.5 kJ/mol
   C. −10.5 kJ/mol
   D. +20 kJ/mol

14. The total concentration of an enzyme in an assay is doubled, with the same substrate and no inhibitor. Compared with the original assay, the Michaelis-Menten parameters become
   A. Vmax unchanged and Km doubled
   B. Vmax doubled and Km unchanged
   C. both Vmax and Km doubled
   D. both unchanged

**Answers and explanations**

1. **C.** Vmax is about 80 μM/s, so half of Vmax is 40 μM/s. The substrate concentration at which v = 40 μM/s is 5.0 μM. That is Km. [5E · Skill 4] (A is the substrate concentration at one third of the maximum rate; B is the substrate concentration at two thirds of the maximum rate; D is half of Vmax, but it is a rate, not a concentration.)
2. **D.** The rate keeps approaching a limit as [S] rises. Using Km = 5.0 μM, v = Vmax [S] / (Km + [S]) gives Vmax = 80 μM/s (for example, at [S] = 50 μM, v = 80 × 50/55 = 72.7). [5E · Skill 4] (A is half of Vmax; B is the highest rate measured, not the limiting value; C doubles the correct value.)
3. **B.** At low [S] the rate is much lower with the inhibitor, but at high [S] it approaches the uninhibited value (66.7 versus 72.7 at 50 μM). That is the signature of a competitive inhibitor: the apparent Km doubles (to about 10 μM) while Vmax is unchanged. [5E · Skill 4] (A at high [S], the rate with inhibitor approaches the uninhibited rate; C both parameters would fall; D the rates with the inhibitor are lower at every substrate concentration.)
4. **A.** A competitive inhibitor does not change Vmax; with enough substrate, the substrate outcompetes the inhibitor and the rate approaches the same Vmax of about 80 μM/s. [5E · Skill 2] (B is half of the uninhibited Vmax; C is the rate measured at 50 μM, not the limit; D a competitive inhibitor can be overcome by excess substrate.)
5. **A.** k_cat = Vmax / [E]total = 80 μM/s / 0.40 μM = 200 s⁻¹. [5E · Skill 2] (B multiplies Vmax by the enzyme concentration; C inverts the ratio; D uses Vmax without dividing by the enzyme concentration.)
6. **B.** Large molecules are excluded from the pores and take the shortest path, so they elute first: R (120 kDa), then Q (60 kDa), then P (30 kDa). [5C · Skill 2] (A reverses the order: small proteins elute last; C does not follow from the masses; D size-exclusion separates by size.)
7. **D.** With SDS, migration depends mainly on mass, and the smallest protein (P, 30 kDa) travels farthest. Without a reducing agent, R's two subunits remain linked, so R behaves as a 120 kDa species. [5C · Skill 2] (A has an intermediate mass; B has the greatest mass; C SDS-PAGE separates by mass.)
8. **C.** A protein is negatively charged when the pH is above its pI. At pH 7.0, P (pI 4.5) and R (pI 6.5) are negative and bind the positive resin; Q (pI 8.5) is positive and flows through. [5C · Skill 2] (A Q is positively charged at pH 7.0, so it is repelled by a positive resin; B R is also negatively charged at pH 7.0; D two of the three are negatively charged.)
9. **B.** In isoelectric focusing a protein migrates until the pH equals its pI, where its net charge is zero. Q has the highest pI (8.5). [5C · Skill 2] (A has the lowest pI; C has an intermediate pI; D proteins stop where pH equals their own pI.)
10. **C.** The reducing agent breaks the disulfide bond, and SDS dissociates the noncovalent contacts, so R separates into two identical 60 kDa subunits, which migrate like Q. [5D · Skill 2] (A this would occur only if the disulfide bond were intact; B R's subunits are 60 kDa each, larger than P; D the proteins are denatured, not destroyed.)
11. **A.** G = C = 24 percent, so G + C = 48 percent. The remaining 52 percent is A + T, split equally: A = T = 26 percent. [5D · Skill 2] (B assumes adenine equals cytosine; C is the combined A + T percentage; D is the combined G + C percentage.)
12. **D.** Diastereomers that differ at exactly one stereocenter other than the anomeric carbon are epimers. [5D · Skill 1] (A differ at the anomeric carbon; B differ at every stereocenter; C differ in the connectivity of the atoms.)
13. **C.** Free-energy changes of coupled reactions add: +20 + (−30.5) = −10.5 kJ/mol, so the coupled process is spontaneous. [5E · Skill 2] (A subtracts the ATP term instead of adding it; B ignores the unfavorable reaction; D ignores the ATP hydrolysis.)
14. **B.** Vmax = k_cat [E]total, so doubling the enzyme doubles Vmax. Km depends on the enzyme and substrate, not on the amount of enzyme. [5E · Skill 2] (A reverses the effects; C Km is a property of the enzyme-substrate pair, not the amount of enzyme; D Vmax is proportional to the total enzyme.)

If you mixed up the direction of the order (who elutes or travels first), write the two contrasting rules next to each other in your notebook and drill them: size-exclusion, big first; SDS-PAGE, small farthest.
