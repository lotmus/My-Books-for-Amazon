# Practice: Mechanics Set

**Timing and conditions**

Fourteen original questions on motion, forces, equilibrium, work and energy (category 4A): two passage sets followed by four independent questions. Allow about 22 minutes (the exam's average is about 1.6 minutes per question). Work in one sitting, with scratch paper and no notes or calculator. Use g = 10 m/s² unless a passage says otherwise. Write your answer letters, with a confidence level (high, medium or low) for each, on scratch paper or in your notebook; an ebook cannot be written in. Check only when you have finished.

This set follows [Lesson 1](chapter:7), [Lesson 2](chapter:8) and [Lesson 3](chapter:9). Take it after you have finished those lessons, and take it cold: no looking back at the lessons until you have finished.

Praxis Tip: Start the clock before you read the first passage, and check it at question 5, question 10 and the end. Running a practice set without a clock is a rehearsal for a different exam.

Praxis Tip: For each passage, spend the first 30 to 60 seconds drawing the situation (a ramp, a table edge) and writing the givens beside it, on your scratch paper. A diagram you drew yourself is worth three you only looked at.

**Questions**

**Passage 1 (questions 1-5)**

A student studies a 2.0 kg cart released from rest at the top of a straight, rough ramp 1.5 m long. She repeats the release at several ramp angles θ above the horizontal and records the cart's acceleration a along the ramp. The friction between cart and ramp is kinetic and has the same coefficient at every angle. (Use g = 9.8 m/s² for this passage.)

| Ramp angle θ | Measured acceleration a (m/s²) |
|---|---|
| 15° | 0.64 |
| 20° | 1.5 |
| 25° | 2.4 |
| 30° | 3.2 |

1. Why does the cart's acceleration increase as the ramp angle increases?
   A. The cart's mass increases with the angle
   B. The component of gravity along the ramp increases while the friction force decreases
   C. The normal force increases with the angle, which pushes the cart down the ramp
   D. Friction becomes zero for all angles above 15°

2. What is the coefficient of kinetic friction, using the 30° measurement?
   A. 0.10
   B. 0.38
   C. 0.58
   D. 0.20

3. The experiment is repeated at 30° with a 4.0 kg cart of the same material. The measured acceleration will be closest to
   A. 3.2 m/s²
   B. 1.6 m/s²
   C. 6.4 m/s²
   D. 0 m/s²

4. At 30°, the cart's speed at the bottom of the 1.5 m ramp is closest to
   A. 1.7 m/s
   B. 4.8 m/s
   C. 3.1 m/s
   D. 9.6 m/s

5. How much mechanical energy is converted to thermal energy by friction as the cart slides the full length of the ramp at 30°?
   A. 9.6 J
   B. 14.7 J
   C. 5.1 J
   D. 24.3 J

**Passage 2 (questions 6-10)**

A spring-loaded launcher fires a 0.020 kg ball horizontally from the edge of a table 1.25 m above the floor. Before release, the spring (spring constant k = 400 N/m) is compressed by 0.050 m. The launcher's mechanism is frictionless, and air resistance may be ignored.

6. How much energy is stored in the compressed spring?
   A. 0.50 J
   B. 1.0 J
   C. 10 J
   D. 20 J

7. The ball's speed as it leaves the launcher is closest to
   A. 5.0 m/s
   B. 14 m/s
   C. 50 m/s
   D. 7.1 m/s

8. How far from the base of the table does the ball land?
   A. 1.8 m
   B. 3.5 m
   C. 7.1 m
   D. 14 m

9. If the spring is compressed by 0.10 m instead of 0.050 m, the horizontal distance from the table at which the ball lands will
   A. stay the same
   B. quadruple
   C. increase by a factor of √2
   D. double

10. The ball's kinetic energy just before it strikes the floor is closest to
   A. 0.75 J
   B. 0.25 J
   C. 0.50 J
   D. 1.25 J

**Questions 11-14**

These four questions are independent of the passages.

11. A uniform plank 2.0 m long weighs 60 N. It is held horizontal by a hinge at its left end and a vertical rope at its right end. The tension in the rope is
   A. 60 N
   B. 15 N
   C. 30 N
   D. 120 N

12. A car rounds a flat curve of radius 50 m at a speed of 10 m/s. The minimum coefficient of static friction needed to keep it from sliding is
   A. 0.02
   B. 0.20
   C. 0.50
   D. 2.0

13. Two forces act on an object: 3.0 N toward the east and 4.0 N toward the north. The magnitude of the net force is
   A. 5.0 N
   B. 7.0 N
   C. 1.0 N
   D. 12 N

14. A 10 kg crate is pushed at constant speed up a frictionless ramp inclined at 30°, over a distance of 4.0 m along the ramp. The work done by the person pushing is closest to
   A. 400 J
   B. 346 J
   C. 0 J
   D. 200 J

**Stop here.** Finish every question and write down your answers (on scratch paper) before you continue to the explanations.

**Answers and explanations**

1. **B.** The down-ramp component of the weight is m g sin θ, which grows with θ. The friction force μ m g cos θ shrinks as cos θ falls. [4A · Skill 2] (A mass does not change with the angle; C the normal force decreases as the ramp steepens; D kinetic friction is present at every angle.)
2. **D.** Newton's second law gives a = g (sin θ − μ cos θ). Then μ = tan θ − a / (g cos θ) = 0.577 − 3.2 / (9.8 × 0.866) = 0.577 − 0.377 = 0.20. [4A · Skill 2] (A halves the correct value; B computes a / (g cos θ) and forgets to subtract it from tan θ; C is tan 30°, the coefficient at which the cart would just barely slide at constant speed, not the one that fits a = 3.2 m/s².)
3. **A.** Both the weight component and the friction force are proportional to the mass, so the mass cancels in a = g (sin θ − μ cos θ). [4A · Skill 2] (B assumes more mass means less acceleration; C assumes more mass means more acceleration; D assumes the cart will not move.)
4. **C.** v² = 2 a L = 2 × 3.2 × 1.5 = 9.6, so v = √9.6 ≈ 3.1 m/s. [4A · Skill 2] (A is √(2L), which leaves out the acceleration; B multiplies a by L without the square root; D reports v² as if it were v.)
5. **C.** Initial potential energy: m g h = 2.0 × 9.8 × (1.5 sin 30°) = 14.7 J. Final kinetic energy: ½ m v² = ½ × 2.0 × 9.6 = 9.6 J. The difference, 5.1 J, is dissipated. [4A · Skill 2] (A is the cart's final kinetic energy; B is the cart's initial potential energy; D adds the kinetic and potential energies.)
6. **A.** Elastic potential energy: ½ k x² = ½ × 400 × (0.050)² = 0.50 J. [4A · Skill 1] (B uses k x² without the factor of one half; C uses ½ k x, which has the wrong dimensions; D uses the spring force k x.)
7. **D.** All the spring energy becomes kinetic energy: ½ m v² = 0.50 J, so v² = 2 × 0.50 / 0.020 = 50 and v = 7.1 m/s. [4A · Skill 2] (A omits the factor of 2 in v = √(2E/m); B doubles the correct speed; C forgets the square root.)
8. **B.** Fall time from h = ½ g t²: t = √(2 × 1.25 / 10) = 0.50 s. The horizontal distance is v t = 7.1 × 0.50 = 3.5 m. [4A · Skill 2] (A uses half of the fall time; C multiplies the speed by one second; D doubles the speed before multiplying.)
9. **D.** The launch speed is proportional to the compression x (v = x √(k/m)), and the fall time is unchanged, so the range doubles. (The stored energy quadruples, but the speed only doubles.) [4A · Skill 2] (A ignores the dependence of the launch speed on the compression; B confuses the stored energy, which quadruples, with the speed; C applies the square root to the wrong quantity.)
10. **A.** Total energy is conserved: 0.50 J from the spring plus m g h = 0.020 × 10 × 1.25 = 0.25 J from the fall gives 0.75 J. [4A · Skill 2] (B includes only the energy gained in the fall; C includes only the spring energy; D reports the table height, 1.25, as if it were an energy.)
11. **C.** Take torques about the hinge. The weight acts at the center (1.0 m from the hinge) and the rope at 2.0 m: T × 2.0 = 60 × 1.0, so T = 30 N. [4A · Skill 2] (A treats the rope as supporting the entire weight; B uses a lever arm of 0.5 m for the weight; D doubles the weight.)
12. **B.** Friction supplies the centripetal force: μ m g = m v² / r, so μ = v² / (g r) = 100 / (10 × 50) = 0.20. [4A · Skill 2] (A uses v / (g r); C inverts the ratio, r / v²; D does not divide by g.)
13. **A.** Perpendicular vectors add by the Pythagorean theorem: √(3.0² + 4.0²) = 5.0 N. [4A · Skill 1] (B adds the magnitudes as if the forces were parallel; C subtracts the magnitudes; D multiplies the magnitudes.)
14. **D.** At constant speed the push balances the weight component along the ramp, so the work equals the gain in potential energy: m g h = 10 × 10 × (4.0 sin 30°) = 200 J. [4A · Skill 2] (A forgets that the vertical rise is only half the ramp length; B applies cos 30° where sin 30° belongs; C argues that constant speed means no work is done.)

**Score and review**

Count your correct answers and compare with your confidence notes. Then log every miss in your error log ([Strategy: The Question Method](chapter:4)) with a cause (content gap, misread, rushed, trap choice, calculation slip), the lesson number, and the category tag shown in brackets. The tags in this set are all 4A; the second part tells you which skill each question was testing.

How to read your result:

- **12 to 14 correct:** you are ready for the physics mixed set. Spend your review time on any misses with high confidence, since those are false beliefs worth finding.
- **9 to 11 correct:** you have the main ideas but are dropping points on setup. Redo the misses cold in three days.
- **8 or fewer:** go back to the lesson that matches each miss: Lesson 1 for Questions 4 and 8, Lesson 2 for Questions 1 to 3 and 11 to 13, and Lesson 3 for Questions 5 to 7, 9, 10 and 14. Then retake this set after a week.

Praxis Tip: Check which kinds of error cost you points. If several misses were "forgot a square root" or "used the wrong trig function," that is a habit to fix with an estimate before every calculation, not a content gap.

Praxis Tip: The questions in Passage 2 share one idea: energy goes in as stored spring energy and comes out as kinetic. If you got one wrong, redo the others before you move on. A pattern across a passage is information.

Praxis Tip: Re-attempt every miss after three days, without looking at the explanation. A question you miss twice is not a mistake any more; it is a topic. Add three flashcards for it.

Praxis Tip: Time check: you should have reached Question 10 by about the 15-minute mark. If you were far behind, practice the flag-and-move rule from [Core Test Skills, Chapter 11] on the next set.
