# Practice: Physics Mixed Set

**Timing and conditions**

Fourteen original questions that mix categories 4B (fluids), 4C (circuits), 4D (light and sound) and 4E (the nucleus): two passage sets followed by four independent questions. Allow about 22 minutes, in one sitting, with scratch paper and no notes or calculator. Use g = 10 m/s² and treat 1 mmHg as 133 Pa unless told otherwise. Write your answer letters, with a confidence level (high, medium or low) for each, on scratch paper or in your notebook; an ebook cannot be written in.

This set follows Lessons [3](chapter:9) to [8](chapter:15), and interleaves them with the mechanics of [Lessons 1 and 2](chapter:7). Mixed sets feel harder than the lessons did. That is the point: you now have to decide which idea applies as well as apply it ([Core Test Skills, Chapter 8]).

Praxis Tip: Before you answer the first question, glance down the page and name the topic of each passage in two words. Naming the topic first tells your brain which toolbox to open.

Praxis Tip: When a question gives you a ratio ("the radius is halved"), try to answer it as a ratio before you reach for the formula. Several questions here can be done with no arithmetic beyond powers of two.

**Questions**

**Passage 1 (questions 1-5)**

A physiology student models a horizontal artery of radius 2.0 mm carrying blood at a speed of 0.30 m/s in the unobstructed region. Plaque narrows a short section of the artery to a radius of 1.0 mm. Treat the blood as an ideal, incompressible fluid with a density of 1,050 kg/m³ for the continuity and Bernoulli parts of the problem.

1. What is the blood speed in the narrowed section?
   A. 0.60 m/s
   B. 0.075 m/s
   C. 1.2 m/s
   D. 2.4 m/s

2. Compared with the pressure in the unobstructed region, the pressure in the narrowed section is
   A. lower by about 7 × 10² Pa
   B. higher by about 7 × 10² Pa
   C. lower by about 8 × 10² Pa
   D. the same, because the artery is horizontal

3. Suppose the narrowed section and an unobstructed section of equal length have the same pressure difference across them and the same blood viscosity. The flow rate through the narrowed section, compared with the unobstructed section, is
   A. 1/2 as large
   B. 1/16 as large
   C. 1/4 as large
   D. 1/8 as large

4. In the circulatory system as a whole, blood moves most slowly in the capillaries. The best explanation is that
   A. capillaries have the highest viscosity
   B. capillaries have the greatest pressure
   C. blood is compressible, so it slows down
   D. the total cross-sectional area of all the capillaries is much larger than that of the aorta

5. The artery runs from the heart to a point 1.3 m lower in a standing person. Because of the weight of the blood column alone, the pressure at the lower point exceeds the pressure at heart level by about
   A. 10 mmHg
   B. 1,000 mmHg
   C. 1 mmHg
   D. 100 mmHg

**Passage 2 (questions 6-10)**

A student connects a 12 V battery (negligible internal resistance) to a 4.0 Ω resistor (R₁) in series with a parallel combination of a 6.0 Ω resistor (R₂) and a 12.0 Ω resistor (R₃).

6. What is the total current supplied by the battery?
   A. 0.55 A
   B. 1.5 A
   C. 3.0 A
   D. 1.0 A

7. What is the current through R₃?
   A. 0.50 A
   B. 1.0 A
   C. 1.5 A
   D. 0.25 A

8. What power is dissipated in R₁?
   A. 6.0 W
   B. 18 W
   C. 9.0 W
   D. 36 W

9. R₃ burns out and becomes an open circuit. The total current supplied by the battery becomes
   A. 1.2 A
   B. 1.5 A
   C. 2.0 A
   D. 0.80 A

10. With all three resistors in place, the potential difference across R₃ is
   A. 12 V
   B. 3.0 V
   C. 8.0 V
   D. 6.0 V

**Questions 11-14**

These four questions are independent of the passages.

11. An object is placed 15 cm from a converging lens of focal length 10 cm. The image is
   A. virtual, upright and 30 cm from the lens
   B. real, inverted and 30 cm from the lens
   C. real, upright and 30 cm from the lens
   D. real, inverted and 6.0 cm from the lens

12. What is the energy of a photon of wavelength 620 nm? (Use E ≈ 1,240 eV·nm / λ.)
   A. 0.50 eV
   B. 1.24 eV
   C. 2.0 eV
   D. 4.0 eV

13. A radioactive sample contains 64 mg of an isotope. After 12 days, 16 mg remains. The half-life is
   A. 3 days
   B. 6 days
   C. 4 days
   D. 12 days

14. The sound from a small speaker is 90 dB at a distance of 1.0 m. Assuming the speaker radiates equally in all directions, the sound level at 10 m is closest to
   A. 80 dB
   B. 60 dB
   C. 9.0 dB
   D. 70 dB

**Stop here.** Finish every question and write down your answers (on scratch paper) before you continue to the explanations.

**Answers and explanations**

1. **C.** By continuity, A₁v₁ = A₂v₂. The area ratio is (2.0/1.0)² = 4, so the speed is 4 × 0.30 = 1.2 m/s. [4B · Skill 2] (A uses the ratio of the radii instead of the ratio of the areas; B inverts the relationship; D multiplies the speed by 8.)
2. **A.** Bernoulli for a horizontal tube: P₁ − P₂ = ½ ρ (v₂² − v₁²) = ½ × 1,050 × (1.44 − 0.09) ≈ 7.1 × 10² Pa (about 5 mmHg). Faster flow means lower pressure. [4B · Skill 2] (B gets the direction of the change wrong; C uses ½ ρ v² of the narrow section only and forgets to subtract the initial term; D ignores the change in speed.)
3. **B.** Poiseuille's law: Q is proportional to r⁴. With the radius halved, Q is (1/2)⁴ = 1/16. [4B · Skill 2] (A treats flow as proportional to radius; C treats flow as proportional to cross-sectional area; D uses the cube of the radius.)
4. **D.** By continuity (A × v = constant for the total flow), a very large total cross-sectional area means a small average speed. [4B · Skill 1] (A viscosity does not explain the lower average speed; B pressure falls along the circuit; C blood is nearly incompressible.)
5. **D.** ΔP = ρ g h = 1,050 × 10 × 1.3 ≈ 1.4 × 10⁴ Pa. Dividing by 133 Pa/mmHg gives about 100 mmHg. [4B · Skill 2] (A is a factor of 10 too small; B is a factor of 10 too large; C is a factor of 100 too small.)
6. **B.** The parallel pair has R = (6.0 × 12.0)/(6.0 + 12.0) = 4.0 Ω. The total resistance is 4.0 + 4.0 = 8.0 Ω, so I = 12/8.0 = 1.5 A. [4C · Skill 2] (A adds all three resistances as if they were in series; C uses only R₁; D uses the wrong equivalent resistance.)
7. **A.** The voltage across the parallel pair is 12 − (1.5 × 4.0) = 6.0 V. The current in R₃ is 6.0/12.0 = 0.50 A. (R₂ carries 6.0/6.0 = 1.0 A, and the two add to 1.5 A.) [4C · Skill 2] (B is the current through R₂; C is the total current; D is half of the correct current.)
8. **C.** The whole current flows through R₁: P = I² R = (1.5)² × 4.0 = 9.0 W. [4C · Skill 2] (A multiplies the current by 4.0 without squaring it, then adjusts; B doubles the correct value; D uses the full battery voltage across R₁.)
9. **A.** With R₃ gone, only R₂ remains in the parallel section. The total resistance is 4.0 + 6.0 = 10 Ω, so I = 12/10 = 1.2 A: removing a parallel path raises the resistance and lowers the current. [4C · Skill 2] (B assumes the removal changes nothing; C assumes removing a resistor increases the current; D uses the wrong resistance.)
10. **D.** R₁ drops 1.5 × 4.0 = 6.0 V, leaving 12 − 6.0 = 6.0 V across the parallel pair, and therefore across R₃. (R₁ also has 6.0 V; the two voltages add to the 12 V battery voltage.) [4C · Skill 2] (A is the full battery voltage; B is half of the correct value; C matches no voltage in this circuit, and equals the total resistance in ohms.)
11. **B.** 1/q = 1/f − 1/p = 1/10 − 1/15 = 1/30, so q = +30 cm (real). The magnification is −q/p = −2, so the image is inverted and twice the size. [4D · Skill 2] (A describes an object inside the focal length; C real images from a single converging lens are inverted; D uses the product over the sum of p and f.)
12. **C.** E = 1,240 / 620 = 2.0 eV. [4D · Skill 1] (A divides by 4; B uses a wavelength of 1,000 nm; D doubles the correct value.)
13. **B.** 64 mg → 16 mg is a factor of 4 = 2², so two half-lives have passed in 12 days: t½ = 6 days. [4E · Skill 2] (A treats the factor of 4 as four half-lives; C counts three half-lives; D assumes one half-life has passed.)
14. **D.** Intensity falls as 1/r², so a tenfold distance reduces the intensity by a factor of 100, which is 20 dB: 90 − 20 = 70 dB. [4D · Skill 2] (A subtracts 10 dB for the factor of 10 in distance; B subtracts 30 dB; C divides by 10.)

**Score and review**

Count your correct answers and compare with your confidence notes. Log every miss in your error log ([Strategy: The Question Method](chapter:4)) with a cause, the lesson number, and the category tag shown in brackets.

How to read your result:

- **12 to 14 correct:** strong. Take a full-length section soon and keep your timing.
- **9 to 11 correct:** you know the tools but lose points on setup or units. Redo the misses cold in three days.
- **8 or fewer:** return to the lessons that match your misses: Lesson 4 for Questions 1 to 5, Lesson 5 for Questions 6 to 10, Lesson 7 for Questions 11 and 12, Lesson 8 for Question 13, and Lesson 6 for Question 14. Retake the set after a week.

Praxis Tip: Passage 1 and Passage 2 are two applications of one idea: a flow through a network with a driving difference (pressure or voltage) and resistances. If you saw the analogy ([Lesson 5](chapter:12)), you could have checked your circuit answers against your fluid intuition. Look for that kind of cross-check next time.

Praxis Tip: Questions 12 to 14 use only a line or two of arithmetic. Did you do them in under a minute each? If not, practice estimation shortcuts: 1,240 divided by a round number, a factor of two per half-life, 10 dB per factor of ten.

Praxis Tip: If you missed a question because you used the wrong lesson's tool (for example, applying the Doppler formula to a decibel question), write the pair in your error log: "mistook X for Y." Confusions between topics are the main thing a mixed set exists to reveal.

Praxis Tip: Time check: you should have finished Passage 1 by roughly the 9-minute mark and Passage 2 by roughly the 17-minute mark. Note where you were at each point so that you know where your time goes.
