# Practice: Full-Length Exam 2, Chemical and Physical Section, Part B

**Timing and conditions**

This is the second half of Full-Length Exam 2: thirty original questions in the style of the Chemical and Physical Foundations section, made up of five passage sets (22 questions) followed by eight independent questions. Allow 48 minutes, which is about 1.6 minutes per question. Take it in one sitting, with scratch paper, no notes and no calculator. You may consult a periodic table from the Appendix "Formula, Constant and Fact Sheet", but nothing else on it; the real exam supplies a periodic table, and the constants list is for review only. Use g = 10 m/s² unless a passage says otherwise. Write your answer letters, with a confidence level (high, medium or low) for each, on scratch paper or in your notebook; an ebook cannot be written in. Check your answers only when you have finished.

Praxis Tip: Start the clock before you read the first passage. A practice exam without a clock is a rehearsal for a different exam, and a pleasant one.

Praxis Tip: Do the independent questions (23 to 30) when you reach them, but if you are running late at Question 22, skip ahead and take the quick ones first. They are worth exactly as much as the passage questions.

Praxis Tip: On each passage, spend 30 seconds on the table or equation, not on the prose. Circle the units on your scratch paper and write the one relation you expect to use. Then read the questions.

**Answers and explanations**

1. **C.** By Hess's law the enthalpy changes of consecutive steps add: −90 + 30 = −60 kJ/mol. [5E · Skill 2] (A subtracts the second step instead of adding it; B reverses the sign of the overall result; D counts only the first step.)
2. **D.** Overall ΔH = −60 kJ/mol and ΔS = −200 + 100 = −100 J/mol·K = −0.100 kJ/mol·K. ΔG = 0 when T = ΔH/ΔS = (−60)/(−0.100) = 600 K. Because ΔS is negative, the reaction is spontaneous only below this temperature. [5E · Skill 2] (A pairs the overall ΔH with the first step's ΔS alone (−60 / −0.200); B pairs the first step's ΔH with the overall ΔS; C mixes kilojoules with joules.)
3. **C.** Since ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, the slope is −ΔS. From the table, (−20 − (−30)) / 100 K = +0.10 kJ/(mol·K), which agrees with ΔS = −0.10 kJ/(mol·K). The intercept, not the slope, equals ΔH (−60 kJ/mol). [5E · Skill 4] (A has the wrong sign for the slope; B ignores that ΔS itself is negative; D reports the intercept as the slope.)
4. **B.** The Pi and H₂O cancel when the two reactions are added, so ΔG°′ values add: +14 + (−30) = −16 kJ/mol. The coupled process is favorable even though reaction 1 alone is not. [5E · Skill 2] (A reverses the sign of the ATP reaction and then adds; C adds the magnitudes of the two values; D reverses the sign of the sum.)
5. **D.** For a uniform field, E = V/d = 1000 V / 0.020 m = 5.0 × 10⁴ V/m. [4C · Skill 2] (A multiplies the voltage by the separation; B uses the separation in centimeters but reports V/m; C treats the potential difference as the field.)
6. **A.** Both ions have charge +e and fall through the same potential difference, so each gains qV of kinetic energy. Since KE = ½mv², the heavier Q⁺ has the lower speed. [4C · Skill 1] (B ignores the role of mass in the speed; C assumes that a heavier ion gains more energy; D assumes kinetic energy scales with mass alone.)
7. **A.** Since r = (1/B)√(2mV/q), d is proportional to √m. The distance for R⁺ is twice that for P⁺, so its mass is 2² = 4 times as large: 4 × 20 = 80 u. Check with Q⁺: √(45/20) = 1.5, and 1.5 × 0.40 = 0.60 m. [4C · Skill 4] (B assumes d is proportional to mass; C interpolates linearly between the table values; D compares the diameter 0.80 m with P⁺'s radius of 0.20 m.)
8. **B.** The radius r = mv/(qB) is inversely proportional to B. Doubling B halves r and halves d, from 0.40 m to 0.20 m. The speed is set by the accelerating voltage, so it does not change. [4C · Skill 2] (A assumes the radius grows with the field; C divides by √2, as if r depended on the square root of B; D assumes the magnetic field does not affect the radius.)
9. **D.** The impact distance depends on √(m/q) at fixed V and B. A mass can be assigned from d only if the charge is known, here +e for every ion. The speeds differ by design, and the voltage must be held constant. [4C · Skill 3] (A speed depends on mass, so it differs between ions; B the identity of the element is irrelevant to the path; C the method requires the same voltage for all ions.)
10. **A.** The amine is basic and is protonated by HCl to form a water-soluble ammonium salt. Benzoic acid is not ionized in strong acid and stays in the ether, as does naphthalene. Sodium benzoate forms only with NaHCO₃. [5C · Skill 1] (B assumes like dissolves like without checking for ions; C requires a base, which has not yet been added; D a neutral hydrocarbon does not react with HCl.)
11. **C.** Rf = distance moved by the spot / distance moved by the solvent front = 3.2 / 8.0 = 0.40. [5C · Skill 4] (A divides by the naphthalene spot distance instead of the solvent front; B inverts the ratio; D reports the distance, which is not dimensionless.)
12. **B.** Silica gel is polar. The carboxylic acid and the amine hydrogen-bond to it and move slowly (Rf 0.20 and 0.40). Naphthalene has no polar groups, adsorbs weakly, and is carried furthest by the eluent (Rf 0.90). [5C · Skill 2] (A reverses the polarity of the compound; C confuses strong retention with fast movement; D misidentifies the stationary phase.)
13. **A.** Spotting the sample and the standard together (co-spotting) distinguishes identical compounds, which stay as one spot, from different compounds that happen to have the same Rf, which would often separate or broaden. [5C · Skill 3] (B shows reproducibility but not identity; C does not distinguish compounds with similar behavior; D has no bearing on identity.)
14. **C.** H₂S, H₂Se and H₂Te are similar in shape. Down the group, the number of electrons and the polarizability increase, so London dispersion forces grow and the boiling point rises. The intramolecular covalent bonds are not broken on boiling. [5B · Skill 2] (A hydrogen bonding is weak for larger, less electronegative atoms; B covalent bonds weaken down the group, and bond strength is not what is overcome in boiling; D a smaller electronegativity difference gives a smaller dipole.)
15. **C.** The two molecules have almost the same size and mass, so dispersion forces are similar. NH₃ has N–H bonds and a lone pair on N, so it forms hydrogen bonds. CH₄ is nonpolar and has only dispersion forces. [5B · Skill 4] (A confuses intramolecular bonds with intermolecular forces; B the molar masses differ by only 1 g/mol; D CH₄ is nonpolar.)
16. **B.** Down a group, atoms gain shells, so radius and polarizability rise. The valence electrons are farther from the nucleus and more shielded, so electronegativity (and ionization energy) decreases. [4E · Skill 1] (A increases as shells are added; C increases down the group; D increases with size.)
17. **A.** Ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH) and dimethyl ether (CH₃OCH₃) are isomers with the same molar mass and similar size. Only ethanol has an O–H group and can hydrogen-bond, so a much higher boiling point for ethanol isolates the effect of hydrogen bonding. [5B · Skill 3] (B changes mass and bonding at once; C involves neither an O–H group nor an isomer; D tests pressure, not the type of intermolecular force.)
18. **B.** E = 13.6 (1/4 − 1/9) = 13.6 × 5/36 ≈ 1.89 eV. Then λ = 1240 / 1.89 ≈ 656 nm, a red line in the visible region. [4E · Skill 2] (A is the n = 4 to n = 2 transition; C is the n = 2 to n = 1 transition; D is the n = 4 to n = 3 transition.)
19. **A.** The data are linear: A/c is constant. ε = A/(bc) = 0.15 / (1.0 cm × 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ M) = 1.5 × 10⁴ M⁻¹cm⁻¹. [4E · Skill 4] (B leaves the concentration in micromolar; C treats 10 μM as 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ M; D inverts the ratio of concentration to absorbance.)
20. **B.** A = −log T, so T = 10^(−0.30). Since log 2 ≈ 0.30, 10^(−0.30) ≈ 1/2, and 50% of the light is transmitted. [4E · Skill 2] (A sets the transmittance equal to the absorbance; C subtracts the absorbance from one; D uses e raised to −A instead of 10 raised to −A.)
21. **D.** The photon energy is 1240/400 = 3.1 eV. KEmax = hf − φ = 3.1 − 2.1 = 1.0 eV. [4E · Skill 2] (A is the photon energy, with no work function subtracted; B is the work function; C adds the work function to the photon energy.)
22. **C.** Light of 700 nm has a photon energy of about 1.8 eV, below the 2.1 eV work function. If no electrons appear however intense the beam, emission requires a minimum frequency, which is the particle (photon) picture. [4E · Skill 3] (A shows only that current grows with intensity above threshold; B tests temperature, not photon frequency; D varies intensity only, above threshold.)
23. **B.** The average speed for uniform deceleration is (30 + 10)/2 = 20 m/s, so t = 400 / 20 = 20 s. Check: a = (10² − 30²)/(2 × 400) = −1 m/s², and t = (10 − 30)/(−1) = 20 s. [4A · Skill 2] (A divides the distance by the initial speed; C divides the distance by the final speed; D divides the speed change by 2 and treats that as a time.)
24. **B.** At rest, the net force along the incline is zero, so the static friction force equals the component of weight along the incline: mg sin 30° = 5.0 × 10 × 0.50 = 25 N. [4A · Skill 2] (A is the normal force, mg cos θ; C comes from using a wrong angle (for example, 50 N × sin 45° ≈ 35 N), and static friction is not μN unless the block is about to slip; D is the whole weight, mg.)
25. **A.** Work against gravity is mgh = 60 × 10 × 4.0 = 2400 J. Power is work divided by time: 2400 / 8.0 = 300 W. [4A · Skill 2] (B reports the work, 2400 J, as the power; C omits g from the weight; D divides the weight by time and ignores the height.)
26. **D.** The pressure from the water is ρgh = 1000 × 10 × 20 = 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa. The absolute pressure adds the atmosphere: 1.0 × 10⁵ + 2.0 × 10⁵ = 3.0 × 10⁵ Pa. [4B · Skill 2] (A gives only the pressure due to the water; B gives only the atmospheric pressure; C makes a power-of-ten error.)
27. **A.** Because x is small compared with 0.10, Ka = x²/0.10, so x² = 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ and [H⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻³ M. The pH is 3.0. The ionization is 1%, which justifies the approximation. [5A · Skill 2] (B treats the acid as completely ionized; C sets pH equal to pKa; D uses Ka × C as [H⁺] without the square root.)
28. **C.** Experiments 1 and 2: doubling [A] doubles the rate, so the reaction is first order in A. Experiments 2 and 3: doubling [B] increases the rate by a factor of 4, so it is second order in B. Rate = k[A][B]², with k = 2.0 M⁻²s⁻¹. [5E · Skill 4] (A treats B as first order, but doubling [B] raised the rate fourfold; B assigns the orders to the wrong reactants; D assumes the same order for A as for B.)
29. **D.** Oxidation requires a hydrogen on the carbinol carbon. 2-Methyl-2-propanol is tertiary and has none, so it is not oxidized. Primary alcohols go to carboxylic acids with dichromate, and secondary alcohols go to ketones. [5D · Skill 1] (A is oxidized to butanoic acid; B is oxidized to 2-butanone; C is oxidized to 2-methylpropanoic acid.)
30. **D.** A competitive inhibitor raises the apparent Km and leaves Vmax unchanged. This is seen only by varying [S] with and without the inhibitor, typically in a double-reciprocal plot in which the lines cross on the 1/v axis. At very high [S] the inhibitor is outcompeted. [5E · Skill 3] (A competitive inhibition is overcome at saturating substrate, so no effect would be seen; B has no inhibitor, so it cannot show inhibition; C does not vary substrate, which is what separates inhibition types.)
