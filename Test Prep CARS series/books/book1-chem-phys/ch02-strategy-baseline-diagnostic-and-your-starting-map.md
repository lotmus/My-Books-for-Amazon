# Strategy: Baseline Diagnostic and Your Starting Map

**What you will be able to do**

- Take a baseline under honest conditions and record it usefully.
- Use the 16-question original diagnostic in this chapter as a coverage check across the lessons.
- Turn the results into a ranked starting map, and know what a small diagnostic cannot tell you.

**Why it matters**

You are about to spend somewhere around 200 hours on this section. Before you choose how, it helps to know where you stand. [Core Test Skills, Chapter 4] explains how to run a diagnostic and why the total score is the least informative number on the page. This chapter applies it: here is a short original diagnostic for this section, and a procedure for reading it.

Praxis Tip: Take the diagnostic cold. Studying first defeats its purpose, and your pride, if consulted, will recommend studying first.

**Two kinds of baseline**

*1. Official practice material.* The most representative practice items are the exam owner's own, and the owner sells official practice exams and question banks and publishes some free materials. They are copyrighted, so use them for your own practice only: do not copy, retype or share them. Check the exam owner's student site for what is currently free and what is sold. Save the official full-length exams for your scheduled simulations ([Core Test Skills, Chapter 25]); they are scarce, and you can take each one fresh only once.

*2. This book's original diagnostic.* The 16 questions below cover the lessons in the book, one or two per lesson area. They are original. They are a coverage check, not a miniature exam. Use them to decide where to begin.

Praxis Tip: Do not use leaked or "recalled" exam questions as a baseline or for any other purpose. They are unreliable, they can violate the exam owner's rules, and they can put your score at risk.

**Taking the diagnostic**

- **Time:** 24 minutes (16 questions at about 1.5 minutes each), in one sitting.
- **Tools:** scratch paper and a periodic table. No calculator, no notes, no phone. Use g = 10 m/s² wherever a question calls for it.
- **Note your confidence** (high, medium or low) for each answer on your answer sheet.
- **Do not look anything up** until you have finished.

Praxis Tip: If a question looks unfamiliar, do not freeze. Eliminate what you can, guess, and mark "low." That is real test behavior, and it makes your results more honest.

**The diagnostic**

1. A ball is thrown straight up at 20 m/s. Ignoring air resistance, how long until it returns to its starting height?
 A. 2 s  B. 4 s  C. 6 s  D. 8 s

2. A 10 kg block is pulled across a horizontal surface by a 50 N horizontal force. The coefficient of kinetic friction is 0.20. What is the block's acceleration?
 A. 3 m/s²  B. 4 m/s²  C. 5 m/s²  D. 6 m/s²

3. A 2 kg object is released from rest at a height of 5 m. Ignoring air resistance, its speed just before it hits the ground is closest to
 A. 5 m/s  B. 7 m/s  C. 10 m/s  D. 14 m/s

4. The gauge pressure at a depth of 3 m in fresh water (density 1,000 kg/m³) is closest to
 A. 3 kPa  B. 30 kPa  C. 300 kPa  D. 3,000 kPa

5. A 6 Ω resistor and a 3 Ω resistor are connected in parallel across a 12 V battery. What current does the battery supply?
 A. 1.3 A  B. 2.7 A  C. 4 A  D. 6 A

6. A sound wave in air has a frequency of 440 Hz, and the speed of sound is 340 m/s. Its wavelength is closest to
 A. 0.77 m  B. 1.3 m  C. 150 m  D. 1.5 × 10⁵ m

7. A converging lens has a focal length of 20 cm. An object is placed 30 cm from the lens. Where is the image?
 A. 12 cm from the lens  B. 30 cm from the lens  C. 50 cm from the lens  D. 60 cm from the lens

8. A radioactive isotope has a half-life of 8 days. What fraction of an initial sample remains after 24 days?
 A. 1/16  B. 1/8  C. 1/4  D. 1/2

9. Which of the following elements has the highest first ionization energy?
 A. Al  B. Mg  C. Na  D. Cl

10. Hydrogen and oxygen react according to 2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O. If 4 mol of H₂ react with 3 mol of O₂, what is the maximum mass of water that can form? (Molar mass of H₂O = 18 g/mol.)
 A. 36 g  B. 54 g  C. 72 g  D. 108 g

11. A sample of ideal gas occupies 10 L at 300 K and 1 atm. It is heated to 600 K at constant pressure. Its new volume is
 A. 5 L  B. 10 L  C. 20 L  D. 40 L

12. A reaction has ΔH = −40 kJ/mol and ΔS = −100 J/(mol·K). What is ΔG at 300 K?
 A. −10 kJ/mol  B. +10 kJ/mol  C. −70 kJ/mol  D. +70 kJ/mol

13. A buffer has a pKa of 6.0 and a ratio [A⁻]/[HA] of 100. What is its pH?
 A. 6  B. 8  C. 10  D. 12

14. Which statement about a galvanic (voltaic) cell is correct?
 A. Electrons flow through the external wire from the anode to the cathode.
 B. Reduction occurs at the anode.
 C. Electrons travel through the salt bridge from cathode to anode.
 D. A spontaneous cell has a negative cell potential.

15. Which of the following compounds can exist as a pair of enantiomers?
 A. CCl₄  B. CH₂Cl₂  C. CHFClBr  D. CH₃CH₂OH

16. An enzyme follows Michaelis-Menten kinetics with Km = 2 mM and Vmax = 100 µmol/min. What is the reaction rate at [S] = 2 mM?
 A. 20 µmol/min  B. 25 µmol/min  C. 40 µmol/min  D. 50 µmol/min

**Answers, explanations and where to study**

1. **B.** Time up = v/g = 2 s, so total time = 2 × 2 = 4 s. (A is only the time to the top.) *Lesson 1.*
2. **A.** Friction = 0.20 × (10 × 10) = 20 N. Net force = 50 − 20 = 30 N. a = 30/10 = 3 m/s². (C forgets friction.) *Lesson 2.*
3. **C.** Energy: m g h = ½ m v², so v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 10 × 5) = √100 = 10 m/s. (B is √(gh), missing the factor of 2.) *Lesson 3.*
4. **B.** P = ρ g h = 1,000 × 10 × 3 = 30,000 Pa = 30 kPa. *Lesson 4.*
5. **D.** R_parallel = (6 × 3)/(6 + 3) = 2 Ω, so I = 12/2 = 6 A. (A is the series current, 12/9. C uses only the 3 Ω resistor.) *Lesson 5.*
6. **A.** λ = v/f = 340/440 ≈ 0.77 m. (D multiplies instead of dividing.) *Lesson 6.*
7. **D.** 1/q = 1/f − 1/p = 1/20 − 1/30 = 1/60, so q = 60 cm (a real image). (A is pf/(p+f); C is p + f.) *Lesson 7.*
8. **B.** 24 days is three half-lives: (1/2)³ = 1/8. *Lesson 8.*
9. **D.** First ionization energy rises generally across a period, so chlorine is highest of these. *Lesson 9.*
10. **C.** H₂ needs half as many moles of O₂ (2 mol of the 3 mol available), so H₂ is limiting. 4 mol H₂ gives 4 mol H₂O = 72 g. (D assumes O₂ is limiting: 6 mol H₂O.) *Lesson 10.*
11. **C.** At constant pressure V/T is constant, so V doubles with T: 20 L. *Lesson 12.*
12. **A.** ΔG = ΔH − TΔS = −40,000 − (300)(−100) = −40,000 + 30,000 = −10,000 J/mol = −10 kJ/mol. (C is a sign slip on TΔS, giving −40 − 30; forgetting to convert J to kJ would give an absurdly large number.) *Lesson 14.*
13. **B.** pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]) = 6 + log 100 = 6 + 2 = 8. *Lesson 16.*
14. **A.** Oxidation occurs at the anode, reduction at the cathode, and electrons flow through the wire from anode to cathode. (B reverses the electrodes. C: ions, not electrons, move through the salt bridge. D: a spontaneous cell has a positive potential.) *Lesson 17.*
15. **C.** A carbon with four different substituents (H, F, Cl, Br) is a stereocenter. The others have identical substituents or no stereocenter. *Lesson 18.*
16. **D.** v = Vmax [S]/(Km + [S]) = 100 × 2/(2 + 2) = 50 µmol/min; at [S] = Km, the rate is half of Vmax. *Lesson 24.*

**Worked Example: Reading a diagnostic**

An invented learner answers 16 questions. She gets Questions 1, 3, 4, 9, 10, 11, 13, 15 and 16 right, and misses 2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 12 and 14. Her confidence marks: she was high on 2, 5 and 12 (all wrong) and low on 7 and 8 (both wrong), medium on the rest.

*Count by group.*

| Group | Questions | Correct | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physics (1-8) | 8 | 3 (Q1, Q3, Q4) | 38% |
| General chemistry (9-14) | 6 | 4 (Q9, Q10, Q11, Q13) | 67% |
| Organic (15) | 1 | 1 | 100% |
| Biochemistry (16) | 1 | 1 | 100% |
| Total | 16 | 9 | 56% |

(Check: 3 + 4 + 1 + 1 = 9 of 16; 9/16 = 56%.)

*Read it.* Physics is the weak area (3 of 8). Three misses came with *high* confidence (Q2, Q5, Q12): these are false beliefs worth finding first. The two low-confidence misses (Q7, Q8) are honest gaps in optics and nuclear decay. With one question per topic, she should not conclude that organic is fine; she should conclude that her single organic question happened to go well.

*Starting map.* Priority 1: Lessons 2, 5 and 14 (high-confidence misses). Priority 2: Lessons 6, 7, 8 and 17 (the remaining misses, all medium or low confidence). Priority 3: everything else, on the normal schedule.

*Skill used:* separating weak from unknown, and ranking by confidence.

Praxis Tip: A high-confidence miss on a single question is a flag, not a finding. Go to that lesson, work its checkpoint questions, and see whether the error repeats.

**Making your starting map**

1. List each miss and each low-confidence right answer by lesson number in your notebook.
2. Put lessons with high-confidence misses first.
3. Put lessons with low-confidence misses or misses next.
4. Keep the remaining lessons on the normal schedule, but still take their checkpoint questions: one diagnostic question is thin evidence.
5. Write the map on one page and date it.

Then, as you finish each lesson, update the page. Your map should change over time; a map that never changes means you are not learning or not checking.

**What a baseline cannot tell you**

This diagnostic has 16 questions and covers 16 topics with one question each, so it samples thinly. It was designed to point at lessons, not to predict a score. A real baseline of that kind needs a full-length official exam ([Core Test Skills, Chapter 25]). Treat anything you learn here as a hypothesis.

**More Praxis Tips**

Praxis Tip: Write "why I chose it" next to each answer immediately. It is the cheapest piece of evidence in this whole chapter.

Praxis Tip: Take the diagnostic again after completing Lessons 1-8 using a fresh set of questions from the practice sets. The comparison between the two is your first trend line.

Praxis Tip: If you scored very high, do not skip the lessons. Skim for gaps, and spend your extra time on timed practice and mixed sets.

Praxis Tip: If you scored very low, do not panic. This section covers a lot, and a first look is meant to show how much is yet to learn. That is why you are here.

**Common mistakes**

- Studying before the baseline.
- Reading a 16-question result as a score prediction.
- Skipping the confidence marks.
- Using leaked questions.
- Taking an official full-length exam too early and using up one of the scarce simulations.

**Do this today**

1. Take the diagnostic under timed conditions.
2. Log every miss with a cause code ([Core Test Skills, Chapter 9]).
3. Make the one-page starting map.
4. Schedule the first official practice exam for a date a few weeks into your plan.

**Recap**

A baseline is a measurement. Use the original diagnostic here to point you at lessons, use official practice material for the scarce full-length simulations, and let high-confidence misses go first.
