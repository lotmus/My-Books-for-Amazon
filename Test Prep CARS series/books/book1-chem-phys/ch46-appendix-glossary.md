# Appendix: Glossary

This glossary collects the terms printed in bold through the lessons and strategy chapters of this book. Each entry gives a short definition and a pointer to the lesson where the idea is developed. Entries from the strategy chapters point to the chapter by number. Use it for quick review, and then return to the lesson for the reasoning behind the term.

**A**

**Absolute pressure** — The total pressure at a point in a fluid, equal to gauge pressure plus atmospheric pressure. (Lesson 4)

**Acceleration** — The rate of change of velocity, a vector; it is nonzero whenever speed or direction changes. (Lesson 1)

**Acetal** — A carbon bonded to two OR groups, formed when a hemiacetal reacts with a second alcohol under acid catalysis; it is stable to base and is used to protect carbonyls. (Lesson 19)

**Acid derivative** — A compound that can be converted to a carboxylic acid by hydrolysis, such as an acyl chloride, anhydride, ester or amide. (Lesson 20)

**Activation energy** — The minimum energy colliding molecules need for a reaction to occur; it is the energy gap between the reactants and the transition state. (Lesson 15)

**Active site** — The region of an enzyme, shaped by a few residues, where the substrate binds and the chemical step occurs. (Lesson 24)

**Activity** — The number of nuclear decays per unit time in a sample, proportional to the number of radioactive nuclei present. (Lesson 8)

**Adiabatic** — Describing a process in which no heat is exchanged with the surroundings (q = 0), so any change in internal energy equals the work done. (Lesson 14)

**Affinity chromatography** — A separation that uses an immobilized ligand to bind one specific protein, which is then released by adding free ligand or changing conditions. (Lesson 22)

**Aldol condensation** — An aldol addition followed by loss of water, giving an α,β-unsaturated carbonyl compound, often driven by heat. (Lesson 19)

**Aldol reaction** — The enolate of one aldehyde or ketone adds to the carbonyl of another to give a β-hydroxy carbonyl compound. (Lesson 19)

**Aldose** — A monosaccharide whose carbonyl group is an aldehyde, located at carbon 1. (Lesson 25)

**Allosteric regulation** — Control of an enzyme or protein by a molecule binding at a site other than the active site, changing its shape and activity. (Lesson 24)

**Alpha carbon** — The carbon adjacent to a carbonyl group, whose hydrogens are weakly acidic. (Lesson 19)

**Alpha decay** — Emission of a helium-4 nucleus, lowering the atomic number by 2 and the mass number by 4. (Lesson 8)

**Alpha helix** — A secondary structure in which the backbone coils, stabilized by hydrogen bonds between each carbonyl oxygen and the amide hydrogen four residues ahead. (Lesson 23)

**Amino acid** — A molecule with a central α carbon bearing an amino group, a carboxyl group, a hydrogen and a variable side chain. (Lesson 23)

**Amphipathic** — Having both a polar (hydrophilic) region and a nonpolar (hydrophobic) region, as phospholipids and fatty acids do. (Lesson 25)

**Amphiprotic** — Able to act as either a proton donor or a proton acceptor, as water and bicarbonate can. (Lesson 16)

**Anion** — A negatively charged ion formed by gaining electrons. (Lesson 9)

**Anode** — The electrode at which oxidation occurs, in both galvanic and electrolytic cells. (Lesson 17)

**Anomer** — One of two cyclic sugar stereoisomers that differ only in configuration at the anomeric carbon. (Lesson 25)

**Anomeric carbon** — The carbon of a cyclic sugar that was the carbonyl carbon in the open chain; it is bonded to two oxygens. (Lesson 25)

**Anti conformation** — A staggered arrangement in which the two largest substituents on adjacent carbons are 180 degrees apart; it is the most stable form of butane. (Lesson 18)

**Antiaromatic** — Describing a planar, fully conjugated ring with 4n pi electrons, which is destabilized relative to a nonaromatic analog. (Lesson 18)

**Antinode** — A point on a standing wave where the amplitude is a maximum. (Lesson 6)

**Antiparallel** — Describing two strands that run in opposite directions, as the 5' to 3' orientations of the two DNA strands do. (Lesson 25)

**Apoenzyme** — The protein part of an enzyme that lacks its required cofactor and is therefore inactive. (Lesson 24)

**Aromatic** — Describing a planar, cyclic, fully conjugated ring with 4n + 2 pi electrons, which is especially stable. (Lesson 18)

**Arrhenius equation** — The relation k = Ae^(-Ea/RT), showing that the rate constant rises as temperature increases or activation energy falls. (Lesson 15)

**Atomic number** — The number of protons in a nucleus (Z), which defines the element. (Lesson 8)

**ATP** — The nucleotide whose hydrolysis releases free energy to drive otherwise unfavorable reactions through coupling. (Lesson 24)

**Aufbau principle** — Electrons fill orbitals from lowest energy to highest, following the building-up order of subshells. (Lesson 9)

**Avogadro's number** — The number of particles in one mole, 6.02 × 10²³. (Lesson 10)

**Axial** — Describing a substituent on a chair cyclohexane that points roughly parallel to the ring axis, up or down; axial positions are more crowded than equatorial. (Lesson 18)

**Azeotrope** — A liquid mixture that boils at a constant composition, so it cannot be separated further by simple distillation. (Lesson 22)

Praxis Tip: When a question gives a rate, a concentration or a quantity of a sample and asks about change, identify first whether the quantity is a state function or a path-dependent one. State functions let you skip the route and compare only the endpoints.

**B**

**Balanced equation** — A chemical equation with equal numbers of each kind of atom, and equal total charge, on both sides. (Lesson 10)

**Base pairing** — The hydrogen bonding of A with T (or U in RNA) by two bonds and G with C by three. (Lesson 25)

**Base peak** — The tallest peak in a mass spectrum, assigned an intensity of 100 percent. (Lesson 21)

**Baseline diagnostic** — An untimed or timed starting test, taken without outside help, that shows current strengths and weaknesses. (Strategy Chapter 2)

**Beer-Lambert law** — Absorbance equals molar absorptivity times path length times concentration (A = epsilon l c), so absorbance is linear in concentration. (Lesson 21)

**Bernoulli's equation** — For ideal fluid flow, P + 1/2 rho v^2 + rho g h is constant along a streamline, expressing conservation of energy. (Lesson 4)

**Beta-lactam** — A four-membered cyclic amide that is strained and reactive; it forms the core of penicillin-type antibiotics. (Lesson 20)

**Beta-pleated sheet** — A secondary structure of extended strands linked side to side by backbone hydrogen bonds. (Lesson 23)

**Binding energy** — The energy released when a nucleus forms from its nucleons, equal to the mass defect times c^2. (Lesson 8)

**Bohr model** — A model of hydrogen with electrons in fixed circular orbits of quantized energy; light is emitted or absorbed when an electron changes levels. (Lesson 9)

**Bond dissociation energy** — The energy needed to break one mole of a particular bond in the gas phase; breaking bonds always costs energy. (Lesson 14)

**Bond order** — The number of bonding electron pairs between two atoms; higher bond order means a shorter, stronger bond. (Lesson 11)

**Brønsted-Lowry acid** — A proton donor; a Brønsted-Lowry base is a proton acceptor. (Lesson 16)

**Buffer** — A solution of a weak acid and its conjugate base (or a weak base and its conjugate acid) that resists changes in pH on addition of small amounts of acid or base. (Lesson 16)

**Buoyancy** — The upward force a fluid exerts on a submerged object, equal to the weight of the fluid displaced. (Lesson 4)

**C**

**Calorimetry** — The measurement of heat transferred in a process, usually from the temperature change of a known mass of water or other substance. (Lesson 14)

**Capacitor** — A device of two conductors separated by an insulator that stores charge and electric energy; its capacitance is C = Q/V. (Lesson 5)

**Carbonyl group** — A carbon double-bonded to oxygen (C=O); the carbon is electrophilic and the oxygen is nucleophilic. (Lesson 19)

**Carboxylate** — The conjugate base of a carboxylic acid, RCOO-, stabilized by resonance over two oxygens. (Lesson 20)

**Catalyst** — A substance that speeds a reaction by providing a lower-energy pathway without being consumed; it does not change the equilibrium constant or the free energy change. (Lesson 15)

**Cathode** — The electrode at which reduction occurs, in both galvanic and electrolytic cells. (Lesson 17)

**Cation** — A positively charged ion formed by losing electrons. (Lesson 9)

**Cellulose** — A polymer of β-D-glucose joined by β-1,4 linkages; humans cannot digest it, but it provides structural strength in plants. (Lesson 25)

**Center of mass** — The point at which an object's mass can be treated as concentrated for translational motion and at which gravity effectively acts. (Lesson 2)

**Centripetal force** — The net inward force required for circular motion, equal to mv^2/r; it is a role played by tension, gravity, friction or another real force. (Lesson 2)

**Chair conformation** — The lowest-energy shape of cyclohexane, free of angle and torsional strain. (Lesson 18)

**Chemical equilibrium** — The state of a reversible reaction in which forward and reverse rates are equal, so concentrations stay constant. (Lesson 15)

**Chiral** — Not superimposable on its mirror image; a typical chiral molecule has a stereocenter. (Lesson 18)

**Cholesterol** — A steroid lipid that is a component of animal cell membranes and the precursor of steroid hormones. (Lesson 25)

**Circularly polarized** — Light whose electric field vector rotates in a circle as the wave propagates. (Lesson 7)

**Coenzyme** — An organic cofactor, often derived from a vitamin, such as NAD+. (Lesson 24)

**Cofactor** — A nonprotein ion or molecule required for an enzyme's activity; coenzymes are organic cofactors. (Lesson 24)

**Colligative property** — A solution property that depends on the number of dissolved particles rather than their identity, such as boiling-point elevation and osmotic pressure. (Lesson 13)

**Column chromatography** — A preparative separation in which a mixture moves down a column of stationary phase, with components eluting at different times according to their affinities. (Lesson 22)

**Common-ion effect** — The decrease in solubility of a salt when a solution already contains one of its ions. (Lesson 13)

**Competitive inhibitor** — An inhibitor that resembles the substrate and binds the active site; it raises apparent Km and leaves Vmax unchanged, and excess substrate overcomes it. (Lesson 24)

**Complex ion** — A metal ion bound to ligands by coordinate covalent bonds; its formation can raise the solubility of a salt. (Lesson 13)

**Concentration cell** — A galvanic cell with identical electrodes in solutions of different concentration; its potential comes from the concentration difference alone. (Lesson 17)

**Conduction** — Heat transfer by direct contact between particles, without bulk movement of the material. (Lesson 14)

**Conjugate acid** — The species formed when a base accepts a proton. (Lesson 16)

**Conjugate base** — The species formed when an acid donates a proton. (Lesson 16)

**Conservative force** — A force whose work depends only on the starting and ending positions, not on the path; gravity and the electrostatic force are examples. (Lesson 3)

**Constructive interference** — Superposition of waves in phase, giving a combined amplitude larger than either wave alone. (Lesson 6)

**Convection** — Heat transfer by the bulk motion of a fluid. (Lesson 14)

**Cooperativity** — The effect in which binding of a ligand to one site of a multisubunit protein changes the affinity of its other sites. (Lesson 24)

**Coulomb's law** — The electrostatic force between two point charges is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. (Lesson 5)

**Covalent bond** — A bond formed by the sharing of electron pairs between atoms. (Lesson 11)

**Critical angle** — The smallest angle of incidence, measured from the normal, above which light going from a higher to a lower index of refraction is totally reflected. (Lesson 7)

**Critical point** — The temperature and pressure above which the liquid and gas phases are indistinguishable. (Lesson 14)

**C-terminus** — The end of a peptide with a free carboxyl group. (Lesson 23)

**Cystine** — Two cysteine residues joined by a disulfide bond. (Lesson 23)

**D**

**Dalton's law** — The total pressure of a gas mixture is the sum of the partial pressures of its components. (Lesson 12)

**Decay constant** — The proportionality constant (lambda) relating decay rate to the number of nuclei; half-life equals 0.693 divided by lambda. (Lesson 8)

**Decibel** — A logarithmic unit of sound level; each increase of 10 dB is a tenfold increase in intensity. (Lesson 6)

**Degrees of unsaturation** — The number of rings plus π bonds in a molecule, calculated from the molecular formula. (Lesson 21)

**Delocalized** — Describing electrons spread over more than two atoms, as in resonance structures and aromatic rings. (Lesson 11)

**Denaturation** — The loss of a protein's native three-dimensional structure, and usually its function, without breaking peptide bonds. (Lesson 23)

**Density** — Mass per unit volume of a substance. (Lesson 4)

**Dependent variable** — The outcome measured in response to the independent variable. (Strategy Chapter 6)

**Deshielded** — Describing a nucleus whose local electron density is reduced, usually by a nearby electronegative atom, so that it appears farther downfield in NMR. (Lesson 21)

**Destructive interference** — Superposition of waves out of phase, giving a combined amplitude smaller than either wave alone. (Lesson 6)

**Dextrorotatory** — Rotating plane-polarized light clockwise, denoted (+); levorotatory compounds rotate it counterclockwise, (-). (Lesson 18)

**Diamagnetic** — Having all electrons paired; weakly repelled by a magnetic field. (Lesson 9)

**Diastereomer** — A stereoisomer that is not a mirror image of another; diastereomers have different physical properties. (Lesson 22)

**Dielectric** — An insulating material placed between capacitor plates that increases capacitance. (Lesson 5)

**Dilution** — Lowering concentration by adding solvent; moles of solute stay constant so M1V1 = M2V2. (Lesson 13)

**Dimensional analysis** — Tracking units through a calculation to check that the result has the expected units. (Strategy Chapter 5)

**Dimer** — Two molecules held together, as carboxylic acids pair through two hydrogen bonds, raising their boiling points. (Lesson 20)

**Diopter** — The unit of lens power, equal to the reciprocal of the focal length in meters. (Lesson 7)

**Dipole moment** — A vector measure of bond or molecule polarity, equal to charge separation times distance; it points toward the more electronegative end. (Lesson 11)

**Dispersion** — The dependence of a medium's index of refraction on wavelength, which separates white light into colors. (Lesson 7)

**Displacement** — A vector giving the straight-line change in position from start to finish. (Lesson 1)

**Disproportionation** — A redox reaction in which one species is both oxidized and reduced. (Lesson 10)

**Distance** — The total length of the path traveled, a scalar. (Lesson 1)

**Distillation** — Separation of liquids by differences in boiling point. (Lesson 22)

**Disulfide bond** — A covalent S-S bond between two cysteine side chains that stabilizes tertiary and quaternary structure. (Lesson 23)

**Doppler effect** — The change in observed frequency of a wave due to relative motion of the source and observer; approach raises the frequency and recession lowers it. (Lesson 6)

**E**

**E configuration** — A double bond whose higher-priority groups lie on opposite sides; the same-side arrangement is Z. (Lesson 18)

**Eclipsed** — A conformation in which substituents on adjacent carbons line up; it is higher in energy because of torsional strain. (Lesson 18)

**Effective nuclear charge** — The net positive charge felt by a valence electron after core-electron shielding; it increases across a period. (Lesson 9)

**Effusion** — The escape of gas through a tiny opening; lighter gases effuse faster. (Lesson 12)

**Electrolyte** — A substance that dissociates into ions in solution and conducts electricity. (Lesson 13)

**Electrolytic cell** — A cell in which an external power source drives a nonspontaneous reaction; its free energy change is positive. (Lesson 17)

**Electromotive force** — The potential difference produced by a galvanic cell, in volts; it is positive for a spontaneous cell. (Lesson 17)

**Electron volt** — The energy gained by an electron moving through a potential difference of one volt, 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. (Lesson 5)

**Electronegativity** — A measure of an atom's attraction for bonding electrons; it increases toward fluorine in the periodic table. (Lesson 9)

**Electrophile** — An electron-poor species that accepts an electron pair from a nucleophile. (Lesson 19)

**Empirical formula** — The simplest whole-number ratio of atoms in a compound. (Lesson 10)

**Enamine** — A compound with an N attached to a C=C bond, formed from a secondary amine and an aldehyde or ketone. (Lesson 19)

**Enantiomer** — One of a pair of nonsuperimposable mirror-image stereoisomers; enantiomers share all properties except the direction they rotate plane-polarized light and their behavior in chiral environments. (Lesson 18)

**Enantiomeric excess** — The difference between the percentages of two enantiomers in a mixture; a racemic mixture has zero. (Lesson 18)

**Endothermic** — Absorbing heat from the surroundings; the enthalpy change is positive. (Lesson 14)

**Endpoint** — The point in a titration at which the indicator changes color, ideally close to the equivalence point. (Lesson 16)

**Enolate** — The resonance-stabilized anion formed by removing an α hydrogen from a carbonyl compound; it is a carbon nucleophile. (Lesson 19)

**Enthalpy** — A state function, H = U + PV, whose change equals heat transferred at constant pressure. (Lesson 14)

**Entropy** — A state function measuring the dispersal of energy or the number of microstates available to a system. (Lesson 14)

**Enzyme** — A biological catalyst, usually a protein, that speeds a reaction by lowering its activation energy. (Lesson 24)

**Epimer** — A diastereomer differing in configuration at exactly one stereocenter, as glucose and galactose do. (Lesson 25)

**Equatorial** — Describing a substituent on a chair cyclohexane that points outward around the ring's equator; bulky groups prefer this position. (Lesson 18)

**Equivalence point** — The point in a titration where moles of titrant equal the moles of analyte required by the stoichiometry. (Lesson 16)

**Error log** — A running record of each missed question with its cause and the fix, used to direct study. (Strategy Chapter 4)

**Excited state** — A state of an atom with an electron in a higher energy level than the lowest available. (Lesson 9)

**Exothermic** — Releasing heat to the surroundings; the enthalpy change is negative. (Lesson 14)

**Expanded octet** — A Lewis structure with more than eight electrons around a central atom from the third period or lower. (Lesson 11)

**Extraction** — Separation of a compound by transferring it between two immiscible solvents according to solubility. (Lesson 22)

Praxis Tip: Keep a one-line memory hook for each pair that students swap under pressure: oxidation is loss of electrons, the anode is where oxidation happens, and the cathode is where reduction happens, in every kind of cell.

**F**

**Faraday's law** — The amount of substance deposited in electrolysis is proportional to the charge passed, moles = It/nF. (Lesson 17)

**Feedback inhibition** — Regulation in which an end product of a pathway inhibits an earlier enzyme, often allosterically. (Lesson 24)

**Fingerprint region** — The complex region of an IR spectrum below about 1500 cm^-1 that is unique to each compound. (Lesson 21)

**First ionization energy** — The energy required to remove the outermost electron from a gaseous atom; it generally increases across a period and decreases down a group. (Lesson 9)

**Flashcard deck** — A set of cards for spaced review, organized by formulas, constants, trends, mechanisms and traps. (Strategy Chapter 3)

**Formal charge** — The charge an atom would have if bonding electrons were shared equally, equal to valence electrons minus nonbonding electrons minus half the bonding electrons. (Lesson 11)

**Fractional distillation** — Distillation with a fractionating column that provides many vaporization-condensation cycles, separating liquids with close boiling points. (Lesson 22)

**Free fall** — Motion under gravity alone, with downward acceleration g of about 10 m/s^2 regardless of mass. (Lesson 1)

**Free-body diagram** — A sketch of a single object showing all forces acting on it as arrows. (Lesson 2)

**Functional group** — A specific group of atoms that determines a molecule's characteristic reactions. (Lesson 18)

**Fundamental** — The lowest resonant frequency of a vibrating string or air column; higher modes are harmonics. (Lesson 6)

**Furanose** — A five-membered cyclic sugar ring containing one oxygen. (Lesson 25)

**G**

**Gabriel synthesis** — A method of preparing primary amines, including amino acids, from phthalimide and an alkyl halide. (Lesson 23)

**Galvanic cell** — A cell in which a spontaneous redox reaction produces electrical energy; the anode is negative and the cathode positive. (Lesson 17)

**Gauche** — A staggered arrangement in which two large groups are 60 degrees apart, creating steric strain. (Lesson 18)

**Gauge pressure** — The pressure above atmospheric pressure. (Lesson 4)

**Gel electrophoresis** — A technique that separates charged molecules by their migration through a gel in an electric field. (Lesson 22)

**Gibbs free energy** — A state function, G = H - TS, whose change predicts spontaneity at constant temperature and pressure; a negative change is spontaneous. (Lesson 14)

**Glycogen** — A highly branched polymer of glucose that stores energy in animals. (Lesson 25)

**Glycosidic bond** — The linkage between the anomeric carbon of a sugar and another molecule, usually an oxygen of another sugar. (Lesson 25)

**Graham's law** — The rate of effusion or diffusion of a gas is inversely proportional to the square root of its molar mass. (Lesson 12)

**Ground state** — The lowest-energy state of an atom or electron. (Lesson 9)

**H**

**Half-equivalence point** — The point in a titration of a weak acid at which half the acid has been neutralized, so pH equals pKa. (Lesson 16)

**Half-life** — The time required for half of a sample to decay or react. (Lesson 8)

**Heat of fusion** — The heat needed to melt a solid at its melting point without changing temperature. (Lesson 14)

**Heat of vaporization** — The heat needed to convert a liquid to a gas at its boiling point without changing temperature. (Lesson 14)

**Heisenberg uncertainty principle** — The position and momentum of a particle cannot both be known exactly at once. (Lesson 9)

**Hemiacetal** — A carbon bearing an OH group and an OR group, formed by adding an alcohol to an aldehyde; ketone analogs are hemiketals. (Lesson 19)

**Henderson-Hasselbalch equation** — pH = pKa + log([A-]/[HA]), relating the pH of a buffer to the ratio of base to acid. (Lesson 16)

**Hess's law** — The enthalpy change of an overall reaction equals the sum of enthalpy changes of its steps, because enthalpy is a state function. (Lesson 14)

**Holoenzyme** — The complete, active enzyme formed by an apoenzyme and its cofactor. (Lesson 24)

**Hund's rule** — Electrons occupy degenerate orbitals singly, with parallel spins, before any orbital is doubly occupied. (Lesson 9)

**Hybridization** — The mixing of atomic orbitals to form equivalent hybrid orbitals, such as sp, sp2 and sp3, used to explain molecular geometry. (Lesson 11)

**Hydration** — The surrounding of dissolved ions or polar molecules by water molecules through ion-dipole attractions. (Lesson 13)

**Hydrogen bond** — A strong dipole-dipole attraction between a hydrogen on N, O or F and a lone pair on another N, O or F. (Lesson 19)

**Hydronium ion** — H3O+, the form in which a proton exists in water. (Lesson 13)

**Hydrophobic effect** — The tendency of nonpolar groups to cluster away from water, driven largely by the entropy of the surrounding water; it is a major force in protein folding. (Lesson 23)

**Hydrostatic pressure** — The pressure due to the weight of a fluid, P = rho g h. (Lesson 4)

**Hypertonic** — Having a higher solute concentration than another solution; cells placed in a hypertonic solution lose water and shrink. (Lesson 13)

**Hypotonic** — Having a lower solute concentration than another solution; cells placed in a hypotonic solution gain water. (Lesson 13)

**I**

**ICE table** — A table of initial, change and equilibrium concentrations used to solve equilibrium problems. (Lesson 15)

**Ideal gas** — A gas of point particles with no intermolecular forces, obeying PV = nRT. (Lesson 12)

**Ideal gas law** — PV = nRT, relating pressure, volume, amount and absolute temperature of an ideal gas. (Lesson 12)

**Imine** — A compound with a C=N double bond, formed from an aldehyde or ketone and a primary amine. (Lesson 19)

**Immiscible** — Not mixing to form a single phase, as with oil and water. (Lesson 22)

**Independent variable** — The factor an experimenter deliberately changes in an experiment. (Strategy Chapter 6)

**Index of refraction** — The ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to the speed in a medium, n = c/v. (Lesson 7)

**Indicator** — A weak acid or base whose color change signals the endpoint of a titration. (Lesson 16)

**Induced fit** — A model in which substrate binding changes the enzyme's shape to fit the substrate more closely. (Lesson 24)

**Infrared spectroscopy** — A method that detects functional groups by their bond-vibration absorptions; a broad band near 3300 cm^-1 indicates O-H. (Lesson 21)

**Intermediate** — A species formed in one step of a mechanism and consumed in a later step; unlike a transition state, it can in principle be detected. (Lesson 15)

**Inverse-square law** — A law in which a quantity falls off as the square of the distance, as for gravity and Coulomb's force. (Lesson 2)

**Ion-exchange chromatography** — A separation based on the attraction of charged molecules to an oppositely charged stationary phase. (Lesson 22)

**Ionic bond** — The attraction between oppositely charged ions, typically between a metal and a nonmetal. (Lesson 11)

**Isobaric** — Occurring at constant pressure. (Lesson 14)

**Isochoric** — Occurring at constant volume, so no PV work is done. (Lesson 14)

**Isoelectric focusing** — Electrophoresis in a pH gradient in which each protein migrates until it reaches its pI and stops. (Lesson 22)

**Isoelectric point** — The pH at which an amino acid or protein carries no net charge. (Lesson 23)

**Isoelectronic** — Having the same number of electrons, as O2-, F-, Ne and Na+ do. (Lesson 9)

**Isomer** — One of two or more compounds with the same molecular formula but different structures. (Lesson 18)

**Isothermal** — Occurring at constant temperature, so the internal energy of an ideal gas is unchanged. (Lesson 14)

**Isotope** — One of two or more atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons. (Lesson 8)

**K**

**Kcat** — The turnover number, the number of substrate molecules converted per enzyme active site per second at saturation. (Lesson 24)

**Keto-enol tautomerism** — The interconversion of a carbonyl compound and its enol by moving a proton and shifting a double bond; the keto form usually dominates. (Lesson 19)

**Ketose** — A monosaccharide whose carbonyl group is a ketone, such as fructose. (Lesson 25)

**Kinetic energy** — The energy of motion, 1/2 mv^2. (Lesson 3)

**Kinetic enolate** — The enolate formed fastest, by removing the less hindered α hydrogen with a bulky base at low temperature. (Lesson 19)

**Kinetic friction** — The friction force on sliding surfaces; it is generally constant and less than the maximum static friction. (Lesson 2)

**Kinetic molecular theory** — A model describing gas particles as small, in constant random motion, with elastic collisions and average kinetic energy proportional to absolute temperature. (Lesson 12)

**Km** — The Michaelis constant, equal to the substrate concentration at which the reaction rate is half of Vmax; a low value suggests high substrate affinity. (Lesson 24)

Praxis Tip: For any enzyme-inhibition passage, sketch the Lineweaver-Burk lines before reading the answer choices. The pattern of where the lines cross (the y-axis, the x-axis, or neither) names the inhibitor type in seconds.

**L**

**Lactam** — A cyclic amide. (Lesson 20)

**Lactone** — A cyclic ester. (Lesson 20)

**Le Chatelier's principle** — A system at equilibrium that is disturbed shifts in the direction that partially counteracts the disturbance. (Lesson 15)

**Leaving group** — The atom or group that departs with the bonding electron pair in a substitution or elimination; weak bases are the best leaving groups. (Lesson 19)

**Lever arm** — The perpendicular distance from the axis of rotation to the line of action of a force. (Lesson 2)

**Lewis acid** — An electron-pair acceptor; a Lewis base is an electron-pair donor. (Lesson 11)

**Lewis structure** — A diagram of bonding and lone pairs that obeys the octet rule where possible. (Lesson 11)

**Limiting reactant** — The reactant that is used up first and thus determines the maximum amount of product. (Lesson 10)

**Line spectrum** — The set of discrete wavelengths emitted or absorbed by an element, reflecting quantized energy levels. (Lesson 9)

**Lineweaver-Burk plot** — A double-reciprocal plot of 1/v against 1/[S] that gives a straight line with y-intercept 1/Vmax and x-intercept -1/Km. (Lesson 24)

**Lock-and-key model** — A model in which the substrate fits the unaltered active site as a key fits a lock. (Lesson 24)

**London dispersion forces** — Weak attractions from temporary dipoles in all molecules; they grow with size and surface area. (Lesson 11)

**Longitudinal wave** — A wave in which particles move parallel to the direction of propagation, as with sound. (Lesson 6)

**M**

**Malus's law** — The intensity of polarized light passing through an analyzer is I = I0 cos^2 theta. (Lesson 7)

**Mass defect** — The difference between the mass of a nucleus and the sum of the masses of its separate nucleons. (Lesson 8)

**Mass number** — The total number of protons and neutrons in a nucleus (A). (Lesson 8)

**Mechanical advantage** — The ratio of output force to input force for a simple machine; it reduces the force needed but not the work. (Lesson 3)

**Meso compound** — A molecule with stereocenters that is achiral because of an internal plane of symmetry. (Lesson 18)

**Mesylate** — A sulfonate ester of methanesulfonic acid that converts a poor leaving group (OH) into an excellent one. (Lesson 19)

**Metalloid** — An element with properties between those of metals and nonmetals, such as silicon. (Lesson 9)

**Michaelis-Menten kinetics** — A model in which reaction rate rises hyperbolically with substrate concentration toward Vmax: v = Vmax[S]/(Km + [S]). (Lesson 24)

**Mixed inhibitor** — An inhibitor that binds both enzyme and enzyme-substrate complex with different affinities, lowering Vmax and changing Km. (Lesson 24)

**Mobile phase** — The fluid that carries a sample through a chromatography system. (Lesson 22)

**Molar mass** — The mass of one mole of a substance in grams per mole. (Lesson 10)

**Molar solubility** — The number of moles of a solute that dissolves per liter of saturated solution. (Lesson 13)

**Mole** — The amount of substance containing 6.02 × 10²³ particles. (Lesson 10)

**Mole fraction** — The moles of one component divided by the total moles; for gases it equals the fraction of the total pressure that is its partial pressure. (Lesson 12)

**Molecular ion peak** — The peak in a mass spectrum from the intact ionized molecule, giving the molecular mass. (Lesson 21)

**Monosaccharide** — A simple sugar, the basic unit of carbohydrates. (Lesson 25)

**Mutarotation** — The interconversion of the alpha and β anomers of a sugar in solution through the open-chain form. (Lesson 25)

**N**

**n + 1 rule** — A signal from hydrogens with n equivalent neighbors is split into n + 1 peaks. (Lesson 21)

**Nernst equation** — E = E0 - (RT/nF) ln Q, giving the cell potential under nonstandard conditions. (Lesson 17)

**Net force** — The vector sum of all forces on an object; it determines acceleration through F = ma. (Lesson 2)

**Newton's second law** — The net force on an object equals its mass times its acceleration. (Lesson 2)

**Nodes** — Points on a standing wave where the amplitude is always zero. (Lesson 6)

**Noncompetitive inhibitor** — An inhibitor that binds a site other than the active site with equal affinity for enzyme and enzyme-substrate complex; it lowers Vmax and leaves Km unchanged. (Lesson 24)

**Nonconservative force** — A force, such as friction, whose work depends on the path and dissipates mechanical energy. (Lesson 3)

**Normal force** — The contact force perpendicular to a surface that prevents an object from passing through it. (Lesson 2)

**N-terminus** — The end of a peptide with a free amino group. (Lesson 23)

**Nuclear force** — The short-range strong attraction between nucleons that overcomes proton repulsion in the nucleus. (Lesson 8)

**Nucleophile** — An electron-rich species that donates an electron pair to form a new bond. (Lesson 19)

**Nucleophilic acyl substitution** — A reaction in which a nucleophile replaces the leaving group of an acyl compound through a tetrahedral intermediate. (Lesson 20)

**Nucleoside** — A sugar bonded to a nitrogenous base; adding a phosphate gives a nucleotide. (Lesson 25)

**Nucleotide** — A nucleoside with one or more phosphate groups, the monomer of nucleic acids. (Lesson 25)

**O**

**Ohmic** — Describing a conductor in which current is proportional to voltage, so resistance is constant. (Lesson 5)

**Optical activity** — The ability of a chiral compound to rotate the plane of polarized light. (Lesson 18)

**Orbital** — A region of space where an electron is likely to be found, described by a wavefunction. (Lesson 9)

**Order** — The exponent of a concentration term in a rate law; the overall order is the sum of the exponents. (Lesson 15)

**Osmosis** — The net movement of water across a semipermeable membrane toward the region of higher solute concentration. (Lesson 13)

**Osmotic pressure** — The pressure needed to stop osmosis, equal to iMRT. (Lesson 13)

**Oxidation** — The loss of electrons, which raises the oxidation number. (Lesson 10)

**Oxidation number** — A bookkeeping charge assigned to an atom assuming all bonding electrons go to the more electronegative atom. (Lesson 10)

**Oxidizing agent** — A species that accepts electrons and is itself reduced. (Lesson 10)

**P**

**Pacing** — Allotting roughly equal time per question so the whole section is attempted; about 95 minutes for 59 questions leaves roughly 1.6 minutes each. (Strategy Chapter 38)

**Paramagnetic** — Having one or more unpaired electrons and being attracted into a magnetic field. (Lesson 9)

**Partition coefficient** — The ratio of a solute's concentration in one solvent to its concentration in a second immiscible solvent at equilibrium. (Lesson 22)

**Pascal's principle** — A pressure change applied to an enclosed incompressible fluid is transmitted undiminished throughout the fluid. (Lesson 4)

**Pauli exclusion principle** — No two electrons in an atom can share the same four quantum numbers, so an orbital holds at most two electrons of opposite spin. (Lesson 9)

**Peptide bond** — The amide bond joining the carboxyl group of one amino acid to the amino group of the next; it has partial double-bond character and is planar. (Lesson 23)

**Percent ionization** — The fraction of a weak acid or base that has ionized, times 100. (Lesson 16)

**Percent yield** — Actual yield divided by theoretical yield, times 100. (Lesson 10)

**Phase diagram** — A graph showing the states of a substance as functions of pressure and temperature. (Lesson 14)

**Phenol** — An aromatic ring bearing an OH group; it is much more acidic than an aliphatic alcohol. (Lesson 19)

**Phosphodiester bond** — The linkage joining nucleotides in nucleic acids through phosphate between the 3' and 5' carbons of adjacent sugars. (Lesson 25)

**Phospholipid** — An amphipathic lipid with a phosphate-containing head group and two fatty acid tails that forms membrane bilayers. (Lesson 25)

**Photoelectric effect** — The ejection of electrons from a metal by light above a threshold frequency, supporting the particle nature of light. (Lesson 9)

**Photon** — A quantum of electromagnetic radiation with energy E = hf. (Lesson 7)

**Pi bond** — A bond formed by side-to-side overlap of p orbitals; it is weaker than a σ bond and prevents rotation. (Lesson 11)

**Poiseuille's law** — Flow rate through a tube is proportional to the fourth power of the radius, so small changes in radius have a large effect on flow. (Lesson 4)

**Polar molecule** — A molecule with a net dipole moment, resulting from polar bonds arranged asymmetrically. (Lesson 11)

**Polarization** — The restriction of light's electric field to a single plane. (Lesson 7)

**Potential energy** — Stored energy due to position or configuration, such as gravitational or elastic energy. (Lesson 3)

**Power** — The rate of doing work or transferring energy, in watts. (Lesson 3)

**Practice-exam review** — A structured pass through every question, right or wrong, that records why each answer was or was not chosen. (Strategy Chapter 41)

**Primary alcohol** — An alcohol whose OH-bearing carbon is bonded to one other carbon; oxidation gives an aldehyde and then a carboxylic acid. (Lesson 19)

**Primary structure** — The amino acid sequence of a polypeptide. (Lesson 23)

**Projectile motion** — Motion in which horizontal velocity stays constant while vertical motion has constant downward acceleration; the two components are independent. (Lesson 1)

**Prosthetic group** — A cofactor bound tightly or covalently to an enzyme. (Lesson 24)

**Protecting group** — A temporary derivative of a functional group that blocks it from reacting while another part of the molecule is modified. (Lesson 19)

**Proton NMR** — A method that reports the number, environment and neighbors of hydrogens in a molecule through signals, shifts, integration and splitting. (Lesson 21)

**Purine** — A double-ring nitrogenous base, adenine or guanine. (Lesson 25)

**Pyranose** — A six-membered cyclic sugar ring containing one oxygen. (Lesson 25)

**Pyrimidine** — A single-ring nitrogenous base, cytosine, thymine or uracil. (Lesson 25)

Praxis Tip: Read each glossary definition aloud, then cover it and say it in your own words. If you cannot give the unit or the direction, you do not yet own the term, and the exam will find the gap.

**Q**

**Quaternary structure** — The assembly of multiple polypeptide subunits into a functional protein. (Lesson 23)

**Quinone** — A conjugated cyclic diketone that undergoes reversible reduction to a hydroquinone, as in ubiquinone of the electron transport chain. (Lesson 20)

**R**

**R configuration** — A stereocenter whose priorities, ranked highest to lowest with the lowest priority pointed away, run clockwise; counterclockwise is S. (Lesson 18)

**Racemic mixture** — An equimolar mixture of two enantiomers, which is optically inactive. (Lesson 18)

**Rate constant** — The proportionality constant k in a rate law, dependent on temperature and catalysts. (Lesson 15)

**Rate law** — An expression relating reaction rate to reactant concentrations, determined experimentally. (Lesson 15)

**Rate-determining step** — The slowest step of a mechanism, which limits the overall rate. (Lesson 15)

**Reaction quotient** — Q, calculated like K but with current concentrations; comparing Q with K shows which way a reaction will proceed. (Lesson 15)

**Real image** — An image formed where light rays actually converge; it can be projected on a screen and is inverted. (Lesson 7)

**Reducing agent** — A species that gives up electrons and is itself oxidized. (Lesson 10)

**Reducing sugar** — A sugar with a free anomeric carbon that can open to an aldehyde and reduce mild oxidizing agents. (Lesson 25)

**Reduction** — The gain of electrons, which lowers the oxidation number. (Lesson 10)

**Resolving agent** — A single enantiomer of a chiral compound that reacts with a racemate to form separable diastereomers. (Lesson 22)

**Resonance** — The representation of a species by several Lewis structures that differ only in electron placement; the true structure is a hybrid. (Lesson 11)

**Retention factor** — In thin-layer chromatography, the distance traveled by a spot divided by the distance traveled by the solvent front. (Lesson 22)

**Retention time** — The time a compound takes to pass through a chromatography column to the detector. (Lesson 22)

**S**

**Salt bridge** — A connection containing inert ions that completes the circuit of a cell and keeps each half-cell electrically neutral. (Lesson 17)

**Saponification** — The base-promoted hydrolysis of an ester, giving a carboxylate salt and an alcohol; it is irreversible. (Lesson 20)

**Saturated fatty acid** — A fatty acid with no carbon-carbon double bonds, which packs tightly and has a higher melting point. (Lesson 25)

**Scalar** — A quantity with magnitude only, such as speed or mass. (Lesson 1)

**SDS-PAGE** — Gel electrophoresis in which sodium dodecyl sulfate coats proteins with uniform charge so they separate by size alone. (Lesson 22)

**Second law of thermodynamics** — The entropy of the universe increases in any spontaneous process. (Lesson 14)

**Secondary alcohol** — An alcohol whose OH-bearing carbon is bonded to two other carbons; oxidation gives a ketone. (Lesson 19)

**Semilog graph** — A graph with one logarithmic axis, where exponential relationships appear as straight lines. (Strategy Chapter 6)

**Side chain** — The variable R group of an amino acid that determines its properties. (Lesson 23)

**Sigma bond** — A bond formed by head-on overlap of orbitals along the bond axis; every single bond is a σ bond. (Lesson 11)

**Significant digits** — The digits in a measurement that carry meaning about its precision. (Strategy Chapter 5)

**Size-exclusion chromatography** — Separation by size in which small molecules enter the pores of the beads and elute later than large ones. (Lesson 22)

**SN1 reaction** — A two-step substitution through a carbocation, favored by tertiary substrates and polar protic solvents, producing racemization. (Lesson 19)

**SN2 reaction** — A one-step substitution with backside attack, favored by unhindered substrates and strong nucleophiles, producing inversion of configuration. (Lesson 19)

**Snell's law** — n1 sin theta1 = n2 sin theta2, relating the angles of incidence and refraction. (Lesson 7)

**Solubility product** — The equilibrium constant, Ksp, for dissolving a sparingly soluble salt. (Lesson 13)

**Specific gravity** — The density of a substance divided by the density of water. (Lesson 4)

**Specific heat capacity** — The heat required to raise one gram of a substance by one degree Celsius or kelvin. (Lesson 14)

**Specific rotation** — The observed optical rotation normalized for path length and concentration; it is a characteristic property of a chiral compound. (Lesson 18)

**Speed** — The rate at which distance is covered, a scalar. (Lesson 1)

**Spontaneous** — Describing a process that occurs without continuous outside input, with a negative change in Gibbs free energy. (Lesson 14)

**Staggered** — A conformation in which substituents on adjacent carbons are as far apart as possible; it has lower energy than the eclipsed form. (Lesson 18)

**Standard enthalpy of formation** — The enthalpy change when one mole of a compound forms from its elements in their standard states. (Lesson 14)

**Standard reduction potential** — The potential of a half-reaction relative to the standard hydrogen electrode; higher values mean stronger tendency to be reduced. (Lesson 17)

**Standing wave** — A pattern of fixed nodes and antinodes formed by two identical waves traveling in opposite directions. (Lesson 6)

**Starch** — A plant storage polysaccharide of glucose, composed of unbranched amylose and branched amylopectin. (Lesson 25)

**State function** — A property that depends only on the current state of a system, not on the path, such as enthalpy, entropy and free energy. (Lesson 14)

**Static equilibrium** — The condition where the net force and the net torque on an object are both zero, and it is at rest. (Lesson 2)

**Static friction** — The friction that keeps surfaces from sliding, up to a maximum of mu_s times the normal force. (Lesson 2)

**Stationary phase** — The fixed material in chromatography on which components are adsorbed or partitioned. (Lesson 22)

**Stereocenter** — An atom, usually carbon, bonded to four different groups. (Lesson 18)

**STP** — Standard temperature and pressure for gases, 0 degrees C and 1 atm, at which one mole of ideal gas occupies 22.4 L. (Lesson 12)

**Strecker synthesis** — The formation of an α-amino acid from an aldehyde, ammonia and cyanide, followed by hydrolysis; it gives a racemic mixture. (Lesson 23)

**Surface tension** — The tendency of a liquid surface to contract because of cohesive forces between molecules. (Lesson 4)

**Surroundings** — Everything outside the system that can exchange energy with it. (Lesson 14)

**System** — The part of the universe chosen for study. (Lesson 14)

**T**

**Terminal velocity** — The constant speed reached when drag force equals the driving force, usually weight, so the net force is zero. (Lesson 2)

**Tertiary alcohol** — An alcohol whose OH-bearing carbon has three carbon neighbors; it resists oxidation. (Lesson 19)

**Tetrahedral intermediate** — The sp3 species formed when a nucleophile adds to a carbonyl carbon during acyl substitution. (Lesson 20)

**Theoretical yield** — The maximum product predicted from the limiting reactant. (Lesson 10)

**Thin-layer chromatography** — A chromatography method using a thin plate of adsorbent, in which polar compounds travel less far on silica. (Lesson 22)

**Threshold frequency** — The minimum frequency of light that ejects electrons from a metal in the photoelectric effect. (Lesson 9)

**Titration** — A procedure in which a solution of known concentration is added to an analyte to determine its amount. (Lesson 16)

**Torque** — The rotational effect of a force, equal to the force times the lever arm. (Lesson 2)

**Tosylate** — A sulfonate ester of p-toluenesulfonic acid, used like a mesylate to activate alcohols. (Lesson 19)

**Total internal reflection** — Complete reflection of light at an interface when the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle. (Lesson 7)

**Transition state** — The highest-energy arrangement along a reaction pathway; it is unstable and cannot be isolated. (Lesson 15)

**Transverse wave** — A wave in which particles move perpendicular to the direction of propagation, as with light. (Lesson 6)

**Triacylglycerol** — A glycerol esterified with three fatty acids, the main energy-storage lipid. (Lesson 25)

**Triage** — Sorting questions into answer now, flag and return, and guess and move so that time goes to the points most likely to be earned. (Strategy Chapter 4)

**Triple point** — The unique temperature and pressure at which solid, liquid and gas coexist in equilibrium. (Lesson 14)

**Turbulent flow** — Chaotic fluid flow with eddies, which has higher resistance than smooth laminar flow. (Lesson 4)

**U**

**Uncompetitive inhibitor** — An inhibitor that binds only the enzyme-substrate complex, lowering both Vmax and apparent Km. (Lesson 24)

**Unsaturated fatty acid** — A fatty acid with one or more double bonds; cis double bonds kink the chain and lower the melting point. (Lesson 25)

**V**

**Van't Hoff factor** — The number of particles a solute produces per formula unit in solution, used in colligative-property formulas. (Lesson 13)

**Vector** — A quantity with both magnitude and direction. (Lesson 1)

**Velocity** — The rate of change of displacement, a vector. (Lesson 1)

**Virtual image** — An image formed where rays only appear to diverge; it cannot be projected on a screen. (Lesson 7)

**Viscosity** — A fluid's resistance to flow caused by internal friction. (Lesson 4)

**Vmax** — The maximum rate of an enzyme-catalyzed reaction when the enzyme is saturated with substrate. (Lesson 24)

**VSEPR theory** — A model in which electron groups around a central atom arrange to minimize repulsion, predicting molecular shape. (Lesson 11)

**W**

**Wavenumber** — The number of waves per centimeter, used to express IR absorptions; it is proportional to energy. (Lesson 21)

**Weak acid** — An acid that ionizes only partially in water, with Ka less than 1. (Lesson 16)

**Weight** — The gravitational force on an object, mg; it varies with location while mass does not. (Lesson 2)

**Work** — The energy transferred by a force acting through a displacement, W = Fd cos theta. (Lesson 3)

**Work function** — The minimum energy needed to remove an electron from a metal surface. (Lesson 9)

**Work-energy theorem** — The net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy. (Lesson 3)

**Z**

**Zwitterion** — A molecule carrying both a positive and a negative charge with zero net charge, such as an amino acid at its isoelectric point. (Lesson 23)

**Zymogen** — An inactive enzyme precursor activated by cleavage, such as pepsinogen. (Lesson 24)
