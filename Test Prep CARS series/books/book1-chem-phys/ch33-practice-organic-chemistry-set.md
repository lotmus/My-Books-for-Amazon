# Practice: Organic Chemistry Set

**Timing and conditions**

Fourteen original questions on organic chemistry from [Lessons 18 to 22](chapter:28): stereochemistry, alcohols and carbonyls, carboxylic acid derivatives, spectroscopy and separations (categories 5B, 5C and 5D). Two passage sets are followed by four independent questions. Allow about 22 minutes, in one sitting, with scratch paper and no notes or calculator. Write your answer letters, with a confidence level (high, medium or low) for each, on scratch paper or in your notebook; an ebook cannot be written in. Check only when you have finished.

Praxis Tip: Organic passages reward drawing. Sketch each structure on your scratch paper as you identify it, and write the formula beside the sketch. A structure on the page is checkable; a structure in your head is a rumor.

Praxis Tip: When a passage gives spectral data, work through the clues in a fixed order: molecular formula and unsaturation, infrared, then NMR. Each clue narrows the field before the next one arrives.

**Questions**

**Passage 1 (questions 1-5)**

A chemist isolates a pleasant-smelling liquid, compound X, with the molecular formula C₅H₁₀O₂. Its infrared spectrum shows a strong, sharp absorption at 1740 cm⁻¹ and no broad absorption between 2500 and 3600 cm⁻¹. Its ¹H NMR spectrum shows three signals:

| Chemical shift (δ) | Integration | Multiplicity |
|---|---|---|
| 1.2 | 6H | doublet |
| 2.0 | 3H | singlet |
| 5.0 | 1H | septet |

Heating X with aqueous sodium hydroxide gives a water-soluble salt, Y, and a neutral compound, Z, with the formula C₃H₈O. Oxidation of Z with acidic potassium dichromate gives acetone.

1. Which functional group is present in compound X?
   A. A carboxylic acid
   B. A ketone
   C. An alcohol
   D. An ester

2. The septet at δ 5.0 arises because the hydrogen on that carbon has how many equivalent neighboring hydrogens?
   A. 6
   B. 7
   C. 5
   D. 3

3. What is compound Z?
   A. 1-Propanol
   B. 2-Propanol
   C. Propanal
   D. Acetone

4. What is the identity of compound X?
   A. Propyl acetate
   B. Ethyl propanoate
   C. Isopropyl acetate (propan-2-yl ethanoate)
   D. Methyl butanoate

5. Aqueous solution Y is acidified with hydrochloric acid. The organic product that forms is
   A. acetic acid
   B. acetaldehyde
   C. ethanol
   D. acetone

**Passage 2 (questions 6-10)**

A student studies the reaction of (S)-2-bromobutane with hydroxide ion in a polar aprotic solvent. The initial rate of formation of 2-butanol was measured at 25 °C:

| Experiment | [2-bromobutane] (M) | [OH⁻] (M) | Initial rate (M/s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 2 | 0.20 | 0.10 | 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 3 | 0.10 | 0.20 | 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ |

She then repeats the study with tert-butyl bromide in aqueous ethanol, with no strong nucleophile present.

6. Which rate law is consistent with the data for 2-bromobutane?
   A. rate = k [2-bromobutane]
   B. rate = k [OH⁻]
   C. rate = k [2-bromobutane][OH⁻]
   D. rate = k [2-bromobutane][OH⁻]²

7. These data, together with the use of a strong nucleophile on a secondary halide, indicate which mechanism?
   A. SN1, with a carbocation intermediate
   B. E1, with a carbocation intermediate
   C. Free-radical substitution
   D. SN2, a single concerted step

8. What is the stereochemical outcome of the reaction of (S)-2-bromobutane with OH⁻?
   A. (S)-2-butanol, formed with retention of configuration
   B. (R)-2-butanol, formed with inversion of configuration
   C. A racemic mixture of 2-butanol
   D. 2-butene

9. With tert-butyl bromide in aqueous ethanol, the rate of substitution would be expected to
   A. depend on the concentration of the alkyl halide only
   B. depend on the concentrations of both the alkyl halide and the solvent molecules, as in SN2
   C. be zero, because tertiary halides cannot react
   D. depend on the concentration of the nucleophile only

10. If a chiral tertiary alkyl bromide undergoes substitution by an SN1 mechanism, the product is expected to be
   A. a single enantiomer with inverted configuration
   B. a single enantiomer with retained configuration
   C. achiral, because the carbocation is planar
   D. largely a racemic mixture

**Questions 11-14**

These four questions are independent of the passages.

11. How many stereoisomers does 2,3-dibromobutane have?
   A. 2
   B. 3
   C. 4
   D. 1

12. Which of the following compounds is aromatic?
   A. Cyclohexene
   B. Cyclobutadiene
   C. Pyridine
   D. Cyclooctatetraene (nonplanar)

13. On a silica gel TLC plate, compound A has an Rf of 0.50 and compound B has an Rf of 0.20 in the same solvent. Which statement is most likely correct?
   A. A is more polar than B
   B. A and B have the same polarity but different molecular masses
   C. The solvent front traveled 0.50 cm
   D. B is more polar than A

14. An ether solution contains benzoic acid and naphthalene (a neutral hydrocarbon). The ether solution is shaken with aqueous sodium bicarbonate. Which statement is correct?
   A. Benzoic acid is extracted into the aqueous layer as its carboxylate, and naphthalene remains in the ether
   B. Naphthalene is extracted into the aqueous layer as its salt
   C. Both compounds stay in the ether, because bicarbonate is a weak base
   D. Both compounds are extracted into the aqueous layer

**Answers and explanations**

1. **D.** The formula C₅H₁₀O₂ has one degree of unsaturation. The strong peak at 1740 cm⁻¹ is a C=O, and the lack of a broad O–H band, together with hydrolysis by NaOH to a salt and an alcohol, identifies an ester. [5D · Skill 2] (A would show a very broad O–H absorption; B has too few oxygen atoms for the formula and does not hydrolyze in base; C would show a broad O–H absorption near 3300 cm⁻¹.)
2. **A.** By the n + 1 rule, a septet (7 lines) means n = 6 equivalent neighbors: the six hydrogens of the two equivalent methyl groups of an isopropyl group. [5C · Skill 4] (B applies the n + 1 rule to the number of lines instead of the neighbors; C miscounts the neighbors; D counts only one methyl group.)
3. **B.** Z is C₃H₈O and oxidizes to acetone, so it is a secondary alcohol: 2-propanol. (Isopropyl alcohol.) [5D · Skill 2] (A would oxidize to propanal or propanoic acid, not acetone; C is an aldehyde with a different formula; D is the oxidation product, not Z.)
4. **C.** The 3H singlet at 2.0 ppm is CH₃C(=O)–; the 6H doublet and 1H septet are an isopropyl group attached to oxygen (5.0 ppm). Hydrolysis gives acetate (Y) and 2-propanol (Z). [5D · Skill 2] (A would show a triplet, a sextet and a triplet for the propyl group; B would show an ethyl quartet and triplet and a different pattern for the acid portion; D would show a methoxy singlet near 3.7 ppm.)
5. **A.** Y is sodium acetate, the carboxylate salt of the acid portion. Protonation with strong acid gives acetic acid. [5D · Skill 2] (B would require a reduction; C would require reduction of the carboxylate; D is the oxidation product of Z, not of Y.)
6. **C.** Doubling either concentration doubles the rate, so the reaction is first order in each reactant. [5E · Skill 4] (A ignores the dependence of the rate on hydroxide; B ignores the dependence on the alkyl halide; D would quadruple the rate when [OH⁻] doubles.)
7. **D.** A rate that depends on both the substrate and the nucleophile is characteristic of a bimolecular, concerted SN2 reaction. [5D · Skill 2] (A would give a rate independent of [OH⁻]; B would also be independent of [OH⁻]; C has no basis in the data.)
8. **B.** SN2 proceeds by backside attack, inverting the configuration at the stereocenter. Because the priorities of the substituents keep the same order (Br or OH > ethyl > methyl > H), the descriptor changes from S to R. [5B · Skill 2] (A SN2 inverts the configuration; C describes an SN1 outcome; D is an elimination product.)
9. **A.** A tertiary halide ionizes in the slow step to a carbocation (SN1), so the rate is first order in the alkyl halide and independent of the nucleophile. [5E · Skill 2] (B tertiary halides are too hindered for SN2; C tertiary halides react readily by SN1; D the rate-determining step does not involve the nucleophile.)
10. **D.** The planar carbocation can be attacked from either face with equal probability, giving both enantiomers in about equal amounts. [5B · Skill 2] (A describes SN2; B ignores the planar carbocation; C the product, unlike the carbocation, has a stereocenter.)
11. **B.** There are two stereocenters, so up to 2² = 4 stereoisomers, but one pair is a meso compound (identical to its mirror image), so there are three: the (R,R) and (S,S) enantiomers and the meso (R,S) form. [5B · Skill 1] (A counts only the enantiomeric pair; C ignores the meso compound; D ignores the stereocenters.)
12. **C.** Pyridine is planar, cyclic and fully conjugated with six π electrons (the nitrogen lone pair lies in the plane of the ring and is not part of the π system). [5D · Skill 1] (A has only one double bond, so it is not conjugated around the ring; B has 4 π electrons and is antiaromatic; D is not planar, so it is not aromatic.)
13. **D.** On a polar stationary phase, more polar compounds are held more strongly and move less, so they have lower Rf values. [5C · Skill 2] (A reverses the relationship on a polar stationary phase; B polarity, not mass, determines Rf on silica; C Rf is a ratio, not a distance.)
14. **A.** Bicarbonate deprotonates the carboxylic acid to give sodium benzoate, which is ionic and dissolves in water. The nonpolar naphthalene stays in the ether. [5C · Skill 2] (B naphthalene has no acidic group; C benzoic acid is strong enough an acid to react with bicarbonate; D naphthalene is nonpolar and insoluble in water.)
