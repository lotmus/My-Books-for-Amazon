**Timing and conditions**

Two passage sets are followed by four independent questions. Allow about 22 minutes, in one sitting, with scratch paper and no notes or calculator. Use R = 8.31 J/(mol·K) and F = 96,500 C/mol where needed. Write your answer letters, with a confidence level (high, medium or low) for each, on scratch paper or in your notebook; an ebook cannot be written in. Check only when you have finished.

**Questions**

**Passage 1 (questions 1-5)**

A chemist studies the reaction A + B → products. Initial rates were measured at 300 K for three starting mixtures:

| Experiment | [A] (M) | [B] (M) | Initial rate (M/s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 2 | 0.20 | 0.10 | 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 3 | 0.20 | 0.20 | 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ |

The rate constant for the reaction was then measured at two temperatures: k = 1.5 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ at 300 K and k = 3.0 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ at 310 K.

1. What are the orders of the reaction with respect to A and B?
   A. First order in A and first order in B
   B. First order in A and zero order in B
   C. Second order in A and zero order in B
   D. Zero order in A and first order in B

2. What is the value of the rate constant k at 300 K?
   A. 1.5 × 10⁻² M⁻¹ s⁻¹
   B. 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹
   C. 1.5 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹
   D. 3.0 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹

3. What is the half-life of the reaction at 300 K?
   A. 4.6 × 10² s
   B. 2.3 × 10² s
   C. 6.7 × 10² s
   D. 9.2 × 10² s

4. The rate constant doubles between 300 K and 310 K. The activation energy of the reaction is closest to
   A. 5.4 kJ/mol
   B. 110 kJ/mol
   C. 0.54 kJ/mol
   D. 54 kJ/mol

5. A catalyst is added to the reaction at 300 K. Which change is expected?
   A. The activation energy decreases and ΔH becomes more negative
   B. The activation energy is unchanged but the equilibrium constant increases
   C. The activation energy increases and the rate constant decreases
   D. The activation energy decreases and the rate constant increases, but ΔH for the reaction is unchanged

**Passage 2 (questions 6-10)**

A 25.0 mL sample of a weak monoprotic acid, HA, is titrated at 25 °C with 0.100 M NaOH. The equivalence point is reached after 20.0 mL of base has been added. After 10.0 mL of base has been added, the pH of the solution is 4.20.

6. What is the concentration of the HA solution?
   A. 0.0800 M
   B. 0.125 M
   C. 0.0200 M
   D. 0.100 M

7. What is the acid dissociation constant, Ka, of HA?
   A. 6.3 × 10⁻⁴
   B. 6.3 × 10⁻⁵
   C. 4.2 × 10⁻⁵
   D. 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁰

8. At the equivalence point, the pH of the solution is
   A. equal to 7
   B. equal to 4.20
   C. greater than 7
   D. less than 4.20

9. Which indicator would be most appropriate for this titration?
   A. Methyl orange, which changes color between about pH 3 and pH 4.5
   B. Methyl red, which changes color between about pH 4.5 and pH 6
   C. Phenolphthalein, which changes color between about pH 8 and pH 10
   D. Bromothymol blue, which changes color between about pH 6 and pH 7.6

10. What is the pH after 15.0 mL of NaOH has been added?
   A. 4.20
   B. 4.68
   C. 3.72
   D. 5.20

**Questions 11-14**

These four questions are independent of the passages.

11. A reaction has ΔH = −100 kJ/mol and ΔS = −200 J/(mol·K). What is ΔG at 400 K?
   A. −20 kJ/mol
   B. +20 kJ/mol
   C. −180 kJ/mol
   D. +180 kJ/mol

12. N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2 NO₂(g) is at equilibrium in a closed container. The volume of the container is suddenly decreased, at constant temperature. The equilibrium will
   A. shift toward NO₂, the side with more moles of gas
   B. not shift, because the temperature is constant
   C. shift toward NO₂ and the equilibrium constant will increase
   D. shift toward N₂O₄, the side with fewer moles of gas

13. A galvanic cell is built from a Zn²⁺/Zn half-cell (E° = −0.76 V) and an Ag⁺/Ag half-cell (E° = +0.80 V). The standard cell potential is
   A. +1.56 V
   B. +0.04 V
   C. −1.56 V
   D. +0.80 V

14. A current of 0.50 A passes through a solution of silver ions for 1,930 s. What mass of silver is deposited? (Molar mass of Ag = 108 g/mol; F = 96,500 C/mol.)
   A. 2.16 g
   B. 1.08 g
   C. 0.54 g
   D. 10.8 g

**Stop here.** Finish every question and write down your answers (on scratch paper) before you continue to the explanations.

**Answers and explanations**

1. **B.** Experiments 1 and 2: doubling [A] doubles the rate, so the order in A is 1. Experiments 2 and 3: doubling [B] does not change the rate, so the order in B is 0. [5E · Skill 4] (A ignores that changing [B] had no effect on the rate; C reads the doubling of the rate as a quadrupling; D reverses the roles of A and B.)
2. **C.** The rate law is rate = k[A]. From Experiment 1, k = 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ / 0.10 = 1.5 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ (which agrees with the value given for 300 K). [5E · Skill 4] (A divides by [A][B], treating the reaction as second order overall; B takes the rate itself as the rate constant; D is the value at 310 K.)
3. **A.** The reaction is first order in A, so t½ = 0.693 / k = 0.693 / (1.5 × 10⁻³) ≈ 4.6 × 10² s. [5E · Skill 2] (B halves the correct value; C uses 1/k instead of 0.693/k; D doubles the correct value.)
4. **D.** ln (k₂/k₁) = (E_a/R)(1/T₁ − 1/T₂), so E_a = (0.693 × 8.31) / (1/300 − 1/310) = 5.76 / (1.08 × 10⁻⁴) ≈ 5.4 × 10⁴ J/mol = 54 kJ/mol. [5E · Skill 2] (A is a factor of 10 too small; B doubles the correct value; C is a factor of 100 too small.)
5. **D.** A catalyst provides a lower-energy pathway: E_a falls and k rises. It does not change the energies of the reactants and products, so ΔH and K are unchanged. [5E · Skill 1] (A a catalyst does not change ΔH; B a catalyst does not change K, and it does change E_a; C reverses the effect of a catalyst.)
6. **A.** Moles of base at equivalence = 0.100 × 0.0200 = 2.00 mmol = moles of HA. Concentration = 2.00 mmol / 25.0 mL = 0.0800 M. [5A · Skill 2] (B inverts the volume ratio; C divides by the wrong volume; D assumes the acid has the same concentration as the base.)
7. **B.** After 10.0 mL, half of the HA (the half-equivalence point) has been converted to A⁻, so [HA] = [A⁻] and pH = pKa = 4.20. Ka = 10⁻⁴·² = 6.3 × 10⁻⁵. [5A · Skill 2] (A misplaces a power of ten; C uses the pH as a coefficient; D is Kb for the conjugate base.)
8. **C.** At equivalence, the HA has been converted entirely to its conjugate base A⁻, which reacts with water to produce OH⁻, so the pH is above 7 (about 8.4 here). [5A · Skill 2] (A applies to a strong acid-strong base titration; B is the pH at the half-equivalence point; D would require an excess of acid.)
9. **C.** The indicator's transition range should include the pH at the equivalence point, about 8.4. Phenolphthalein fits. [5A · Skill 2] (A changes color far below the equivalence-point pH; B changes color far below the equivalence-point pH; D changes color before the equivalence point is reached.)
10. **B.** NaOH added = 1.50 mmol. Remaining HA = 2.00 − 1.50 = 0.50 mmol; A⁻ formed = 1.50 mmol. pH = pKa + log (1.50 / 0.50) = 4.20 + log 3 = 4.20 + 0.48 = 4.68. [5A · Skill 2] (A is the pH at the half-equivalence point; C inverts the ratio [A⁻]/[HA]; D adds a full pH unit to the pKa.)
11. **A.** ΔG = ΔH − TΔS = −100,000 − (400)(−200) = −100,000 + 80,000 = −20,000 J/mol = −20 kJ/mol. The reaction is spontaneous at 400 K. [5E · Skill 2] (B gets the sign of the TΔS term wrong in the final result; C subtracts 80 kJ in the wrong direction; D combines both sign errors.)
12. **D.** Decreasing the volume raises the pressure, and the system shifts toward the side with fewer moles of gas (1 mole of N₂O₄ versus 2 of NO₂). K is unchanged. [5E · Skill 2] (A reverses the effect of increasing the pressure; B ignores the effect of pressure on a reaction with a change in moles of gas; C K depends only on temperature.)
13. **A.** Silver is reduced at the cathode and zinc is oxidized at the anode: E°_cell = E°_cathode − E°_anode = 0.80 − (−0.76) = +1.56 V. [4C · Skill 2] (B adds the potentials with opposite signs; C reverses the cathode and anode; D uses the cathode potential alone.)
14. **B.** Charge = 0.50 × 1,930 = 965 C = 0.0100 mol of electrons. Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag, so 0.0100 mol Ag = 0.0100 × 108 = 1.08 g. [4C · Skill 2] (A doubles the correct amount; C counts two electrons per silver ion, which halves the amount; D is a factor of 10 too large.)
