# Strategy: Your Test-Day Plan

**What you will be able to do**

- Build a checklist for the week, the night, the morning and the room.
- Handle a bad section without letting it spill over.
- Prepare for logistics and rules so that nothing surprises you.

**Why it matters**

By test day, the learning is mostly done. What remains is delivery. The best preparation can be undone by a missing ID, a forgotten lunch, or a morning spent arguing with traffic. This chapter is a checklist and a few mental routines. It is less glamorous than studying and more likely to rescue a test.

Praxis Tip: Write the plan down and read it the week before. You will not think clearly enough on the morning to make a plan up.

**Rules and logistics first**

Everything here depends on the exam owner's current rules, so read them. Confirm from the official source:

- What identification is required, and the exact name requirements.
- What you may and may not bring (food, medication, watches, notes, scratch material).
- Check-in time and the penalty for late arrival.
- The test center address, parking and travel time, or the technical requirements for an at-home test.
- Break rules and what is permitted during breaks.
- Calculator or tool rules ({TEST_FORMAT}).
- What happens if there is a technical problem, illness or emergency.

**Accommodations.** If you need testing accommodations for a documented disability or health condition, requests commonly involve paperwork and lead time that can run to weeks or longer. Check the owner's accommodations page early, and apply as soon as you know you need to.

Praxis Tip: Do a trial run of the journey, at the same time of day as the exam, at least once. Traffic and transit are hard to guess.

**The checklist**

*One week before*
- Confirm registration, ID, location and time.
- Do your last simulation earlier than this (Chapter 25), and use this week for light review and rest.
- Plan meals and caffeine as in your tested routine.

*Two days before*
- Pack the bag: ID, confirmation, allowed items, layers of clothing, snacks and water for breaks as allowed.
- Lay out clothes.
- Stop new material. Review your error log and formula card only.

*The night before*
- Eat a normal dinner.
- Stop studying early; do something calming.
- Set two alarms. Go to bed at your planned time (Chapter 22).

*Morning*
- Eat the breakfast you have practiced.
- Skip experiments with new foods or caffeine.
- Leave early enough to arrive with a buffer, not just in time.
- Do one minute of slow breathing (Chapter 23).

*In the room*
- Settle your things, adjust your seat, check your screen or paper.
- Write your checkpoint times on scratch paper if allowed.
- Open each section with the question method (Chapter 10).

*Breaks*
- Use the toilet, drink, eat the snack, move around. Avoid discussing the last section.

Praxis Tip: Pack the night before and put the bag by the door. Locating your ID at 7:15 a.m. is a form of exam stress you can simply skip.

**Worked Example: A morning timeline**

Suppose check-in is 8:30 a.m., the center is 40 minutes away, and you want a 30-minute arrival buffer.

- Arrive by 8:00 a.m. (30 minutes before check-in).
- Leave home by 7:20 a.m. (40 minutes of travel before 8:00).
- Allow 20 minutes of margin for traffic: leave by 7:00 a.m.
- Breakfast 45 minutes before leaving: 6:15 a.m.
- Wake at 5:45 a.m. (30 minutes to wake up and get ready before eating).
- If you need 8 hours of sleep, be asleep by 9:45 p.m.

Check: 8:30 − 0:30 = 8:00; 8:00 − 0:40 = 7:20; 7:20 − 0:20 = 7:00; 7:00 − 0:45 = 6:15; 6:15 − 0:30 = 5:45; 5:45 − 8:00 = 9:45 p.m. the night before.

*Skill used:* planning backward from a fixed time.

**Handling a bad section**

A section will sometimes go badly. It happens even to well-prepared candidates. The task is to prevent it from becoming two bad sections.

1. **Between sections,** take a slow breath and say, "That section is done." You cannot change it.
2. **Do not estimate your score** from how you felt. Candidates are poor judges, since the exam deliberately includes hard questions.
3. **Return to the process:** question method, pacing, next question.
4. **At the break,** don't compare notes or replay questions.

Praxis Tip: Hard questions are normal on a well-designed exam. If every question felt easy, the test might not be measuring much.

**After the exam**

Whatever the result, eat, rest, and do something unrelated to studying. If you can, jot down impressions for your own use, particularly if you may retake it (Chapter 28), but do not share question content with others. Doing so can violate the owner's rules.

**More Praxis Tips**

Praxis Tip: Bring a layer. Rooms are often colder than you expect, and cold is distracting.

Praxis Tip: Visit the toilet before check-in. Breaks have a limited length.

Praxis Tip: If something goes wrong with the testing equipment or room, tell the proctor immediately and ask what the process is. Don't struggle silently.

Praxis Tip: Keep a copy of your confirmation and a number to call in your bag.

**Common mistakes**

- Reading the rules the night before.
- Trying new foods or drinks.
- Studying late into the night.
- Discussing sections at the break.
- Leaving it to chance that the journey will take 40 minutes.

**Do this today**

1. Read the official rules and write down each requirement.
2. Make your own checklist from the list above.
3. Plan the morning timeline backward from check-in time.
4. Find out about accommodations deadlines if relevant.

**Recap**

Check the rules, prepare the logistics, keep the routine you have practiced, and treat a bad section as finished. Make the morning boring and the exam will be the only exciting part.
