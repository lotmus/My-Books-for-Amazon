# Strategy: Algebra and Number Sense Under Time Pressure

**What you will be able to do**

- Translate word problems into equations without losing a factor of two.
- Use plug-in numbers and back-solving as shortcuts when they save time.
- Avoid the handful of algebra slips that account for most lost points.

**Why it matters**

Algebra is rarely the point of an exam question. It is the vehicle. The question tests whether you can set the problem up, and then the algebra either carries you smoothly to an answer or runs you into a ditch. Under time pressure, the ditch is usually a sign error, a dropped parenthesis, or a half-remembered rule. Fortunately, there are very few ways to go wrong, and all of them can be rehearsed.

Praxis Tip: When you finish an algebra problem, substitute the answer back into the original statement. This takes ten seconds and catches most mistakes.

**Translating words into equations**

| Words | Math |
|---|---|
| "is," "equals," "results in" | = |
| "of" (with a fraction or percent) | × |
| "more than" | + (but order matters: "5 more than x" is x + 5) |
| "less than" | − (reversed: "5 less than x" is x − 5) |
| "twice as old as" | 2 × |
| "per," "each" | ÷ or a rate |
| "at least" | ≥ |
| "at most" | ≤ |

Define each unknown in a sentence before using it: "Let b = Ben's age now." This removes a surprising number of errors.

Praxis Tip: Write down what the question is asking you to find *before* you start. Solving for b and then answering with 2b is a classic.

**Worked Example 1: An age problem**

*Question.* Ana is twice as old as Ben. In 6 years, the sum of their ages will be 42. How old is Ben now?

Let b = Ben's age now. Then Ana's age is 2b. In 6 years they will be b + 6 and 2b + 6. Their sum:

(b + 6) + (2b + 6) = 42
3b + 12 = 42
3b = 30
b = 10

Ben is 10. *Check:* Ana is 20; in six years they are 16 and 26, and 16 + 26 = 42 ✓.

**Worked Example 2: Back-solving**

*Question.* Which value of x satisfies 3(x − 2) + 4 = 2x + 7?
A. 5   B. 7   C. 9   D. 11

*Algebra:* 3x − 6 + 4 = 2x + 7 → 3x − 2 = 2x + 7 → x = 9.

*Back-solve:* start with a middle choice, say B (7): left side 3(5) + 4 = 19, right side 2(7) + 7 = 21. Left is smaller, so a larger x is needed (the left side grows faster). Try C (9): left 3(7) + 4 = 25, right 2(9) + 7 = 25 ✓. 

Both routes agree: **C**.

Back-solving works best when choices are numbers and the algebra looks messy. Start with the middle value so that you can tell which direction to go.

**Worked Example 3: Plug-in numbers**

*Question.* If a and b are positive and a/b = 3/4, what is (a + b)/b?

Choose numbers: a = 3, b = 4. Then (3 + 4)/4 = 7/4. 

*Algebra check:* (a + b)/b = a/b + 1 = 3/4 + 1 = 7/4 ✓.

Plug-in numbers are especially useful when the answer choices are expressions with letters. Pick small numbers that satisfy the conditions, avoid 0 and 1 (which can make several choices come out equal), and test every choice that survives.

**Worked Example 4: A rate problem**

*Question.* One tap fills a tank in 6 hours; another fills it in 3 hours. How long do they take together?

Rates add: 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/6 + 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2 tank per hour. Time = 1 ÷ (1/2) = 2 hours. 

Sanity check: together they must beat the faster tap alone (3 hours), so an answer greater than 3 would be impossible. 2 passes.

*Skill used:* choosing the right shortcut (algebra, back-solving or plug-in numbers) and checking the answer.

**Slips to watch for**

- **Sign errors when distributing:** −(x − 3) = −x + 3, not −x − 3.
- **Dropping parentheses:** write them in until you are sure you can remove them.
- **Inequalities:** multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the inequality. For −2x > 6, divide by −2 and flip: x < −3.
- **Fractions:** a/b + c/d needs a common denominator; it is not (a + c)/(b + d).
- **Squaring sums:** (x + 3)² = x² + 6x + 9, not x² + 9.
- **Cancelling terms rather than factors:** you can cancel a common *factor*, not a common *term* in a sum.

Praxis Tip: Keep a list of the slips you actually make (your error log from Chapter 9 will show them). Most people have two or three favorites.

**More Praxis Tips**

Praxis Tip: Do the algebra on paper, in a column, one step per line. Compressing steps saves seconds and costs points.

Praxis Tip: If the algebra looks long, pause and ask whether a plug-in or a back-solve would be shorter.

Praxis Tip: Use the answer choices as information. If they are all positive and your answer is negative, check your work before you check the key.

Praxis Tip: Practice solving the same problem two ways. The second route is your error check, and it teaches you which method suits you.

Praxis Tip: Keep a tidy margin of scratch work with the problem number beside each line. When you return to a flagged question, you can resume instead of restarting.

**Common mistakes**

- Answering for the wrong unknown.
- Forgetting to flip an inequality.
- Choosing plug-in numbers that break the problem's conditions.
- Back-solving from an end choice instead of the middle.
- Skipping the substitution check.

**Do this today**

1. Solve five word problems, writing "Let ... = ..." before each.
2. For three of them, solve a second way (back-solve or plug-in).
3. Log any algebra slip with its cause in your error log.

**Recap**

Define the unknown, translate carefully, and pick the shortest safe method: algebra, back-solving or plug-in numbers. Check by substituting.
