# Practice: Logic and Argument Drills

**Timing and conditions**

Ten original questions. Allow about 15 minutes. No notes. For each question, apply the question method (Chapter 10): read, predict, eliminate, verify. After the set, log your misses with a cause (Chapter 9).

Praxis Tip: Before looking at the choices, write the conclusion of each argument in a few words. If you cannot, you have not yet read the question.

**Questions**

1. "If a vehicle is a bicycle, it has two wheels." Vehicle V has three wheels. Which must be true?
 A. V is a bicycle.  B. V has a motor.  C. V has been modified.  D. V is not a bicycle.

2. A school moved its start time later and noticed that test scores rose that year. The principal concludes that the later start time raised the scores. The principal's argument assumes that
 A. no other factor that could raise scores changed over the same period.
 B. students enjoy sleeping later.
 C. all schools should adopt later start times.
 D. test scores are the only measure of success.

3. Which of the following, if true, most seriously weakens the principal's argument in question 2?
 A. Attendance also improved.
 B. Many parents approve of the later start.
 C. In the same year, the district introduced a free tutoring program for all students.
 D. Teachers were surveyed about start times.

4. Which of the following, if true, most strengthens the principal's argument in question 2?
 A. The school's scores were already higher than the district average.
 B. Schools in the district that did not change their start times saw no change in scores that year.
 C. The new start time is popular with students.
 D. The school's lunch menu was updated.

5. "To enter the contest, you must be over 18." Maria is over 18. What follows?
 A. Being over 18 is necessary but, from this statement alone, not shown to be sufficient.
 B. Maria can enter the contest.
 C. Maria cannot enter the contest.
 D. Maria must enter the contest.

6. "Every successful coach I have met drinks coffee. So drinking coffee makes coaches successful." The reasoning is flawed because it
 A. relies on the opinion of a single expert.
 B. contradicts itself.
 C. appeals to emotion.
 D. mistakes a pattern in a few cases for proof of cause, ignoring other explanations.

7. "All nurses at the clinic are certified. Some certified staff are volunteers." Which must be true?
 A. Some nurses are volunteers.
 B. Anyone who is a nurse at the clinic is certified.
 C. All volunteers are nurses.
 D. Some volunteers are nurses.

8. A poll of people leaving a gym finds that 90 percent say regular exercise is important. The pollster concludes that 90 percent of the town's residents think regular exercise is important. The conclusion is most vulnerable to criticism because
 A. 90 percent is too high a number.
 B. the poll was conducted outdoors.
 C. gym-goers may not represent the town as a whole.
 D. exercise is not important.

9. "The bridge has cracked supports, and cracked supports can fail. Therefore, the bridge should be closed for inspection." The main conclusion of this argument is that
 A. the bridge should be closed for inspection.
 B. the bridge has cracked supports.
 C. cracked supports can fail.
 D. the bridge will fail.

10. "No reptiles are warm-blooded. All snakes are reptiles." Which statement follows?
 A. All warm-blooded animals are snakes.
 B. Some snakes are warm-blooded.
 C. All reptiles are snakes.
 D. No snakes are warm-blooded.

**Answers and explanations**

1. **D.** The contrapositive of "bicycle → two wheels" is "not two wheels → not a bicycle." V has three wheels, so it is not a bicycle. (A is wrong; B and C go beyond the statement.)
2. **A.** The argument moves from "scores rose after the change" to "the change caused the rise." That requires that no other relevant factor changed. (B, C, D are irrelevant to the link.)
3. **C.** A district-wide tutoring program is an alternative explanation for the rise. (A is consistent with either explanation. B and D are irrelevant.)
4. **B.** If schools without the change saw no rise, the change looks more likely to be responsible, since a general trend is made less likely. (A, C, D do not bear on cause.)
5. **A.** "Must be over 18" states a necessary condition. It does not say that being over 18 is enough (there may be other requirements). B and C overreach; D is unrelated.
6. **D.** Observing a pattern among a few coaches does not show that coffee causes success; other explanations (long hours, culture) exist. (A is not the problem. B and C do not describe the argument.)
7. **B.** It restates the first premise. A and D do not follow (volunteers who are certified may not be nurses). C reverses the relationship.
8. **C.** The sample (gym-goers) is probably unrepresentative. (A, B and D are not logical criticisms.)
9. **A.** The word "therefore" signals the conclusion. B and C are premises. D overstates the argument ("can fail," not "will fail").
10. **D.** If all snakes are reptiles and no reptiles are warm-blooded, no snakes are warm-blooded. (A and C reverse the relationships; B contradicts the premises.)

**Score and review**

Record each miss with its cause. Misses on 1 and 5 usually mean confusing necessary with sufficient or flipping a conditional. Misses on 3 and 4 usually mean attacking the wrong link. Redo them cold after three days.

Praxis Tip: For weaken and strengthen questions, restate the argument as "Because P, therefore C, assuming A." Then pick the choice that acts on A.

Praxis Tip: When an answer choice is true but irrelevant, cross it out and say "irrelevant." Naming the trap protects you next time.

Praxis Tip: Draw a small circle diagram for questions 7 and 10. Pictures catch reversed statements instantly.

Praxis Tip: If you found yourself arguing with the premise, stop. On these questions the premises are given. Your job is to reason from them.

Praxis Tip: Time yourself per question. The conditional and quantifier questions should be faster than the weaken and strengthen ones. If not, practice them more.
