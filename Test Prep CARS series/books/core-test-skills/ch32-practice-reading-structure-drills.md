# Practice: Reading Structure Drills

**Timing and conditions**

Two original passages, five questions each. Allow about 20 minutes. Read each passage once for structure (Chapter 15), writing a five-word note for each paragraph on scratch paper. Then answer the questions, returning to the text for detail. After the set, log your misses.

Praxis Tip: Write the paragraph notes first. Do not read the questions until you have a skeleton of the passage.

**Passage 1**

(1) Cities across the world are replacing older street lamps with light-emitting diodes (LEDs), and the case for doing so is easy to state. LEDs use less electricity, last longer, and cost less to maintain, savings that municipal budgets notice.

(2) The picture is less simple at night. Some LEDs emit a high proportion of blue-rich light, which scatters more in the air and can add to sky glow. A number of researchers have suggested that such light may also disturb nocturnal wildlife and, for residents, may interfere with sleep, although the evidence is still developing and varies by setting.

(3) None of this means cities should keep the old lamps. Warmer-toned fixtures, shields that direct light downward, and dimming late at night can capture most of the savings while reducing the drawbacks. The sensible course is therefore not to choose between energy and darkness but to choose the lamp, and the schedule, with care.

1. The main idea of the passage is that
 A. LEDs are clearly better than older lamps in every respect.
 B. LED lighting is harmful to wildlife.
 C. cities should adopt LEDs but choose and manage them carefully.
 D. municipal budgets should determine lighting decisions.

2. The second paragraph primarily serves to
 A. prove that LEDs harm human health.
 B. introduce complications to the case made in the first paragraph.
 C. describe how LEDs are manufactured.
 D. argue that older lamps should be restored.

3. Which of the following is NOT stated in the passage as an advantage of LEDs?
 A. They use less electricity.
 B. They last longer.
 C. They cost less to maintain.
 D. They are brighter than any other kind of lamp.

4. The passage most strongly suggests that
 A. the choice of fixture and schedule can change LEDs' side effects.
 B. all LED fixtures affect wildlife in the same way.
 C. cities should return to the older lamps.
 D. savings from LEDs are insignificant.

5. The author's tone is best described as
 A. alarmed  B. balanced and measured  C. dismissive  D. enthusiastic

**Passage 2**

(1) The public library is often imagined as a quiet warehouse for books, and for much of its history that description was not far wrong. Yet in recent decades many libraries have broadened their role, offering computers, meeting rooms, job-search help and classes alongside their shelves.

(2) Skeptics ask whether this drift dilutes the library's purpose. A library, they argue, is defined by its collection, and every hour spent on a résumé workshop is an hour not spent on reading.

(3) The objection overlooks what the library's founders were actually after: access to information and the means of self-improvement for people who could not buy their own. In an age when information arrives through screens as much as through bound pages, providing a computer and someone to help use it serves that old aim directly. The tools have changed; the purpose has not.

6. The primary purpose of the passage is to
 A. describe the history of printed books.
 B. criticize libraries for abandoning their collections.
 C. argue that broadened library services are consistent with the library's original purpose.
 D. propose that libraries close their computer rooms.

7. The skeptics described in paragraph 2 would most likely agree that
 A. libraries should offer more classes.
 B. computers have no place in public life.
 C. books are obsolete.
 D. libraries should focus chiefly on their collections.

8. The sentence "The tools have changed; the purpose has not" functions to
 A. summarize the author's central claim.
 B. introduce a new topic.
 C. concede the skeptics' point.
 D. give an example of a job-search workshop.

9. The passage implies that the library's founders wanted to
 A. limit the library to printed books.
 B. restrict membership to scholars.
 C. widen access for people who could not afford their own resources.
 D. replace schools.

10. Which of the following, if true, would most weaken the author's argument?
 A. Many libraries offer computers.
 B. Historical records show the founders explicitly intended libraries to hold books only and to refuse other services.
 C. Library visits have risen in recent years.
 D. Some libraries have meeting rooms.

**Answers and explanations**

1. **C.** Paragraph 3 states it directly. (A ignores paragraph 2. B and D are partial or unsupported.)
2. **B.** Paragraph 2 begins "The picture is less simple" and complicates paragraph 1. (A overstates "may" and "still developing.")
3. **D.** "Brighter than any other lamp" is never stated. A, B and C are stated in paragraph 1.
4. **A.** Paragraph 3 says warmer-toned fixtures, shields and dimming reduce drawbacks. (B and C are contradicted; D contradicts paragraph 1.)
5. **B.** The author gives both sides and ends with a measured recommendation.
6. **C.** The passage's thesis is in paragraph 3. (B reverses the position.)
7. **D.** The skeptics "argue that a library is defined by its collection."
8. **A.** It compresses the author's argument into a single sentence.
9. **C.** Paragraph 3: "access to information and the means of self-improvement for people who could not buy their own."
10. **B.** It directly undermines the author's claim about the founders' aims. (A, C and D describe current practice, not the founders' intent.)

**Score and review**

Questions 3 and 9 reward careful returning to the text; if you missed them, check whether you answered from memory. Questions 5 and 8 test tone and function, which depend on the structure map. Log the cause of each miss.

Praxis Tip: For "NOT stated" questions, confirm that three choices are in the text and one is not. Do the confirming rather than trusting your impression.

Praxis Tip: For weaken questions about an argument's premise, look for evidence that attacks the premise directly.

Praxis Tip: After each passage, state the main point in one sentence without looking. If you cannot, reread the first and last sentences of each paragraph.

Praxis Tip: Be wary of choices with extreme words (clearly, every, never). Passages that discuss trade-offs rarely support them.

Praxis Tip: Practice with dull passages on purpose. Interest is not a prerequisite for mapping.
