# Practice: Estimation and Number Sense Drills

**Timing and conditions**

Twelve original questions. Allow about 12 minutes (one minute per question), with no calculator and no notes. Work on scratch paper. Estimate first; calculate only when the choices are close. After the set, read the explanations and log every miss in your error log (Chapter 9).

Praxis Tip: Time yourself, but also note which questions felt like they needed a calculation. Those are the ones to practice estimating.

**Questions**

1. About what is 49.8 × 20.1?
 A. 1,000  B. 2,000  C. 5,000  D. 10,000

2. What is 15 percent of 240?
 A. 12  B. 24  C. 30  D. 36

3. A price falls 30 percent, then rises 30 percent. Compared with the original price, the final price is
 A. the same  B. 9 percent lower  C. 9 percent higher  D. 30 percent lower

4. Which fraction is largest?
 A. 3/5  B. 5/9  C. 4/7  D. 7/12

5. What is (4 × 10⁶)(5 × 10⁻²)?
 A. 2 × 10⁴  B. 9 × 10⁴  C. 2 × 10⁵  D. 2 × 10⁸

6. A train travels 180 km in 2.5 hours at a steady speed. What is its speed in meters per second?
 A. 7.2 m/s  B. 14.4 m/s  C. 18 m/s  D. 20 m/s

7. Blue and yellow paint are mixed in the ratio 3 : 5, making 40 liters in total. How many liters are yellow?
 A. 15  B. 20  C. 25  D. 30

8. Which is the most plausible time for a person to walk 10 km at a normal walking pace?
 A. 20 minutes  B. About 2 hours  C. 10 hours  D. 24 hours

9. What is the average of 12, 15, 18, 21 and 24?
 A. 12  B. 15  C. 16  D. 18

10. A quantity rises from 80 to 100. What is the percent increase?
 A. 25 percent  B. 20 percent  C. 80 percent  D. 125 percent

11. What is 0.0045 × 2,000,000?
 A. 900  B. 9,000  C. 90,000  D. 900,000

12. Which equals 1/8 expressed as a percent?
 A. 0.125 percent  B. 8 percent  C. 12.5 percent  D. 125 percent

**Answers and explanations**

1. **A.** 49.8 ≈ 50 and 20.1 ≈ 20, and 50 × 20 = 1,000. (Choices are well separated; estimation suffices. Exact: 1,000.98.)
2. **D.** 10 percent = 24, 5 percent = 12, so 15 percent = 24 + 12 = 36. (A is 5 percent. B is 10 percent. C is 12.5 percent.)
3. **B.** Use 100: 100 × 0.70 = 70; 70 × 1.30 = 91, which is 9 percent below 100. (A is the "cancel-out" trap: the changes apply to different bases.)
4. **A.** As decimals: 3/5 = 0.600, 5/9 ≈ 0.556, 4/7 ≈ 0.571, 7/12 ≈ 0.583. The largest is 3/5.
5. **C.** (4 × 5) × 10⁶⁻² = 20 × 10⁴ = 2 × 10⁵. (A forgets to carry the factor of ten. B adds the coefficients. D adds the exponents instead of 6 + (−2).)
6. **D.** 180 ÷ 2.5 = 72 km/h; divide by 3.6 to get m/s: 72 ÷ 3.6 = 20 m/s. (A, B and C divide 72 by 10, 5 and 4: the right divisor is 3.6.)
7. **C.** Total parts 3 + 5 = 8; one part = 40 ÷ 8 = 5 liters; yellow = 5 × 5 = 25 liters. (A is blue: 3 × 5 = 15.)
8. **B.** A normal walking pace is about 5 km/h, so 10 km takes about 2 hours. The other choices fail a plausibility check.
9. **D.** The numbers are evenly spaced, so the mean equals the middle value, 18. (Check: 90 ÷ 5 = 18.)
10. **A.** (100 − 80) ÷ 80 = 20/80 = 25 percent. (B uses the new value as the base. D adds 100 + 25.)
11. **B.** 0.0045 = 4.5 × 10⁻³ and 2,000,000 = 2 × 10⁶. Product = 9 × 10³ = 9,000.
12. **C.** 1/8 = 0.125 = 12.5 percent. (A forgets to multiply by 100.)

**Score and review**

Count your correct answers. Then log every miss with a cause: content gap, misread, rushed, trap choice, or calculation slip (Chapter 9). Questions 3, 5 and 10 are the classic traps (base error, exponent error, base error); if you missed those, rehearse them from Chapter 13.

Praxis Tip: Redo your misses after three days, cold. If you get one wrong twice, make it a flashcard.

Praxis Tip: For any question where you reached for pencil math, ask whether a friendlier number would have worked. Practicing that question is the point of the set.

Praxis Tip: If your score is high but slow, repeat the set with a 9-minute limit. If it is fast but low, repeat with a 14-minute limit and a rule that every answer must pass a plausibility check.

Praxis Tip: Check that your answers were spread across A-D. If you picked the same letter for six in a row, look again at those six.

Praxis Tip: Write down the one estimation habit that helped most. Use it first next time.
