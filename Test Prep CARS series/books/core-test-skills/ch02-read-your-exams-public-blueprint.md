# Strategy: Read Your Exam's Public Blueprint

**What you will be able to do**

- Find the official, current description of {EXAM} and extract the facts that matter.
- Fill in the exam fact sheet that every later chapter refers to.
- Recognize unreliable sources, such as rumors about "what is really on it".

**Why it matters**

Your study plan can only be as accurate as your picture of the exam. People routinely prepare for the version of a test they heard about from a cousin, a forum, or a video made four years ago. The exam owner publishes the format, content areas, and scoring description for free, and it is the only source that is allowed to be right. Everything else is hearsay with better lighting.

This chapter is a worksheet. It will take about an hour and save considerably more.

Praxis Tip: Do this chapter before buying anything. A surprising amount of money has been spent on books that do not match the current exam.

**Where to look**

1. **The exam owner's official website.** Search for the exam name plus "official" and confirm the address belongs to the organization that runs the test. Look for pages called something like "About the exam," "Content outline," "Test format," "Scoring," and "Registration."
2. **The official free guide or candidate handbook**, if one exists. Many exam owners publish a PDF.
3. **Your institution, if relevant.** For a course exam, the syllabus and the instructor's stated objectives play the role of the public blueprint.
4. **Official sample or practice materials the owner provides.** You may study these for yourself, but they are copyrighted, so you must never copy, retype, or paraphrase them into your own materials.

Praxis Tip: Write down the date you last checked each page. Exam formats change, and "I read it last year" is not a verification.

**Worked Example: Filling the sheet for an invented exam**

The "Sample Standardized Exam" (SSE) below is fictional, used only to show the process. Do not copy these facts anywhere.

| Item | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Sections | Quantitative, Verbal, Data (three) | SSE candidate guide, p. 4 |
| Time per section | 60, 50, 40 minutes | same |
| Questions | 40, 35, 30 | same |
| Penalty | No penalty for wrong answers | same, p. 9 |
| Format | Computer-based, one 10-minute break | same, p. 6 |
| Calculators | On-screen basic calculator in Data only | same, p. 7 |

*Reading the sheet.* Pace per question is time divided by questions. For Quantitative: 60 minutes ÷ 40 questions = 1.5 minutes each. Verbal: 50 ÷ 35 ≈ 1.43 minutes each. Data: 40 ÷ 30 ≈ 1.33 minutes each. Two things pop out: the Data section is the fastest-paced, and with no penalty for wrong answers, leaving an answer blank is never smart.

*Skill used:* turning a published format into a pacing target and a strategic rule.

Praxis Tip: Compute your minutes-per-question for every section now and write it on a card. It will anchor your practice in Chapter 11.

**Spotting unreliable sources**

- **Forum posts and videos** claiming "the real exam always includes X": treat as a lead to check against the official page, not as a fact.
- **Anything undated.** An old format guide is worse than none, since it is confident and wrong.
- **Pages that promise "leaked" questions or "real exam questions".** Aside from being unreliable, circulating actual exam questions typically violates the owner's rules and copyright. Stay away from them; they can also get your score canceled.
- **Commercial pages that quote scoring as a guarantee** ("score 20 points higher"). Official sites describe the test, not your future.

Praxis Tip: If two sources disagree, the official page wins. If the official page is vague, email the exam owner and keep the reply.

**More Praxis Tips**

Praxis Tip: Screenshot or save the official page with its date. Pages change quietly, and "it used to say" is not evidence.

Praxis Tip: Note which content areas the owner says are emphasized and which are merely listed. Time should follow the stated weighting, not your favorite topic.

Praxis Tip: If registration deadlines or retake rules matter to your plan, put them in your calendar the day you find them.

**Common mistakes**

- Using last year's format.
- Mixing up two similar exams with similar names.
- Assuming your institution's requirement matches the exam's own scale.
- Never checking whether calculators or notes are allowed, then discovering the answer on test day.

**Do this today**

1. Open the exam owner's official page for {EXAM}.
2. Copy the fact sheet into your notebook, fill in every row, and write "not stated" in any gap.
3. Compute minutes per question for each section.
4. Save the sheet where you can find it. Every later chapter will ask for it.

**Recap**

The official blueprint is free, current and authoritative. Fill in the fact sheet, calculate pacing, and treat everything else as hearsay until it agrees with the official source.
