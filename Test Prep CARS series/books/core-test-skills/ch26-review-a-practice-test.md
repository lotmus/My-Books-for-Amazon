# Strategy: Review a Practice Test

**What you will be able to do**

- Run a structured review of a simulation within 24 hours.
- Analyze results by topic, cause, timing and confidence.
- Turn the findings into a one-week fix plan and a re-attempt schedule.

**Why it matters**

A practice test is worth roughly the effort you spend afterward. Many people take a test, look at the score, feel something, and move on. This is like weighing yourself and calling it a diet. The review is where the learning happens, and it takes longer than the test itself. For a simulation, plan at least as long on review as on the sitting.

Praxis Tip: Schedule the review before you take the test. Without a time set, it quietly never happens.

**The review in five passes**

**Pass 1: Score and feelings (10 minutes).** Record the raw result by section. Note how you feel. Then put the feelings aside. A single score is a noisy estimate.

**Pass 2: Every wrong or guessed answer.** For each, record in your error log (Chapter 9): topic, cause code, what you chose and why, and the correct reasoning. Include lucky guesses and slow-but-right answers.

**Pass 3: Timing.** Compare your actual checkpoints with the plan from Chapter 11. Where did time go? Which question types ran long?

**Pass 4: Confidence.** Compare confidence ratings with accuracy (Chapter 12). Find high-confidence misses first.

**Pass 5: Patterns and a plan.** Count errors by topic and by cause. Pick the top two issues and write one concrete fix for each.

Praxis Tip: Review in the order you took the test. Patterns across the sitting (fatigue, a late slump) only appear in sequence.

**Worked Example: Reading a pace curve**

On the fictional SSE Quantitative section (40 questions, 60 minutes, plan from Chapter 11: 1.375 minutes per question, 55 minutes of working time, 5 for review), a learner recorded her clock times at four checkpoints.

| Checkpoint | Plan (min) | Actual (min) | Segment time actual | Segment plan | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q10 | 13.75 | 15 | 15 | 13.75 | +1.25 |
| Q20 | 27.5 | 31 | 16 | 13.75 | +2.25 |
| Q30 | 41.25 | 44 | 13 | 13.75 | −0.75 |
| Q40 | 55 | 60 | 16 | 13.75 | +2.25 |

Check: segments 15 + 16 + 13 + 16 = 60 minutes. Differences: +1.25 + 2.25 − 0.75 + 2.25 = +5.0 minutes over plan, which exactly uses up the 5-minute review buffer.

*Reading it.* She ran about 3.5 minutes behind plan by question 20, recovered a little in questions 21-30, and then lost time again in the last block, finishing with no review time at all. The two slow blocks (Q11-20 and Q31-40) are where to look. Pulling up those questions shows which ones ate time (long calculations, a data-interpretation set), and the fix is the flag-and-move rule applied to that question type.

*Skill used:* using timing data to locate a specific, fixable problem.

**Re-attempt rules**

1. **Do not look at the answer first.** Try the question again after a gap of several days.
2. **Redo misses cold:** same conditions, no notes.
3. **Redo wrong guesses and lucky guesses** as well.
4. **Retire a question** after you have answered it right twice in a row, separated by days.
5. **Track the re-attempt result** in the log. A question you still miss is high priority.

Praxis Tip: A question you miss twice is not a mistake any more. It is a topic. Add a short lesson or a card set for it.

**A one-week fix plan**

After the review, build a single page:

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Top two issues | e.g., misreads on data questions; slow on long calculations |
| Fix for each | One concrete action each |
| Study blocks | Which sessions this week go to which issues |
| Re-attempt dates | When missed questions come back |
| Next checkpoint | The next simulation or mini-test date |

Praxis Tip: Limit yourself to two fixes. A fix list with ten items is a wish list.

**More Praxis Tips**

Praxis Tip: Do the review when you are fresh, not immediately after the test while you are tired and emotional. A rest, a meal, a walk, then the review.

Praxis Tip: Look at questions you got right but felt unsure about. They show what you know without knowing that you know it.

Praxis Tip: Compare your performance across sections for stamina. If the last section drops, work on endurance and food/break strategy.

Praxis Tip: Keep a running chart of simulation scores and causes of error. A steady decline in misreads is a better sign than a one-off high score.

**Common mistakes**

- Looking at the score and stopping.
- Reviewing only the wrong answers and ignoring guesses.
- Reviewing the same day when too tired to think.
- Making a fix list too long to follow.
- Never re-attempting the missed questions.

**Do this today**

1. Schedule the review for your next practice test, 24 hours after taking it.
2. Make the one-page fix-plan template.
3. If you already have a recent test, compute your pace curve and find the slow segments.

**Recap**

Review in five passes: score, errors, timing, confidence, patterns. Make a two-item fix plan and re-attempt the missed questions after a gap. The review is the lesson.
