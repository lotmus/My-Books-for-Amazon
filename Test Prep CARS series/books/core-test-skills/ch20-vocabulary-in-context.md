# Strategy: Vocabulary in Context

**What you will be able to do**

- Work out the meaning of an unfamiliar word from the sentence around it.
- Use word parts (prefixes, roots, suffixes) as clues.
- Build a vocabulary list that actually sticks.

**Why it matters**

Whether or not {EXAM} tests vocabulary directly, vocabulary quietly limits how much of every passage you understand. The good news is that learning words is a routine job, and the better news is that you rarely need to know a word fully to use it well. A rough sense of "positive, negative, big, small" is often enough to answer the question.

Praxis Tip: Aim for a working sense of meaning, not a dictionary entry. In a passage, knowing that "ephemeral" means short-lived beats not knowing it, even if you could not define it perfectly.

**Context clues**

When you meet an unknown word, look in the surrounding sentences for one of these clues.

| Clue type | What it looks like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | The text defines the word | "The mayor was *taciturn*, a man of few words." |
| Synonym | A similar word nearby | "The *loquacious*, talkative guest ..." |
| Antonym / contrast | An opposite word, often after "but" | "Unlike her *garrulous* brother, she rarely spoke." |
| Example | Examples show the meaning | "*Ephemeral* things, such as soap bubbles and morning mist ..." |
| Inference | The overall situation implies the meaning | "After the speech, the audience was *rapt*, not a cough in the room." |

Praxis Tip: Read the whole sentence, and often the one before it. The clue is rarely right beside the word.

**Worked Example: Three words, three clues**

1. "Unlike her *garrulous* brother, who talked through every meal, Ada barely said a word."
 *Clue:* contrast ("unlike") with talking through every meal. *Meaning:* very talkative.

2. "The festival was *ephemeral*; by Monday morning, the stalls and the crowds were gone."
 *Clue:* "by Monday ... gone." *Meaning:* lasting a very short time.

3. "Her *circumspect* reply avoided saying anything she might later regret."
 *Clue:* the reply avoids anything regrettable. *Meaning:* careful, cautious.

*Skill used:* converting surrounding information into an approximate meaning, then testing it by substituting your guess into the sentence.

Praxis Tip: Always put your guessed meaning back into the sentence. If it does not fit smoothly, your guess is off.

**Word parts**

Many English words are built from Latin and Greek pieces. A handful of common ones open up hundreds of words.

| Part | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| bene- | good, well | benevolent (wishing well), beneficial |
| mal- | bad | malevolent (wishing harm), malfunction |
| circum- | around | circumspect (looking around, careful), circumference |
| -spect / spec | look | inspect, spectator, circumspect |
| contra-/counter- | against | contradict, contrary |
| ambi- | both | ambivalent (pulled both ways), ambidextrous |
| loqu- | speak | loquacious (talkative), eloquent |
| bi- | two | bilateral, biennial |
| -ology | study of | biology, sociology |
| un-/in-/im- | not | unlikely, incorrect, impossible |

Word parts are clues, not guarantees. Meanings drift. Use them to make a good first guess, then check it against the context.

Praxis Tip: When a word has a familiar part, say the part's meaning aloud ("circum... around, spect... look") before guessing. It gives your guess a head start.

**Building a list that sticks**

1. **Collect words from your own reading,** including the passages you practice with. Words you actually meet are the ones you will meet again.
2. **Record the sentence,** not just the word. Context is how memory fixes the meaning.
3. **Write a plain-English definition** in your own words.
4. **Make a flashcard** (Chapter 6) with the word on the front and a short definition and a new example sentence on the back.
5. **Aim for a modest number,** say 5 new words a day at most, and review them on the ladder from Chapter 6.
6. **Use the word** in speech or writing within a day or two.

Praxis Tip: Learn words in families (talkative: loquacious, garrulous, voluble) so each one reinforces the others, but keep a note of the small differences between them.

**More Praxis Tips**

Praxis Tip: When two answer choices are near-synonyms, look for the one that matches the *tone* (positive, negative, neutral) of the sentence.

Praxis Tip: Beware of words you think you know. Common words have secondary meanings ("fine," "novel," "mean") and exams enjoy these.

Praxis Tip: Read widely and a little above your comfort level: long-form journalism, popular science, well-edited essays. Vocabulary grows by exposure far more than by lists alone.

Praxis Tip: If you cannot guess the meaning, work out whether the word is positive or negative from its role in the sentence. Even this crude sorting often settles a question.

**Common mistakes**

- Stopping at the dictionary definition and never using the word.
- Learning isolated words without context.
- Trusting a root over the context.
- Ignoring secondary meanings of familiar words.
- Trying to learn 50 words a day and retaining none.

**Do this today**

1. Read a page of a good article and write down three unfamiliar words.
2. For each, write the sentence, a guessed meaning, and the clue that led to it.
3. Check the meaning, make a flashcard, and use one of the words today.

**Recap**

Use context first, word parts second, and a small steady list built from your own reading. A good guess about an unknown word is usually enough.
