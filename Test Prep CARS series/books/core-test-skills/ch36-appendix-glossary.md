# Appendix: Glossary

Plain definitions for terms used in this book.

Praxis Tip: Add terms from your own exam as you meet them. A personal glossary is a quiet but dependable study tool.

**A**

**Assumption.** An unstated claim that must be true for an argument to work.

**B**

**Back-solving.** Testing the answer choices in the problem, starting with a middle value, to find which one works.

**Baseline diagnostic.** A practice test taken before studying, used to measure your starting point.

**Buffer.** Deliberately unscheduled time in a plan, used to absorb delays.

**C**

**Calibration.** How well your confidence matches your accuracy.

**Chunking.** Grouping separate items into meaningful units so that more can be held in memory.

**Cloze deletion.** A flashcard format with a word or phrase blanked out.

**Contrapositive.** For "if P, then Q," the statement "if not Q, then not P." Always equivalent to the original.

**Control group.** The comparison group in an experiment that does not receive the treatment.

**Converse.** For "if P, then Q," the statement "if Q, then P." Not equivalent to the original.

**Correlation.** A statistical relationship in which two things vary together. It does not by itself show cause.

**D**

**Dependent variable.** The outcome measured in an experiment.

**E**

**Error bars.** Marks on a graph showing variability or uncertainty. Check what they represent.

**Error log.** A record of mistakes with their causes and fixes.

**Estimation.** Calculating approximately, using rounded numbers, to find or check an answer.

**Expected value (EV).** The average outcome of a decision over many repetitions.

**F**

**Flag-and-move.** Guessing, marking a question, and moving on when it exceeds a time ceiling.

**I**

**Independent variable.** The factor an experimenter changes on purpose.

**Interleaving.** Mixing different topics or problem types within a practice session.

**L**

**Leitner method.** A paper-box system for spacing flashcard reviews.

**M**

**Mean.** The sum of values divided by their count.

**Median.** The middle value of a sorted list.

**Method of loci.** A memory technique that places vivid images at stops along a familiar route.

**Mnemonic.** A memory aid built on something familiar, such as an acronym or rhyme.

**Mode.** The most frequent value.

**N**

**Necessary condition.** Something that must be true for an outcome to occur, though it may not be enough by itself.

**O**

**Outlier.** A value far from the others in a data set.

**P**

**P-value.** The probability, if there were no real effect, of observing results at least as extreme as those found. It is not the probability that a hypothesis is true.

**Pacing.** Distributing your time across a section so that you finish it.

**Premise.** A reason offered in support of a conclusion.

**R**

**Retrieval practice.** Recalling information from memory, without looking, in order to strengthen it.

**S**

**Sample.** The subset of a population that is actually measured.

**Sampling bias.** A systematic tilt in who is included in a sample.

**Simulation.** A full-length practice test under realistic conditions.

**Spaced repetition.** Reviewing material at increasing intervals.

**Standard deviation (SD).** A measure of typical distance from the mean.

**Sufficient condition.** Something that guarantees an outcome.

**T**

**Teach-back.** Explaining an idea aloud, as to a newcomer, to find gaps in understanding.

**Truncated axis.** A graph axis that does not start at zero, which can exaggerate differences.

**V**

**Verification.** Checking an answer against the question and the source before committing.
