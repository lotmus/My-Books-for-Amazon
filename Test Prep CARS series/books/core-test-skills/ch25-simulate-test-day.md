# Strategy: Simulate Test Day

**What you will be able to do**

- Plan and run a full-length practice test under realistic conditions.
- Schedule simulations across your study plan.
- Use simulations to build stamina, pacing and calm, not just to get a score.

**Why it matters**

Knowing the material is one skill. Performing under the exam's conditions for the exam's full length is another. A runner who has trained on short sprints should not be surprised when the marathon is long. Full-length simulations teach you how your attention, energy and nerves behave across {TIME_PER_SECTION} per section and the full sitting for {EXAM}, and they teach it while the stakes are low.

Praxis Tip: A simulation is rehearsal, not judgment. Run it to learn, not to be graded.

**How many, and when**

- Plan about **four to six** full-length simulations across a typical plan, spaced every few weeks. Fewer is fine if time is short; more is rarely better than careful review of fewer.
- Take the first early enough to inform the plan (after the baseline in Chapter 4 and a first block of study).
- Take the **last one at least about 10 days before the exam,** so you have time to act on what you learn and recover.
- Put them on the calendar now. Unscheduled simulations tend to be postponed indefinitely.

Praxis Tip: Do not take a simulation in the last two or three days. It cannot teach you anything you can use, and a bad one can rattle you.

**Use legitimate material**

Use official practice material provided by the exam owner, or reputable original practice tests. Do not use leaked or "real recalled" questions. They are unreliable, can violate the owner's rules, and can put your score at risk. Official material is a limited resource, so save the most representative ones for the final simulations.

Praxis Tip: Keep a record of which official practice tests you have used. They are scarce, and retaking one you have already seen tells you little.

**Recreating conditions**

Match as many of these as you can:

| Condition | How |
|---|---|
| Timing | Use the real section lengths and break lengths from your fact sheet ({TIME_PER_SECTION}, {TEST_FORMAT}) |
| Start time | Begin at the time of day your real exam is scheduled |
| Interface | Use the same format (computer or paper) and allowed tools |
| Environment | Quiet, uninterrupted, a desk, minimal notes |
| Phone | Off and in another room |
| Food and drink | Eat what you plan to eat on test day; take breaks only when the real exam does |
| Clothing | Dress as you plan to dress |
| Scratch paper | Use only what the exam allows |

Praxis Tip: Do at least one simulation at a test center or a library, somewhere unfamiliar. It is cheap rehearsal for the feeling of being in a new room.

**Worked Example: A simulation timeline for the fictional SSE**

From Chapter 2, the invented SSE has three sections of 60, 50 and 40 minutes and a 10-minute break. Allow 20 minutes at the start for check-in and instructions.

- Check-in and instructions: 20 minutes.
- Section 1: 60 minutes. Section 2: 50 minutes. Break: 10 minutes. Section 3: 40 minutes.
- Total sitting: 20 + 60 + 50 + 10 + 40 = 180 minutes = 3 hours.
- Add 15 minutes before for arrival and settling, and about 90 minutes afterward for a first look at results and a note of impressions. Block roughly 5 to 5.5 hours in total.

Plan the review (Chapter 26) separately, within 24 hours, at a time you will be fresh.

*Skill used:* turning a published format into a realistic calendar block.

**Dealing with what the simulation shows**

A simulation will show you things that questions in isolation cannot:

- **Stamina:** does accuracy drop in the last section?
- **Pacing:** where does time disappear?
- **Nerves:** when does your attention wobble?
- **Routine:** do your breakfast, break food and caffeine plan work?

Write down observations immediately after finishing, in a few lines. They fade quickly.

Praxis Tip: Within 15 minutes after finishing, write: what felt easy, what felt hard, where I lost time, how my energy changed. These notes are gold for the review.

**More Praxis Tips**

Praxis Tip: Treat the break as part of the test. Practice eating, drinking and stretching in the allowed time, without checking your phone.

Praxis Tip: Do not stop the clock for interruptions. If something genuinely derails you, note it and consider the section void.

Praxis Tip: Use the real checkpoints from Chapter 11. A simulation is where the pacing plan gets tested.

Praxis Tip: Do not look at the answers until the whole test is finished. Peeking turns a simulation into a series of small quizzes.

**Common mistakes**

- Taking a simulation in several short sittings.
- Pausing the timer.
- Looking at answers mid-test.
- Scheduling them too close to the exam.
- Treating the score as a prediction and the review as optional.

**Do this today**

1. Count how many simulations your plan allows and put the dates on your calendar.
2. Check which official practice tests exist for {EXAM} and note them.
3. Draft the day's timeline using the table above.

**Recap**

Simulate the real thing, with real timing and real conditions, on a handful of scheduled dates. Take notes straight after, and spend more effort on the review than on the test.
