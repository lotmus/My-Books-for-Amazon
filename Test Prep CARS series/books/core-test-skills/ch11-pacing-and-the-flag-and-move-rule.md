# Strategy: Pacing and the Flag-and-Move Rule

**What you will be able to do**

- Convert the exam's time limit into a per-question budget and checkpoints.
- Apply a flag-and-move rule so one hard question cannot eat a section.
- Practice pacing deliberately, not just hope for it.

**Why it matters**

Time is a fixed resource, and every question on the exam is worth about the same. A question that takes five minutes is worth the same as one that takes forty seconds, so the five-minute question is, in cold arithmetic, a bad purchase. Learners who run out of time almost never do so because they were slow throughout; they do so because one or two questions swallowed a whole chunk of the section.

Praxis Tip: Think of time as a budget. You would not spend a quarter of your monthly income on a single sandwich, however good the sandwich.

**Building a pacing plan**

1. **Get the numbers** from your fact sheet (Chapter 2): {TIME_PER_SECTION} and {QUESTION_COUNT}.
2. **Reserve a review buffer.** Keep roughly 5 minutes (or about 8 to 10 percent) at the end of each section for flagged questions.
3. **Compute the working budget.** Minutes available for questions = total − buffer. Minutes per question = working budget ÷ number of questions.
4. **Set checkpoints** at regular intervals (every 10 questions, or each quarter of the section), noting the clock time you should reach.
5. **Practice with the checkpoints** until the pace feels familiar.

Praxis Tip: Write the checkpoint times on your scratch paper at the start of the section if the exam allows it. Doing so takes 20 seconds and gives you a map for the next hour.

**Worked Example: A pacing plan for the fictional SSE**

From Chapter 2, the invented Quantitative section has 40 questions in 60 minutes.

- Buffer: 5 minutes. Working time: 60 − 5 = 55 minutes.
- Per question: 55 ÷ 40 = 1.375 minutes (about 1 minute 23 seconds).
- Checkpoints:
 - After question 10: 10 × 1.375 = 13.75 minutes, so about 14 minutes elapsed.
 - After question 20: 27.5 minutes.
 - After question 30: 41.25 minutes.
 - After question 40: 55 minutes, leaving 5 for flagged questions.

If at question 20 the clock reads 31 minutes, the learner is about 3.5 minutes behind. The right response is not panic, it is a small adjustment: take the next few easy questions a little faster and flag anything that needs more than about two and a half minutes.

*Skill used:* turning a time limit into a map you can check mid-section.

**The flag-and-move rule**

Decide in advance a ceiling for any single question. A common choice is roughly twice your average time (here, about 2.5 to 3 minutes). When you hit it:

1. Make your best guess now (Chapter 12). Never leave an answer blank when blanks are not penalized.
2. Flag the question if the interface allows.
3. Move on without looking back at it.
4. Return only if there is time at the end.

The first pass should collect all the easy and medium points. The second pass is for the hard ones.

Praxis Tip: Guess *before* you flag. Then, if the clock runs out, you have still given yourself the odds on that question.

**When you are behind, and when you are ahead**

*Behind:* do not skip ahead blindly. Speed up on the easy questions by trimming your verification step, and flag the long calculations for the end.

*Ahead:* do not spend the saved time rushing out of the section. Use the spare minutes to verify flagged questions, not to relax into a lower gear.

Praxis Tip: Being slightly ahead early is a good thing, but do not bank it. Hard questions are often bunched together, and the early minutes may be needed later.

**More Praxis Tips**

Praxis Tip: Practice flag-and-move in ordinary study sets, with a timer, not only in simulations. A skill learned only on test day is mostly a rumor.

Praxis Tip: Learn how your exam's interface flags questions and navigates between them (see the {TEST_FORMAT} details in your fact sheet). Fumbling with buttons costs time and composure.

Praxis Tip: Do not look at the clock after every question. Look at the checkpoints. Constant clock-watching feels like control and behaves like anxiety.

Praxis Tip: After each practice set, note where the time went. If one question type always runs long, either train it or plan to postpone it.

**Common mistakes**

- Giving every question equal time, regardless of difficulty.
- No buffer for review.
- Refusing to leave a question unfinished because of pride.
- Leaving an answer blank when there is no penalty.
- Learning the pacing rule once and never practicing it.

**Do this today**

1. Compute your per-question time and checkpoints for each section.
2. Write them on an index card.
3. Do ten practice questions with a timer and flag anything that exceeds your ceiling.
4. Note where your time went.

**Recap**

Budget the time, set checkpoints, and decide in advance when you will move on. One question rarely deserves a quarter of a section.
