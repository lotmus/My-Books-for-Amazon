# Strategy: Build a Schedule That Survives Real Life

**What you will be able to do**

- Turn your weekly hour budget into a concrete weekly template.
- Place review before new material, and protect rest days.
- Recover from a lost week without panic or doubling up.

**Why it matters**

A schedule exists to make the next study session automatic. Its enemy is not laziness; it is the first Tuesday something goes wrong. Most plans fail by being designed for a week in which nothing goes wrong, which is a week no one has ever had. A good schedule expects interruptions and says, in advance, what gets dropped.

Praxis Tip: Judge a schedule by what it does on a bad week. If its answer is "fall behind and feel guilty," it is not finished.

**The building blocks**

**Fixed blocks.** Choose specific days and times, not "some evenings." A decision made in advance costs no willpower later.

**Block length.** Many people concentrate well for 45 to 60 minutes and then benefit from a short break. Find your own number by experiment; the point is to have a number. Long unbroken sessions tend to turn into reading the same paragraph with increasing sincerity.

**Review first.** Begin each session with 10 to 15 minutes of retrieval on earlier material (Chapters 6 and 7), then move to new material. Review placed at the end gets skipped when time runs short.

**One long block weekly.** Reserve one longer session, often on a weekend, for timed practice or mixed sets. Stamina is part of the skill.

**A protected day off.** Schedule at least one day with no study. It is not a reward; it is maintenance.

**Buffer.** Leave about one session in six empty on purpose, so slippage has somewhere to land.

Praxis Tip: Put the first five minutes of each session on a checklist: open the error log, open the flashcards, set a timer. A routine beats motivation.

**Worked Example: A 12-hour week**

A learner has 12 reliable hours per week (from Chapter 3).

| Day | Time | Hours | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | evening | 1.5 | review 15 min, new content |
| Tue | evening | 1.5 | review 15 min, new content |
| Wed | evening | 1.5 | review 15 min, practice questions on Mon-Tue topics |
| Thu | evening | 1.5 | review 15 min, new content |
| Fri | evening | 1.0 | error log and flashcards only |
| Sat | morning | 4.0 | timed mixed set, then full review of every miss |
| Sun | short | 1.0 | plan next week, light flashcards |

Total: 1.5 × 4 + 1.0 + 4.0 + 1.0 = 6 + 1 + 4 + 1 = 12 hours ✓. Notice what is absent: a block that quietly assumes you will "catch up" on Sunday. Sunday is already full.

*Skill used:* turning an hour budget into a calendar with built-in review.

**When the week falls apart**

You will lose a week now and then: illness, travel, a family emergency, an assignment that exploded. Use this triage order:

1. **Keep reviews.** Spaced review keeps what you have already learned from fading. Even 15 minutes a day counts.
2. **Postpone new content**, not review.
3. **Do not double up** the next week to "make up the hours." Doubling tends to produce tired, shallow sessions.
4. **Reduce scope if necessary.** If you are more than two weeks behind, cut the lowest-yield topics and say so out loud. Dropping something on purpose is a plan; dropping it by accident is a hole.
5. **Reset the calendar** and note the change in your plan so your buffer weeks reflect reality.

Praxis Tip: Keep a "bad day minimum" of 15 minutes: one set of flashcards and one error-log entry. Done on the worst days, it protects the habit when nothing else survives.

**More Praxis Tips**

Praxis Tip: Schedule the hardest topic at your best time of day, not at the time that is merely free.

Praxis Tip: Study in the same place when you can. A familiar desk shortens the runway into focus.

Praxis Tip: End each session by writing the first task for the next one. Starting is the hardest part, and you have just done half of it in advance.

Praxis Tip: Put your phone in another room during blocks. A phone face-down on the desk is still on the desk.

Praxis Tip: Review the schedule once a week, in ten minutes, at a fixed time. More often than that and you are procrastinating with a spreadsheet.

**Common mistakes**

- Scheduling in vague blocks like "evenings."
- No buffer, no day off.
- Putting new material first and review last.
- Making up lost hours by cramming.
- Rebuilding the whole schedule every weekend.

**Do this today**

1. Open your calendar and block next week's sessions at fixed times.
2. Put one day off and one buffer session on your calendar.
3. Write your 15-minute bad-day minimum on a sticky note.
4. Decide what you will cut first if a week collapses.

**Recap**

Fixed blocks, review first, one long session, a day off, and a buffer. Plan the bad week in advance: keep review, postpone new content, and never try to double up.
