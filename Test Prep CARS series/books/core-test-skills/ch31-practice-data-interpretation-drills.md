# Practice: Data Interpretation Drills

**Timing and conditions**

Ten original questions based on three small data sets. Allow about 15 minutes. No calculator. Read the labels and units first (Chapter 17), then the questions. After the set, log misses with a cause (Chapter 9).

Praxis Tip: Before answering, state in one sentence what each data set shows. If you cannot, reread the title and column headings.

**Data Set A: Monthly rainfall and temperature in a town (invented data)**

| Month | Rainfall (mm) | Mean temperature (°C) |
|---|---|---|
| January | 80 | 8 |
| February | 60 | 9 |
| March | 50 | 11 |
| April | 40 | 14 |
| May | 35 | 17 |
| June | 30 | 20 |

1. In which month was rainfall lowest?
 A. January  B. March  C. May  D. June

2. By what percent did rainfall decrease from January to June?
 A. 50 percent  B. 62.5 percent  C. 80 percent  D. 167 percent

3. What was the approximate mean monthly rainfall from January to June?
 A. 35 mm  B. 42 mm  C. 49 mm  D. 55 mm

4. Which statement is best supported by the table?
 A. Across these six months, rainfall decreased as mean temperature increased.
 B. Rising temperatures caused the decrease in rainfall.
 C. Rainfall will continue to decrease in July.
 D. The town is in a desert.

**Data Set B: A reaction-time experiment (invented data)**

Sixty volunteers were randomly assigned to three groups of 20. Each group drank a beverage containing no caffeine, a low dose, or a high dose, and then completed a reaction-time test. Mean reaction times (in milliseconds; lower is faster) and the standard deviation (SD) are:

| Group | n | Mean reaction time (ms) | SD (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|
| No caffeine | 20 | 310 | 25 |
| Low dose | 20 | 295 | 25 |
| High dose | 20 | 289 | 25 |

5. What is the independent variable?
 A. Reaction time  B. The number of volunteers  C. The SD  D. The caffeine dose

6. Approximately how much faster, in percent, was the high-dose group than the no-caffeine group?
 A. 3.5 percent  B. 6.8 percent  C. 21 percent  D. 70 percent

7. Which conclusion is best supported?
 A. In this sample, mean reaction time was lower with caffeine, but the 6 ms difference between low and high doses is small compared with the spread of the data, so it may not be reliable.
 B. High-dose caffeine makes everyone react 21 ms faster.
 C. Caffeine is harmful.
 D. The experiment proves low and high doses have identical effects.

8. Which change would most increase confidence in the findings?
 A. Letting volunteers choose their group.
 B. Reporting only the means.
 C. Using a larger sample and repeating the experiment.
 D. Testing only the high-dose group.

**Data Set C: A satisfaction chart (invented data)**

A bar chart compares customer satisfaction scores for two stores on a 5-point scale. Store X scores 4.1 and Store Y scores 4.3. The vertical axis runs from 4.0 to 4.5.

9. On the chart, the bar for Store Y looks how many times as tall as the bar for Store X?
 A. 1.05 times  B. 2 times  C. 3 times  D. 4 times

10. Compared with Store X, Store Y's actual score is about
 A. 5 percent higher  B. 30 percent higher  C. 3 times higher  D. 50 percent higher

**Answers and explanations**

1. **D.** June, 30 mm.
2. **B.** (80 − 30) ÷ 80 = 50/80 = 62.5 percent. (A uses the wrong base. C uses 80 as the percent. D divides by the new value.)
3. **C.** (80 + 60 + 50 + 40 + 35 + 30) ÷ 6 = 295 ÷ 6 ≈ 49 mm.
4. **A.** It describes the pattern without claiming cause or predicting beyond the data. (B infers cause. C extrapolates. D is unsupported.)
5. **D.** The experimenters changed the caffeine dose.
6. **B.** (310 − 289) ÷ 310 = 21/310 ≈ 6.8 percent. (A is about half the true value. C is the raw ms difference. D is not a percent of anything here.)
7. **A.** It states the pattern and notes that a 6 ms difference is small compared with SD = 25 ms. (B overgeneralizes "everyone". C has no support. D claims proof of no difference; absence of a clear difference is not proof of none.)
8. **C.** Larger samples and replication reduce the role of chance. (A breaks random assignment. B hides spread. D removes the comparison.)
9. **C.** The bar heights above the 4.0 baseline are 0.1 for X and 0.3 for Y, so Y looks three times as tall.
10. **A.** 4.3 ÷ 4.1 ≈ 1.049, so about 5 percent higher. The truncated axis exaggerates a small difference.

**Score and review**

Questions 2, 6 and 10 test the base for percent change. Questions 4, 7 and 9 test the gap between what the data show and what a tempting conclusion claims. Log the cause of each miss.

Praxis Tip: For every conclusion, ask "Would the data look any different if this were false?" If not, the data don't support it.

Praxis Tip: Always compute the effect size relative to the spread (Question 7). A difference smaller than the SD is rarely striking.

Praxis Tip: When you see a truncated axis, convert bar heights back to actual values before comparing.

Praxis Tip: In experiments, check how subjects were assigned. Random assignment supports causal claims; self-selection does not.

Praxis Tip: Redo the set after a week with fresh eyes, covering the answers.
