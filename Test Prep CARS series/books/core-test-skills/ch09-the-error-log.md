# Strategy: The Error Log

**What you will be able to do**

- Record every mistake in a form that tells you what to fix.
- Classify errors by cause and find the biggest one.
- Turn the weekly log review into a short, specific to-do list.

**Why it matters**

Practice questions are not valuable because you answered them. They are valuable because of what you do with the ones you got wrong. Most learners check the key, think "ah, of course," and move on. Two weeks later they make the same mistake with the same expression of surprise. An error log is a plain notebook or spreadsheet that interrupts this cycle by forcing you to name what went wrong.

The log is the most reliably useful tool in this course, and it costs almost nothing. It is also a little humbling, which is probably why it works.

Praxis Tip: Log errors the same day. Memory edits its own history with remarkable generosity.

**What to record**

For each wrong answer (and each lucky guess), write one line with these fields:

| Field | What to write |
|---|---|
| Date and source | When and which set or test |
| Topic | Use the content areas from your fact sheet (Chapter 2) |
| What I chose and why | One sentence, honest |
| Correct answer and why | One sentence, in your own words |
| Cause code | One of the five below |
| Fix | One concrete action |
| Redo date | When you will try a similar question again |

**The five cause codes**

1. **Content gap (CG):** you did not know or had forgotten the material.
2. **Misread (MR):** you knew it, but misread the question, a number, or a qualifier such as NOT or EXCEPT.
3. **Rushed (RU):** you hurried, guessed to save time, or ran out of time.
4. **Trap choice (TC):** you chose a plausible wrong answer that matched a common misconception or half the right idea.
5. **Calculation slip (CS):** your method was right and your arithmetic or algebra was not.

Some errors have two causes. Pick the earliest one in the chain: if you rushed *and* misread, record "Rushed," since that is the cause behind the misreading.

Praxis Tip: Write the "why I chose it" line before you read the explanation. After the explanation it always seems you were never really tempted.

**Fixes that fit the cause**

| Cause | Typical fix |
|---|---|
| Content gap | Add flashcards and a short lesson; redo similar questions in 2 days and 7 days |
| Misread | Note the qualifiers and the final question before choosing; restate the question in your own words |
| Rushed | Practice with the pacing checkpoints from Chapter 11; use the flag-and-move rule |
| Trap choice | Write down the misconception the wrong choice relies on; make a card for it |
| Calculation slip | Estimate first (Chapter 13); write units; recompute with a different route |

Praxis Tip: A fix that says "be more careful" is not a fix. Replace it with an action: "note the NOT," or "estimate before calculating."

**The weekly review**

Once a week, for 20 minutes:

1. Count errors by cause.
2. Pick out the largest category.
3. Pick **one** fix to focus on next week.
4. Check last week's redo dates, and try those questions again from scratch.
5. Delete or retire entries you have since gotten right twice in a row.

Praxis Tip: Work on one fix at a time. Three simultaneous improvements tend to become zero improvements and a feeling of being busy.

**Worked Example: A week of mistakes**

A learner logs 24 errors over a week.

| Cause | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Content gap | 7 | 29% |
| Misread | 8 | 33% |
| Rushed | 4 | 17% |
| Trap choice | 3 | 13% |
| Calculation slip | 2 | 8% |
| Total | 24 | 100% |

Check: 7 + 8 + 4 + 3 + 2 = 24. Shares: 7/24 ≈ 29%, 8/24 ≈ 33%, 4/24 ≈ 17%, 3/24 ≈ 13%, 2/24 ≈ 8%. (Rounded shares add to 100.)

*Reading the table.* The biggest single cause is misreads, not content gaps. If this learner had spent the week on extra content review, she would have worked on the second-biggest problem. Misreads and rushed errors together make 12 of 24, half of all mistakes, and none of them require learning a new fact.

*Choosing the fix.* For the coming week she picks one: "Before choosing an answer, note the last sentence of the question and any word like NOT, EXCEPT, LEAST." She will count misreads again next Sunday and see whether the number moves.

*Skill used:* converting a pile of mistakes into one measurable change.

Praxis Tip: Compute the share of errors that need no new content. If it is more than a third, your fastest gains are in how you take the test.

**More Praxis Tips**

Praxis Tip: Keep the log in one place. A spreadsheet with a filter on the cause column makes the weekly count a ten-second job.

Praxis Tip: Log lucky guesses too. A right answer you could not defend is a wrong answer in disguise.

Praxis Tip: When an error repeats, write "REPEAT" and the date. A repeated error deserves a stronger fix than the first one got.

Praxis Tip: Read the log before each full-length simulation. It is a short list of your own known failure modes, which is a better pep talk than most.

**Common mistakes**

- Logging only the topic and not the cause.
- Fixing everything at once.
- Forgetting to log when a set goes well; you also need to know what worked.
- Skipping the redo step. Without it, the log is a diary.
- Blaming every miss on carelessness, which cannot be fixed, instead of naming a specific habit.

**Do this today**

1. Make the log (notebook or spreadsheet) with the seven fields.
2. Take your last set of practice questions and log every miss with a cause.
3. Count by cause and pick out the largest.
4. Choose one fix and write it on a sticky note.

**Recap**

Record the cause, not just the topic. Count by cause weekly, fix the largest one with a concrete action, and redo the question later to check.
