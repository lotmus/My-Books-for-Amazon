# Strategy: Spaced Repetition and Flashcards That Work

**What you will be able to do**

- Build flashcards that test one idea each, in question form.
- Set a review ladder and estimate your daily review load.
- Decide when to retire, rewrite, or split a card.

**Why it matters**

Flashcards are the most popular study tool and the most commonly misused. A card that says "mitosis" on one side and a paragraph on the other is not a flashcard; it is a small, awkward textbook. A good card asks a single question that forces a single piece of recall. Paired with a sensible review schedule, such cards let you maintain a large body of material in a surprisingly modest amount of time each day.

Praxis Tip: Spend your effort on writing good cards the first time. A bad card is reviewed again and again, which is an efficient way of wasting time.

**Writing good cards**

1. **One idea per card.** If the answer has three parts, make three cards.
2. **Ask a question, do not name a topic.** "Why does a higher temperature usually speed up a reaction?" beats "Temperature and reaction rate."
3. **Make the answer short.** A good answer fits in a sentence or a number with a unit.
4. **Prefer "why" and "how" for concepts**, and plain recall for definitions, formulas and facts that really are just facts.
5. **Use cloze deletion** when helpful: "The mean is sensitive to ______, but the median is not." (outliers).
6. **Add a tiny example or image** only if it makes the answer clearer.
7. **Write the card in your own words**, from understanding. Copying the textbook produces a card you can answer without thinking.

Praxis Tip: If you can answer a card by recognizing its first three words, rewrite it. The card should require you to produce the answer, not to complete a familiar phrase.

**The review ladder**

Spaced repetition means seeing a card again just as it begins to fade, then waiting longer each time you succeed. A simple ladder:

- Learn on day 0.
- Review on day 1.
- If correct, review on day 3, then day 7, then day 21, then every month or so.
- If you miss a card at any step, send it back to the start of the ladder.

The exact numbers are an adjustable starting point, not a law. If you remember everything easily, stretch the intervals; if cards are slipping, shorten them. Software tools can automate the scheduling, and a paper box with dividers (the Leitner method) works too.

Praxis Tip: Do reviews daily, and in short sessions. Ten minutes spread across the day beats one heroic hour on Sunday.

**Worked Example: How big is the daily load?**

Suppose you add 10 new cards each day and use the ladder day 1, 3, 7, 21 with no misses.

Each card gets four scheduled reviews over its first 21 days. Once the system has been running for 21 days, on any given day there are 10 cards at each of the four review stages, so reviews per day = 10 × 4 = 40. Add the 10 new cards, and you handle about 50 cards per day. At roughly 10 to 15 seconds per card, that is about 8 to 12 minutes. (50 × 10 s = 500 s ≈ 8 min; 50 × 15 s = 750 s ≈ 12.5 min.)

Misses add to the load, and cards that survive past day 21 add a small monthly review. So expect the real number to run somewhat higher, and plan 15 to 20 minutes a day, not 10.

*Skill used:* estimating a recurring workload from a rule, before committing to it.

Praxis Tip: If your daily reviews swell past what you can finish, pause new cards, not reviews. A backlog is a debt that grows interest.

**Maintaining the deck**

- **Retire** cards you have answered correctly many times and no longer find at all hard, after the long intervals.
- **Rewrite** cards you keep missing: they are usually too vague, too long, or testing two things.
- **Split** a card that has a multi-part answer.
- **Delete** cards for material the exam will not test. (Check against your fact sheet from Chapter 2.)
- **Tag** cards by topic so that you can build mixed sets (Chapter 8).

Praxis Tip: When you miss a card, ask why before you re-read the answer. Was the card unclear, or was the knowledge missing? Fix the cause, not just the card.

**More Praxis Tips**

Praxis Tip: Say the answer aloud, or write it, before flipping. Thinking "I knew that" after seeing the answer is not recall.

Praxis Tip: Review in different places and at different times of day when you can. Variety makes the memory less dependent on one setting.

Praxis Tip: Cap each card's answer at one line. If the answer needs a paragraph, the card is secretly a lesson.

**Common mistakes**

- Making cards from material you have not yet understood.
- Copying whole paragraphs.
- Adding 50 new cards a day with no plan for the reviews.
- Flipping cards without genuinely trying to recall first.
- Treating the deck as the entire preparation. Cards store facts; they do not teach reasoning.

**Do this today**

1. Pick a topic from this week's study and write 8 cards, one idea each, in question form.
2. Write down your ladder: day 1, 3, 7, 21.
3. Decide on a tool (paper box or an app) and set a daily 15-minute review time.
4. Set a cap on new cards per day.

**Recap**

One idea per card, written as a question, reviewed on an expanding ladder. Watch the daily load, fix bad cards, and use cards for facts while saving reasoning for practice questions.
