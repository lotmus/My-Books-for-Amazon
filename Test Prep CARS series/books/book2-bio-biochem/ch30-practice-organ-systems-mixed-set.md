# Practice: Organ Systems Mixed Set

**Timing and conditions**

Fourteen original questions on the organ systems of [Lessons 19 to 22](chapter:26): the digestive and excretory systems, muscle, skeleton and skin, the immune and lymphatic systems, and reproduction and development (category 3B, with links to 2C). Three passage sets of four questions are followed by two independent questions. Four of the fourteen questions are deliberate review from earlier parts of the book (metabolism, genetics and cells), so do not assume that every question comes from the lessons just finished. Allow about 22 minutes, in one sitting, with scratch paper and no notes or calculator. Write your answer letters, with a confidence level (high, medium or low) for each, on scratch paper or in your notebook; an ebook cannot be written in. Check only when you have finished.

Praxis Tip: In a table passage, say the pattern in one sentence before you read any question: "urine volume up, concentration down, filtration unchanged." Most of the questions then turn out to be a restatement of that sentence, and the rest are asking what you would test next.

Praxis Tip: When a question quietly goes back to an earlier part (a gene, a pump, a pathway), do not panic. The review questions use only what the earlier lessons taught, and the same reasoning habits apply. Read the stem for the one idea it needs.

Praxis Tip: Organ-system questions reward a short chain of cause and effect. Write the chain in three or four words ("less ADH, fewer aquaporins, dilute urine") before looking at the options, and the wrong choices will usually break the chain visibly.

**Questions**

**Passage 1 (questions 1-4)**

A physiologist studies how a healthy volunteer's kidneys handle water under three conditions. Inulin, a plant polysaccharide that is freely filtered and is neither reabsorbed nor secreted, is infused throughout to hold its plasma concentration at 0.50 mg/mL. Plasma osmolarity stays near 290 mOsm/L in every condition, and blood pressure and heart rate do not change. In Condition 1 the volunteer drinks normally. In Condition 2 the volunteer takes no fluid for 12 hours. In Condition 3 the volunteer drinks normally and takes Drug X, an experimental compound that acts on a single nephron segment. Urine is collected over timed intervals and analyzed. Clearance of a substance equals its urine concentration times the urine flow rate, divided by its plasma concentration. Each urine sample was collected over a period long enough for the values to be steady, and the volunteer's weight did not change during the study.

| Condition | Urine flow (mL/min) | Urine inulin (mg/mL) | Urine osmolarity (mOsm/L) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Normal drinking | 1.0 | 60 | 600 |
| 2. No fluid, 12 hours | 0.5 | 120 | 1,000 |
| 3. Drug X | 4.0 | 15 | 120 |

1. What is the glomerular filtration rate of the volunteer in Condition 1, as estimated from inulin clearance?
   A. 60 mL/min
   B. 120 mL/min
   C. 30 mL/min
   D. 1.0 mL/min

2. During water restriction (Condition 2) the urine inulin concentration doubles compared with Condition 1. The best explanation is that
   A. inulin was secreted into the tubule at a higher rate
   B. the GFR fell to half its previous value
   C. inulin was reabsorbed from the tubule
   D. more water was reabsorbed from the collecting duct while the same amount of inulin stayed in the tubule

3. Which action of Drug X is most consistent with the data for Condition 3?
   A. Blocking the action of ADH on the collecting duct
   B. Constricting the afferent arteriole to lower the GFR
   C. Stimulating aldosterone secretion
   D. Blocking only the Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter of the thick ascending limb

4. In the ADH-sensitive cells of the collecting duct, water moves from the lumen into the cell mainly by
   A. active transport by a water pump that uses ATP
   B. simple diffusion through the lipid bilayer alone, so that ADH has no role in membrane permeability
   C. osmosis through aquaporin channels inserted into the apical membrane, without direct ATP use
   D. opening of ligand-gated sodium channels by ADH

**Passage 2 (questions 5-8)**

Adult mice were immunized on day 0 and again on day 28 with a purified protein antigen, X, mixed with an adjuvant. Blood was drawn on the days shown, and the titer (the reciprocal of the highest serum dilution that still gave detectable binding to antigen X) of antigen-specific IgM and IgG was measured. A second group, athymic mice that lack T cells but have normal numbers of B cells, received the same schedule. The athymic mice made a small IgM response (peak titer 20) and the IgG titers listed. Mice in both groups appeared healthy throughout, and all samples were assayed in the same run so that titers are directly comparable between days and between groups. Each group contained eight mice, and the values shown are group medians. The investigators noted that the adjuvant alone, given to a control group, produced no antigen-specific antibody.

| Day | IgM, normal mice | IgG, normal mice | IgG, athymic mice |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 40 | 10 | below 10 |
| 14 | 160 | 80 | below 10 |
| 28 | 20 | 320 | below 10 |
| 35 | 40 | 5,120 | below 10 |

5. By what factor did the IgG titer in the normal mice rise between day 28 and day 35?
   A. 16-fold
   B. 4-fold
   C. 64-fold
   D. 512-fold

6. The much larger IgG response in the normal mice after the second immunization is best explained by
   A. maternal IgG transferred across the placenta before the experiment
   B. memory carried by neutrophils and macrophages of the innate system
   C. new antibody genes made after the antigen is seen, in response to its shape
   D. memory B cells formed during the first response, which respond faster and class-switch to IgG

7. What do the data on the athymic mice show about the response to antigen X?
   A. B cells need the thymus in order to mature
   B. Making a high IgG titer to this protein antigen generally depends on T cells
   C. IgM is made by T cells, and IgG by B cells
   D. A secondary response is impossible without complement

8. Compared with a neuron in the same mouse, the DNA of an IgG-secreting plasma cell
   A. is identical at every site, because differentiation never alters DNA
   B. lacks all genes except those for antibodies
   C. differs at the antibody gene loci, because gene segments were cut and joined during B cell development
   D. has half as many chromosomes, because it is terminally differentiated

**Passage 3 (questions 9-12)**

In a study of the human menstrual cycle, blood was sampled from healthy volunteers with regular 28-day cycles (day 1 is the first day of menstrual bleeding). Mean hormone concentrations in the control group are shown. Each group contained ten women of similar age. A second group took a combined oral contraceptive, containing an estrogen and a progestin, for three months. In that group the mean LH concentration on day 14 was 6 mIU/mL and the mean progesterone concentration on day 21 was 0.8 ng/mL. No participant in either group was pregnant, and all samples were drawn in the morning and assayed in a single batch so that values can be compared between days. Luteal-phase progesterone in a cycle in which ovulation occurs is expected to rise well above its follicular-phase value.

| Day | Estradiol (pg/mL) | LH (mIU/mL) | Progesterone (ng/mL) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 40 | 5 | 0.5 |
| 10 | 150 | 6 | 0.6 |
| 13 | 320 | 10 | 0.8 |
| 14 | 180 | 45 | 1.0 |
| 21 | 120 | 6 | 12 |
| 27 | 30 | 5 | 1.5 |

9. The LH surge on day 14 follows a peak of estradiol on day 13. The best explanation is that
   A. progesterone from the corpus luteum is rising and triggers the surge
   B. human chorionic gonadotropin from an implanting embryo is acting like LH
   C. falling estradiol removes negative feedback
   D. high, sustained estradiol from the dominant follicle acts by positive feedback on the pituitary

10. Which conclusion do the data from the contraceptive group best support?
   A. Ovulation was probably suppressed, because there was no LH surge and no luteal-phase rise in progesterone
   B. A corpus luteum formed normally but secreted progesterone that the assay did not detect
   C. Ovulation occurred normally, with the LH surge delayed until after day 14
   D. The pituitary stopped making LH altogether

11. A researcher wants to know whether the estrogen component, the progestin component, or only the combination suppresses the LH surge. Which design is best?
   A. Repeat the combined pill with a threefold larger dose and measure LH on day 14
   B. Measure estradiol on day 21 in the control group only
   C. Add groups that receive estrogen alone, progestin alone and a placebo, and measure LH on day 14 and progesterone on day 21 in every group
   D. Give the combined pill to the original group and measure LH on day 3

12. LH and estradiol both act on cells of the ovary. Which statement about their mechanisms is correct?
   A. LH diffuses through the membrane to an intracellular receptor, and estradiol binds a surface receptor
   B. LH binds a receptor on the cell surface and acts through a second messenger, while estradiol, a steroid, enters the cell and binds an intracellular receptor that alters transcription
   C. Both bind only intracellular receptors, because both are made in the ovary
   D. Both bind surface receptors linked to ion channels, so neither affects transcription

   C. removes CO₂ produced by the citric acid cycle

**Answers and explanations**

1. **B.** Clearance of inulin equals GFR: C = (U × V) / P = (60 mg/mL × 1.0 mL/min) / 0.50 mg/mL = 120 mL/min. The same calculation gives 120 mL/min in Conditions 2 and 3 as well, so filtration did not change. [3B · Skill 4] (A multiplies urine concentration by flow but forgets to divide by the plasma concentration of 0.50 mg/mL; C multiplies by the plasma concentration instead of dividing by it; D is the urine flow rate, not a clearance.)
2. **D.** Inulin is filtered and then neither reabsorbed nor secreted, so the amount excreted per minute is constant (60 mg/mL × 1.0 mL/min = 120 mg/mL × 0.5 mL/min = 60 mg/min). A lower urine volume with the same inulin load means a higher concentration, which means more water was taken back, as expected from increased ADH acting on the collecting duct. [3B · Skill 2] (A secretion would make the inulin clearance exceed the GFR, but the calculated clearance stayed at 120 mL/min; B the calculated clearance was 120 mL/min in both conditions, so the GFR did not change; C reabsorption would lower the urine inulin concentration relative to the amount filtered, and inulin is by definition not reabsorbed.)
3. **A.** The volume rose eightfold with no change in GFR, and the urine was more dilute than plasma. A collecting duct that has become water-tight (no ADH effect, no aquaporins in the membrane) lets the dilute fluid leave the ascending limb unchanged, and a large volume of dilute urine follows. [3B · Skill 2] (B the calculated GFR was unchanged at 120 mL/min; C aldosterone increases sodium and water reabsorption, which would reduce urine volume, not increase it; D this would generally leave the urine closer to isosmotic with plasma (about 290 mOsm/L) rather than markedly dilute at 120 mOsm/L.)
4. **C.** ADH binds a receptor on the cell, a cAMP-based signal causes vesicles carrying aquaporins to fuse with the apical membrane, and water then flows down its osmotic gradient into the salty medulla. This is a review of membranes, transport and signaling from Lesson 12. [2A · Skill 1] (A there is no pump for water itself; water follows osmotic gradients that are set up by solute transport; B water crosses a lipid bilayer slowly, which is why the insertion of aquaporins by ADH changes the permeability greatly; D ADH binds a G protein-coupled receptor and acts through cAMP; it does not gate a sodium channel directly.)
5. **A.** 5,120 / 320 = 16. Reading the correct pair of rows is the whole skill here: each wrong choice is a true ratio for a different pair of days. [3B · Skill 4] (B is the rise between day 14 and day 28 (80 to 320); C is the rise between day 14 and day 35 (80 to 5,120); D is the rise between day 7 and day 35 (10 to 5,120).)
6. **D.** The first exposure selects B cells whose receptors fit antigen X and leaves long-lived memory cells. A second exposure finds a larger, faster population that makes IgG (the class-switched, higher-affinity antibody), as the day 35 titer shows. [3B · Skill 2] (A this would be passive immunity, present from the start and not rising after the second dose; B innate cells generally have no memory and respond the same way each time; C receptors are generated before exposure, and the antigen selects among them (clonal selection) rather than instructing their form.)
7. **B.** Athymic mice have B cells (they made a little IgM) but lack T cells, and they made essentially no IgG even after two doses. For most protein antigens, full B cell activation and class switching need help from CD4 T cells, so the missing T cells explain the missing IgG. A good next control would be to restore T cells and look for IgG. [3B · Skill 3] (A B cells mature in the bone marrow, and the athymic mice still made some IgM, which shows that they have working B cells; C both IgM and IgG are secreted by B lineage cells (plasma cells), not by T cells; D the data say nothing about complement, so this cannot be concluded.)
8. **C.** Cells of one organism generally carry the same genome, and cell types differ in which genes are expressed. B and T cells are the exception: V(D)J recombination permanently rearranges the receptor genes. This is a review of gene expression and DNA rearrangement from Lessons 7 and 8. [1B · Skill 2] (A lymphocyte antigen-receptor genes are an exception, because V(D)J recombination rearranges the DNA; B differentiation generally keeps the whole genome, and the plasma cell needs thousands of genes for its ordinary functions; D a plasma cell is diploid like other body cells; only gametes are haploid.)
9. **D.** Estradiol normally inhibits the pituitary, but once it is high for long enough the feedback becomes positive and LH surges. Ovulation follows about a day later, and the ruptured follicle becomes the corpus luteum, which produces the progesterone seen on day 21. [3B · Skill 4] (A progesterone is only 1.0 ng/mL on day 14, and the corpus luteum forms after ovulation, not before; B no participant was pregnant, and hCG maintains the corpus luteum rather than triggering the cycle; C estradiol was at its peak (320 pg/mL) on day 13, just before the surge, not falling.)
10. **A.** A steady supply of estrogen and progestin keeps negative feedback on the pituitary constant, so the follicular rise and the LH surge do not occur. Without ovulation there is no corpus luteum, so progesterone stays low on day 21. [3B · Skill 4] (B a functioning corpus luteum raised measured progesterone to 12 ng/mL in the control group, and nothing in the data suggests a detection problem; C a delayed surge would likely still have produced a luteal progesterone rise by day 21, and none was seen; D the LH of 6 mIU/mL is close to the baseline control values (5 to 6 mIU/mL), so basal LH was still made.)
11. **C.** To attribute an effect to one component you must test each component separately and include a placebo control, then measure the same outcomes (the surge and the luteal progesterone) in every group. Dose escalation of the combination or measurements at the wrong time cannot separate them. [3B · Skill 3] (A a larger dose of the combination cannot separate the contribution of each component; B this adds no information about either drug component, and a group without the drugs gives no comparison; D LH on day 3 is a baseline time point, not when the surge occurs, and the design still tests only the combination.)
12. **B.** Peptide and protein hormones cannot cross the bilayer and use surface receptors and second messengers; steroid hormones are lipid soluble and bind intracellular receptors that act as transcription factors. This is a review of signal transduction from Lesson 12, with the hormones of Lesson 16. [2A · Skill 1] (A this reverses the two: LH is a protein hormone and estradiol is a lipid-soluble steroid; C LH is made in the pituitary, and as a large protein hormone it cannot cross the membrane; D steroid hormones such as estradiol act mainly through intracellular receptors that regulate gene expression.)
13. **A.** Enteropeptidase on the brush border converts trypsinogen to trypsin, and trypsin activates chymotrypsinogen and procarboxypeptidase. Without it the pancreatic protease cascade does not start, and protein digestion is poor, although pepsin still works in the stomach. [3B · Skill 2] (B lactase is a separate brush-border enzyme and is not activated by enteropeptidase; C bile salts are made in the liver from cholesterol and are unrelated to enteropeptidase; D pepsinogen is made by gastric chief cells and activated by acid, not by enteropeptidase.)
14. **D.** Glycolysis needs a steady supply of NAD⁺ to keep the glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate step going. When the electron transport chain cannot reoxidize NADH quickly enough, reducing pyruvate to lactate oxidizes NADH to NAD⁺ and lets glycolysis run on. This links the energy sources of Lesson 20 to the glycolysis of Lesson 4. [1D · Skill 2] (A the conversion of pyruvate to lactate makes no ATP; the ATP comes from glycolysis; B gluconeogenesis occurs mainly in the liver and kidney, and the Cori cycle returns lactate to glucose there; C the reaction makes no CO₂ and is not part of the citric acid cycle.)
