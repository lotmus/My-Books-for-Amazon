**Questions**

**Passage 1 (questions 1-4)**

A neurobiologist records from an isolated giant axon bathed in a solution that mimics extracellular fluid (145 mM Na⁺ and 5 mM K⁺). The axon contains about 10 mM Na⁺ and 140 mM K⁺. One electrode records the membrane potential. A second electrode delivers a brief current pulse of fixed size that, in an untreated axon, always brings the membrane to threshold. All recordings are made at 37 °C, and each value is the mean of eight axons. In separate preparations the bath was changed in one of three ways: Toxin A was added, Toxin B was added, or most of the extracellular Na⁺ was replaced by an impermeant cation, leaving 48 mM Na⁺. Neither toxin enters the axon. After the current pulse in the Toxin A preparation, the membrane potential rose only a few millivolts and then returned to rest.

| Condition | Resting (mV) | Peak (mV) | Width (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control | −70 | +35 | 1.0 |
| Toxin A | −70 | none | none |
| Toxin B | −70 | +36 | 3.5 |
| 48 mM Na⁺ | −72 | +10 | 1.0 |

1. Toxin A most likely
   A. blocks voltage-gated K⁺ channels
   B. blocks voltage-gated Na⁺ channels
   C. blocks the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase
   D. opens additional leak K⁺ channels

2. Toxin B most likely
   A. blocks voltage-gated Na⁺ channels
   B. blocks voltage-gated K⁺ channels
   C. blocks leak K⁺ channels
   D. blocks voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels at the terminal

3. In the 48 mM Na⁺ bath, the peak is lower (+10 mV) mainly because
   A. the Na⁺ channels no longer open
   B. the K⁺ equilibrium potential has become less negative
   C. the Na⁺ equilibrium potential is less positive, so the rising phase has a lower limit
   D. the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase now runs backward

4. The researcher wants to show that the loss of spikes in the Toxin A preparation is caused by the toxin and not by damage to the axon during impalement. Which additional experiment is best?
   A. Add a second dose of Toxin A to the same axon
   B. Rinse the toxin out of the bath and test whether spikes return
   C. Raise extracellular K⁺ and repeat the recording
   D. Repeat the recording with Toxin B only

**Passage 2 (questions 5-8)**

Four patients with symptoms suggesting a disorder of thyroid function are evaluated. In each, blood thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) and free thyroxine (T4) are measured. Each patient then receives an injection of thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH), and TSH is measured again 30 minutes later. Blood is drawn in the morning after an overnight fast, every patient receives the same TRH dose, and all samples are assayed together in one laboratory run. In a healthy adult, TSH is generally about 0.5 to 4 mU/L and free T4 about 10 to 22 pmol/L, and TRH injection raises TSH several-fold.

| Patient | TSH (mU/L) | Free T4 (pmol/L) | TSH after TRH |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 28 | 4 | 90 |
| 2 | 1.0 | 5 | 9 |
| 3 | 0.8 | 5 | 0.9 |
| 4 | 0.02 | 38 | 0.02 |

None of the patients takes any medication that affects the thyroid axis. Each patient has one of four problems: failure of the thyroid gland, failure of the anterior pituitary, failure of the hypothalamus, or a thyroid that secretes hormone without needing TSH.

5. Which diagnosis best fits Patient 1?
   A. Failure of the thyroid gland itself
   B. Failure of the anterior pituitary
   C. Failure of the hypothalamus
   D. A thyroid secreting hormone independently of TSH

6. Which patient most likely has a hypothalamic problem?
   A. Patient 1
   B. Patient 3
   C. Patient 2
   D. Patient 4

7. Which explanation is most consistent with Patient 4?
   A. A TSH-secreting tumor of the pituitary
   B. The thyroid secretes T4 on its own, and the high T4 suppresses TSH by negative feedback
   C. Iodine deficiency in the diet
   D. Failure of the hypothalamus

8. Investigators want to test whether thyroid hormone itself reduces the TSH response to TRH in healthy people. Which design is best?
   A. Give TRH to healthy volunteers and show that TSH rises
   B. Compare the TSH of Patient 1 and Patient 4 only
   C. Measure T4 in Patient 1 before and after TRH
   D. Give one group of volunteers T4 and another group placebo for several days, then give TRH to both groups and compare the TSH responses

**Passage 3 (questions 9-12)**

Four adults at rest are compared. For each, oxygen content is estimated as 1.34 × [Hb] × saturation (mL O₂ per dL of blood, with [Hb] in g/dL and saturation as a fraction), and oxygen delivery as content × 10 × cardiac output (mL/min, with cardiac output in L/min; the factor 10 converts per dL to per L). Dissolved oxygen is ignored in these estimates, but it equals 0.003 mL/dL for each mmHg of arterial PO₂, and Subject A has an arterial PO₂ of 100 mmHg. All four are seated and at rest, and cardiac output (CO) was measured by the same method in each. Subject A is healthy. Subject B has iron-deficiency anemia. Subject C has chronic lung disease with thickened alveolar walls. Subject D has lived at an altitude of about 4,000 m for several months.

| Subject | Hb (g/dL) | Saturation (%) | CO (L/min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 15 | 98 | 5.0 |
| B | 7.5 | 98 | 5.0 |
| C | 15 | 80 | 5.0 |
| D | 19 | 88 | 5.0 |

9. What is the oxygen content of Subject A's arterial blood, ignoring dissolved oxygen?
   A. About 20.1 mL/dL
   B. About 14.7 mL/dL
   C. About 20.5 mL/dL
   D. About 19.7 mL/dL

10. Compared with Subject A, the oxygen delivery of Subject B is about
   A. half as great
   B. the same, because the saturation is the same
   C. one quarter as great
   D. twice as great

11. Which statement best explains why Subject D's hemoglobin concentration is higher than Subject A's?
   A. The inspired PO₂ is higher at altitude
   B. An increase in plasma volume concentrated the red cells
   C. More oxygen is dissolved in plasma at altitude
   D. Low oxygen delivery raised erythropoietin production in the kidney, which increased red blood cell production

12. If dissolved oxygen is included for Subject A, the estimated oxygen content increases by about
   A. 1.5 percent
   B. 0.3 percent
   C. 5 percent
   D. 15 percent

**Answers and explanations**

1. **B.** The pulse depolarized the axon a few millivolts, so the passive membrane still responded, but the regenerative Na⁺ entry that drives the rising phase never started. Resting potential is unchanged, which points to voltage-gated channels and not to leak channels. [3A · Skill 4] (A would slow repolarization and widen the spike, not abolish it, as Toxin B does; C acts on a pump whose direct electrical effect on one spike is small, and would not remove the spike at once; D would hyperpolarize the resting potential, which stayed at −70 mV.)
2. **B.** Repolarization depends on the voltage-gated K⁺ channels that open late. If they are blocked, the rising phase and peak are normal but the falling phase is slow, so the spike widens (1.0 to 3.5 ms). [3A · Skill 4] (A would lower or abolish the peak, but the peak is unchanged at +36 mV; C would depolarize the resting potential, which stayed at −70 mV; D would affect transmitter release, not the spike recorded along the axon.)
3. **C.** The Nernst potential for Na⁺ is 61.5 log(145/10), about +71 mV, in the control bath, and 61.5 log(48/10), about +42 mV, in the low-Na⁺ bath. The peak moves toward E(Na) but never reaches it, so a lower E(Na) means a lower peak. Resting potential barely changes because the resting membrane is mostly permeable to K⁺. [3A · Skill 4] (A a spike still occurs, so the channels do open; B extracellular K⁺ was not changed, so it is unchanged at about −89 mV; D does not follow from lowering extracellular Na⁺ and would not change one spike.)
4. **B.** A reversible effect shows that the axon was healthy and that the toxin was responsible. Good designs also run a vehicle-only preparation, so any effect of the solvent is excluded. Washout is the best single addition listed. [3A · Skill 3] (A gives no way to separate toxin action from damage, because a dead axon would also fail to fire; C changes a second variable and does not test reversibility; D tests a different manipulation, which says nothing about Toxin A.)
5. **A.** T4 is low and TSH is high, so the pituitary is working and responding to the missing negative feedback. The defect is at the end of the axis (primary failure), and the exaggerated TSH rise after TRH is consistent with that. [3A · Skill 4] (B would give a low or inappropriately normal TSH, not 28 mU/L; C would give a low or inappropriately normal TSH, not a high one; D would give a high T4, whereas T4 is low.)
6. **C.** Patient 2 has a low T4 with a TSH that is not raised (the pituitary is not being driven), yet TRH produces a clear TSH rise. The pituitary can respond; it is simply not receiving TRH. Patient 3 has the same resting pattern but cannot respond to TRH, which places the defect in the pituitary. [3A · Skill 4] (A has a high TSH, so the hypothalamus and pituitary are responding to low T4; B shows no TSH response to TRH, so the pituitary is the problem; D has a high T4 and a suppressed TSH, a different pattern.)
7. **B.** A high T4 with a suppressed TSH that does not respond to TRH shows the long-loop negative feedback operating on the pituitary (high thyroid hormone suppresses TSH and TRH). The source of the excess T4 must therefore be the thyroid, working independently of TSH. [3A · Skill 4] (A would give a high TSH, not 0.02 mU/L; C would lower T4 and raise TSH; D would give a low T4, not 38 pmol/L.)
8. **D.** The hypothesis is about the effect of T4 on the TSH response. The experiment must change T4 (treatment versus placebo), hold everything else constant, and measure the TSH response to the same TRH dose. [3A · Skill 3] (A shows that TRH stimulates TSH, but not that T4 inhibits the response; B compares two people who differ in many ways, with no control for the T4 itself; C has no comparison group and manipulates TRH, not T4.)
9. **D.** Content = 1.34 × 15 × 0.98 = 19.7 mL/dL. [3B · Skill 4] (A leaves out the saturation (1.34 × 15 only); B leaves out the binding capacity of 1.34 mL per gram (15 × 0.98 only); C divides by the saturation instead of multiplying.)
10. **A.** Content for B is 1.34 × 7.5 × 0.98 = 9.9 mL/dL, half of A's, and the cardiac output is equal, so delivery is half: about 490 mL/min against about 985 mL/min. The PO₂ and the saturation are normal in anemia, and the deficit is in the carrying capacity. [3B · Skill 4] (B ignores that content depends on the amount of hemoglobin as well as its saturation; C would need the content to fall to a quarter, but Hb fell by half; D reverses the direction of the change.)
11. **D.** Hypoxia stimulates the kidney to make erythropoietin, which raises red cell production over days to weeks and raises the hematocrit. The result is that D's calculated content is the highest of the four (about 22 mL/dL) despite the lower saturation. [3B · Skill 2] (A is the opposite of the situation, because PO₂ falls with the lower total pressure; B an increase in plasma volume would dilute the red cells and lower the concentration; C dissolved oxygen falls when PO₂ falls, and in any case it is a small fraction.)
12. **A.** Dissolved oxygen = 0.003 × 100 = 0.3 mL/dL. As a fraction of 19.7 mL/dL this is 0.3 / 19.7, about 1.5 percent, which is why hemoglobin carries nearly all the oxygen (Henry's law, Book 1, Lessons 12 and 13). [3B · Skill 4] (B confuses the 0.3 mL/dL of dissolved oxygen with a percentage of the total; C would need about 1 mL/dL dissolved, or a PO₂ above 300 mmHg; D would need about 3 mL/dL dissolved, ten times the true amount.)
13. **A.** Poiseuille's law (Book 1, Lesson 4) gives flow proportional to r⁴. With r multiplied by 0.8, flow is multiplied by 0.8⁴ = 0.41. This sensitivity is why arterioles control blood pressure and blood distribution. [3B · Skill 2] (B assumes flow is proportional to the radius; C assumes flow is proportional to the radius squared; D assumes flow is proportional to the radius cubed.)
14. **C.** 0.03 × 60 = 1.8, so the ratio is 24 / 1.8 = 13.3. log 13.3 = 1.12, and pH = 6.1 + 1.12 = 7.22. A raised PCO₂ with a normal bicarbonate is respiratory acidosis (Book 1, Lesson 16 and Lesson 18). [3B · Skill 2] (A is the normal pH, which needs a PCO₂ of 40 mmHg; B is the pH at a PCO₂ of about 25 mmHg, the opposite direction; D would need a PCO₂ of about 100 mmHg.)
