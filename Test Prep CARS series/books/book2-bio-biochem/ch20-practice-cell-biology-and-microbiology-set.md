# Practice: Cell Biology and Microbiology Set

**Timing and conditions**

Fourteen original questions on the cell biology and microbiology of [Lessons 12 to 14](chapter:17): cell structure, membranes and transport ([Lesson 12](chapter:17)), the cell cycle, division and cancer ([Lesson 13](chapter:18)), and prokaryotes and viruses ([Lesson 14](chapter:19)) (categories 2A, 2B and 2C). Three passage sets are followed by two independent questions. Allow about 22 minutes, in one sitting, with scratch paper and no notes or calculator. Write your answer letters, with a confidence level (high, medium or low) for each, on scratch paper or in your notebook; an ebook cannot be written in. Check only when you have finished.

Treat the clock seriously: about 6 minutes per passage set and 2 minutes for the last two questions. Use the flag-and-move rule on any question that runs past two minutes.

Praxis Tip: Before you read the questions on an experiment passage, say in one sentence what was changed, what was measured and what the control is. This takes fifteen seconds and removes about half the confusion, which is a better exchange rate than most things in life. See [Strategy: Reading Biology Experiments](chapter:3).

Praxis Tip: In a table, read the pattern in the control row first, and only then ask how each treated row differs from it. The question is nearly always about the difference.

Praxis Tip: Where a calculation appears, write the formula and the units on scratch paper before you touch the numbers. Dilution factors and doubling times are the two usual places where good biologists lose points.

**Questions**

**Passage 1 (questions 1-4)**

Cultured intestinal epithelial cells take up glucose from the medium through a sodium-glucose cotransporter. The uptake rate (in nmol per minute per mg of protein) was measured at several glucose concentrations in three conditions: normal medium containing Na⁺, medium in which the Na⁺ was replaced by choline (a cation that does not cross the membrane), and normal medium after the cells had been exposed to ouabain, an inhibitor of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, for one hour. Uptake was timed over a short interval so that the intracellular glucose concentration stayed low.

| [Glucose] (mM) | Na⁺ medium | Na⁺-free medium | Na⁺ medium after ouabain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 | 33.3 | 3.3 | 13.3 |
| 0.5 | 50.0 | 5.0 | 20.0 |
| 1 | 66.7 | 6.7 | 26.7 |
| 2 | 80.0 | 8.0 | 32.0 |
| 5 | 90.9 | 9.1 | 36.4 |
| 20 | 97.6 | 9.8 | 39.0 |

1. Based on the Na⁺ medium column, which value is closest to the glucose concentration at half-maximal uptake (the Km)?
   A. 0.25 mM
   B. 0.5 mM
   C. 1 mM
   D. 50 mM

2. In the ouabain column, the maximum uptake rate is about 60 percent lower, while the half-maximal glucose concentration is unchanged. The reduced uptake after ouabain is best explained by
   A. loss of the Na⁺ gradient that drives the cotransporter, because the pump no longer exports Na⁺
   B. a competitive block of the glucose-binding site by ouabain
   C. depletion of the ATP that the cotransporter hydrolyzes directly
   D. a shift to simple diffusion of glucose through the bilayer

3. Compared with untreated cells, cells exposed to ouabain for one hour would be expected to have
   A. a lower intracellular Na⁺ concentration and a higher intracellular K⁺ concentration
   B. unchanged Na⁺ and K⁺ concentrations, because only the cotransporter is affected
   C. a higher intracellular Na⁺ concentration and an unchanged K⁺ concentration
   D. a higher intracellular Na⁺ concentration and a lower intracellular K⁺ concentration

4. A researcher wants to show that the low uptake in the Na⁺-free medium is caused by the absence of Na⁺ and not by a toxic effect of choline. Which experiment is the best test?
   A. Return cells from the Na⁺-free medium to normal medium and show that the uptake rate recovers
   B. Raise the glucose concentration to 100 mM in the Na⁺-free medium
   C. Add ouabain to the Na⁺-free medium
   D. Measure the uptake of glucose in normal medium at a lower temperature

**Passage 2 (questions 5-8)**

A human cell line (2n = 46) is grown without synchronization and divides every 20 hours. In each condition, the DNA content of thousands of cells was measured with a fluorescent dye, and cells were sorted into those with 2C (one set of each chromosome), between 2C and 4C, and 4C DNA content. Drug D was added to the parental line for 24 hours. Microscopy of D-treated cells showed condensed chromosomes, no nuclear envelope, and no cells in anaphase; the chromosomes were not lined up at the equator. Drug E was added for 24 hours to the parental line and to a derivative line in which both copies of the Rb gene are deleted (the untreated derivative line has the same distribution as the untreated parental line).

| Condition | 2C (%) | Between (%) | 4C (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Untreated | 55 | 20 | 25 |
| Drug D, parental | 8 | 12 | 80 |
| Drug E, parental | 90 | 4 | 6 |
| Drug E, Rb-deleted | 54 | 21 | 25 |

5. Assuming steady, asynchronous growth, how long does the untreated cell line spend in G1?
   A. 5 hours
   B. 11 hours
   C. 4 hours
   D. 9 hours

6. The data for drug D are most consistent with cells held in
   A. prometaphase or metaphase by the spindle assembly checkpoint
   B. G1 by the restriction point
   C. S phase because replication cannot be completed
   D. G2, because the G2/M checkpoint detected unreplicated DNA

7. Drug E arrests normal cells but has no effect on the Rb-deleted line. The best interpretation is that drug E
   A. inactivates Rb itself
   B. disrupts the spindle, so Rb status is irrelevant
   C. has no effect because the Rb-deleted cells are in G0
   D. blocks the cycle through a pathway that requires Rb, such as inhibiting the G1 kinase that phosphorylates Rb

8. A single drug D-arrested cell contains how many chromosomes and how many chromatids?
   A. 46 chromosomes and 46 chromatids
   B. 92 chromosomes and 92 chromatids
   C. 46 chromosomes and 92 chromatids
   D. 92 chromosomes and 184 chromatids

**Passage 3 (questions 9-12)**

A donor strain of bacteria carries a plasmid with a gene that makes it resistant to antibiotic X, and it cannot grow without added methionine. A recipient strain makes its own methionine and is sensitive to X. The two strains were mixed in liquid medium for an hour, diluted 10⁻², and 0.10 mL was spread on minimal medium (no methionine) containing X. Only recipient cells that acquired resistance can form colonies. In other arms of the experiment, DNase was added to the mixed culture, or the strains were grown for an hour in the two sides of a U-tube separated by a filter whose pores (0.2 μm) pass free DNA and viruses but not bacteria.

| Condition | Colonies per plate |
|---|---|
| Donor and recipient mixed | 82 |
| Mixed, with DNase | 79 |
| Strains separated by the filter | 0 |

9. Which process is best supported by the data?
   A. Transformation, because DNase had only a slight effect
   B. Transduction, because the colonies appeared only when the donor was present
   C. Conjugation, because transfer needed direct contact between cells
   D. Spontaneous mutation in the recipient

10. What is the concentration of antibiotic-resistant recipient cells (CFU/mL) in the mixed culture, based on the first row?
   A. 8.2 × 10²
   B. 8.2 × 10³
   C. 8.2 × 10⁵
   D. 8.2 × 10⁴

11. Which result would best show that the recovered colonies acquired the resistance gene from the donor plasmid, instead of becoming resistant in another way?
   A. The donor's plasmid sequence is detected in DNA from the recipient colonies
   B. The colonies grow in medium lacking methionine
   C. The colonies stain pink in a Gram stain
   D. The colonies are smaller than donor colonies

12. Resistant recipient colonies are picked and used as donors in a second mating, where they transfer resistance to a new sensitive strain. This is best explained if the
   A. resistance gene was transferred alone and integrated into the recipient chromosome
   B. donor strain had lysed and released free DNA
   C. plasmid carried the genes for pilus formation and transfer as well as the resistance gene, and was transferred whole
   D. recipient had mutated to resistance spontaneously

**Questions 13-14**

These two questions are independent of the passages.

13. A drug that inhibits reverse transcriptase is added to helper T cells at the time they are exposed to HIV. Which step is directly prevented?
   A. Binding of the virus to the CD4 receptor
   B. Synthesis of a DNA copy of the viral RNA genome
   C. Transcription of a provirus already integrated in the host chromosome by host RNA polymerase
   D. Translation of viral proteins from viral mRNA by host ribosomes

14. A drug inhibits phospholipase C. A cell is then exposed to a ligand that acts through a Gq-coupled receptor. Compared with an untreated cell, the drug-treated cell will show
   A. more cAMP, because phospholipase C normally breaks down cAMP
   B. less IP₃ and less release of Ca²⁺ from the endoplasmic reticulum
   C. normal Ca²⁺ release, because diacylglycerol alone opens the ER channels
   D. permanent activation of protein kinase C

**Answers and explanations**

1. **B.** The maximum is about 100 (the rate at 20 mM is 97.6 and still approaching a limit). Half of that is 50, which is the rate measured at 0.5 mM glucose. [2A · Skill 4] (A is the concentration at which uptake is only one third of the maximum; C is the concentration at which uptake is two thirds of the maximum; D is the uptake rate at half-maximum, not a glucose concentration.)
2. **A.** Ouabain inhibits the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, so intracellular Na⁺ rises and the inward Na⁺ gradient shrinks. The cotransporter is untouched, but it has less driving force. The Km is unchanged (20 is half of 40 at 0.5 mM), which argues against a competitive effect on glucose binding. [2A · Skill 4] (B would raise the apparent Km, but the half-maximal concentration is unchanged; C the cotransporter does not hydrolyze ATP; it uses the Na⁺ gradient; D glucose is polar and does not cross a bilayer unaided, and the remaining uptake is still saturable.)
3. **D.** The pump exports 3 Na⁺ and imports 2 K⁺ per ATP. When it is inhibited, Na⁺ leaks in and K⁺ leaks out down their gradients, so the gradients decline over time. [2A · Skill 2] (A is the opposite of the effect of stopping the pump; B ignores that the pump is the source of both gradients; C ignores that the pump also moves K⁺ into the cell.)
4. **A.** A rescue (reversal) experiment shows that the effect of removing Na⁺ is reversible and not a result of damaged cells. If uptake recovers when Na⁺ returns, the loss was due to the missing Na⁺. [2A · Skill 3] (B tests saturation, not the cause of the low rate; C adds a second inhibitor and does not test choline toxicity; D changes a different variable and leaves the choline question untouched.)
5. **B.** Cells with 2C DNA are in G1 (or G0). They make up 55 percent of the population, and 0.55 × 20 hours = 11 hours. [2C · Skill 4] (A is the time for the 4C population (G2 and M); C is the time for the cells between 2C and 4C (S phase); D uses the fraction of cells that are not in the 2C group.)
6. **A.** The cells have replicated their DNA (4C) and entered mitosis (condensed chromosomes, no nuclear envelope) but cannot proceed to anaphase, as expected when chromosomes are not properly attached to the spindle. [2C · Skill 4] (B would increase the 2C group, but the 4C group rose; C would increase the group between 2C and 4C, which fell to 12 percent; D G2 cells have an intact nuclear envelope and uncondensed chromosomes, unlike the cells observed.)
7. **D.** Drug E causes a G1 arrest (2C rises to 90 percent) in cells with Rb but not in cells without it. The effect depends on Rb, which fits inhibition of the cyclin-CDK that phosphorylates Rb, leaving Rb bound to E2F. Without Rb, there is no brake to keep applied. [2C · Skill 3] (A would release E2F and remove the brake in normal cells instead of arresting them; B would increase the 4C group; the 2C group rose instead; C the Rb-deleted cells show 21 percent in S phase, so they are actively cycling.)
8. **C.** After S phase, each of the 46 chromosomes has two sister chromatids, giving 92 chromatids. They stay joined until anaphase, and chromosomes are counted by centromere, so there are still 46 chromosomes. [2C · Skill 2] (A describes a G1 cell, before replication; B describes a cell after sister chromatids have separated in anaphase; D doubles both counts.)
9. **C.** Transfer occurred only when the cells were mixed together and not when they were separated by a filter that passes DNA and viruses. DNase made little difference, so the DNA was not free in the medium. [2B · Skill 4] (A naked DNA would cross the filter, and DNase would be expected to reduce the colonies markedly; B a virus would cross the filter, and no virus was added to the mixed culture; D would give colonies in the filter arm too, since recipients were present there.)
10. **D.** CFU/mL = colonies / (volume plated in mL × dilution) = 82 / (0.10 × 10⁻²) = 82 / 10⁻³ = 8.2 × 10⁴. [2B · Skill 4] (A divides by the plated volume but ignores the dilution; B divides by the dilution but ignores the plated volume; C uses a dilution of 10⁻³ instead of 10⁻².)
11. **A.** Finding the donor's plasmid DNA in the resistant recipient colonies directly links resistance to a transferred genetic element. The other observations would be the same whether or not the plasmid had moved. [2B · Skill 3] (B is true of any recipient cell, and does not show that a donor gene was acquired; C would apply equally to resistant and sensitive cells of the same strain; D depends on growth rate and says nothing about the source of the resistance.)
12. **C.** A plasmid with its own transfer genes lets the new host become a donor. A recipient that gets only a chromosomal fragment would generally not be able to form a pilus or transfer DNA in this way. [2B · Skill 2] (A a chromosomal copy would not by itself provide the machinery for transfer; B the filter arm showed that free DNA did not produce colonies; D a spontaneous mutation does not explain the transfer to a third strain.)
13. **B.** Reverse transcriptase copies the single-stranded RNA genome into DNA. Without it, there is no DNA to integrate as a provirus, so the later steps do not occur. [2B · Skill 2] (A occurs on the cell surface before the viral enzyme is involved; C uses a host enzyme and begins after reverse transcription and integration; D uses host ribosomes, not reverse transcriptase.)
14. **B.** Phospholipase C cleaves PIP₂ into IP₃ and DAG. IP₃ opens Ca²⁺ channels in the ER, so inhibiting the enzyme reduces both second messengers and the calcium signal. [2A · Skill 2] (A phosphodiesterase breaks down cAMP, and phospholipase C cleaves PIP₂; C IP₃ opens the ER Ca²⁺ channels, and the drug reduces its production; D less diacylglycerol is made, so kinase C activation is reduced.)

Praxis Tip: Passage 1 was a transport question disguised as a table. If you missed Question 2, check whether you tried to find a "direct" effect of ouabain on the cotransporter. The drug acts on the pump; the cotransporter simply loses its fuel.

Praxis Tip: In Passage 2, the pattern across the three columns told you the phase before the text did. A rise in the 2C column means a G1 block; a rise in the 4C column means a block after S. Practice naming the block from the shape of the numbers alone.

Praxis Tip: In Passage 3, the strongest evidence was the arm that failed (the filter). In a design question, ask which arm excludes the most alternative explanations, and give that arm the most weight.

Praxis Tip: For any dilution or doubling calculation, finish by asking whether the answer is the right order of magnitude. A culture count lower than the number of colonies you counted is a sign that you divided where you should have multiplied.
