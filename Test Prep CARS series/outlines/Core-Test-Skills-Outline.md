# Core Test Skills: shared outline for every exam book

**Series author:** U. B. Smart  
**Genre in Book Writer:** Exam Prep Course  **Chapters:** 36  **Status:** outline, not yet drafted
**Purpose:** one exam-agnostic book whose chapters are written and verified once, then reused in every exam project (MCAT series, SAT, ACT, GRE, GMAT, LSAT, DAT/OAT/PCAT, AP/CLEP, and undergraduate course exams). It can also be sold on its own as a general test-skills course.

## Design rules

- **No exam facts inside CORE text.** Anything exam-specific (timing, question counts, scoring, section names) uses a placeholder, listed below, and is filled in per exam.
- **Examples come from neutral subject matter** (everyday quantitative problems, short original passages, small made-up datasets). Never use released-exam items or commercial prep-book material.
- **Learning-science claims stay modest and accurate.** Spaced practice, retrieval practice, interleaving, sleep and feedback are well supported; present them as evidence-informed habits, not guarantees. Anything uncertain gets `[VERIFY]` for expert review, and any named study must be real and listed in the ledger.
- Every chapter keeps the course template: objectives, content, worked example, at least 8 Praxis Tips, a "Do this today" list, recap.

## Placeholders (fill per exam)

`{EXAM}` exam name used descriptively  `{SECTIONS}` section names  `{TIME_PER_SECTION}`  `{QUESTION_COUNT}`  `{SCORE_SCALE}` (plain description, no predictions)  `{TEST_FORMAT}` (computer or paper, breaks)  `{LEVEL}` (high school, undergraduate, graduate/professional)

## Premise (paste into Book Writer)

> A self-paced course in the study and test-taking skills that apply to any high-stakes exam: how to plan a schedule, learn and retain material, practice effectively, read and reason under time pressure, handle numbers, arguments and data, manage stress and test day, and learn from every practice test. Skills are taught with original examples and many Praxis Tips (practical techniques), with exam-specific details left as fill-in slots. All content is original or openly licensed; no released exam content or commercial prep-book text is used. For learners in high school, undergraduate or graduate programs. Not affiliated with or endorsed by any exam owner.

## Chapter list

### Part 1. Learn how to learn

1. **Strategy: How This Course Works and How Learning Works** Retrieval, spacing, interleaving, feedback, and why re-reading feels productive but often isn't.
2. **Strategy: Read Your Exam's Public Blueprint** A fill-in worksheet: collect `{EXAM}` format, `{TIME_PER_SECTION}`, `{QUESTION_COUNT}`, `{SCORE_SCALE}` from the official free source and nowhere else.
3. **Strategy: Set a Target and Plan Backwards** Choose a goal from program requirements, work back to weekly hours. Praxis Tip: write the target and the date on one card.
4. **Strategy: Take a Baseline Diagnostic** How to run one, read it by topic and by error cause, and what not to conclude from one score.
5. **Strategy: Build a Schedule That Survives Real Life** Time blocks, buffer weeks, zero-study days, adjusting after a bad week.
6. **Strategy: Spaced Repetition and Flashcards That Work** Interval ladders (for example 1, 3, 7, 21 days), card design (one idea per card, question not statement), when to retire cards.
7. **Strategy: Retrieval Practice and Teach-Back** Closed-book recall, blank-page summaries, explaining aloud.
8. **Strategy: Interleaving and Mixed Practice** Why mixing topics feels harder and helps transfer; how to build a mixed set.

### Part 2. Practice with purpose

9. **Strategy: The Error Log** Categories (content gap, misread, rushed, trap choice, calculation slip), weekly review, fixing the biggest cause first.
10. **Strategy: The Question Method** Read, predict, eliminate, verify; reading the question stem first when appropriate.
11. **Strategy: Pacing and the Flag-and-Move Rule** Checkpoints, per-question time budgets, when to skip.
12. **Strategy: Guessing Smart and Calibrating Confidence** Elimination odds, rating confidence on each answer, finding where you are over- or under-confident. Praxis Tip: check whether the exam penalizes wrong answers before adopting any guessing rule.

### Part 3. Reasoning skills

13. **Strategy: The Quantitative Toolkit** Estimation, powers of ten, percents, ratios, unit tracking, sanity checks.
14. **Strategy: Algebra and Number Sense Under Time Pressure** Setting up equations, plugging in numbers, working backwards from choices, common slips.
15. **Strategy: Reading Dense Passages** Skim for structure, annotate purpose not detail, build a one-line map per paragraph, handle unknown words.
16. **Strategy: Arguments and Logic Basics** Premise and conclusion, assumptions, necessary vs sufficient, common fallacies, strengthen/weaken.
17. **Strategy: Reading Tables, Graphs and Experiments** Axes and units first, variables and controls, trends vs outliers, correlation vs causation.
18. **Strategy: Statistics for Test Takers** Mean, median, spread, probability basics, sampling bias, reading error bars, interpreting p-values without overclaiming.
19. **Strategy: Writing Under Time** A simple structure for essays and short responses, planning in two minutes, editing checklist.
20. **Strategy: Vocabulary in Context** Roots, prefixes, context clues, building a word list from your own reading.

### Part 4. Body, mind and support

21. **Strategy: Memory Techniques That Respect Understanding** Chunking, mnemonics, loci, when a mnemonic helps and when it hides a gap.
22. **Strategy: Focus, Sleep and Energy** Study environment, phone discipline, breaks, sleep consistency, exercise and food basics. Not medical advice.
23. **Strategy: Test Anxiety and Mindset** Reframing, breathing, rehearsal, what to do in a blank moment; when to seek professional support.
24. **Strategy: Study Groups, Tutors and Office Hours** Choosing partners, running a session, preparing questions, using feedback.

### Part 5. Simulation and test day

25. **Strategy: Simulate Test Day** Full-length protocols for `{TEST_FORMAT}`: timing, breaks, environment, when to take them in the schedule.
26. **Strategy: Review a Practice Test** Error-log analysis, one-week fix plan, re-attempting misses after a gap.
27. **Strategy: Your Test-Day Plan** Logistics checklist, what to bring, pacing checkpoints, handling a bad section.
28. **Strategy: After the Score** Reading a score report, retake decision framework, applications timeline. Praxis Tip: decide your retake rule before you test.

### Part 6. Original skill drills

29. **Practice: Estimation and Number Sense Drills** Original timed drill set with explanations.
30. **Practice: Logic and Argument Drills** Original short arguments and questions.
31. **Practice: Data Interpretation Drills** Original small tables, graphs described in text, and experiments.
32. **Practice: Reading Structure Drills** Original passages with structure-mapping questions.

### Backmatter

33. **Appendix: Source and License Ledger** Template, licensing basics, release checklist.
34. **Appendix: Study Planner Templates** Weekly and monthly planners, spaced-repetition tracker.
35. **Appendix: Error Log Template and Analysis Sheet** Columns, cause codes, weekly summary.
36. **Appendix: Glossary** Test-prep, learning and statistics terms.

## Reuse workflow

1. Draft this book first in its own saved project and have it expert-reviewed, including every claim about learning, sleep and anxiety.
2. For a new exam, create the project, then copy the needed Core chapters' text in (project files are JSON, or paste through the Edit a Manuscript flow), fill the placeholders, and renumber cross-references.
3. Add the exam-specific Lessons and original Practice chapters on top.
4. Keep one version number for Core. When a fix is made, apply it to every exam project that imported the chapter, and note it in each project's ledger.
5. Never let an exam-specific fact (timing, scoring) live in the Core project. It belongs only in the per-exam copy.
