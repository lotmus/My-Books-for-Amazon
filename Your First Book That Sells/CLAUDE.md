## House style (inferred from the one chapter that exists — treat as provisional)

- Terse, declarative, second person (“you”). No filler, no hedging beyond what
  the facts require.
- Sections are numbered with capital roman numerals and a plain-language title
  (`I. Selling a few copies is not a business`), each running long enough to
  make one complete argument.
- Concrete numbers over adjectives: prices, percentages, day counts, not
  “expensive” or “soon.”
- Amazon/KDP rules are stated as constraints (cite the actual policy); anything
  about ranking or reader behavior that isn't a published rule is explicitly
  labeled as inference or observation, not fact.
- Curly quotes and apostrophes (`“ ” ‘ ’`), spaced em dashes (` — `). No straight
  quotes.
- A chapter ends by naming the myths it just disproved (`Claims that do not
  survive a spreadsheet`) and a one-line source/citation paragraph, plus a
  disclaimer that it is not legal advice and not an Amazon publication.
- Case studies cite named, checkable sources (author, title, publication/venue,
  year) rather than composite or anonymized anecdotes.
