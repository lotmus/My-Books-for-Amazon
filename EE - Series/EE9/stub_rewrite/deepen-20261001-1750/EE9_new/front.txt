SUBTITLE: Advanced Packages, Power Delivery, Board Materials, Layout, Cooling, MEMS, and the Robot
HOW: The earlier books designed circuits, devices, radios, converters, and test methods. This last book is about where they meet: the package that holds the chips, the copper that carries their current and signals, the board materials and layout that shape those signals, and the heat they produce. Chapter 1 uses the AI accelerator of 2026 to show why these have become first-rank problems. Chapters 2 to 6 treat packages, power delivery, board materials, layout, and cooling, and Chapter 7 is the series' survey of MEMS. Chapters 8 to 16 deepen each thread: chiplets and yield, package parasitics and reliability, decoupling, stack-up, crosstalk, high-speed loss and equalization, optical interconnect, and inertial sensors. Chapter 17 places the robot, which uses nearly every book at once, in the picture.
HOW: Each chapter states its question, develops the idea in words, and works it through with declared numbers. Every number was computed, and the answers to every practice problem are in Appendix B. Where a figure describes a 2026 product, it is labeled as a snapshot and its source is given; the physics and arithmetic are meant to outlast the products.
HOW: The series gives each subject one home. Conversion efficiency and the regulators that make the processor's current are Book 8's. Transmission lines, matching, and the reference laminate are Book 4's. Emission limits and the test chamber are Book 7's. Device physics and piezoelectric films are Book 3's. Control loops, including the robot's tracker, are Book 2's. This book's anchor numbers are an HBM4 stack at about 2.8 TB/s against 51.2 GB/s for one DDR5-6400 channel, a ratio of about 55, and the 875 A that a 700 W processor draws at 0.8 V. The texts in Appendix C go deeper; this book's explanations, examples, and problems are its own.
