H1: Chapter 21. IoT Radios: BLE, 802.15.4, and NB-IoT
P: Most radios in the world are not phones. They are sensors, tags, locks, and meters that send a few bytes now and then and must run for years from a coin cell or a small battery. Their physical layers are designed around energy per delivered bit rather than spectral efficiency. This chapter examines three families, Bluetooth Low Energy, IEEE 802.15.4 (the base of Zigbee and Thread), and NB-IoT, and shows how the duty cycle, the spreading, and the repetitions set battery life and range.
H2: 21.1 The energy budget of a coin cell
P: A CR2032 lithium coin cell stores about 225 mAh at a nominal 3 V, which is about 2430 J. A radio that draws milliamperes while active can only last years if it is active for a tiny fraction of the time. The average power is the active current times the duty cycle plus the sleep current, all times the supply voltage, and the sleep current, often a microampere or two, can dominate.
EQ: Pavg = V (Iactive × ton/T + Isleep),   life = Ebattery / Pavg
H2: Worked Example 21.1
WEP: A BLE beacon draws 5 mA for 1 ms once every second from a 3 V CR2032 (2430 J). Find the average power and the battery life, first ignoring sleep current and then with 1 µA of sleep current.
WES: The average current is 5 mA × 1 ms/1 s = 5 µA, so Pavg = 15 µW and the life is 2430/15×10⁻⁶ = 1.62×10⁸ s, about 5.1 years. With 1 µA of sleep current the average becomes 6 µA, 18 µW, and the life falls to about 4.3 years. One microampere of sleep costs most of a year; self-discharge and temperature cut further in practice.
H2: 21.2 Bluetooth Low Energy
P: BLE uses 40 channels of 2 MHz in the 2.4 GHz band, three of which are advertising channels placed to avoid the most common Wi-Fi channels. The modulation is Gaussian frequency-shift keying at 1 Msymbol/s, with an optional 2 Mbit/s mode for speed. For range, the Coded PHY repeats each bit with forward error correction: S = 2 gives 500 kbit/s and S = 8 gives 125 kbit/s. Eight coded symbols per bit give 10 log₁₀ 8 = 9.0 dB more energy per bit, which, after the coding gain, extends the range by roughly a factor of two to four in open space.
H2: 21.3 IEEE 802.15.4 and direct-sequence spreading
P: In the 2.4 GHz band, 802.15.4 sends 4-bit symbols, each mapped to one of 16 nearly orthogonal sequences of 32 chips at 2 Mchip/s, with offset-QPSK modulation. The symbol lasts 16 µs and the data rate is 250 kbit/s. The spreading is Chapter 12's direct-sequence idea: 2 Mchip/s carrying 250 kbit/s gives a processing gain of 10 log₁₀ 8 = 9.0 dB, which buys robustness against narrowband interference. Zigbee and Thread build mesh networks on top of this PHY, so a message can hop from node to node to cover a building.
EQ: Rb = 4 bits / 16 µs = 250 kbit/s,   Gp = 10 log₁₀(2×10⁶/2.5×10⁵) = 9.0 dB
H2: Worked Example 21.2
WEP: Find the 802.15.4 symbol duration and the processing gain from the chip rate and data rate.
WES: Thirty-two chips at 2 Mchip/s take 16 µs, which carry 4 bits, so Rb = 250 kbit/s. The ratio of chip rate to bit rate is 8, and Gp = 9.0 dB.
KEY: Key idea. IoT radios trade rate for energy and range. Duty cycle and sleep current set battery life; spreading and coding add energy per bit where it counts.
H2: 21.4 NB-IoT and coverage by repetition
P: NB-IoT is a cellular IoT system inside a 180 kHz carrier, a single LTE resource block, which can be deployed in-band, in an LTE guard band, or standalone. Its main tool for deep coverage, in basements and meter cabinets, is repetition: the same block is sent many times and the receiver combines the copies. Each doubling of repetitions doubles the energy per bit and gains about 3 dB, so the target of about 20 dB of extra coverage over normal LTE needs about 100 repetitions, at the cost of a longer airtime and more energy per message. Power-saving mode and extended discontinuous reception let the device sleep for hours between contacts.
EQ: gain ≈ 10 log₁₀ R,   R ≈ 10^(gain/10)
H2: Worked Example 21.3
WEP: How many repetitions give a 20 dB coverage gain, and what does the airtime of a 10 ms block become?
WES: R = 10^(20/10) = 100 repetitions, so the airtime rises to about 1 s. In practice coding and channel-estimation losses mean slightly more is needed. The energy per message rises in proportion, which is why NB-IoT devices in deep coverage last fewer years than those near the base station.
