H1: Chapter 13. Channel Estimation and Pilots
P: The one-tap OFDM equalizer of Chapter 11 needs the channel's complex gain on every subcarrier, and the MIMO receivers of Chapter 7 need the whole channel matrix. The receiver learns them from pilots, known symbols placed at known positions in time and frequency. Pilots cost capacity, so a system places as few as it can while still following the channel's changes. This chapter shows how the channel's coherence bandwidth and coherence time from Chapter 5 set the pilot spacing, how noise in the estimate costs SNR, and how practical systems arrange their pilots.
H2: 13.1 Sampling the channel
P: Pilots sample the channel's time-frequency response, and the sampling theorem of Chapter 2 applies. In frequency, the channel's response varies on the scale of the coherence bandwidth, set by the delay spread; pilots spaced Δfp apart can represent a channel whose delays extend up to 1/Δfp. In time, the channel varies on the scale of the coherence time, set by the Doppler spread; pilots spaced Tp apart can follow a Doppler of up to 1/(2Tp). Between pilots, the receiver interpolates.
EQ: τmax < 1/Δfp,   fD,max < 1/(2Tp)
H2: Worked Example 13.1
WEP: An LTE-like system has 15 kHz subcarriers with reference signals every third subcarrier after staggering, and a pilot interval of 0.5 ms in time. Find the maximum delay and Doppler the pattern supports, and the speed at a 2 GHz carrier.
WES: Δfp = 45 kHz, so τmax = 1/45,000 = 22.2 µs, well beyond the 4.7 µs cyclic prefix. With Tp = 0.5 ms, fD,max = 1/(2 × 0.0005) = 1000 Hz. At 2 GHz, λ = 0.150 m, so the speed is fD λ = 150 m/s, about 540 km/h. Practical estimators reach only part of these limits, because noise and imperfect interpolation degrade the estimate near them, but the margin explains why the pattern serves high-speed trains.
H2: 13.2 The least-squares estimate and its noise
P: At a pilot, the received value divided by the known pilot symbol is the least-squares estimate of the channel. It is unbiased but noisy: its error variance is the noise power divided by the pilot power. Using a noisy estimate to equalize the data adds a second noise term, so the effective SNR is lower than the data SNR. Averaging the estimates of K pilots within the coherence region divides their noise by K, and a minimum mean-square-error estimator, using the channel's known correlation across time and frequency, does better still.
EQ: SNReff ≈ 1 / (1/SNR + 1/SNRpilot),   SNRpilot = K × Ep/N0
H2: Worked Example 13.2
WEP: The data SNR is 20 dB. Find the effective SNR if the channel is estimated from a single pilot at the same SNR, and if eight pilots are averaged.
WES: With one pilot, SNRpilot = 100, and SNReff = 1/(0.01 + 0.01) = 50, or 17.0 dB, a 3 dB loss. Averaging eight pilots raises SNRpilot to 800, 29.0 dB, and SNReff = 1/(0.01 + 0.00125) = 88.9, or 19.5 dB, a loss of only 0.5 dB.
KEY: Key idea. Pilots sample the channel in time and frequency, so their spacing must satisfy a sampling theorem set by the delay spread and the Doppler. Their number and power set how much SNR the noisy estimate costs.
H2: 13.3 Pilot overhead and patterns
P: Every pilot replaces a data symbol, so the pilot density is a direct overhead. A pattern with one pilot in six subcarriers on two of seven symbols spends 2/42 = 4.8% of its resources on pilots per antenna port; multiple antennas multiply that, since each transmit antenna needs its own pilots, kept orthogonal by placing them on different resources or codes. Wi-Fi uses 4 pilot subcarriers out of 52 in a 20 MHz channel, 7.7%, to track phase, plus a training field at the start of each packet for the full channel. 5G NR makes its demodulation reference signals configurable, adding more for fast-moving users and fewer for static ones, an adaptive version of the same trade.
H2: 13.4 Blind and decision-directed methods
P: Once data symbols have been decided, the receiver can treat them as extra pilots, refining the channel estimate without any overhead, as the decision-directed equalizer of Chapter 11 does. Errors in the decisions then corrupt the estimate, so the method works best at high SNR and in combination with the decoder, iterating between estimation and decoding. Blind methods that use only the signal's statistics need no pilots at all but converge slowly and leave a phase ambiguity, so practical systems keep at least a few pilots.
