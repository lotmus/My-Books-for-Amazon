SUBTITLE: From Bits to Waveforms: Sampling, Modulation, Fading, OFDM, MIMO, and the 2026 Band Plan
HOW: A modem turns bits into a waveform and the waveform back into bits. This book is about that waveform: how many bits it carries, how much spectrum it uses, how much noise and fading it tolerates, and how a receiver written largely in software recovers it. Chapters 1 to 4 build the link on a channel that only adds noise: Shannon's limit, sampling and complex baseband, pulse shaping, and the constellations with their error rates. Chapters 5 to 7 add the real channel and the techniques that defeat it: multipath and diversity, OFDM, coding, and multiple antennas. Chapter 8 places the result in the 2026 band plan and in a software-defined receiver. Chapters 9 to 15 go inside the modem: its digital filters, carrier and timing synchronization, equalization, FSK and spread spectrum, channel estimation from pilots, multiple access and scheduling, and the link adaptation and HARQ that set real throughput. Chapters 16 to 21 apply the modem to whole systems: ranging and positioning, interleaving and error detection, massive MIMO and beamforming, interference and the cellular SINR, satellite links and non-terrestrial networks, and the low-energy IoT radios.
HOW: Each chapter states its question, develops the idea in words, and works it through with declared numbers. Every number in a worked example was computed, and the answers to every practice problem are in Appendix B. Readers should be comfortable with complex numbers, the Fourier transform at the level of Book 1, and basic probability.
HOW: The series gives each subject one home. Antenna gain and the Friis path factor are Book 4's. The power amplifier, transmitter watts, the synthesizer, and the receiver noise cascade are Book 4's. Emission masks, the test chamber, and certification are Book 7's. This book uses those results where it needs them and does not recompute them. The texts in Appendix C go deeper; this book's explanations, examples, and problems are its own.

CONST: Ideal converter: SNR = 6.02N + 1.76 dB; processing gain 10 log₁₀(fs/B) for complex sampling; jitter limit −20 log₁₀(2πf tj).
