H1: Chapter 2. Packages: Four Words and the Advanced Package
P: A bare silicon die cannot be soldered to a board. It needs a package that protects it, spreads its connections to a pitch a board can accept, carries its current and heat, and, increasingly, connects it to other dies. Packaging has its own vocabulary, and the words are often used loosely. This chapter fixes four of them precisely, because each names a different kind of thing, and then uses them to describe the advanced packages that made the AI accelerators of 2026 possible.
H2: 2.1 Four words for four different things
P: Flip-chip names a join: the die is turned face down and connected by solder bumps or copper pillars across its whole area, instead of by wires from its edge. Chip-scale names a size: a chip-scale package is no more than about 1.2 times the die's linear dimensions. Lead frame names a skeleton: a stamped metal frame to which the die is attached and wire-bonded, then molded, which is the basis of the SOIC, QFN, and TO packages. Interposer names extra wiring: a layer, usually of silicon, glass, or organic material, between the dies and the package substrate, that carries fine-pitch connections between dies. The four answer different questions, how it is joined, how big it is, what holds it, and what wiring is added, so a package can be described by several at once. A die can be flip-chip mounted without any interposer, and a silicon interposer is almost never used without flip-chip or hybrid-bonded dies on top of it. A fan-out package, in which a redistribution layer is built on molded dies to spread their connections beyond the die edge, is a package, not an interposer.
H2: 2.2 Connection density
P: The number of connections a package can make through an area falls with the square of the pitch. Wire bonds are limited to the die's perimeter. Flip-chip solder bumps, called C4 bumps, use the whole area at a pitch of about 130 to 150 µm. Microbumps between stacked dies or onto an interposer reach about 25 to 55 µm. Hybrid bonding, which joins copper pads and oxide directly without solder, reaches below 10 µm. Each step increases the density by roughly an order of magnitude and shortens the connections, which lowers the energy per bit of Chapter 1.
EQ: connections per mm² = 10⁶ / p²   (p in µm, square grid)
H2: Worked Example 2.1
WEP: Find the connection density for pitches of 130 µm, 40 µm, and 9 µm. How many C4 bumps could an 800 mm² die have at 130 µm, and how many connections at 40 µm?
WES: 10⁶/130² = 59 per mm², 10⁶/40² = 625 per mm², and 10⁶/9² = 12,346 per mm². The 9 µm hybrid bond is 209 times denser than the C4 bump. An 800 mm² die could carry up to 47,300 C4 bumps, of which most would be power and ground, and 500,000 connections at 40 µm. An HBM4 interface needs over 2000 data signals per stack, plus clocks, commands, and power, which is only practical at microbump pitch on a silicon interposer or bridge.
KEY: Key idea. Name the join, the size, the skeleton, and the wiring separately. Then pitch decides the rest: every halving of connection pitch quadruples the density and shortens the wires.
H2: 2.3 The advanced packages of 2026
P: A lithography tool can expose a field of at most 26 × 33 mm, 858 mm², the reticle limit, so no single die can be larger. The largest accelerators therefore combine two or more dies at the reticle limit with several HBM stacks on one package. TSMC's CoWoS family is the leading example. CoWoS-S places the dies on a single silicon interposer. CoWoS-L, used for the largest accelerators through about 2028, embeds small local silicon interconnect bridges in an organic redistribution interposer, allowing interposers several times the reticle size. Intel's EMIB embeds silicon bridges in the package substrate, and its Foveros stacks dies face to face. SoIC and other hybrid-bonded stacks join dies at single-digit micrometer pitch. Advanced packaging capacity, particularly CoWoS, was among the scarcest resources of the 2024 to 2026 AI build-out, and on-die or more tightly integrated memory beyond HBM remains on the roadmaps rather than in products.
H2: Worked Example 2.2
WEP: An interposer is 3.3 times the reticle area. Find its area. Can it hold two 800 mm² compute dies and eight memory stacks of about 11 × 11 mm each, and how much area is left for spacing?
WES: The area is 3.3 × 858 = 2831 mm². The two compute dies take 1600 mm² and the eight stacks 8 × 121 = 968 mm², a total of 2568 mm², or 91% of the interposer, leaving 263 mm² for the gaps between dies, which must be wide enough for underfill and assembly tolerances. That is close to the two-die, eight-stack layout of the leading accelerators of 2025 and 2026, and it shows why such interposers had to grow beyond three reticles.
H2: 2.4 Substrates
P: Below the interposer, the package substrate spreads connections to the board's pitch, about 0.8 to 1 mm for a ball grid array. Substrates are built from BT resin or from build-up films such as Ajinomoto's ABF, with fine lines and microvias, and their availability has been a constraint in its own right. Low-temperature co-fired ceramic serves RF modules, and glass core substrates, which are flatter and more stable, are entering production. These module substrates are not the motherboard materials of Chapter 4; they are part of the package.
