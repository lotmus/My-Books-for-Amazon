## Description

Every conversion wastes power, and the waste is P(1/η − 1). Book 6 follows electrical energy from the grid to the processor and the battery and shows, with numbers, where each watt goes and how to keep it.

Part I builds the converters: efficiency curves and chains, the linear regulator and the TL431, the buck with real inductor and capacitor selection, the boost with its real gain, current-mode control and slope compensation, the SEPIC, the flyback, LLC, and the GaN USB-C charger, magnetics sized by area product and Steinmetz loss, boost and totem-pole PFC against the IEC harmonic limits, and high voltage: Marx generators, creepage, and stored energy.

Part II goes inside: losses, capacitor life, honest efficiency measurement, heatsinks, compensation for current-mode and digital control, light load with valley switching, burst mode, and PFM, batteries with state-of-charge estimation, balancing, and fast-charge limits, and motor drives with field-oriented control, dead time, and 800 V SiC inverters.

Part III covers the facility and the grid: the 48 V data-center chain and rack batteries, oversubscription, UPS storage from VRLA to supercapacitors, redundancy, grid codes with weak-grid SCR, frequency reserve, and harmonic limits, transformer aging, selectivity, and RCDs.

Part IV treats specialized converters: multiphase regulators with coupled inductors, phase shedding, and current sensing; gate drivers with ringing, desaturation, and CMTI; pulsed-power charging, solid-state Marx and inductive adders; solar with bypass diodes, storage coupling, and yield; and wireless power to SAE J2954 and Qi2.

More than 120 worked examples and nearly 200 practice problems, every answer in the back of the book.
