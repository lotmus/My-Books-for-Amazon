EE SERIES - BOOK PLAN
=====================
Unit of publication: ONE BOOK PER TIER. 21 books total.
Each tier folder is the outline for its book; each leaf topic folder inside
it is one chapter. 221 topics -> 21 books.

NAMING (matches the Math series convention, e.g. Abstract_Algebra_Book37.docx)
  Finished book : EE - Series/<Snake_Case_Title>_Book<N>.docx
  Chapter drafts: EE - Series/<NN. Tier>/Drafts/<Snake_Case_Chapter>.docx
  Do NOT leave .docx files loose inside leaf topic folders.

          Copper, taught here and drawn in Books 11 and 18. An LDO
          dies when the feedback divider is far from the pins, when
          the output capacitor's ESR and ESL are ignored, or when a
          shared return wrecks PSRR. A buck dies when the hot loop

          large, when AGND and PGND meet in more than one place, or

          snubber and the auxiliary winding to that same hot-loop
          rule. A regulator fails from the Bode plot or from that
          copper about equally often.

UMSPANNEN (2026-09-30)
  One Faraday law, four shelves. V = N dPhi/dt. Raising frequency
  lets you cut turns or core area for the same voltage, until
  copper AC resistance and core loss eat the gain. Do not write
  a 50 Hz tank, a flyback, a pulse transformer, and an RF balun
  as one chapter.

  Line frequency and distribution, 16 2/3, 50, and 60 Hz, plus
  400 Hz aircraft iron. Home: Book 15, shelf B, with shelf C
  when the insulation is oil-paper, a bushing, or a lightning
  impulse. Heathcote, J and P Transformer Book, or Kulkarni and
  Khaparde, Transformer Engineering. IEEE C57, including C57.110
  and K-factor, because a silicon-carbide inverter on a "50 Hz"
  transformer is a harmonics problem in the 2-9 kHz band.
  Flanagan, Handbook of Transformer Design and Applications,
  when one volume has to span power, audio, wideband, and pulse.
  McLyman remains the sizing procedure (area product, core
  geometry, wire, insulation, thermal) and his HF chapters are
  used on the switch-mode shelf, not instead of Heathcote.
  German shelf, if that language is the one open on the bench:
  Boedefeld and Sequenz, Kuechler, Hochspannungstechnik, and the
  VDE transformer specs. Kuffel and Arora/Mosch stay the
  insulation books.

  High-frequency power magnetics, kilohertz to low megahertz.
  Home: Book 15, the isolated-converter chapters, not shelf B.
  Kazimierczuk, High-Frequency Magnetic Components, is the
  frequency book: skin, proximity, Dowell, core loss, winding
  optimization. McLyman for the procedure. Erickson and
  Maksimovic magnetics chapters, and Hurley's magnetics-design
  material, for AC copper loss. Ferrite catalogs are design
  data, not optional reading: TDK/EPCOS, Ferroxcube, Magnetics
  Inc., with Steinmetz or improved-Steinmetz coefficients.
  Pairing for an offline or isolated supply: McLyman,
  Kazimierczuk, and Basso. Silicon steel is the 50/60 Hz core.
  Eddy currents remove it in the kilohertz. Ferrite, powder,
  and amorphous alloys take over, with lower peak flux and
  loss that grows as frequency and flux density rise. "It
  cooked at 8 kHz" is Kazimierczuk plus C57.110 plus the
  ferrite loss chart.

  Pulse transformers, nanoseconds to microseconds. Home:
  Book 15, shelf C, and Book 13 when the pulse is a radar
  modulator. Flanagan's pulse chapter. Lehr and Ron for pulse
  transformers and pulse-forming networks. Grossner,
  Transformers for Electronic Circuits, older and still the
  pulse and wideband reference. TI, ADI, and Infineon gate-drive
  notes for the small pulse transformer on a switch. Leakage
  inductance, capacitance, volt-seconds, and insulation dv/dt
  dominate, not iron loss at 50 Hz.

  RF and microwave, baluns, ununs, combiners. Home: Books 4, 8,
  and 17. Sevick and Mack, Sevick's Transmission Line
  Transformers, is this shelf's "umspannen": Guanella, Ruthroff,
  ferrite, high-power baluns. Once the winding is a fraction of
  a wavelength, the design quantity is even-mode and odd-mode
  impedance, and the core is a common-mode ballast, not the
  energy-storage path. Grebennikov's transmitter book for
  combiners. Bowick and the ARRL Antenna Book for amateur-power
  baluns. Marchand baluns and coax hybrids: Pozar plus vendor
  notes, not a laminated core.
  Short stack for a kilowatt HF balun: Sevick and Grebennikov.

  Copper and core rules every shelf states in its own words.
  Skin depth in copper is about 66/sqrt(f in hertz) millimeters.
  Above a few kilohertz the center of a solid wire is wasted:
  Litz, foil, or many strands. Proximity loss in a multilayer
  winding often exceeds skin loss. Leakage inductance and
  interwinding capacitance set bandwidth, pulse overshoot, and
  the snubber, and they set the first resonance a VNA or an
  impulse test will see. Impulse and dv/dt stress turn-to-turn
  insulation harder than 50 Hz rms does.

  Minimal magnetics pile, if a reader asks for three titles:
  McLyman to size it, Kazimierczuk for why frequency hurts the
  copper and the core, Sevick when the part has become RF.
  Add IEEE C57 and Heathcote when the iron is on the grid.
