**Author:** Lothar J. Musiol
**Format:** Kindle (master file: Communications_Wireless_and_SDR_Book5.docx)

## Description

How many bits can a radio channel carry, and how does a receiver written largely in software pull them back out of noise, echoes, and Doppler? Book 5 answers with numbers, from Shannon's limit to a sensor on a coin cell.

Part I builds a complete link: AM, FM, and PAM4 line codes; sampling, bandpass sampling, and complex baseband; pulse shaping and spectral regrowth; constellations, Gray coding, soft decisions, and an EVM budget; path loss, shadowing, and fading; OFDM with its frequency-offset and phase-noise limits; coding from Viterbi to LDPC and polar codes; MIMO capacity and Alamouti; the 2026 band plan; and the SDR hardware you can buy, from the RTL-SDR to the USRP B210.

Part II opens the modem: fixed-point filters, half-band and Farrow resamplers, Wi-Fi and 5G synchronization, MLSE and frequency-domain equalization, GMSK, GPS spreading and LoRa sensitivity, pilot design and channel aging, power control, OFDMA and random access, the 3GPP peak-rate formula, outage, and why Wi-Fi aggregates.

Part III applies it to systems: GNSS error budgets, RTK, Bluetooth channel sounding and 5G positioning; CRC, scrambling, LDPC base graphs, and Reed-Solomon; massive MIMO precoding, pilot contamination, beam management, and calibration; ACLR, noise rise, densification, and unlicensed sharing; satellite link budgets, rain fade, and constellation sizing; and LoRaWAN, BLE battery life, and passive RFID.

More than 120 worked examples use real systems (Wi-Fi 6 and 7, LTE, 5G NR, DVB-S2, GPS) and real parts (SX1276, nRF52840, AD9361), and over 200 practice problems have every answer in the back of the book.
