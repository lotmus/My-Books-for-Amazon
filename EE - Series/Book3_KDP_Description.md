## Description

Why does a diode "turn on" at 0.7 V, and why is that a useful sketch but a bad physical claim? Book 3 opens the blocks that Book 2 dropped into schematics and explains, with numbers, why devices behave as their data sheets say.

Inside you will find:

- Carriers and junctions: the silicon lattice, density of states and the Fermi level, doping, freeze-out and compensation, drift, diffusion, and velocity saturation, Hall and four-point-probe measurements, the pn junction and its C-V profile, the diode law derived from the law of the junction, Schottky contacts, and real diodes read from their data sheets (1N4148, 1N4007, BAT54, SS34, BZX84C5V1, SMBJ5.0A).
- GaAs, GaN, and InP: the two-dimensional electron gas, watts per millimeter of gate, and why a GaN power amplifier is not a silicon one.
- The bipolar transistor from Ebers-Moll to the hybrid-π, with a complete common-emitter design.
- The MOSFET from the capacitor up: body effect, MOS C-V, short-channel limits, FinFET fin counting, and gate-all-around nanosheets.
- Inside the amplifier: mirrors, differential pairs, Pelgrom matching, the cascode, a two-stage op-amp built from its transistors (the 741 and TL072 explained), and device noise with optimum source resistance for the NE5532 and OPA1612.
- CMOS as a chip: inverter delay and power, DVFS, SRAM, DRAM retention, flash and the new memories, ESD, latch-up, aging, and soft errors.
- Light and power: LEDs, photodiodes with a BPW34 transimpedance design, solar cells, breakdown, and the 650 V choice among silicon superjunction, SiC, and GaN.
- Making and modeling: the fab sequence, die yield and cost, piezoelectric AlN and ScAlN films for BAW filters, compact models, PDKs, and Monte Carlo yield.

Each chapter states its question, develops the idea in words, and works it through with declared numbers and real parts. More than 80 worked examples and over 150 practice problems, every one with its answer in the back of the book, make it a working reference for students, technicians, and engineers moving from circuits to the devices inside them.
