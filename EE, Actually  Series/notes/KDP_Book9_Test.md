## Description (KDP HTML)

<p><b>Choose the right instrument, connect it safely, and know what its reading means.</b></p>
<p>A meter can load the circuit it is measuring. A scope can show a convincing waveform while its probe changes the result. A spectrum analyzer can miss a signal because the settings do not match the question. <i>Test and Measurement Instruments</i> explains how instruments work, which specifications matter, how to connect them safely, and how to judge the uncertainty in a result.</p>
<p>Across 38 chapters, the book covers uncertainty and calibration; power supplies, source-measure units, loads, and switching; DMMs and LCR meters; oscilloscopes, probes, logic analyzers, and TDR; signal generators and analyzers; spectrum, noise, phase-noise, and network measurements; modulation analysis; optical, thermal, and EMC pre-compliance instruments; SCPI and VISA automation; production test; and building a capable home bench.</p>
<p>The examples connect instrument settings to real measurements and device parameters. Python listings show instrument-control patterns with simulation modes, while the text explains where vendor-specific commands, ratings, and manuals remain authoritative. Safety chapters cover measurement categories, protective earth, isolation, and appropriately rated differential or isolated measurement systems. Never defeat an oscilloscope's protective earth.</p>
<p>Inside: 50 figures, 111 worked examples, 234 practice problems with worked solutions, and instrument-selection and specification references. “From the Bench” and “On the Job” notes connect the theory to characterization labs, RF test, EMC work, and production lines.</p>
<p>For engineering students, electronics engineers, technicians, and makers who want to make measurements they can trust. The series assigns instrument operation and measurement practice to this volume; the earlier books develop the circuits and signals being measured. Book 9 of the nine-book Electrical Engineering Series.</p>


<p><b>Reader level:</b> Recommended for ages 18 and up: engineering students and adults with basic circuit knowledge. Lab work requires suitable instruments and safety training; hazardous measurements belong in a properly equipped, supervised setting. Age is not a safety qualification.</p>
