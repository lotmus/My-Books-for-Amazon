# KDP listing: RF, Microwave, and Transceivers (Electrical Engineering Series, Book 4)

Author: Lothar J. Musiol

## Description (KDP HTML)

<p><b>From the transmission line to the complete radio: matching, filters, antennas, amplifiers, receivers and synthesizers, with the numbers an RF engineer actually works with.</b></p>
<p>Why does a 10 pF capacitor turn into an inductor at 2.45 GHz? Why does half a decibel of cable cost a ground station almost two decibels of sensitivity? Why do base stations use the Doherty amplifier? <i>RF, Microwave, and Transceivers</i> answers those questions in 52 chapters:</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Passive structures:</b> transmission lines and reflection, L and pi matching, the Smith chart at work, S-parameters, filters and waveguide, antennas and arrays, couplers, microstrip by design, the Bode-Fano limit, cavities, propagation, circulators and antenna measurement.</li>
<li><b>Active circuits:</b> the load line, amplifier classes, linearity and the Doherty, heat and combining, receiver noise and dynamic range, phase noise and the PLL, radar, the LNA, mixers and frequency planning, and the link budget.</li>
<li><b>The transmitter in depth:</b> ruggedness and protection, vacuum tubes, RF exposure, transceiver isolation, class E and F design, envelope tracking and outphasing, PA measurement, digital predistortion, fractional-N and DDS synthesis, power control, and the classes side by side.</li>
<li><b>Device technologies and PA design:</b> CMOS power amplifiers that beat their breakdown limit with stacking, differential cores and transformer combining; a design cookbook with one complete numeric design per class, A to inverse F; the GaAs HBT handset amplifier and pHEMT LNA; GaN broadband class J and a 3.5 GHz Doherty; RF SOI switches, SiGe and InP at millimeter waves; and SAW, BAW and XBAR acoustic filters for the smartphone front end.</li>
<li><b>Measured data, sensing, light and the bench radio:</b> Touchstone .s2p files and noise parameters with a Python reader, FMCW radar front ends at 24, 60 and 77 GHz, microwave photonics and optical phased arrays, and projects a reader can build: crystal and FM radios, a 40 m Morse transmitter with its harmonic filter, an SDR receiver, LoRa and GPS antennas, satellite Doppler and a backyard hydrogen-line telescope.</li>
</ul>
<p>Every chapter ends with <b>What You'll Actually Use</b>, an honest list that separates the everyday working tools from the theory you mainly need for exams or deeper study. <b>From the Bench</b> notes add the practical layer: self-resonance, stack-ups from the fabricator, LNA placement, ruggedness testing, spur charts and coupler directivity.</p>
<p>Inside: 54 diagrams, including Smith-chart matching and noise circles, filter and antenna responses, load lines, class waveforms, a simulated acoustic ladder filter, noise cascades and link budgets, all computed from the worked examples; 319 practice problems, each worked in full with a check and the usual mistake; <b>On the Job</b> notes on the RF careers behind the chapters; and a linked source list. Written for engineering students and working engineers moving into RF. Book 4 of the nine-book Electrical Engineering Series; builds on <i>Foundations of Electronics</i>, and Book 5, <i>Communications, Wireless, and SDR</i>, builds on it.</p>


<p><b>Reader level:</b> Recommended for ages 18 and up: engineering students and adults with circuit-analysis, phasor, and complex-number foundations. The material is advanced and equation-heavy; Book 1 is the recommended starting point. Age is a guide; preparation matters more.</p>
