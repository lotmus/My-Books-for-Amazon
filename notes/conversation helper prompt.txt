===================================
Do a full speaker-attribution pass on this manuscript. Goal: every line of dialogue should be unambiguously attributable to a speaker — either by an explicit tag or by context so clear a reader/TTS system can't get it wrong — without making the prose read mechanically.
METHOD
1. Re-read the actual current file fresh — never trust memory of prior content, especially if the author edits manually between sessions.
2. For every quoted line, determine the speaker from context before touching anything. Build a full speaker map chapter by chapter — not just the lines that look hard.
3. Leave clean two-person alternation alone. If exactly two people are in the scene and a tag anchors the pattern (even loosely, even several lines back), don't add "X said" to every line — that produces the exact clunky, over-tagged prose to avoid. Re-tag periodically only where the original already does (that's normal, healthy style, not a gap).
4. Fix only genuine ambiguity: a bare line where two or more people are plausible and nothing — not alternation, not content, not an address like "Ms Wren" — resolves it. Use the minimal fix: attach a short tag to the existing sentence rather than inventing a new beat.
5. Raise the bar the moment a third voice is active in the scene. Two-party alternation tracking breaks down with 3+ speakers. At every point a new character's dialogue turn enters what had been a two-party exchange, and at any bare line where 3+ people have spoken recently, check it explicitly rather than assuming alternation still holds — especially in the first several lines after a large cast assembles, before the reader has locked onto who's who.
6. Merge the paragraph break between a naming beat and the quote it introduces. If a narration sentence names a character and the very next paragraph is a bare quote from that same character, merge them into one paragraph ("Schrottfinger pushed it aside. 'Now...'") instead of leaving them as two separate <p> blocks. This measurably helps disambiguation and reads better besides.
   — CRITICAL: verify the named character is actually the speaker before merging. A name near the end of a narration sentence is often the object of the action (whose hand was shook, whose chair was taken, who is being addressed as "Ms X"), not the speaker. Confirm from the following quote's content and from what happens after it, not from proximity alone.
7. Hunt specifically for "same speaker twice in a row with nothing between." Check every pair of consecutive dialogue-bearing paragraphs (adjacent, nothing else between them) that resolve to the same speaker. Two outcomes:
   - Legitimate: one continuous speech interrupted only by a descriptive beat ("Everyone looked at him. Bellew had not moved. He sat with his forearms on the table... 'Werner says...'") — no other character speaks in between, it's genuinely one turn split for pacing.
   - An error: what should actually be two different speakers alternating got both attributed to one person (by an earlier fix, or in the original). Re-derive from context: does the content read as one person building a point, or as a back-and-forth? A useful tiebreaker — if a reading exists that produces clean alternation with no repeats, and it's at least as well-supported by content/character voice as the repeated-speaker reading, prefer the alternating one.
8. When automating any of the above with search/regex, always hand-verify every hit before acting. Simple "name near 'said'" pattern matching produces false positives constantly — it will flag "'Professor Heitmann,' said Pilbeam" as a Heitmann line, because the name is close to "said" even though Pilbeam is the grammatical subject. Never bulk-apply a fix; read the surrounding paragraphs and confirm the actual speaker every time.
9. When a spot is genuinely a coin flip — two readings are both defensible, nothing in the text breaks the tie — say so explicitly rather than silently picking one. Give the reasoning for the choice made and flag it as a judgment call the author can override.
10. Validate mechanically after every batch of edits: the file must still be well-formed XML/HTML, quote marks must balance, and if repackaging an EPUB, the zip must be intact and every referenced file must actually exist on disk (don't trust a single unzip/extraction pass without a final diff against the source — silent extraction failures happen and are easy to miss).
11. Report what changed with enough specificity that the author can spot-check it: which lines, which chapters, and — for judgment calls — why.
