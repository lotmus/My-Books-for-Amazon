## Product description

For millions of years, Earth belonged to sensible creatures. Then came the land apes.

Dolphins have no libraries. They have coastlines, and coastlines are where humans do their loudest talking. For several thousand years they have listened to sailors read aloud, harbors gossip, and, lately, radios playing on deck. Now the International Dolphin Historical Society — whose votes are complicated by the fact that dolphins sleep one half of the brain at a time — has written the whole thing up.

The Dolphins' View of History runs from the first kept flame to the humanoid robots now being built with hands. Sumer and Egypt, China and India, Greece and Rome, the allegedly Dark Ages, the Silk Roads and the Black Death, the kingdoms of Africa, the Americas before Columbus, empires, revolutions, two world wars, six great countries that had terrible decades, the Cold War, the Moon landings and the 2026 return to lunar space, the physics a dolphin finds obvious, medicine, climate, and a present that cannot agree on what is true. Each of the 41 chapters ends with a dolphin verdict. The book ends with humanity's report card. Wisdom: Inconsistent. Treatment of Oceans: Needs Improvement. See attached plastic.

At the back: a timeline from the first whale ancestor to the heat death of the universe, a short appendix on what physics is actually for and the one theory it has not yet found, and a reading list for humans who want the full story from their own historians.

The jokes are the dolphins'. The history is real. Where the record is thin, the dolphins say so. Where it is disputed, they say that too, and try not to pick a side merely to sound decisive. War, greed, and racism are named plainly wherever they were the real reason something happened.

A dry, funny, and unexpectedly fair history of us, told by the neighbors.
