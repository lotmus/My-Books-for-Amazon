# REV4 Content and Continuity Audit

**Date:** 17 October 2026  
**Scope:** Current REV4 master and its active chapter Markdown/concatenated manuscript copies. This is an editorial and factual audit, not a full line edit. The current master notice in `notes/CLAUDE.md` says REV4 DOCX is authoritative and explicitly forbids rebuilding it with the older generator; no manuscript text was changed during this audit.

## Priority fixes

### 1. Cold War: Petrov’s report is described inaccurately — factual correction

Chapter 29 says Petrov “did not pass it up the chain.” He did report his assessment that the warning was false to the army command center/headquarters. The consequential choice was that he reported a false alarm rather than an apparent incoming attack; the text should not imply he withheld the event from command. Suggested replacement: **“Stanislav Petrov judged the warning false and reported that assessment to the army command center; he did not report an incoming attack.”** This also avoids overstating the counterfactual that his action alone prevented a launch. See Pavel Podvig’s account and the [American Physical Society history](https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2026/09/1983-averting-potential-nuclear-war).

### 2. First World War: broken Lusitania sentence — copy edit

Chapter 24 has: “...and in May 1915 the liner Lusitania, with the loss of 1,198 lives, an event...” The sentence has no finite verb for the Lusitania clause. Suggested repair: **“In May 1915, a German U-boat sank the liner Lusitania, killing 1,198 people; the attack helped draw the United States into the war two years later.”** Check the existing source note before changing the casualty phrasing.

### 3. Physics chapter: analogy exceeds the science — substantial qualification

Chapter 31 repeatedly treats echolocation as if it replaces vision in dolphin perception and as if it gives dolphins an intuitive route to relativity. Dolphins use both vision and echolocation; echolocation is especially important for some tasks, but is not their exclusive perceptual world. Research reports visual/echoic integration, including cross-modal matching ([Herman et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3893789/); [Harley et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8618101/)). Recast the contrast as a difference in available sensory channels, not “humans see, dolphins hear.”

The 1919 eclipse observation tested the gravitational deflection of starlight, not signal delay. The line that compares that result with a dolphin click confirming “the same underlying point” is scientifically misleading. The eclipse result was a measured change in apparent star positions ([NASA](https://eclipse2017.nasa.gov/testing-general-relativity); [ESO](https://www.hq.eso.org/public/images/potw1926a/)). Keep the dolphin aside only if it is clearly marked as a limited metaphor for timing and measurement.

The special-relativity section does say echolocation “is not relativity,” but then gives it more explanatory work than it can bear. Time-of-flight sensing does not demonstrate relativity of simultaneity or the invariant speed of light. Make that boundary explicit and avoid suggesting dolphins have a privileged intuitive understanding of relativity.

The wave/particle passage says objects behave as particles or waves “depending on how observed.” That shorthand can imply a classical object switches between two forms. More precise wording: experiments show wave-like interference and particle-like detection outcomes depending on the experimental arrangement. The APS pedagogy paper cautions against interpreting duality as particles following classical wave trajectories: [Physics Education Research paper](https://journals.aps.org/prper/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.6.020121).

### 5. Chapter endings: verdict rule and exception list disagree

The Book Plan says every chapter ends with a “Dolphin verdict.” Six numbered chapters currently lack one: Africa’s Kingdoms, Ships, Latin America, Ottoman, Soviet, and Jewish History. The current exception note lists only four (Africa, Ships, Soviet, Jewish), while the Ottoman chapter itself explains its deliberate lack of a verdict. Latin America has no corresponding exception or explanation. Either add a concise verdict to Latin America or document it as an intentional exception; update the instructions/reader guidance to name all deliberate exceptions. This is a book-wide consistency issue, not a request to force identical endings onto chapters whose subjects remain unresolved.

## Proportionality and structure

### 6. Aliens/SETI: one speculative case is over-elaborated

Chapter 36’s overall length is within the stated chapter range, and its main SETI/Fermi arc is coherent. The roughly 130-word ʻOumuamua paragraph gives one artificial-origin speculation more space than its place in the history warrants. Reduce it to two sentences: identify it as the first confirmed interstellar visitor, note that one Harvard astronomer proposed an artificial origin, and state that natural explanations such as outgassing remain the mainstream account. NASA summarizes the natural-origin evidence here: [ʻOumuamua](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/what-we-knowand-dont-knowabout-oumuamua/). The ancient-astronaut passage is already compact and usefully distinguishes claims from evidence; it does not need a blanket cut.

Chapter 13 spends several sentences on Fomenko’s “new chronology” and a related society. This is a minor fringe claim inside a short chapter whose real subject is why “Dark Ages” is a misleading label. Reduce the tangent to one dry sentence or remove it; the existing Petrarch/Carolingian/Lindisfarne material carries the argument without it.

The boto/vaquita wildlife paragraph is a roughly 100-word digression between U.S. intervention and dictatorship/disappearance material. Shorten it to a brief transition or remove the less relevant example, then make only enough further cuts to return near 1,100 while preserving the chapter’s central sequence and the unresolved contemporary consequences. This is a targeted trim; the chapter’s political history should not be compressed uniformly.

## Other factual and scientific precision

- **Medical language about dolphins:** Chapters 1 and 37 joke that dolphins have never needed doctors / have no medicine. The manuscript itself describes major disease mortality, and research suggests some dolphins may rub against bioactive corals or sponges in a way consistent with possible self-medication. The study presents this as a possibility, not proven intent ([iScience, 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2589004222005417)). The safe distinction is “no formal medical system or veterinarians,” with the self-medication evidence cautiously qualified.
- **Anthropocene vote:** Chapter 38 says the proposal failed “not because the evidence was wrong but because” members could not agree on its rank. The official ICS/IUGS statement confirms rejection of the formal epoch proposal but does not endorse that single explanation ([ICS statement](https://stratigraphy.org/news/152)). The sentence attributes motive too confidently. Say the proposal was rejected as a formal epoch while the geological evidence and wider use of “Anthropocene” remain; explain the formal classification dispute only with a source that directly supports the stated reason.
- **Washington and the crown:** Chapter 20’s “Washington refused a crown” formulation compresses several distinct episodes and can perpetuate a familiar myth. Lewis Nicola’s 1782 letter proposed a monarchical arrangement; Washington rejected it in a surviving reply ([Library of Congress letter](https://www.loc.gov/resource/mgw3h.002/?sp=308&st=text)). That is not the same as being formally offered and refusing an actual crown. If retaining the anecdote, name Nicola’s proposal and Washington’s written rejection rather than saying a crown was offered.
- **GPS:** The basic explanation that relativistic clock-rate offsets matter to GPS is sound, though the “pre-set slow by ~38 μs/day” phrasing compresses the combined gravitational and orbital effects. NIST’s account supports the net scale ([NIST](https://www.nist.gov/atomic-clocks/a-powerful-tool-for-science/putting-einstein-test)); retain only if the chapter makes clear that this is the net correction, not a single effect.
