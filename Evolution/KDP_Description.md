# KDP listing — *Life: Science for Everyone*

**Title:** Life: Science for Everyone
**Subtitle:** A Popular Science Book on Evolution
**Author:** Lothar J. Musiol
**Series:** Physics, Actually

## Product description

Ask a child what is alive and you get an answer in a second. Ask a biologist and you get a pause, then a list, then an argument about the list. A virus has genes but does nothing on its own. Fire eats, grows, and spreads, and nobody calls it alive. This book starts in that pause and follows it for four billion years.

Life: Science for Everyone walks from chemistry to consciousness. How atoms and water made chemistry that could copy itself. Why life stayed microscopic for about three billion years. How Darwin's idea arrived, how Mendel's peas rescued it, and how DNA explained it. Why sex is evolution's most expensive habit. Why cooperation evolved at all. The Cambrian explosion, the mass extinctions that reset the board, and the finches on one small Galápagos island that were measured almost every year for four decades, starting in 1973 — evolution caught in the act.

Then it keeps going where most evolution books stop: Hofstadter's strange loop, Dennett's dangerous idea, whether a mind could be built, and a gently satirical look at Hans Moravec's robot successors. The last chapter asks whether life on Earth arrived from space.

Written for a reader who never studied biology. Short sentences. Every term explained the moment it appears. Dry humor, never silly. Where the science is unsettled, the book says so.
