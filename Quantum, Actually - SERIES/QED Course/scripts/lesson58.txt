# How to use this lesson

Pair production, γ(k₁)+γ(k₂)→e⁻(p₁)+e⁺(p₂), completes the set of four two-body QED processes at leading order that this course examines in detail. It shares its diagram topology with electron-positron annihilation (Lesson 56, the same diagrams read in reverse) and, as this lesson shows, its vanishing interference term with both Lessons 56 and 56. The result is a lesson that reuses a great deal of prior work, lands on exactly Lesson 56's formula, as time reversal requires, and closes with a first, concrete physical threshold: the two photons need enough combined energy to create the pair at all. Expect five to seven hours.

## Learning objectives

- Set up the two diagrams for γγ→e⁻e⁺ and identify their internal momenta with t=(p₁−k₁)² and u=(p₁−k₂)².

- Show that the direct and crossed squared-amplitude traces, T_AA and T_BB, reduce to 8tu each, matching Lesson 56's formula exactly.

- Show that the interference trace T_AB vanishes identically, by the same interleaved-contraction mechanism as Lessons 56 and 56.

- Assemble the complete result (1/4)Σ|ℳ|²=2e⁴(t/u+u/t).

- Derive the threshold condition s≥4m² for pair production to occur at all, and relate it to the center-of-momentum energy.

- Reproduce the complete calculation and the threshold condition from memory.

# 1 The process and its diagrams

## 1.1 Setup

Following the identical Wick-contraction method as Lesson 56, but with the roles of initial and final states reversed relative to that lesson's process: ψ̄(x₁) creates the outgoing electron (ū(p₁)e^(ip₁x₁)), ψ(x₂) creates the outgoing positron (v(p₂)e^(ip₂x₂)), and A_α(x₁), A_β(x₂) absorb the two incoming photons (ε_α(k₁)e^(−ik₁x₁), ε_β(k₂)e^(−ik₂x₂)); the leftover ψ(x₁), ψ̄(x₂) form the internal propagator. The vertex integrals fix the direct diagram's internal momentum to q_A=p₁−k₁ (q_A²=t) and the crossed diagram's to q_B=p₁−k₂ (q_B²=u), the pattern of Lesson 50 and Lesson 56 applied to this process's own external legs.

## 1.2 The two amplitudes

$$ i ℳ_A = (ie)² ū(p₁) γ^α [i(q̸_A+m)/(q_A²−m²+iε)] γ^β v(p₂) ε_α(k₁)ε_β(k₂) , qquad i ℳ_B = (ie)² ū(p₁) γ^β [i(q̸_B+m)/(q_B²−m²+iε)] γ^α v(p₂) ε_α(k₁)ε_β(k₂)

### Worked example 1

Explain, in one sentence, why this process's amplitude structure closely resembles Lesson 56's ℳ_A, ℳ_B rather than Lesson 57's. Both this process and Lesson 56's involve one electron line and one positron line connected by a photon-emitting or photon-absorbing internal propagator (ū(p₁)⋯v(p₂) in both cases), whereas Compton scattering's amplitude instead has the same electron, ū(p′)⋯u(p), on both ends of the fermion line; the specific spinor sandwich, not merely the number of diagrams, determines which earlier calculation a new one most resembles.

# 2 Squaring: reusing two different results

## 2.1 The direct and crossed traces

Spin-averaging over the two unmeasured initial photon polarizations (a factor of 1/4, as for two unmeasured initial degrees of freedom in Lesson 56) and using electron completeness Σu(p₁)ū(p₁)=p̸₁+m, positron completeness Σv(p₂)v̄(p₂)=p̸₂−m, and the covariant photon polarization sum,

$$ T_(AA) = Tr[(p̸₁+m)γ^α(q̸_A+m)γ^β(p̸₂−m)γ_β(q̸_A+m)γ_α]

Comparing index placement with Lesson 56, Section 2.2's T_AA (which had the identical pattern with p₁↔p̸₁, p₂↔p̸₂ and q_A unchanged in role), the calculation is identical term for term. In the massless limit,

$$ T_(AA) = 8tu

by the same derivation as Lesson 56, Section 3, with t in place of that lesson's t and the roles otherwise unchanged (Exercise 4 verifies every substituted dot product). By the same s↔u-style relabeling used throughout this course (here, an α↔β, q_A↔q_B relabeling),

$$ T_(BB) = 8tu

## 2.2 The interference trace

T_AB, however, has a different index structure:

$$ T_(AB) = Tr[(p̸₁+m)γ^α(q̸_A+m)γ^β(p̸₂−m)γ_α(q̸_B+m)γ_β]

Here α surrounds the three-object chain (q̸_A+m)γ^β(p̸₂−m), interleaved with β, the pattern Lesson 57, Section 3.1, identified for Compton scattering's interference term, and the same as Lesson 56's.

### Worked example 2

State which of the two earlier lessons' methods therefore applies to T_AB, and why. Lesson 57's method applies, because the defining feature that made Compton's T_AB reduce so simply was the interleaved placement of the two contracted indices, not any detail specific to Compton scattering itself; whenever that same interleaved pattern recurs, as it does here, the identical reduction goes through.

# 3 The interference trace vanishes

## 3.1 The reduction

Applying the three-object reversal identity (Lesson 55, Section 3.1) to the chain surrounded by α, in the massless limit,

$$ γ^α [ q̸_A γ^β p̸₂ ] γ_α = − 2 p̸₂ γ^β q̸_A

so

$$ T_(AB) = Tr[ p̸₁ ( −2 p̸₂γ^βq̸_A ) q̸_Bγ_β ] = − 2 Tr[ p̸₁p̸₂ γ^β q̸_Aq̸_B γ_β ]

## 3.2 The vanishing

The remaining β-contraction surrounds q̸_Aq̸_B, a product of exactly two slashed vectors, so γ^βq̸_Aq̸_Bγ_β=4(q_A·q_B)I (Lesson 21). Following the identical computation as Lesson 57, Section 3.3, with q_A=p₁−k₁, q_B=p₁−k₂,

$$ q_A·q_B = p₁² − p₁·k₂ − k₁·p₁ + k₁·k₂ = 0 − (−u/2) + t/2 + s/2 = (s+t+u)/2 = 0

the same vanishing dot product found in Lessons 56 and 57, now appearing a third time. So

$$ T_(AB) = −2 Tr[p̸₁p̸₂ · 0] = 0

### Worked example 3

State, having now seen q_A·q_B=0 arise in three different calculations (Lessons 56, 57, and this lesson), what general feature of these processes is responsible. In each case, q_A and q_B are the two possible internal momenta obtained by subtracting one or the other of two "partner" momenta (the two photons, or a photon and its counterpart) from a common third momentum, and the specific combination s+t+u=0 (the massless sum rule, Lesson 53) forces their dot product to vanish regardless of which specific process or diagram assignment produced them; this is a structural consequence of the kinematics of any massless 2→2-type process, not a coincidence specific to any one calculation.

# 4 The complete result

$$ (1/4) Σ|ℳ|² = e⁴ [ 8tu/t² + 8tu/u² + 0 ] / 4 = 2 e⁴ ( u/t + t/u )

$$ (1/4) Σ|ℳ|² = 2 e⁴ ( t/u + u/t )

This is Lesson 56's 2e⁴[t/u+u/t] exactly. The two processes are time reverses of each other, with the same four-state average, so their spin-averaged squares must agree. Lesson 57's −2e⁴(s/u+u/s) is the same function with s and t exchanged by crossing, and a minus sign for the crossed fermion.

### Worked example 4

Verify that Section 4's result is positive for physical pair production, where t and u are both negative. With t<0 and u<0, t/u>0 and u/t>0, so their sum is manifestly positive, and Section 4's formula, a positive constant times this positive sum, is positive, consistent with it being a spin-and-polarization-averaged sum of |ℳ|².

# 5 The energy threshold

## 5.1 Why pair production has a threshold

Producing two massive particles (mass m each) from two massless photons requires at least enough energy, in the center-of-momentum frame, to create both particles: √s≥2m, that is,

$$ s ≥ 4 m²

Below this threshold, no real values of p₁, p₂ exist that conserve both energy and momentum with two on-shell electrons, so the process simply cannot occur, regardless of how large the coupling e is.

## 5.2 At threshold

At exactly s=4m², both outgoing particles are produced at rest relative to the center-of-momentum frame (their combined kinetic energy is zero, all the available energy going into their rest mass); for s only slightly above 4m², they are produced with a small, computable momentum.

### Worked example 5

Estimate the minimum photon energy, in the lab frame, needed to produce an electron-positron pair by colliding a high-energy photon with a photon of negligible energy (such as a low-energy background photon), given the threshold s=4m². Approximating one photon as having negligible energy compared to the other, s≈2E_(high)E_(low)(1−cosθ) for a head-on-ish collision; for a fixed, very small E_low, the required E_high to reach s=4m² grows correspondingly large, illustrating why pair production by a single energetic photon requires either a second photon of substantial energy or, as pursued in Lesson 86's later discussion of QED in external fields, an external field to supply the needed momentum transfer instead of a second real photon.

# 7 Summary

1. Pair production's two diagrams have internal momenta q_A=p₁−k₁ (t) and q_B=p₁−k₂ (u), with an amplitude structure resembling Lesson 56's (ū(p₁)⋯v(p₂)).

2. T_AA=T_BB=8tu, identical in form to Lesson 56's result.

3. T_AB=0, by the same interleaved-contraction mechanism as Lesson 57's Compton calculation, tracing to q_A·q_B=0.

4. The complete result is (1/4)Σ|ℳ|²=2e⁴(t/u+u/t), the same as Lesson 56's, as time reversal requires.

5. Pair production requires s≥4m², the center-of-momentum energy sufficient to create both particles' rest mass.

# Solutions

*Attempt each exercise before reading its solution. If you are stuck, read only the first sentence of the solution, then try again.*

**1.** ψ̄(x)=ψ†(x)γ⁰ contains b†, the electron creation operator (Lesson 49, Section 2.2), so it is the field that produces an outgoing electron; ψ(x) contains d†, the positron creation operator (Lesson 49, Section 3.1), so it produces an outgoing positron. Using the reversed fields (ψ for the electron, ψ̄ for the positron) would instead correspond to annihilating an incoming electron or positron, not creating outgoing ones.

**2.** By direct substitution into the given definitions, q_A²=(p₁−k₁)·(p₁−k₁)=(p₁−k₁)²=t and q_B²=(p₁−k₂)·(p₁−k₂)=(p₁−k₂)²=u, matching the notation table.

**3.** The two diagrams differ only in which of the two photon field insertions, A_α(x₁) or A_β(x₂), is paired with the incoming photon k₁ versus k₂; since photon fields are bosonic, this reassignment carries no minus sign (Lesson 48, Section 2.1), so the two diagrams add with a plus sign, as in Lesson 56, Section 1.2.

**4.** Following Lesson 56, Section 3's derivation with the substitutions appropriate to this process (q_A·p₁=t/2, q_A·p₂=−t/2, q_A²=t, p₁·p₂=s/2, all derived by the identical method as Lesson 56, Exercise 9, using t=(p₁−k₁)²=(p₂−k₂)² and momentum conservation p₁+p₂=k₁+k₂), T_AA=32(t/2)(−t/2)−16t(s/2)=−8t²−8ts=−8t(t+s)=8tu, using s+t+u=0 to write t+s=−u.

**5.** Exchanging α↔β and q_A↔q_B (equivalently t↔u) throughout Exercise 4's derivation, since T_BB's own internal momentum q_B takes over the role q_A played, gives T_BB=8ut=8tu, the same numerical value.

**6.** T_AB=Tr[(p̸₁+m)γ^α(q̸_A+m)γ^β(p̸₂−m)γ_α(q̸_B+m)γ_β] has α surrounding the three-object chain (q̸_A+m)γ^β(p̸₂−m), an interleaved pattern. Lesson 56's T_AB and Lesson 57's T_AB are interleaved in the same way, as every interference term between two diagrams with exchanged photons is.

**7.** By the identity γ^μABCγ_μ=−2CBA (Lesson 55, Section 3.1) with A=q̸_A, B=γ^β, C=p̸₂ (massless, m=0 in each factor), γ^α[q̸_Aγ^βp̸₂]γ_α=−2p̸₂γ^βq̸_A, reversing the order of the three factors.

**8.** Substituting Exercise 7's result into T_AB=Tr[p̸₁(−2p̸₂γ^βq̸_A)q̸_Bγ_β] (setting m=0 throughout) gives T_AB=−2Tr[p̸₁p̸₂γ^βq̸_Aq̸_Bγ_β], using cyclicity to reorder as needed to match Section 3.1's stated form.

**9.** q_A·q_B=(p₁−k₁)·(p₁−k₂)=p₁²−p₁·k₂−k₁·p₁+k₁·k₂. From t=(p₁−k₁)²=p₁²−2p₁·k₁+k₁²=−2p₁·k₁ (massless), p₁·k₁=−t/2, so −k₁·p₁=t/2 in the expansion above; similarly p₁·k₂=−u/2 from u=(p₁−k₂)²; and k₁·k₂=s/2 from s=(k₁+k₂)²=2k₁·k₂. Substituting, with p₁²=0: q_A·q_B=0−(−u/2)+t/2+s/2=(s+t+u)/2=0.

**10.** Combining Exercise 8's T_AB=−2Tr[p̸₁p̸₂γ^βq̸_Aq̸_Bγ_β] with γ^βq̸_Aq̸_Bγ_β=4(q_A·q_B)I=0 (Exercise 9), T_AB=−2Tr[p̸₁p̸₂·0]=0.

**11.** (1/4)Σ|ℳ|²=(e⁴/4)[T_AA/t²+T_BB/u²+2T_AB/(tu)]=(e⁴/4)[8tu/t²+8tu/u²+0]=(e⁴/4)[8u/t+8t/u]=2e⁴(u/t+t/u)=2e⁴(t/u+u/t).

**12.** For physical scattering, t<0 and u<0 (both spacelike momentum transfers, Lesson 53, Section 3.2, extended to this process), so t/u and u/t are each ratios of two negative numbers, hence positive; their sum is positive, and Section 4's formula, 2e⁴ times this positive sum, is positive.

**13.** Energy conservation in the center-of-momentum frame requires √s (the total available energy) to equal or exceed the minimum possible total energy of the final state, which is 2m (both particles produced at rest relative to the center-of-momentum frame, the configuration minimizing total energy for fixed rest masses); √s≥2m squares to s≥4m².

**14.** As s→4m² from above, the available momentum p→0 (both particles produced nearly at rest); as s→∞, p→√s/2 (both particles become effectively massless compared to the available energy, and the momentum grows without bound, approaching half the total center-of-momentum energy each).

**15.** Lesson 45, Section 4, showed that a single vertex cannot conserve on-shell momentum, since it would require the photon's own four-momentum to satisfy an impossible condition (p·k=0 there, forcing a zero-energy photon); pair production from a single photon would require an analogous single-vertex process, γ→e⁻e⁺, subject to the identical kinematic obstruction, so at least two photons (or, alternatively, an external field supplying additional momentum, as in Lesson 86) are needed, exactly as Compton scattering needed at least two vertices to occur at all.

**16.** Lessons 56 and 58 both give 2e⁴(t/u+u/t), a function of t and u: the processes are time reverses, with the same spin and polarization average, so the results must agree. Lesson 57's −2e⁴(s/u+u/s) depends on s and u. Crossing annihilation into Compton scattering sends t to s and leaves u in place, and moving one fermion across the reaction brings an overall minus sign. None of the three has an interference contribution: in each, T_AB is proportional to q_A·q_B, which vanishes for massless particles.
