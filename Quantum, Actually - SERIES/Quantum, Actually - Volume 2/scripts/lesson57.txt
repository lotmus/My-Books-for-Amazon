# How to use this lesson

Lesson 50 set up Compton scattering in full: the two diagrams, the vertex factors, the propagator, and the momentum-conservation bookkeeping that fixes each diagram's internal momentum to s=(p+k)² and u=(p−k′)² respectively. What Lesson 50 left undone was the spin sum. This lesson finishes it, reusing the trace-reduction method of Lessons 55 and 56 essentially unchanged, and finds again what Lesson 56 found: the interference term between the two diagrams vanishes identically, for the same reason, q_A·q_B=0. What remains is one of the cleanest formulas in this course. Expect six to eight hours.

## Learning objectives

- Recall Compton scattering's two diagrams from Lesson 50 and identify their internal momenta with s=(p+k)² and u=(p−k′)².

- Reduce the direct and crossed squared-amplitude traces to T_AA=T_BB=−8su using the same method as Lesson 56.

- Show that the interference trace vanishes identically, T_AB=0, because q_A·q_B=0.

- Assemble the complete result (1/4)Σ|ℳ|²=−2e⁴(s/u+u/s).

- Explain why no relative sign is needed between Compton's two diagrams, in contrast with Lesson 55's Møller scattering.

- Reproduce the complete calculation, and the vanishing of the interference term in particular, from memory.

# 1 Completing Compton scattering

IMG: L57_compton.png | 6.0 | Figure 57.1. Compton scattering: the s-channel (absorb, then emit) and u-channel (emit, then absorb) diagrams.

## 1.1 Recap

Lesson 50 derived iℳ_A=(ie)²ū(p′)γ^β[i(q̸_A+m)/(q_A²−m²+iε)]γ^αu(p)ε_α(k)ε_β(k′), with q_A=p+k the direct diagram's internal momentum (q_A²=s), and identified a second, crossed diagram with internal momentum q_B=p−k′ (q_B²=u, Lesson 50, Section 3.1). Both enter with a plain plus sign: the two diagrams differ in which of the two photon fields is contracted with the incoming versus outgoing photon, an ordinary bosonic reassignment carrying no minus sign, as in Lesson 56, Section 1.2.

## 1.2 The two amplitudes and the three traces

$$ i ℳ_A = (ie)² ū(p′)γ^β [i(q̸_A+m)/(q_A²−m²+iε)] γ^α u(p) ε_α(k)ε_β(k′) , qquad i ℳ_B = (ie)² ū(p′)γ^α [i(q̸_B+m)/(q_B²−m²+iε)] γ^β u(p) ε_α(k)ε_β(k′)

Averaging over the initial electron spin and photon polarization (a factor of 1/4, two unmeasured initial degrees of freedom, as in Lesson 56) and using electron completeness on both ends (Σu(p)ū(p)=p̸+m, Σu(p′)ū(p′)=p̸′+m) and the covariant photon polarization sum,

$$ T_(AA) = Tr[(p̸′+m)γ^β(q̸_A+m)γ^α(p̸+m)γ_α(q̸_A+m)γ_β]

$$ T_(BB) = Tr[(p̸′+m)γ^α(q̸_B+m)γ^β(p̸+m)γ_β(q̸_B+m)γ_α]

$$ T_(AB) = Tr[(p̸′+m)γ^β(q̸_A+m)γ^α(p̸+m)γ_β(q̸_B+m)γ_α]

with (1/4)Σ|ℳ|²=(e⁴/4)[T_AA/(q_A²−m²)²+T_BB/(q_B²−m²)²+2T_AB/((q_A²−m²)(q_B²−m²))].

### Worked example 1

Compare T_AA's index structure with T_AB's and identify the one difference. T_AA has α contracted directly around (p̸+m) alone (γ^α(p̸+m)γ_α, an adjacent pair), and separately β contracted around (q̸_A+m) on both sides; T_AB instead has α contracted around the longer chain (p̸+m)γ_β(q̸_B+m) (with a γ_β sitting inside it), an interleaved rather than adjacent pattern, which is exactly what Section 4 exploits.

# 2 The direct and crossed traces

## 2.1 The α-contraction

As in Lesson 56, Section 3.1, γ^α(p̸+m)γ_α=−2p̸+4m.

## 2.2 The massless result

Following the identical steps as Lesson 56, Section 3.2 (Exercise 5 carries out every term), setting m=0,

$$ T_(AA) = 32(q_A·p)(q_A·p′) − 16 q_A²(p·p′)

Using q_A·p=s/2, q_A·p′=s/2, q_A²=s, and p·p′=−t/2 (Exercise 6 derives each from t=(p−p′)², s=(p+k)²=(p′+k′)², and momentum conservation),

$$ T_(AA) = 32(s/2)(s/2) − 16s(−t/2) = 8s² + 8st = 8s(s+t) = −8su

using s+t+u=0 (all four particles taken massless in this limit) to write s+t=−u.

### Worked example 2

State T_BB by the same relabeling argument used in Lesson 56, Worked example 3. Exchanging s↔u throughout Section 2.2's derivation (since q_B²=u plays the role q_A²=s played for T_AA) gives T_BB=−8us=−8su, the identical value.

# 3 The interference trace vanishes

## 3.1 A different contraction pattern

Worked example 1 noted that T_AB's α-contraction surrounds the longer chain (p̸+m)γ_β(q̸_B+m), not (p̸+m) alone. Applying the three-object reversal identity (Lesson 55, Section 3.1) to this chain, in the massless limit,

$$ γ^α [ p̸ γ_β q̸_B ] γ_α = − 2 q̸_B γ_β p̸

so

$$ T_(AB) = Tr[ p̸′ γ^β q̸_A ( −2 q̸_B γ_β p̸ ) ] = − 2 Tr[ p̸′ γ^β q̸_A q̸_B γ_β p̸ ]

## 3.2 The remaining contraction

The β-contraction now surrounds q̸_Aq̸_B, a product of exactly two slashed vectors, so the two-object identity γ^μγ^aγ^bγ_μ=4g^ab (Lesson 21) applies directly:

$$ γ^β q̸_A q̸_B γ_β = 4 (q_A·q_B) I

Section 3.3 shows q_A·q_B=0 in the massless limit, so this vanishes identically, and

$$ T_(AB) = − 2 Tr[ p̸′ · 0 · p̸ ] = 0

## 3.3 Why q_A·q_B=0

$$ q_A·q_B = (p+k)·(p−k′) = p² − p·k′ + k·p − k·k′

Using p²=0, p·k′=−u/2, k·p=s/2, and k·k′=−t/2 (Exercise 9 derives the last from (k−k′)²=(p′−p)²=t),

$$ q_A·q_B = 0 − (−u/2) + s/2 − (−t/2) = (s+t+u)/2 = 0

the same vanishing dot product already found in Lesson 56, Exercise 13, for the analogous momenta of electron-positron annihilation.

### Worked example 3

State why the vanishing of T_AB is a genuinely useful simplification, not merely an aesthetic curiosity. Without it, the full result would require evaluating a nontrivial cross term depending on all of s, t, and u together; its vanishing means the complete answer depends only on T_AA and T_BB, each a function of a single Mandelstam variable, which is exactly why Section 4's final formula is so compact.

# 4 The complete result

$$ (1/4) Σ|ℳ|² = (e⁴/4) [ −8su/s² + (−8su)/u² + 0 ] = (e⁴/4) [ −8u/s − 8s/u ] = − 2 e⁴ ( s/u + u/s )

a result depending on only two Mandelstam-like combinations, symmetric under s↔u, and, since s>0 and u<0 for physical scattering (Lesson 53, Section 3.2, applied to this process), manifestly positive: s/u is negative, so −(s/u+u/s) is positive.

### Worked example 4

Explain, referring to Lesson 56, how this lesson's final formula is related to electron-positron annihilation's 2e⁴[t/u+u/t]. The two processes are related by crossing symmetry (previewed in Lesson 56, Exercise 16): move the positron of annihilation to an outgoing electron and one outgoing photon to an incoming photon. That exchanges the roles of s and t, so annihilation's t/u+u/t becomes s/u+u/s here. Crossing one fermion also brings an overall minus sign, which turns 2e⁴(s/u+u/s) into −2e⁴(s/u+u/s). In both processes the interference trace vanishes, through the same zero q_A·q_B=0.

# 6 Summary

1. Compton scattering's two diagrams, set up in Lesson 50, combine with a plus sign, since the exchanged role (which photon field pairs with which external photon) is bosonic.

2. Spin-averaging and summing, using electron completeness and the covariant photon polarization sum, reduces the calculation to three traces, T_AA, T_BB, T_AB.

3. T_AA=T_BB=−8su in the massless limit, derived by the same method as Lesson 56.

4. T_AB=0 identically, because the interference trace's middle contraction is proportional to q_A·q_B, which vanishes in the massless limit as it did in Lesson 56.

5. The complete result is (1/4)Σ|ℳ|²=−2e⁴(s/u+u/s), compact, symmetric under s↔u, and manifestly positive.

# Solutions

*Attempt each exercise before reading its solution. If you are stuck, read only the first sentence of the solution, then try again.*

**1.** The direct diagram has internal momentum q_A=p+k (q_A²=s); the crossed diagram has internal momentum q_B=p−k′ (q_B²=u) (Lesson 50, Sections 2.3 and 3.1). They combine with a plus sign because the two diagrams differ only in which of the two photon field insertions is paired with the incoming versus outgoing photon, a bosonic reassignment carrying no minus sign (Lesson 48, Section 2.1; Lesson 56, Section 1.2).

**2.** T_AA=Tr[(p̸′+m)γ^β(q̸_A+m)γ^α(p̸+m)γ_α(q̸_A+m)γ_β]: α is adjacent, directly surrounding (p̸+m); β surrounds the pair (q̸_A+m)...(q̸_A+m) with everything in between. T_AB=Tr[(p̸′+m)γ^β(q̸_A+m)γ^α(p̸+m)γ_β(q̸_B+m)γ_α]: α now surrounds the longer chain (p̸+m)γ_β(q̸_B+m), interleaved with the β contraction rather than nested inside it.

**3.** Two initial degrees of freedom are unmeasured and unprepared: the incoming electron's spin and the incoming photon's polarization; Lesson 52, Section 1.2, assigns a factor of 1/2 per unmeasured initial spin or polarization, giving (1/2)(1/2)=1/4 overall.

**4.** Identical to Lesson 56, Exercise 7: γ^α(p̸+m)γ_α=γ^αp̸γ_α+mγ^αγ_α=−2p̸+4m, using γ^μγ^aγ_μ=−2γ^a and γ^μγ_μ=4.

**5.** Following Lesson 56, Exercise 8's method exactly (with p₁→p, p₂→p′, and q_A unchanged), the leading (mass-independent) term of the expansion, after sandwiching by γ^β⋯γ_β and tracing against (p̸′+m), is 32(q_A·p)(q_A·p′)−16q_A²(p·p′), with every mass-dependent term vanishing as m→0.

**6.** From s=(p+k)²=p²+2p·k+k²=2p·k (massless), p·k=s/2, so q_A·p=(p+k)·p=p²+k·p=s/2. From s=(p′+k′)² similarly, p′·k′=s/2. Using momentum conservation k=p′+k′−p, k·p′=(p′+k′−p)·p′=p′²+k′·p′−p·p′=s/2−p·p′; and from t=(p−p′)²=−2p·p′ (massless), p·p′=−t/2, so k·p′=s/2+t/2. Then q_A·p′=(p+k)·p′=p·p′+k·p′=−t/2+(s/2+t/2)=s/2.

**7.** T_AA=32(s/2)(s/2)−16s(−t/2)=32(s²/4)+8st=8s²+8st=8s(s+t). Using s+t+u=0, s+t=−u, so T_AA=8s(−u)=−8su.

**8.** By the s↔u relabeling of Worked example 2 (since q_B²=u takes over the role q_A²=s played), T_AA's formula 8s(s+t) becomes 8u(u+t); using s+t+u=0, u+t=−s, giving T_BB=8u(−s)=−8us=−8su.

**9.** (k−k′)²=k²−2k·k′+k′²=−2k·k′ (both massless); using momentum conservation k−k′=p′−p, so (k−k′)²=(p′−p)²=(p−p′)²=t, giving −2k·k′=t, so k·k′=−t/2.

**10.** u=(p−k′)²=p²−2p·k′+k′²=−2p·k′ (both massless), so p·k′=−u/2.

**11.** q_A·q_B=(p+k)·(p−k′)=p²−p·k′+k·p−k·k′=0−(−u/2)+s/2−(−t/2)=u/2+s/2+t/2=(s+t+u)/2=0.

**12.** Applying the three-object reversal γ^μABCγ_μ=−2CBA (Lesson 55, Section 3.1) with A=p̸, B=γ_β, C=q̸_B (as fixed matrices, with the μ-contraction here relabeled α): γ^α p̸γ_βq̸_B γ_α=−2q̸_Bγ_βp̸, exactly reversing the order of the three factors.

**13.** γ^βq̸_Aq̸_Bγ_β=p_(A,a)p_(B,b)γ^βγ^aγ^bγ_β=p_(A,a)p_(B,b)(4g^ab)=4(q_A·q_B)I, using the two-object identity of Lesson 21 contracted with the momentum components of q_A and q_B. Substituting q_A·q_B=0 (Exercise 11) gives γ^βq̸_Aq̸_Bγ_β=0, so T_AB=−2Tr[p̸′·0·p̸]=0.

**14.** (1/4)Σ|ℳ|²=(e⁴/4)[T_AA/s²+T_BB/u²+2T_AB/(su)]=(e⁴/4)[−8su/s²−8su/u²+0]=(e⁴/4)[−8u/s−8s/u]=−2e⁴(u/s+s/u)=−2e⁴(s/u+u/s).

**15.** As a spin-and-polarization-averaged sum of |ℳ|² over specific configurations, the quantity is a sum of non-negative numbers and so must itself be non-negative (and, barring an exactly-vanishing amplitude at every configuration, strictly positive). For physical scattering, s=(p+k)²>0 (a timelike total four-momentum) and u=(p−k′)²<0 (spacelike, Lesson 53, Section 3.2, extended to this process), so s/u<0, making −(s/u+u/s) a sum of two positive numbers (since u/s is also negative, so −u/s is positive), confirming positivity directly.

**16.** Crossing the incoming positron of annihilation to an outgoing electron (p₂→−p′) and the photon k₁ to an incoming photon (k₁→−k) maps t′=(p₁−k₁)² to (p+k)²=s, u′=(p₁−k₂)² to (p−k′)²=u, and s′=(p₁+p₂)² to (p−p′)²=t. Lesson 56's 2e⁴(t′/u′+u′/t′) becomes 2e⁴(s/u+u/s), and moving one fermion between the initial and final states multiplies the spin-summed square by −1, giving −2e⁴(s/u+u/s), this lesson's result. Neither process has an interference term: in both, T_AB is proportional to q_A·q_B, which vanishes in the massless limit.
