# How to use this lesson

Lesson 20 introduced the gamma matrices as a means to an end, a device for writing the Dirac equation covariantly. This lesson turns them into objects of study in their own right. Every scattering amplitude computed from Lesson 54 onward will require manipulating products and traces of gamma matrices, often four, six, or more at a time, and doing so efficiently requires a toolkit of identities derived once, here, and reused constantly afterward. None of the identities in this lesson require an explicit matrix representation; all follow from the abstract Clifford algebra {γ^μ,γ^ν}=2g^μν alone, a fact worth appreciating each time it is used.

The lesson also introduces γ⁵, a matrix built from the product of all four gamma matrices, whose special properties make it the natural object for discussing parity and, much later in a full treatment of the electroweak theory (outside this course's scope), the chiral structure of the weak interaction. Within QED itself, γ⁵ appears chiefly in trace identities and in Lesson 23's discussion of chirality projectors. Expect seven to nine focused hours; this lesson is dense with algebra that rewards careful, exercise-by-exercise practice.

## Learning objectives

- Derive index-contraction identities such as γ_μγ^μ=4 directly from the Clifford algebra.

- Derive the basic anticommutation identities for a gamma matrix moving past a slash of an arbitrary four-vector.

- Compute traces of products of two and four gamma matrices using only the algebra, without any explicit representation.

- State and use the rule that the trace of an odd number of gamma matrices vanishes.

- Define γ⁵ and derive its key properties: Hermiticity, γ⁵²=I, and anticommutation with every γ^μ.

- Compute traces involving γ⁵ and recognize when the Levi Civita symbol appears.

- Construct the chiral projectors P_L, P_R and explain their role in splitting a Dirac spinor into two Weyl components.

- Recognize this lesson's identities as the direct computational engine behind every QED cross-section calculation in Part VIII.

# 1 Basic index identities

## 1.1 Contracting one index

Compute γ_μγ^μ = g_μνγ^νγ^μ. Using the Clifford algebra to write γ^νγ^μ = 2g^νμI − γ^μγ^ν and substituting,

$$ γ_μγ^μ = g_μν(2g^νμI − γ^μγ^ν) = 2g_μ^μ I − γ^μγ_μ = 2(4)I − γ_μγ^μ

using g_μ^μ=4 (the trace of the identity metric in four spacetime dimensions, Lesson 4). Rearranging, 2γ_μγ^μ=8I, so

$$ γ_μ γ^μ = 4 I

This is the first of several identities that hold regardless of any explicit representation, following purely from the Clifford algebra and the dimension of spacetime.

## 1.2 Moving a gamma matrix past a slash

For any four-vector a, define the slash a̸=γ^μa_μ as in Lesson 20. The Clifford algebra gives directly

$$ γ^μ a̸ = γ^μγ^νa_ν = (2g^μν−γ^νγ^μ)a_ν = 2a^μ − a̸γ^μ

$$ γ_μ a̸ γ^μ = −2a̸

The second identity, derived in Exercise 1, is used constantly to simplify long chains of gamma matrices with a contracted index somewhere in the middle.

### Worked example 1

Verify γ_μγ^νγ^μ = −2γ^ν, the case a^μ=δ^μ_ν (informally, "a=e_ν") of the identity in Section 1.2. From γ^μa̸=2a^μ−a̸γ^μ with a̸=γ^ν, γ_μγ^νγ^μ is obtained by contracting the free index with g_μρ and setting a=e_ν; direct substitution into the general slash identity, γ_μa̸γ^μ=−2a̸, with a̸=γ^ν, gives γ_μγ^νγ^μ=−2γ^ν immediately.

# 2 Traces of gamma matrix products

## 2.1 The trace of the identity

By convention (matching the 4×4 Dirac representation of Lesson 20, though the result is representation-independent), Tr[I]=4.

## 2.2 The trace of one or three gamma matrices vanishes

For any odd number of gamma matrices, the trace vanishes. The proof uses γ⁵ (introduced fully in Section 4) and is deferred to Exercise 12; a direct argument for a single gamma matrix uses only the Clifford algebra: γ^μ = γ^μ(γ⁵)² (since γ⁵²=I, shown in Section 4.2) = −γ⁵γ^μγ⁵ (using {γ^μ,γ⁵}=0, also shown in Section 4.2), so Tr[γ^μ] = −Tr[γ⁵γ^μγ⁵] = −Tr[γ⁵γ⁵γ^μ] (cyclicity of trace) = −Tr[γ^μ], forcing Tr[γ^μ]=0. The same trick, inserting (γ⁵)² and using cyclicity plus anticommutation, extends to any odd number of gamma matrices.

## 2.3 The trace of two gamma matrices

$$ Tr[γ^μγ^ν] = (1/2)Tr[{γ^μ,γ^ν}] = (1/2)Tr[2g^μνI] = 4g^μν

using that Tr[γ^μγ^ν+γ^νγ^μ] = Tr[γ^μγ^ν]+Tr[γ^νγ^μ] = 2Tr[γ^μγ^ν] by cyclicity of the trace, so Tr[γ^μγ^ν] is automatically the symmetric part of the product's trace, directly proportional to the Clifford algebra's right-hand side.

### Worked example 2

Compute Tr[a̸b̸] for two four-vectors a, b. Tr[a̸b̸] = a_μb_νTr[γ^μγ^ν] = a_μb_ν(4g^μν) = 4a·b, using Section 2.3 and the definition of the scalar product from Lesson 4. This single identity, Tr[a̸b̸]=4a·b, is used repeatedly in cross-section calculations from Lesson 54 onward, wherever a trace of two slashed momenta appears.

## 2.4 The trace of four gamma matrices

Using the Clifford algebra repeatedly to move the last γ^σ past the preceding three, then cyclicity of the trace, gives (the derivation is carried out step by step in Exercise 6)

$$ Tr[γ^μγ^νγ^ργ^σ] = 4(g^μνg^ρσ − g^μρg^νσ + g^μσg^νρ)

Every trace of four gamma matrices reduces to this pattern of three terms, alternating in sign, pairing up the four indices in every possible way (three ways to pair four objects into two pairs). This identity, together with Worked example 2's two-index version, is the computational core of nearly every unpolarized cross-section calculation in Part VIII.

### Worked example 3

Compute Tr[a̸b̸c̸d̸] for four four-vectors. Contracting each gamma matrix index in Section 2.4 with the corresponding vector, Tr[a̸b̸c̸d̸] = 4[(a·b)(c·d) − (a·c)(b·d) + (a·d)(b·c)], a direct and frequently used consequence of the trace identity, appearing for instance in the electron-muon scattering calculation of Lesson 54.

# 3 The Levi Civita symbol and trace identities with γ⁵

## 3.1 Preview

Traces involving γ⁵ typically produce the Levi Civita symbol ε^μνρσ of Lesson 4, rather than combinations of the metric alone. The general four-gamma trace with γ⁵ inserted is

$$ Tr[γ⁵γ^μγ^νγ^ργ^σ] = −4i ε^μνρσ

stated here for reference; its derivation requires an explicit representation or a more involved algebraic argument than this course pursues in full, but its consequence, that any trace containing γ⁵ and fewer than four other gamma matrices vanishes (there being no way to build a nonzero Lorentz-invariant, γ⁵-odd combination from fewer than four independent vector indices), is used directly in Exercise 13.

### Worked example 4

State why Tr[γ⁵γ^μ]=0 and Tr[γ⁵γ^μγ^ν]=0 without computing either explicitly. A trace with γ⁵ and one free vector index μ would have to be proportional to some vector built from nothing but the metric and ε (since no external vectors are given), but no such single-index object with the correct behavior under parity (γ⁵ is a parity-odd combination, foreshadowing Lesson 22) can be built from ε^μνρσ alone with only one free index; the same absence of enough indices to saturate the four slots of ε^μνρσ rules out the two-index case as well. Both vanish by this index-counting argument, consistent with the general pattern that a nonzero γ⁵ trace needs at least four gamma matrices to supply enough indices for ε.

# 4 The chirality matrix γ⁵

## 4.1 Definition

$$ γ⁵ = i γ⁰γ¹γ²γ³

The factor of i is a convention chosen to make γ⁵ Hermitian, verified in Exercise 8, given that γ⁰ is Hermitian while γ¹,γ²,γ³ are anti-Hermitian in the standard convention (Exercise 7), so their product picks up a compensating phase.

## 4.2 Key properties

$$ (γ⁵)² = I,    {γ⁵, γ^μ} = 0  for every μ

The first follows from repeatedly anticommuting the four distinct gamma matrices past each other to bring γ⁰γ¹γ²γ³γ⁰γ¹γ²γ³ into a form where each γ^μ meets its own square (Exercise 9 works through the sign-counting explicitly); the second follows because γ⁵ contains each of the four gamma matrices exactly once, so moving any single γ^μ through the product γ⁰γ¹γ²γ³ requires exactly three transpositions past the other three distinct gamma matrices (each contributing a factor of −1, since distinct gamma matrices anticommute), giving an overall sign of (−1)³=−1, hence γ^μγ⁵=−γ⁵γ^μ.

### Worked example 5

Verify {γ⁵,γ⁰}=0 by direct sign counting. γ⁰γ⁵ = γ⁰(iγ⁰γ¹γ²γ³) = i(γ⁰γ⁰)γ¹γ²γ³ = i(γ⁰)²γ¹γ²γ³ = iγ¹γ²γ³ (using (γ⁰)²=I). Meanwhile γ⁵γ⁰ = iγ⁰γ¹γ²γ³γ⁰; moving γ⁰ from the end to the front past γ³,γ²,γ¹ (three anticommutations, since γ⁰ is distinct from each) gives γ⁵γ⁰ = i(−1)³γ⁰γ⁰γ¹γ²γ³ = −iγ¹γ²γ³ (using (γ⁰)²=I again). Comparing, γ⁰γ⁵=iγ¹γ²γ³ and γ⁵γ⁰=−iγ¹γ²γ³, so γ⁰γ⁵+γ⁵γ⁰=0, confirming the anticommutation.

## 4.3 Chiral projectors

Since (γ⁵)²=I, the eigenvalues of γ⁵ are ±1, as for any operator squaring to the identity (Lesson 11, Exercise 13, applied to γ⁵ instead of parity). Define

$$ P_L = (1−γ⁵)/2,    P_R = (1+γ⁵)/2

Direct computation using (γ⁵)²=I confirms P_L²=P_L, P_R²=P_R (each is a projector, in the sense of Lesson 1, Section 6.4), P_LP_R=P_RP_L=0 (they project onto orthogonal subspaces), and P_L+P_R=I (they resolve the identity). A Dirac spinor ψ splits as ψ=P_Lψ+P_Rψ = ψ_L+ψ_R, where ψ_L and ψ_R are the left- and right-handed (chiral) components, each a genuine two-component object once expressed in the Weyl representation mentioned in Lesson 20, Section 2.1, and used again in Lesson 23.

### Worked example 6

Verify P_L²=P_L. P_L² = [(1−γ⁵)/2]² = (1−2γ⁵+(γ⁵)²)/4 = (1−2γ⁵+1)/4 = (2−2γ⁵)/4 = (1−γ⁵)/2 = P_L, using (γ⁵)²=I from Section 4.2.

# 5 A worked trace calculation

## 5.1 Setup

To illustrate the identities of this lesson in combination, consider a trace of the form needed in Lesson 54's electron-muon scattering amplitude, Tr[(p̸₁+m)γ^μ(p̸₂+m)γ^ν], with m the particle's mass and p₁,p₂ two of its four-momenta.

## 5.2 Expansion

Expand the product using linearity of the trace.

$$ Tr[(p̸₁+m)γ^μ(p̸₂+m)γ^ν] = Tr[p̸₁γ^μp̸₂γ^ν] + m Tr[γ^μp̸₂γ^ν] + m Tr[p̸₁γ^μγ^ν] + m² Tr[γ^μγ^ν]

By Section 2.2, any trace of an odd number of gamma matrices vanishes, eliminating the two middle terms (each a trace of three gamma matrices). The first term is a four-gamma trace of the type in Worked example 3, and the last uses Worked example 2 directly.

$$ = 4[p₁^μp₂^ν − (p₁·p₂)g^μν + p₁^νp₂^μ] + 4m²g^μν

$$ = 4[p₁^μp₂^ν + p₁^νp₂^μ − g^μν(p₁·p₂ − m²)]

### Worked example 7

Verify the sign and grouping in Section 5.2's final line by matching each term of Worked example 3's formula, Tr[a̸b̸c̸d̸]=4[(a·b)(c·d)−(a·c)(b·d)+(a·d)(b·c)], to a=p₁, b=γ^μ's vector index, c=p₂, d=γ^ν's vector index treated formally. With the indices tracked as p₁^μp₂^ν (from the a·d, b·c term pairing μ directly with p₁ and ν with p₂) minus g^μν(p₁·p₂) (from the a·c term) plus p₁^νp₂^μ (from the remaining pairing), the result matches Section 5.2 exactly once the m²g^μν term from Worked example 2 is added back in.

# 7 Summary

1. γ_μγ^μ=4 and γ_μa̸γ^μ=−2a̸ follow from the Clifford algebra alone, independent of any explicit representation.

2. The trace of an odd number of gamma matrices vanishes; this follows from inserting (γ⁵)²=I and using anticommutation with γ⁵.

3. Tr[γ^μγ^ν]=4g^μν and Tr[a̸b̸]=4a·b are the simplest nontrivial trace identities.

4. Tr[γ^μγ^νγ^ργ^σ]=4(g^μνg^ρσ−g^μρg^νσ+g^μσg^νρ) is the four-gamma trace identity used throughout cross-section calculations.

5. Traces involving γ⁵ generically produce the Levi Civita symbol and vanish unless at least four other gamma matrices are present.

6. γ⁵=iγ⁰γ¹γ²γ³ is Hermitian, squares to the identity, and anticommutes with every γ^μ.

7. The chiral projectors P_L=(1−γ⁵)/2 and P_R=(1+γ⁵)/2 split a Dirac spinor into left- and right-handed components.

8. Combining these identities allows systematic evaluation of traces such as Tr[(p̸₁+m)γ^μ(p̸₂+m)γ^ν], the standard building block of QED cross sections.

# Solutions

*Attempt each exercise before reading its solution. If you are stuck, read only the first sentence of the solution, then try again.*

**1.** From γ^μa̸=2a^μ−a̸γ^μ, multiply both sides on the left by γ_μ: γ_μγ^μa̸ = 2γ_μa^μ−γ_μa̸γ^μ. Using γ_μγ^μ=4 (Section 1.1) and γ_μa^μ=a̸, the left side is 4a̸, giving 4a̸=2a̸−γ_μa̸γ^μ, so γ_μa̸γ^μ=2a̸−4a̸=−2a̸.

**2.** Write γ_μa̸b̸γ^μ = a_ρb_σγ_μγ^ργ^σγ^μ. Move γ_μ past γ^ρ using the Clifford algebra, γ_μγ^ρ=2δ_μ^ρI−γ^ργ_μ: γ_μγ^ργ^σγ^μ = 2δ_μ^ργ^σγ^μ − γ^ργ_μγ^σγ^μ = 2γ^σγ^ρ − γ^ρ(−2γ^σ) = 2γ^σγ^ρ+2γ^ργ^σ = 2{γ^ρ,γ^σ} = 4g^ρσI, using γ_μγ^σγ^μ=−2γ^σ from Section 1.2 in the middle step. Contracting with a_ρb_σ gives γ_μa̸b̸γ^μ = 4a_ρb_σg^ρσ = 4a·b.

**3.** Setting b=a in Tr[a̸b̸]=4a·b gives Tr[a̸a̸]=4a·a=4a².

**4.** Tr[γ^μγ^νγ^ρ]=0, an immediate application of the general rule that any trace of an odd number of gamma matrices vanishes (Section 2.2), with no further computation needed.

**5.** For μ=ν=0: Tr[γ⁰γ⁰]=4g⁰⁰=4(1)=4=Tr[I], and directly Tr[(γ⁰)²]=Tr[I]=4 since (γ⁰)²=I, consistent. For μ=ν=1: Tr[γ¹γ¹]=4g¹¹=4(−1)=−4, and directly Tr[(γ¹)²]=Tr[−I]=−4 since (γ¹)²=−I, also consistent.

**6.** γ^μγ^νγ^ργ^σ: move γ^σ past γ^ρ using γ^ργ^σ=2g^ρσ−γ^σγ^ρ, giving γ^μγ^ν(2g^ρσ−γ^σγ^ρ) = 2g^ρσγ^μγ^ν − γ^μγ^νγ^σγ^ρ. Repeating on the second term, move γ^σ past γ^ν: γ^νγ^σ=2g^νσ−γ^σγ^ν, giving −γ^μ(2g^νσ−γ^σγ^ν)γ^ρ = −2g^νσγ^μγ^ρ+γ^μγ^σγ^νγ^ρ. Move γ^σ past γ^μ similarly: γ^μγ^σ=2g^μσ−γ^σγ^μ, giving 2g^μσγ^νγ^ρ−γ^σγ^μγ^νγ^ρ. Collecting, γ^μγ^νγ^ργ^σ = 2g^ρσγ^μγ^ν−2g^νσγ^μγ^ρ+2g^μσγ^νγ^ρ−γ^σγ^μγ^νγ^ρ. Taking the trace and using cyclicity, Tr[γ^σγ^μγ^νγ^ρ]=Tr[γ^μγ^νγ^ργ^σ], so this last term moves to the left side: 2Tr[γ^μγ^νγ^ργ^σ] = 2g^ρσTr[γ^μγ^ν]−2g^νσTr[γ^μγ^ρ]+2g^μσTr[γ^νγ^ρ] = 8(g^ρσg^μν−g^νσg^μρ+g^μσg^νρ), using Tr[γ^μγ^ν]=4g^μν throughout. Dividing by 2 gives Tr[γ^μγ^νγ^ργ^σ]=4(g^μνg^ρσ−g^μρg^νσ+g^μσg^νρ), matching Section 2.4.

**7.** In the Dirac representation of Lesson 20, γ⁰=β=[[I,0],[0,−I]], manifestly Hermitian (real, diagonal, symmetric). γⁱ=βαⁱ=[[0,σᵢ],[σᵢ,0]] (using β's diagonal form and αᵢ's off-diagonal σᵢ blocks; direct computation gives [[0,σᵢ],[−σᵢ,0]]); its Hermitian conjugate swaps the off-diagonal blocks and conjugate-transposes each Pauli matrix (which are individually Hermitian), giving (γⁱ)†=[[0,−σᵢ],[σᵢ,0]]=−γⁱ, confirming anti-Hermiticity.

**8.** (γ⁵)† = (iγ⁰γ¹γ²γ³)† = −i(γ³)†(γ²)†(γ¹)†(γ⁰)† (using (ABC D)†=D†C†B†A† and complex conjugation of i) = −i(−γ³)(−γ²)(−γ¹)(γ⁰) = −i(−1)³γ³γ²γ¹γ⁰ = iγ³γ²γ¹γ⁰. Reordering γ³γ²γ¹γ⁰ back to γ⁰γ¹γ²γ³ requires moving γ⁰ from the last position to the first past three distinct gamma matrices (three anticommutations, sign (−1)³=−1), then γ¹ similarly, etc.; carrying this through (or citing that reversing the order of n distinct anticommuting factors gives a sign (−1)^(n(n−1)/2) = (−1)^6=+1 for n=4) confirms iγ³γ²γ¹γ⁰=iγ⁰γ¹γ²γ³=γ⁵, so (γ⁵)†=γ⁵: Hermitian.

**9.** (iγ⁰γ¹γ²γ³)(iγ⁰γ¹γ²γ³) = −γ⁰γ¹γ²γ³γ⁰γ¹γ²γ³. To sort this into (γ⁰)²(γ¹)²(γ²)²(γ³)², move the second γ⁰ leftward past γ³,γ²,γ¹ (3 transpositions), then the second γ¹ (now second from left after that move) past γ³,γ² (2 transpositions), then the second γ² past γ³ (1 transposition); the second γ³ is already in place (0 transpositions). The total sign is (−1)^(3+2+1+0) = (−1)⁶ = +1, so the product becomes (γ⁰)²(γ¹)²(γ²)²(γ³)² = (I)(−I)(−I)(−I) = −I. Hence (γ⁵)² = −(−I) = I.

**10.** P_R² = [(1+γ⁵)/2]² = (1+2γ⁵+(γ⁵)²)/4 = (1+2γ⁵+1)/4 = (1+γ⁵)/2 = P_R. P_LP_R = [(1−γ⁵)/2][(1+γ⁵)/2] = (1−(γ⁵)²)/4 = (1−1)/4 = 0.

**11.** Tr[γ⁵] must be a number built from the algebra alone, with no free indices and no external vectors to contract against; it must therefore be a pure (dimensionless, representation-independent) constant. Since γ⁵ is a product of four distinct, mutually anticommuting matrices, and no combination of the metric tensor alone (the only invariant tensor with no free indices available, since ε^μνρσ needs four free indices to be nonvanishing) can produce a nonzero scalar from this structure, the only self-consistent value is Tr[γ⁵]=0, confirmed by any explicit representation as well.

**12.** Tr[γ^μγ^νγ^ρ] = Tr[γ^μγ^νγ^ρ(γ⁵)²] = Tr[γ^μγ^νγ^ρ(γ⁵)²]. Move one γ⁵ to the front: (γ⁵)² commutes trivially with itself, but to use anticommutation, write γ^μγ^νγ^ρ γ⁵ γ⁵ and move the first γ⁵ leftward past γ^ρ, γ^ν, γ^μ (three anticommutations, sign (−1)³=−1): = −γ⁵γ^μγ^νγ^ργ⁵. Taking the trace and using cyclicity, Tr[γ^μγ^νγ^ρ] = −Tr[γ⁵γ^μγ^νγ^ργ⁵] = −Tr[γ⁵γ⁵γ^μγ^νγ^ρ] (cyclicity) = −Tr[γ^μγ^νγ^ρ] (using (γ⁵)²=I), forcing Tr[γ^μγ^νγ^ρ]=0.

**13.** Both are direct applications of the stated rule: Tr[γ⁵γ^μγ^νγ^ρ] has γ⁵ plus only three other gamma matrices, fewer than the four required for a nonzero ε^μνρσ-type result, so it vanishes; Tr[γ⁵] has γ⁵ plus zero other gamma matrices, even further below the threshold, so it vanishes as well, consistent with the independent argument of Exercise 11.

**14.** Tr[(p̸+m)(p̸+m)] = Tr[p̸p̸] + 2mTr[p̸] + m²Tr[I] = 4p² + 0 + 4m² = 4(p²+m²), using Tr[p̸p̸]=4p² (Exercise 3), Tr[p̸]=0 (odd trace, Section 2.2), and Tr[I]=4. On-shell, p²=m², giving 4(m²+m²)=8m².

**15.** ψ_L+ψ_R = P_Lψ+P_Rψ = (P_L+P_R)ψ = Iψ = ψ, using P_L+P_R=I. And P_Lψ_R = P_L(P_Rψ) = (P_LP_R)ψ = 0·ψ = 0, using P_LP_R=0 from Section 4.3/Exercise 10.

**16.** Write a̸=a_σγ^σ and use Tr[γ^μγ^νγ^ργ^σ]=4(g^μνg^ρσ−g^μρg^νσ+g^μσg^νρ) from Section 2.4, contracting the σ index with a_σ: Tr[γ^μγ^νγ^ρa̸] = a_σTr[γ^μγ^νγ^ργ^σ] = 4a_σ(g^μνg^ρσ−g^μρg^νσ+g^μσg^νρ) = 4(g^μνa^ρ−g^μρa^ν+g^νρa^μ), using g^ρσa_σ=a^ρ and similarly for the other terms; this trace, containing four gamma-type factors in total (three explicit plus one slashed vector), is even, so the odd-trace vanishing rule of Section 2.2 does not apply, and the nonzero result above is the correct evaluation.

# Mastery checklist

- I can derive γ_μγ^μ=4 and γ_μa̸γ^μ=−2a̸ from the Clifford algebra without consulting a reference.

- I can state and use Tr[γ^μγ^ν]=4g^μν and Tr[a̸b̸]=4a·b.

- I can derive the four-gamma trace identity by repeated application of the Clifford algebra and cyclicity of the trace.

- I can state, and give the reasoning behind, why any trace of an odd number of gamma matrices vanishes.

- I can define γ⁵, verify its Hermiticity and (γ⁵)²=I, and show it anticommutes with every γ^μ.

- I can construct the chiral projectors P_L, P_R and verify they are orthogonal projectors summing to the identity.

- I can state when a trace involving γ⁵ vanishes by an index-counting argument, without computing it explicitly.

- I can evaluate a trace of the form Tr[(p̸₁+m)γ^μ(p̸₂+m)γ^ν] using the identities of this lesson in combination.
