SUB: How They Work, How to Choose Them, and How to Measure Right: From Power Supplies and Switch Boxes to Oscilloscopes, Spectrum and Network Analyzers, and the Analysis of Every Kind of Modulation
H1: How to Use This Book
P: Every number an engineer trusts came out of an instrument, and every instrument changes the thing it measures a little, adds a little of its own noise, and reports the result with a little uncertainty. This book is about those instruments: what is inside them, which specifications matter and what they mean, how to choose one, and how to use it so the number on the screen is the number in the circuit. It runs from the bench power supply and the switch box to the network analyzer and the vector signal analyzer that grades a Wi-Fi 7 transmitter.
P: Part I, Chapters 1 to 4, sets the ground rules: accuracy, resolution and precision, uncertainty budgets and calibration, grounding and loading, and safety. Part II, Chapters 5 to 11, covers the instruments that source, load, characterize, and route: power supplies, source-measure units, the curve tracer at work on diodes and transistors, the measurement of every datasheet parameter and the fitting of SPICE models, electronic loads, switch matrices, and the buses that tie a rack together. Part III, Chapters 12 to 15, covers the basic measurements: multimeters, LCR meters, low-level instruments, and counters. Part IV, Chapters 16 to 18, is the time domain: the oscilloscope, its probes, its triggers, logic analyzers, and TDR. Part V, Chapters 19 and 20, makes signals, from function generators to vector signal generators. Part VI, Chapters 21 to 24, is the frequency domain: spectrum analyzers, power meters, and noise-figure and phase-noise analyzers. Part VII, Chapters 25 to 27, is network analysis. Part VIII, Chapters 28 to 32, analyzes modulation of every kind, from AM to 5G NR and LoRa chirps. Part IX, Chapter 33, covers optical, thermal, and EMC precompliance instruments. Part X, Chapters 34 to 36, automates the bench: SCPI, VISA, Python, and C#; a full chapter on LabVIEW and TestStand; and production test and data. Part XI, Chapters 37 and 38, builds a home lab, from a NanoVNA and a tinySA to instruments you make yourself.
P: Each chapter opens with a question from real work, says where you meet the instrument, explains how it works before it lists specifications, and then works real measurements through with numbers. From the Bench notes give the practical habits; On the Job notes describe how the instrument is used in semiconductor characterization labs, RF test, production lines, and EMC labs. Every chapter ends with practice problems graded Core, Extension, and Challenge, whose worked solutions in Appendix B also name the usual mistake, and with a short list of what you will actually use. Appendix A collects the formulas, a specification cheat-sheet for each instrument, and the SCPI basics.
P: The series gives each subject one home, and the theory this book measures lives in Books 1 to 8: uncertainty and decibels in Book 1, loading and noise in Book 2, devices in Book 3, S-parameters, receiver noise, phase noise, and amplifier measurement in Book 4, modulation and OFDM in Book 5, regulators and batteries in Book 6, signal integrity and EMC in Book 7, and bench software in Book 8. This book points to those chapters and does not derive their results again. Its own subject is the instrument: how it works, how to choose it, and how to measure right with it.
P: Instruments are named as examples, from Keysight, Rohde & Schwarz, Tektronix, Keithley, National Instruments, Rigol, and Siglent to the open hobby tools such as the NanoVNA and the tinySA. No brand is recommended over another, and none paid to appear. Specifications quoted for a named product were checked against its current datasheet or product page (Appendix C lists them); everything else is labeled typical or example. Prices are typical 2026 street prices and change often.
P: The Python listings control instruments through PyVISA and SCPI, and each one has a simulation mode, so it runs and was checked without hardware; the printed results are the numbers in the text. The C# listing was built with the .NET SDK. Command spellings differ between vendors, so the programming manual of your instrument is the final reference. The figures were computed from the same models and numbers as the worked examples, and a check script recomputes every worked-example number.
P: Safety comes first in a measurement book. Mains, battery packs, charged capacitors, and RF power amplifiers can injure or kill. Chapter 4 explains measurement categories and isolation; follow it, use instruments and probes rated for the job, and never defeat a protective earth to float an oscilloscope.
