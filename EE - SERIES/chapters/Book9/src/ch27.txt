H1: Chapter 27. Scalar Analysis, Amplifier Test, and Load Pull
P: The power amplifier makes its 10 W into the 50 Ω test fixture and 6 W in the phone. The difference is not in the transistor; it is in the load: the phone's antenna and matching present an impedance the amplifier was never tuned for. Load pull is how designers find what load a transistor wants, and amplifier test is how they prove what it does there.
MEET: Power and low-noise amplifier development, production test of RF front-end modules, transmitter linearity, satellite and radar amplifiers, and device modeling for PA design.
H2: 27.1 Scalar analysis
P: A scalar network analyzer measures only magnitudes, with broadband diode detectors and a swept source, or with a spectrum analyzer and its tracking generator. It is fast and cheap for filters, attenuators, and cable loss, and adequate wherever phase is not needed; its weaknesses are the lack of vector error correction (mismatch errors remain) and, for detectors, the response to harmonics and spurs. A spectrum analyzer's tracking generator turns it into a scalar analyzer with a high dynamic range and selective measurement.
H2: 27.2 Gain, compression, and intermodulation
P: An amplifier's gain is S21 at small signal; its output 1 dB compression point (P1dB) is the output power at which the gain has fallen by 1 dB, found with a power sweep at fixed frequency on a VNA (with a calibrated source power and receiver power) or with a generator and power meter. Third-order intermodulation is measured with two equal tones: the third-order products at 2f1 − f2 and 2f2 − f1 grow 3 dB per dB of input, and the output third-order intercept is OIP3 = Pout(per tone) + ΔIM3/2, where ΔIM3 is how far below each tone the products sit. Check that the generators do not intermodulate with each other (isolators or a hybrid combiner) and that the analyzer is not the source (Chapter 21).
WE: Worked Example 27.1: OIP3 and PAE
PROB: With two tones of 0 dBm each at the output, the third-order products are 50 dB below the tones. Find OIP3. At its rated output, the amplifier delivers 40 dBm from 25 dBm of drive on a 28 V, 0.8 A supply. Find the power-added efficiency (PAE).
SOL: OIP3 = 0 + 50/2 = +25 dBm. Check at another level: at −10 dBm per tone, the products should be 70 dB down; if they are not, the measurement is not in the third-order regime or is limited by the analyzer. PAE = (Pout − Pin)/Pdc = (10 W − 0.316 W)/(28 V × 0.8 A) = 9.68/22.4 = 43.2 %.

H2: 27.3 Active device test: pulsed, hot, and modulated
P: Power transistors are often tested with pulsed RF and pulsed bias (to control temperature, as in Chapter 7), with "hot" S22 measured while the amplifier is driven, and with real modulated signals for ACLR and EVM (Chapters 29 and 30). The test system then combines a VSG, a VSA, power meters, couplers, and a bias supply with current measurement, and the efficiency is measured from the DC supply's voltage and current at the same time as the RF powers.
H2: 27.4 Load pull
P: A load-pull system presents controlled impedances to the device's output (and source pull to its input) and measures its output power, efficiency, gain, and linearity at each impedance, producing contours on the Smith chart from which the designer chooses the matching network's target. Passive load pull uses mechanical tuners (slugs that move along a slab line), limited in the reflection magnitude they can reach at the device by the loss between tuner and device; active load pull injects a signal into the output to synthesize any reflection, including |Γ| near 1, at the cost of an extra amplifier; hybrid systems combine both. Harmonic load pull controls the impedances at 2f and 3f as well, for high-efficiency classes. Systems from Maury Microwave and Focus Microwaves are common.
WE: Worked Example 27.2: Reaching a low impedance
PROB: A GaN transistor wants an optimum load of 12 + j8 Ω at 2 GHz. What reflection must the tuner present at the device plane, and what VSWR is that? If 0.5 dB of loss lies between tuner and device, what reflection must the tuner itself make?
SOL: Γ = (12 + j8 − 50)/(12 + j8 + 50) = 0.621 at 160.8°, a VSWR of 4.28. The reflection passes the loss twice, 1 dB: the tuner must make |Γ| = 0.621 × 10^(1/20) = 0.697. Higher-power devices want lower impedances (a few ohms, |Γ| above 0.9), which passive tuners cannot reach through fixture loss; that is the case for prematching or active load pull.

JOB: An RF power-amplifier engineer at a base-station or handset front-end company spends the week in the load-pull lab: characterizing a new GaN or GaAs transistor at several frequencies with harmonic load pull, extracting the optimum loads for efficiency and linearity, designing the matching network, and verifying the finished amplifier with modulated signals and digital predistortion. A typical issue: the efficiency contours shift by 10 % between two measurement days, traced to a heatsink whose thermal paste had dried, raising the device temperature. Tools: Maury or Focus load-pull systems with Keysight or R&S VNAs and signal analyzers, pulsed DC supplies, and thermal monitoring.
MIS: P1dB is the maximum output power. Most amplifiers can deliver more power above P1dB, at worse linearity; the saturated output power (Psat) and the power at a stated ACLR or EVM are the numbers for transmit design.
BENCH: Protect the analyzer and the device: compute the output power at the highest drive before connecting anything, put attenuators and couplers rated for that power in the path, and never switch the RF drive on before the bias, or remove the bias with RF applied. Many GaN parts also need the gate bias applied before the drain voltage.
H2: Practice
PP: 1. (Core) Tones of +5 dBm each at the output and IM3 products at −55 dBc. OIP3?
PP: 2. (Core) An amplifier's gain at small signal is 22.0 dB; at what gain is it at P1dB?
PP: 3. (Core) Why do two generators for a two-tone test need isolation between them?
PP: 4. (Extension) Pout 33 dBm, Pin 20 dBm, 12 V at 0.5 A: PAE and drain efficiency?
PP: 5. (Extension) What |Γ| corresponds to a 5 Ω load in a 50 Ω system, and what tuner reflection is needed behind 0.3 dB of loss?
PP: 6. (Challenge) Plan the P1dB measurement of a 5 W, 2.4 GHz amplifier with a VNA whose source reaches +10 dBm and whose receivers compress at +10 dBm. List the equipment, the calibration, and the protections.
ANS: 1. OIP3 = 5 + 55/2 = 32.5 dBm. The usual mistake is to add the full ΔIM3.
ANS: 2. 21.0 dB. The usual mistake is to take P1dB at the point where the output is 1 dB below the input plus 22 dB at some arbitrary drive, rather than where the gain has dropped by 1 dB.
ANS: 3. Without isolation, each generator's output enters the other's output stage and mixes there, producing intermodulation products in the generators that look like the amplifier's. Isolators, a hybrid combiner with good port isolation, or attenuators fix it. The usual mistake is to combine two generators with a simple tee.
ANS: 4. Pout = 2.0 W, Pin = 0.1 W, Pdc = 6 W: PAE = (2.0 − 0.1)/6 = 31.7 %; drain efficiency = 2.0/6 = 33.3 %. The usual mistake is to report drain efficiency as PAE.
ANS: 5. Γ = (5 − 50)/(5 + 50) = −0.818; behind 0.3 dB (0.6 dB round trip) the tuner must make 0.818 × 10^(0.6/20) = 0.877. The usual mistake is to count the loss once.
ANS: 6. A driver amplifier after the VNA's port 1 to reach the needed drive (about +20 dBm for a 5 W amplifier with 17 dB gain), a coupler at the driver output feeding the VNA's reference receiver (or a calibrated source power), a 30 dB high-power attenuator at the amplifier output before port 2 (so the receiver sees below 0 dBm), a source power calibration with a power meter at the amplifier input plane and a receiver calibration at the output plane through the attenuator. Power sweep at 2.4 GHz from small signal to beyond P1dB; read the gain against output power. Protections: compute worst-case powers, use an attenuator rated for 5 W or more, pulsed RF if the amplifier overheats, bias sequencing. The usual mistake is to drive the VNA receiver directly with the amplifier's output.
