PART: Part VIII. Modulation and Signal Analysis
H1: Chapter 28. Vector Signal Analysis and Analog Modulation
P: The FM transmitter passes its power test and fails in the field: listeners hear distortion on loud passages. The spectrum looks normal, a hump of sidebands around the carrier. A vector signal analyzer demodulates it and plots frequency against time: the deviation flattens at 68 kHz on every loud peak, well short of the 75 kHz the station may use: the audio limiter was set for the wrong pre-emphasis and clips. A spectrum shows power; demodulation shows the message.
P: Where you meet this: Radio and transmitter service, broadcast compliance, two-way radio alignment, signal-generator verification, avionics and navigation (VOR, ILS), audio links, and the first step of any digital-modulation measurement.
H2: 28.1 From spectrum analyzer to vector signal analyzer
P: A modern signal analyzer downconverts a band to an intermediate frequency, digitizes it, and turns the samples into complex baseband I/Q: I(t) + jQ(t), the signal's amplitude and phase relative to a reference at the center frequency (Book 6 has the theory). Everything afterward is mathematics: an FFT gives the spectrum, the magnitude gives the envelope (AM), the phase gives PM, and its derivative gives the instantaneous frequency (FM). A VSA is that software, running inside the analyzer or on a PC that reads the I/Q (Keysight 89600 VSA, R&S VSE, Tektronix SignalVu, and the vendors' built-in "I/Q analyzer" and "analog demod" modes).
FIG: fig28_1.png | Figure 28.1 — The vector signal analyzer: the RF front end of a spectrum analyzer feeds an ADC; a digital downconverter makes I/Q at a sample rate set by the span; software computes the spectrum, demodulation, and modulation quality from the same record. | A block diagram from left to right: RF input, attenuator, mixer with local oscillator, IF filter, ADC, digital downconverter with numerically controlled oscillator producing I and Q, a capture memory, and three outputs labeled spectrum (FFT), analog demodulation (AM, FM, PM), and digital demodulation (constellation, EVM).
P: Three settings govern a capture. The span sets the sample rate (typically about 1.25 times the span, complex samples) and must contain the whole modulated signal. The record length T sets the frequency resolution (about 1/T) and the time the analysis covers. The trigger (free run, IF power, external, or periodic) decides which part of a bursty signal is captured. Gap-free capture into memory, followed by analysis, is what lets a VSA show the frequency hop or the AGC transient that a swept analyzer misses.
H2: 28.2 AM: depth from the envelope or the sidebands
P: Amplitude modulation by a tone at depth m produces a carrier and two sidebands, each at 20 log(m/2) dBc. A VSA reads m directly from the envelope, (max − min)/(max + min); a spectrum analyzer reads it from the sidebands. The envelope method also shows distortion (a flattened trough is overmodulation) and incidental phase modulation that the sideband method hides.
H2: Worked Example 28.1: AM depth from a spectrum
P: Problem. A spectrum analyzer shows the carrier of an AM signal with sidebands at −12.0 dBc at ±1 kHz. What is the modulation depth, and by how much does the modulation raise the total power?
P: Solution. m = 2 × 10^(−12.0/20) = 0.502, essentially 50 %. Each sideband carries (m/2)² = 0.0625 of the carrier power; together they add m²/2 = 0.125: total power 1.125 times the carrier, +0.51 dB. A thermal power sensor reads the 1.125; a peak sensor reads the envelope peaks at (1 + m)² = 2.25 times the carrier power, +3.5 dB.
H2: 28.3 FM and PM: deviation, Carson, and the Bessel null
P: Frequency modulation with peak deviation Δf by a tone fm has a modulation index β = Δf/fm and a spectrum of lines at multiples of fm whose amplitudes follow the Bessel functions Jn(β). About 98 % of the power lies within Carson's bandwidth, 2(Δf + fm). Phase modulation is the same spectrum with β set directly in radians. A VSA demodulates FM by differentiating the unwrapped phase; the post-detection filter (for example 300 Hz to 3 kHz for voice, 50 Hz to 15 kHz for broadcast) and de-emphasis must match the standard or the reading means nothing.
P: The Bessel null is the classic absolute check: the carrier line vanishes at β = 2.405 (then 5.520, 8.654). Set the audio frequency so that the null falls at the deviation you want, adjust until the carrier disappears on a narrow-RBW display, and the deviation is known to the accuracy of the audio frequency. It is how deviation meters were calibrated and is still the fastest check of a VSA's FM reading.

H2: Worked Example 28.2: Calibrating deviation with the null
P: Problem. You need to verify a two-way radio's 5.0 kHz deviation setting. What audio tone makes the carrier vanish at exactly 5.0 kHz deviation? Then, a broadcast FM transmitter runs 75 kHz deviation with 15 kHz audio: what span should the VSA use?
P: Solution. First null at β = 2.405: fm = 5.0/2.405 = 2.079 kHz. Feed 2.079 kHz, raise the audio level from zero, and stop at the first carrier null; the deviation is then 5.0 kHz. Carson's bandwidth for broadcast is 2(75 + 15) = 180 kHz; with the stereo pilot and RDS subcarriers the real occupied bandwidth is wider, so a 400 kHz span leaves margin and keeps the sample rate modest.
P: Listing 28.1 — Analog demodulation from an I/Q record: FM deviation from the phase derivative, AM depth from the envelope, and the carrier level at a Bessel null (Python with NumPy; simulation mode; the I/Q fetch command depends on the vendor).
P: """Analog demodulation from I/Q captures: FM deviation from the derivative of the phase, AM depth from theenvelope, and the Bessel-null check (carrier vanishes at beta = 2.405) for calibrating a deviation meter.The instrument part fetches I/Q from a signal analyzer's I/Q (basic) mode; command names differ by vendor(Keysight X-Series: CONF:WAV, READ:WAV0?; R&S FSx: TRAC:IQ ON, TRAC:IQ:DATA?). Check the programming guide.Run:  python l28_1_fm_deviation.py --sim"""import argparseimport mathimport numpy as npdef fm_iq(fm=1e3, dev=2.405e3, fs=1e6, n=200000, snr_db=60, am=0.0, seed=28):    t = np.arange(n) / fs    phase = dev / fm * np.sin(2 * np.pi * fm * t)                       # beta = dev/fm    env = 1 + am * np.cos(2 * np.pi * fm * t)    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)    noise = (rng.normal(0, 1, n) + 1j * rng.normal(0, 1, n)) * 10 ** (-snr_db / 20) / math.sqrt(2)    return env * np.exp(1j * phase) + noise, fsdef demod(z, fs):    """Return (peak deviation in Hz, AM depth) of an I/Q record; deviation from the instantaneous frequency."""    fi = np.diff(np.unwrap(np.angle(z))) * fs / (2 * np.pi)    fi -= fi.mean()                                                     # remove carrier offset    k = np.ones(25) / 25; fi = np.convolve(fi, k, mode='valid')         # post-detection low-pass (~40 kHz)    env = np.abs(z); m = (env.max() - env.min()) / (env.max() + env.min())    return math.sqrt(2) * fi.std(), m                                    # sine tone: peak = sqrt(2) x rmsdef carrier_dbc(z):    """Carrier level relative to total power, from an FFT of the record (Bessel null check)."""    s = np.abs(np.fft.fft(z * np.hanning(z.size))) ** 2    return 10 * math.log10(s[0] / s.sum() * 1.5 + 1e-30)                # Hann: main lobe spans 3 bins (sum 1.5)class SimVSA:    def __init__(self, dev): self.dev = dev    def query(self, cmd): return 'SIM,VSA,0,1.0'    def iq(self): return fm_iq(dev=self.dev)def main():    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser(); ap.add_argument('resource', nargs='?'); ap.add_argument('--sim', action='store_true')    a = ap.parse_args()    if not a.sim:        raise SystemExit('connect a real analyzer: replace SimVSA.iq() with the vendor I/Q fetch (see docstring)')    for dev in (1.0e3, 2.405e3, 5.0e3):        z, fs = SimVSA(dev).iq()        d, m = demod(z, fs)        print('set %.3f kHz: measured deviation %.3f kHz, AM depth %.4f, carrier %.1f dBc'              % (dev / 1e3, d / 1e3, m, carrier_dbc(z)))    print('Carson bandwidth for 75 kHz deviation, 15 kHz audio: %.0f kHz' % (2 * (75 + 15)))if __name__ == '__main__':    main()
P: The listing measures 0.999, 2.402, and 4.995 kHz for settings of 1.000, 2.405, and 5.000 kHz, and the carrier at −2.3 dBc, −80.8 dBc, and −15.0 dBc; J0(1)² is −2.3 dB and J0(5)² is −15.0 dB, so the three readings agree with the Bessel table.
H2: 28.4 Residual modulation and what limits it
P: A generator's residual FM (in a stated post-detection bandwidth) and residual AM are its phase noise and amplitude noise seen through a demodulator; Chapter 20 relates the numbers. When you measure a quiet source, the analyzer's own residual FM sets the floor: check it with a better source, or use a phase-noise analyzer (Chapter 24).
P: Common misconception. The analyzer's span only needs to cover the carrier. An FM signal with sidebands cut off by a narrow span demodulates with distortion and reads low deviation; the span must cover Carson's bandwidth or more.
BENCH: Before believing a deviation reading, put the generator you trust into the analyzer with a Bessel-null setting. Two minutes with a tone at 2.079 kHz has caught more than one wrongly configured de-emphasis and post-detection filter.
