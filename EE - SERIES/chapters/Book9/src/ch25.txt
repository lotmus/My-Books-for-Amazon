H1: Chapter 25. The Vector Network Analyzer
P: The antenna's return loss is −8 dB at the design frequency on the bench and −18 dB in the simulation. The engineer moves the cable, and the return loss changes by 3 dB. Before the antenna is wrong, the measurement plane is: the analyzer was calibrated at its own ports, and the 1 m cable, its adapter, and the PCB connector are all part of the "antenna." A VNA measures exactly what is between its calibration planes, and nothing tells it where you meant those planes to be.
P: Where you meet this: Filter, amplifier, and antenna design and production test, cable and connector qualification, PCB channel characterization, matching networks, material measurements, and every RF lab. For PCB test-coupon design and correlation of measured stack-up loss with signal-integrity models, see Book 7 Chapter 20.
H2: 25.1 How a VNA works
P: A VNA has a swept source, a switch that sends it to one port or another, directional couplers or bridges that separate the incident and reflected waves at each port, and receivers that measure those waves in amplitude and phase relative to the reference channel (a narrow IF filter, the IF bandwidth, sets the noise). The ratios give the S-parameters: S11 and S22 are reflections, S21 and S12 transmissions, each a complex number at every frequency. Two-port analyzers are the standard; four-port analyzers measure differential and mixed-mode parameters. Examples: Keysight's ENA and PNA, R&S's ZNA and ZNB, Anritsu's ShockLine and VectorStar, Copper Mountain's USB VNAs, and the NanoVNA at the hobby end (Chapter 37).
H2: 25.2 Reading the displays
P: The same S-parameter can be shown in several formats. Log magnitude (dB) for return loss and insertion loss; phase; group delay, −dφ/dω, for filters and cables; the Smith chart for reflection as impedance; and polar plots. Return loss RL = −20 log |Γ| (a positive number of dB by the strict definition, often shown as a negative S11); VSWR = (1 + |Γ|)/(1 − |Γ|). Markers read values and convert formats; marker search finds the −3 dB points of a filter.
H2: Worked Example 25.1: Return loss, VSWR, and impedance
P: Problem. An S11 of −20 dB and one of −10 dB: what are |Γ| and the VSWR? A load reads 25 + j25 Ω on the Smith chart; find Γ, its return loss, and VSWR.
P: Solution. −20 dB: |Γ| = 0.1, VSWR = 1.1/0.9 = 1.22. −10 dB: |Γ| = 0.316, VSWR = 1.92. For 25 + j25 Ω: Γ = (Z − 50)/(Z + 50) = (−25 + j25)/(75 + j25) = −0.20 + j0.40, |Γ| = 0.447 at 116.6°, a return loss of 7.0 dB and a VSWR of 2.62. A series capacitor of −j25 Ω would bring it to 25 Ω, and a quarter-wave transformer or an L-network the rest of the way (Book 4).

H2: Worked Example 25.2: Group delay of a filter
P: Problem. A band-pass filter's S21 phase falls by 36° between 1999.5 and 2000.5 MHz. What is its group delay? If the IF bandwidth is reduced from 10 kHz to 100 Hz, how do the trace noise and sweep time change?
P: Solution. Group delay = −Δφ/(360° × Δf) = 36°/(360° × 1 MHz) = 100 ns. Group delay is a derivative, so its trace is noisy; the aperture (the Δf over which it is computed) trades noise for resolution. Reducing the IF bandwidth by 100 lowers the noise floor by 20 dB (and the trace noise by a factor of 10) and makes each point about 100 times slower.
H2: 25.3 Specifications that matter
P: Frequency range; dynamic range (the ratio between the maximum power at the receiver and the noise floor at a stated IF bandwidth, often 120 to 140 dB at 10 Hz), which decides how deep a filter's stopband you can see; trace noise; output power range (for amplifiers and for compression measurements); raw directivity and source match (before calibration), and the corrected values after calibration (Chapter 26), which set the measurement uncertainty; and stability of the calibration with temperature and time.

P: Common misconception. Return loss is the same thing as S11 in dB. It is the negative of it: a good match has a high return loss (20 dB) and a low S11 (−20 dB). Mixing the two signs is the most common error in RF reports.
BENCH: Before measuring the device, measure the calibration: connect a load to each port and check the return loss (better than about 40 dB after a good calibration), connect a short and check it sits at the left edge of the Smith chart, and connect a through and check S21 is 0 dB ±0.05 dB with flat phase. Five minutes here saves a day of chasing the device.
