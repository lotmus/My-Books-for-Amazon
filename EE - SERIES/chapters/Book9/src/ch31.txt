H2: 31.1 OFDM in one page
P: Orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing sends many slow subcarriers side by side, each a QAM symbol, spaced at the inverse of the symbol time so they do not interfere (Book 6 has the theory). A cyclic prefix (CP) copied from the end of each symbol absorbs multipath delay. Pilots on known subcarriers let the receiver track phase and channel. The analyzer does what the receiver does: find the frame, remove the CP, FFT each symbol, estimate the channel from pilots or reference signals, equalize, and compare each subcarrier with its ideal QAM point.
P: The numbers to know: LTE uses 15 kHz subcarriers (66.7 µs symbols, normal CP 4.69 µs) and 180 kHz resource blocks (12 subcarriers), 100 blocks in 20 MHz. 5G NR scales the spacing by 2^µ: 15, 30, 60, 120 kHz and higher, with symbols and CPs shrinking by the same factor; a 100 MHz carrier at 30 kHz uses 273 blocks. Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) and Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be) use 78.125 kHz spacing (12.8 µs symbols, guard intervals 0.8, 1.6, or 3.2 µs), 1024-QAM and, for Wi-Fi 7, 4096-QAM, and channels up to 160 MHz and 320 MHz.
H2: Worked Example 31.1: Numerology
P: Problem. A 5G NR carrier is 100 MHz wide with 30 kHz subcarrier spacing. Find the symbol time, the normal CP, the occupied bandwidth with 273 resource blocks, and the guard on each side. How much path-length difference does the CP absorb?
P: Solution. Symbol time 1/30 kHz = 33.3 µs; normal CP 4.69 µs/2 = 2.34 µs (the 15 kHz value scaled by 2^µ = 2). Occupied 273 × 12 × 30 kHz = 98.28 MHz, leaving (100 − 98.28)/2 = 0.86 MHz on each side. A 2.34 µs CP absorbs a path difference of 2.34 µs × 3e8 m/s = 703 m, enough for most urban cells; the wider spacings at higher bands trade CP length for phase-noise tolerance.
H2: 31.2 What the analyzer reports
P: An OFDM demodulator reports composite EVM (rms over all data subcarriers and symbols, sometimes separately for pilots and data), EVM against subcarrier and against symbol, frequency error, symbol clock error, I/Q offset (LO leakage at the center subcarrier, which most standards exclude or limit separately), spectral flatness (the equalizer's channel estimate, which must stay within a mask), and common phase error. The standards set the EVM limits: for NR base stations (3GPP TS 38.104) 17.5 % for QPSK, 12.5 % for 16-QAM, 8 % for 64-QAM, and 3.5 % for 256-QAM; for Wi-Fi 6 MCS 11 (1024-QAM) −35 dB; for Wi-Fi 7 4096-QAM −38 dB. Use the standard's own demodulation settings (the equalizer training, the measurement interval, the exclusion of transient periods); a generic OFDM demodulator gives a different number.

H2: Worked Example 31.2: The Wi-Fi 6 EVM budget
P: Problem. A Wi-Fi 6 transmitter must meet −35 dB EVM for MCS 11. The analyzer's residual EVM is −45 dB and the transmitter's synthesizer has 0.5° rms phase noise. What EVM may everything else in the transmitter contribute?
P: Solution. −35 dB is 1.78 %; −45 dB is 0.56 %; 0.5° is 0.00873 rad, 0.87 %. The remainder: √(1.78² − 0.56² − 0.87²) = 1.44 %, −36.8 dB, shared by the PA's nonlinearity, I/Q imbalance, and noise. The analyzer takes a tenth of the error power and the phase noise a quarter; if the measurement reads −34.5 dB, check the analyzer's level and the synthesizer before redesigning the amplifier.
P: Listing 31.1 — Per-subcarrier EVM of a 64-QAM OFDM signal with 3276 subcarriers: noise after a band-limiting filter, a spur on one subcarrier, and phase noise with the common phase error removed by the pilots (Python with NumPy).
P: """Per-subcarrier EVM of an OFDM signal (64-QAM, 30 kHz spacing, 3276 used subcarriers as in a 100 MHz5G NR carrier) with three classic impairments: noise added after a band-limiting filter (edge subcarrierslose SNR), a spur on one subcarrier (-60 dBc of the whole carrier), and phase noise (common phase error removed by the pilots, the restspreads as inter-carrier interference).On an analyzer, the EVM-vs-subcarrier trace comes from the standard's demodulation personality; thislisting reproduces the shape so you can read one. Run:  python l31_1_ofdm_evm.py"""import mathimport numpy as npNFFT, USED, SCS = 4096, 3276, 30e3def qam64(n, rng):    lev = np.arange(-7, 8, 2)    return (rng.choice(lev, n) + 1j * rng.choice(lev, n)) / math.sqrt(42)def run(nsym=60, snr_db=40.0, edge_db=6.0, spur_dbc=-60.0, spur_sc=900, pn_rms_deg=0.3, seed=31):    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)    k = np.arange(-USED // 2, USED // 2)                                  # subcarrier index (DC included)    x = k / (USED / 2)    h = 10 ** (-edge_db * x ** 8 / 20)                                      # filter droop toward the band edges    err = np.zeros(USED); ref_p = 0.0    for _ in range(nsym):        S = qam64(USED, rng)        X = np.zeros(NFFT, complex); X[k % NFFT] = S * h        t = np.fft.ifft(X) * math.sqrt(NFFT)        pn = np.cumsum(rng.normal(0, 1, NFFT)); pn = pn - pn.mean()        pn *= math.radians(pn_rms_deg) / pn.std()        t = t * np.exp(1j * pn)        n = (rng.normal(0, 1, NFFT) + 1j * rng.normal(0, 1, NFFT)) * 10 ** (-snr_db / 20) / math.sqrt(2)        p_avg = USED / NFFT                                                     # average carrier power per sample        spur = math.sqrt(p_avg * 10 ** (spur_dbc / 10)) * np.exp(2j * np.pi * (spur_sc % NFFT) * np.arange(NFFT) / NFFT)        t = t + n + spur                                                    # spur_dbc relative to the whole carrier        Y = np.fft.fft(t) / math.sqrt(NFFT)        R = Y[k % NFFT] / h                                                 # equalizer: known channel        cpe = np.angle(np.vdot(S, R)); R = R * np.exp(-1j * cpe)            # pilots remove the common phase        err += np.abs(R - S) ** 2    evm_sc = np.sqrt(err / nsym)                                            # reference power is 1    total = math.sqrt(np.mean(err / nsym))    return k, 100 * evm_sc, 100 * totalif __name__ == '__main__':    k, e, tot = run()    mid = np.abs(k) < 400    print('composite EVM %.2f %% (%.1f dB)' % (tot, 20 * math.log10(tot / 100)))    print('center EVM %.2f %%, band-edge EVM %.2f %%, spur subcarrier %.1f %%'          % (np.sqrt(np.mean(e[mid] ** 2)), np.sqrt(np.mean(e[np.abs(k) > 1600] ** 2)), e[k == 900][0]))    print('NR limits (3GPP TS 38.104): 64-QAM 8 %, 256-QAM 3.5 %')
P: On the Job
P: A wireless test engineer at a module maker or a certification lab spends the week turning standards into test plans: setting up the analyzer's Wi-Fi 7 and NR personalities, writing the automated sequences (Chapter 36) that step through channels, bandwidths, and modulation orders, and chasing the failures. A typical issue: EVM failing only on channel 149 at 160 MHz, traced to a spur from the module's own DC-DC converter harmonic landing on a subcarrier; the fix was a frequency-plan change in the converter's switching frequency. Tools: Keysight, R&S, and LitePoint testers, shielded boxes and RF switch matrices, and Python or TestStand automation.
P: Common misconception. A good composite EVM means every subcarrier is good. One spur or a band-edge droop can ruin individual subcarriers while barely moving the composite; always look at EVM against subcarrier and against symbol.
BENCH: When EVM is worse than expected, change one thing at a time: lower the analyzer's input level by 5 dB (if EVM improves, the analyzer was compressing), raise it by 5 dB (if EVM improves, the analyzer was noise-limited), loop the generator directly into the analyzer (setup check), and then look at the per-subcarrier and per-symbol traces.
