H2: 31.1 OFDM in one page
P: Orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing sends many slow subcarriers side by side, each a QAM symbol, spaced at the inverse of the symbol time so they do not interfere (Book 6 has the theory). A cyclic prefix (CP) copied from the end of each symbol absorbs multipath delay. Pilots on known subcarriers let the receiver track phase and channel. The analyzer does what the receiver does: find the frame, remove the CP, FFT each symbol, estimate the channel from pilots or reference signals, equalize, and compare each subcarrier with its ideal QAM point.
P: The numbers to know: LTE uses 15 kHz subcarriers (66.7 µs symbols, normal CP 4.69 µs) and 180 kHz resource blocks (12 subcarriers), 100 blocks in 20 MHz. 5G NR scales the spacing by 2^µ: 15, 30, 60, 120 kHz and higher, with symbols and CPs shrinking by the same factor; a 100 MHz carrier at 30 kHz uses 273 blocks. Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) and Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be) use 78.125 kHz spacing (12.8 µs symbols, guard intervals 0.8, 1.6, or 3.2 µs), 1024-QAM and, for Wi-Fi 7, 4096-QAM, and channels up to 160 MHz and 320 MHz.
WE: Worked Example 31.1: Numerology
PROB: A 5G NR carrier is 100 MHz wide with 30 kHz subcarrier spacing. Find the symbol time, the normal CP, the occupied bandwidth with 273 resource blocks, and the guard on each side. How much path-length difference does the CP absorb?
SOL: Symbol time 1/30 kHz = 33.3 µs; normal CP 4.69 µs/2 = 2.34 µs (the 15 kHz value scaled by 2^µ = 2). Occupied 273 × 12 × 30 kHz = 98.28 MHz, leaving (100 − 98.28)/2 = 0.86 MHz on each side. A 2.34 µs CP absorbs a path difference of 2.34 µs × 3e8 m/s = 703 m, enough for most urban cells; the wider spacings at higher bands trade CP length for phase-noise tolerance.
H2: 31.2 What the analyzer reports
P: An OFDM demodulator reports composite EVM (rms over all data subcarriers and symbols, sometimes separately for pilots and data), EVM against subcarrier and against symbol, frequency error, symbol clock error, I/Q offset (LO leakage at the center subcarrier, which most standards exclude or limit separately), spectral flatness (the equalizer's channel estimate, which must stay within a mask), and common phase error. The standards set the EVM limits: for NR base stations (3GPP TS 38.104) 17.5 % for QPSK, 12.5 % for 16-QAM, 8 % for 64-QAM, and 3.5 % for 256-QAM; for Wi-Fi 6 MCS 11 (1024-QAM) −35 dB; for Wi-Fi 7 4096-QAM −38 dB. Use the standard's own demodulation settings (the equalizer training, the measurement interval, the exclusion of transient periods); a generic OFDM demodulator gives a different number.

WE: Worked Example 31.2: The Wi-Fi 6 EVM budget
PROB: A Wi-Fi 6 transmitter must meet −35 dB EVM for MCS 11. The analyzer's residual EVM is −45 dB and the transmitter's synthesizer has 0.5° rms phase noise. What EVM may everything else in the transmitter contribute?
SOL: −35 dB is 1.78 %; −45 dB is 0.56 %; 0.5° is 0.00873 rad, 0.87 %. The remainder: √(1.78² − 0.56² − 0.87²) = 1.44 %, −36.8 dB, shared by the PA's nonlinearity, I/Q imbalance, and noise. The analyzer takes a tenth of the error power and the phase noise a quarter; if the measurement reads −34.5 dB, check the analyzer's level and the synthesizer before redesigning the amplifier.

JOB: A wireless test engineer at a module maker or a certification lab spends the week turning standards into test plans: setting up the analyzer's Wi-Fi 7 and NR personalities, writing the automated sequences (Chapter 36) that step through channels, bandwidths, and modulation orders, and chasing the failures. A typical issue: EVM failing only on channel 149 at 160 MHz, traced to a spur from the module's own DC-DC converter harmonic landing on a subcarrier; the fix was a frequency-plan change in the converter's switching frequency. Tools: Keysight, R&S, and LitePoint testers, shielded boxes and RF switch matrices, and Python or TestStand automation.
MIS: A good composite EVM means every subcarrier is good. One spur or a band-edge droop can ruin individual subcarriers while barely moving the composite; always look at EVM against subcarrier and against symbol.
BENCH: When EVM is worse than expected, change one thing at a time: lower the analyzer's input level by 5 dB (if EVM improves, the analyzer was compressing), raise it by 5 dB (if EVM improves, the analyzer was noise-limited), loop the generator directly into the analyzer (setup check), and then look at the per-subcarrier and per-symbol traces.
H2: Practice
PP: 1. (Core) NR at 60 kHz subcarrier spacing: symbol time and normal CP?
PP: 2. (Core) LTE 10 MHz has 50 resource blocks. Occupied bandwidth?
PP: 3. (Core) How many 78.125 kHz subcarrier positions fit in a 160 MHz Wi-Fi channel?
PP: 4. (Extension) A 256-QAM NR signal measures −30 dB EVM. Margin to the 3.5 % limit, in dB?
PP: 5. (Extension) Measured EVM 2.0 %, analyzer residual 0.8 %. Device EVM?
PP: 6. (Challenge) EVM against subcarrier rises at both edges, and EVM against symbol rises in the first symbol of every slot. Diagnose both and say what you would measure next.
ANS: 1. 1/60 kHz = 16.7 µs; CP 4.69/4 = 1.17 µs. The usual mistake is to scale the CP by the spacing in the wrong direction.
ANS: 2. 50 × 180 kHz = 9 MHz. The usual mistake is to use the 10 MHz channel width.
ANS: 3. 160 MHz/78.125 kHz = 2048 positions (a 2048-point FFT); fewer are used for data. The usual mistake is to forget that guard and DC subcarriers are among them.
ANS: 4. −30 dB = 3.16 %; margin 20 log(3.5/3.16) = 0.9 dB. The usual mistake is to compare the dB value against −35 dB (1.78 %) from a different standard.
ANS: 5. √(2.0² − 0.8²) = 1.83 %. The usual mistake is to subtract linearly.
ANS: 6. Edges: amplitude or group-delay droop of a filter (the IF or RF channel filter, or the PA's matching), reducing edge SNR after equalization; measure the spectral flatness trace and the filter's S21 and group delay with a VNA. First symbol: a transient (the PA or bias settling at burst start, or the PLL settling after a frequency change); measure the power and phase against time at the burst start, and check the PA enable timing. The usual mistake is to tweak the PA bias for a problem that is in a filter or in timing.
