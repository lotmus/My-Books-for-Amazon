P: 8. When the short-circuit ratio is low, the grid's Thevenin impedance is high enough that the inverter's current can measurably change its terminal voltage. The phase-locked loop (PLL) uses that voltage to estimate grid angle; a current change can therefore shift the PLL input, alter the commanded current, and feed back into terminal voltage. If the PLL and current-control loops interact poorly with the grid impedance, the response can oscillate or lose synchronism. SCR is a screening metric, so the actual plant still needs impedance and stability studies.
P: 9. An ideal LCL filter has an undamped resonance at f_res = (1/(2 pi)) sqrt((L1 + L2)/(L1 L2 C)), where L1 is the converter-side inductance, L2 the grid-side inductance, and C the shunt capacitor. Near this frequency, energy exchanges between the inductors and capacitor and the filter transfer function develops a peak; the exact current or voltage response depends on which port is driven and observed. A grid harmonic near the peak can therefore produce excessive current, while inverter control delay and grid impedance can reduce stability margin. Passive damping adds loss, often through a resistor associated with C; active damping uses measured signals and control action but must itself be checked for delay and stability. Choose and verify damping with the actual component tolerances, grid impedance range, switching/control design, and applicable harmonic limits.
