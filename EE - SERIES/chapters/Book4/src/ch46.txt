H1: Chapter 46. RF SOI, SiGe, InP, and the Materials Still in the Lab
P: Between a phone's amplifiers and its antennas sit dozens of switches, each a stack of a dozen transistors on a thin film of silicon over glass-like oxide. At the other end of the spectrum, silicon-germanium radar chips see the road at 77 GHz and indium phosphide transistors have amplified at a terahertz. This chapter designs the switch, sets the limits of the fast technologies, and surveys what comes next.
P: Where you meet this: Antenna and band switches and antenna tuners in phones, automotive and industrial radar chips, optical-link and instrument front ends, sub-terahertz research systems, and the tunable components of reconfigurable radios.
P: Chapter 19 introduced RF switches and their figures of merit; Chapter 42 used stacks to raise a CMOS amplifier's voltage. Silicon on insulator, SOI, makes stacks practical, and it now builds nearly every switch and tuner in a phone. SiGe heterojunction bipolar transistors and InP devices extend silicon and compound technologies to millimeter and sub-millimeter waves. The figure of merit for each is different, and the chapter keeps them apart.
H2: 46.1 The RF SOI switch
P: In RF SOI the transistors sit in a thin silicon film over a buried oxide on a high-resistivity, trap-rich substrate. With no junction to the substrate, a transistor's body floats or is tied through a resistor, so a stack of many devices shares the RF voltage without any one junction breaking down, and the substrate adds little loss or nonlinearity. A switch arm is a series stack, turned on with a positive gate voltage and off with a negative one. Its figure of merit is the product Ron·Coff of one device, which does not depend on width: wider devices lower Ron and raise Coff in proportion. Leading processes reach about 80 to 90 fs.
EQ: Vpk = √(2 P Z0),   N ≥ (1 + |Γ|) Vpk / Vdevice,   IL ≈ 20 log(1 + Ron/(2Z0)),   Coff = (Ron·Coff)/Ron
H2: Worked Example 46.1
P: Problem. Size a series switch arm for 35 dBm in a 50 Ω system with a 3:1 VSWR margin, each device holding 2.5 V. With a total on-resistance of 1.5 Ω and Ron·Coff = 100 fs, find the insertion loss, the off capacitance, and the isolation of the series arm alone at 2 GHz.
P: Solution. 35 dBm is 3.16 W, so Vpk = √(2 × 3.16 × 50) = 17.8 V. At 3:1, |Γ| = 0.5 and the voltage can reach 1.5 × 17.8 = 26.7 V, so N ≥ 26.7/2.5 = 10.7, and 11 devices are stacked. The loss is 20 log(1 + 1.5/100) = 0.13 dB. The off capacitance is 100 fs/1.5 Ω = 66.7 fF, a reactance of 1,190 Ω at 2 GHz, and the isolation of the series arm alone is 20 log(|100 − j1,190|/100) = 21.6 dB. That is too little for most specifications, which is why practical switches add a shunt arm to ground on the off path, the series-shunt topology of Chapter 19, raising the isolation to 35 to 45 dB.
P: An antenna tuner is the same technology used as a switched capacitor or inductor bank across the antenna's feed or aperture. A small, high-Q antenna stores energy, so the voltage across the tuning capacitors can exceed the transmitted signal's voltage several times, and the stack height is set by that voltage. Tuners let one compact antenna cover bands from 600 MHz to 6 GHz in turn.
H2: 46.2 SiGe: radar and millimeter waves
P: SiGe HBTs grade germanium into the base to accelerate electrons, reaching fT around 300 GHz and fmax around 500 GHz in the fastest production BiCMOS processes, with the CMOS logic of a radar chip on the same die. The price is a low breakdown, BVCEO about 1.5 V for the fastest devices, so SiGe amplifiers deliver tens of milliwatts, not watts. A transistor's maximum available gain falls about 6 dB per octave toward fmax, roughly 20 log(fmax/f), so a 500 GHz fmax gives about 16 dB at 77 GHz, enough for two or three stages of a radar receiver and the voltage-controlled oscillators of Chapter 49.
H2: 46.3 InP: toward a terahertz
P: Indium phosphide HEMTs and double-heterojunction bipolar transistors have the highest electron velocities of the mature technologies. InP HEMTs give the lowest noise figures at millimeter waves, which places them in radio-astronomy receivers, and in 2014 a ten-stage InP HEMT amplifier gained 9 dB at 1.0 THz, a record. InP DHBTs drive the fastest optical-link electronics. The wafers are small and expensive, so InP is used where nothing else performs.
H2: 46.4 The figure of merit across materials
P: The Johnson figure of merit, the product of breakdown field and saturated velocity divided by 2π, bounds the product of a transistor's breakdown voltage and its cutoff frequency. With representative values it is about 480 GHz·V for silicon, 640 GHz·V for gallium arsenide, and 7.9 THz·V for gallium nitride, which is why GaN holds tens of volts at frequencies where GaAs holds a few. It is a bound, not a design rule: SiGe's fast devices sit close to silicon's limit, and real GaN devices reach a fraction of theirs.
EQ: JFOM = Ec vsat / (2π)  ≥  BV × fT

H2: 46.5 Tunable components: BST and MEMS
P: Two technologies make reconfigurable filters and matching networks. Barium strontium titanate, BST, is a ferroelectric whose permittivity falls with applied field, so a thin-film capacitor tunes by about 2 to 3 to 1 with tens of volts, continuously but with some loss and nonlinearity. Microelectromechanical, MEMS, switches and capacitors move a metal beam electrostatically: they have almost no loss or distortion, but need tens of volts, switch in microseconds, and must survive billions of cycles. An electrostatic actuator is stable only until the gap closes to two-thirds of its rest value; beyond that, the beam snaps down, at the pull-in voltage.
EQ: Vpull-in = √(8 k g0³ / (27 ε0 A))
H2: Worked Example 46.2
P: Problem. A MEMS switch has a spring constant of 10 N/m, a 2 µm gap, and a 100 µm × 100 µm electrode. Find its pull-in voltage.
P: Solution. Vpi = √(8 × 10 × (2×10⁻⁶)³/(27 × 8.854×10⁻¹² × 10⁻⁸)) = √(6.4×10⁻¹⁶/2.39×10⁻¹⁸) = √268 = 16.4 V. A charge pump on the controller supplies it. The cube of the gap dominates: halving the gap would lower the voltage by 2.8 times, but would also lower the off-state isolation and the power the switch can hold before the RF voltage itself pulls the beam down.
H2: 46.6 Materials still in the laboratory
P: Gallium oxide, Ga2O3, has a bandgap near 4.8 eV and a projected breakdown field near 8 MV/cm, attractive for power switching, but its thermal conductivity is poor. Diamond has the highest thermal conductivity of any material and a wide bandgap; diamond transistors are research devices, while diamond heat spreaders under GaN, GaN on diamond, already lower channel temperatures in demonstrations. Aluminum nitride and high-aluminum AlGaN channels push the breakdown field higher still. Graphene, carbon nanotubes, and other two-dimensional materials have shown high carrier mobilities and cutoff frequencies in research, but no power gain at the frequencies that matter and no manufacturing path yet. For a designer, these are reasons to watch the literature, not to change a design.
P: Bipolar version. Among these technologies the bipolar ones are SiGe and InP DHBTs; their designs follow the HBT rules of Chapter 44, ballast and all, at much lower voltages.
P: Key idea. Each technology has its own figure of merit: Ron·Coff and stack voltage for SOI switches, fmax and breakdown for SiGe and InP, the Johnson limit for power at frequency. Choose the technology by the figure that matters for the block, and check it against the voltage the block will see under mismatch.
BENCH: When characterizing a stacked switch, measure the second and third harmonics at the highest specified power and at the worst VSWR, not only the insertion loss. A stack whose voltage division is uneven, because of a gate resistor or a body-tie fault on one device, still passes small signals with normal loss but distorts or breaks down long before its rated power.
P: On the Job
P: Front-end module integration engineers combine power amplifiers, SOI switches and tuners, acoustic filters, and low-noise amplifiers in phone modules. They manage module specifications and interactions, such as a switch harmonic landing in a receive band or a filter detuning when a tuner moves. The work uses system budgets, circuit and electromagnetic simulation, and test campaigns. Experience in a component specialty can be useful; degree requirements vary by employer.
