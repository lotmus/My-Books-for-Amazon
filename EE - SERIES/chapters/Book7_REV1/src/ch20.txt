H1: Chapter 20. Measuring the Interconnect: TDR and the VNA
P: Send a fast step into a cable and listen for the echo, as a bat does. A bump in the reflection a nanosecond later says that a section a few centimeters away is 61 Ω instead of 50 Ω, before anyone has cut the board open. The time-domain reflectometer and its frequency-domain partner, the vector network analyzer, are how simulations meet copper.
P: Where you meet this: Every impedance coupon on a fabrication panel, every S-parameter file a link simulator needs, every cable fault locator, and every NanoVNA on a hobbyist's bench.
P: Chapters 5, 12, 13, and 14 predicted the impedance, crosstalk, and loss of traces and vias. Those predictions must be checked on fabricated boards, where laminate properties, etching, plating, connectors, and launches affect the result. Book 9 Chapters 18, 25, and 26 cover TDR/VNA operation, displays, and calibration. This chapter focuses on coupon geometry, stack-up correlation, and deciding whether a production channel meets its design budget.
H2: 20.1 The time-domain reflectometer
P: Book 9 Chapter 18 explains the TDR instrument, its trace, and time-to-distance conversion. Here, use the trace as a fabrication check on a coupon that copies the production stack-up and return plane. Positive excursions indicate higher impedance, often a narrow trace; negative excursions indicate lower impedance, often a wide trace or capacitive discontinuity. Use the actual edge rise time at the board and compare each feature with the layout; cable and launch features remain in the trace unless the reference plane excludes them.
P: For the impedance conversion and rise-time resolution relations, see Book 9 Chapter 18; Worked Example 20.1 applies them to a fabricated coupon.

H2: Worked Example 20.1
P: Problem. A production coupon contains a 30 mm narrow section on a 50 ohm stripline with epsilon_r = 3.55. Its calibrated TDR shows a reflection coefficient of +0.10 at the first edge. Find the section impedance, round-trip time between its edges, and whether a 35 ps edge can resolve both edges.
P: Solution. The narrowed section impedance is 50 x (1 + 0.10)/(1 - 0.10) = 61.1 ohms. The wave speed is 2.998 x 10^8/sqrt(3.55) = 1.59 x 10^8 m/s, so the 30 mm section produces a round-trip interval of 2 x 0.030/(1.59 x 10^8) = 377 ps. A 35 ps edge gives about 2.8 mm spatial resolution; the 30 mm section is resolvable by a wide margin. The reflected edge travels to the feature and back.
H2: 20.2 Timing and length
P: The same instrument measures delay. On the reference stripline, 100 mm of trace takes 628 ps one way, so its reflection arrives 1257 ps after the launch. Comparing the delay of two traces checks length matching for a differential pair or a memory bus directly, without knowing εr precisely, because both traces share the same laminate.
H2: 20.3 The vector network analyzer and calibration
P: Book 9 Chapters 25 and 26 explain VNA operation, S-parameter displays, error models, calibration standards, and de-embedding. This chapter uses those methods to validate board interconnects. Put the calibration plane at the coupon launch or use an on-board TRL standard when fixture effects dominate; make the coupon dielectric, copper, reference plane, and via geometry match the product stack-up. Select line standards for the product band; the example below sizes a two-line set for board measurements.
P: The 20-to-160-degree phase span is the usable first-pass band for each on-board line; Book 9 Chapter 26 explains calibration limits.
H2: Worked Example 20.2
P: Problem. A board coupon must support on-board TRL calibration from 2 to 16 GHz on stripline with epsilon_r = 3.55. Use two lines whose 90-degree frequencies are 4.24 and 12.65 GHz. Find their excess lengths over the through and the usable band of each line, using the 20-to-160-degree criterion.
P: Solution. The wave speed is 2.998 x 10^8/sqrt(3.55) = 1.59 x 10^8 m/s. Quarter-wave excess lengths are v/(4f): 9.38 mm at 4.24 GHz and 3.14 mm at 12.65 GHz. The usable ranges are 0.94 to 7.54 GHz and 2.81 to 22.49 GHz, respectively. Together they cover 2 to 16 GHz with overlap. These are first-pass lengths; use the fabricator effective-permittivity model and verify the standards after fabrication.
P: Key idea. Book 9 owns instrument operation and calibration theory. Book 7 uses calibrated measurements to test whether fabricated interconnects match the intended stack-up and channel budget; an on-board TRL coupon is a board-design choice that moves the reference plane to the structure.
H2: 20.4 De-embedding and correlation
P: A panel-edge coupon lets each lot be measured without probing the product itself. Book 9 Chapter 26.3 covers de-embedding algorithms; here, use a two-length coupon or on-board standards to remove launches, then compare measured loss per unit length with the Chapter 14 field-solver model. If measured loss is higher, check assumed dielectric loss, copper roughness, plating, and coupon geometry, then revisit the channel budget before release.
H2: 20.5 Reading S-parameters: insertion loss, return loss, and consistency checks
P: Book 9 Chapter 25.2 explains S-parameter formats and displays. For board acceptance, compare S21 insertion loss, S11 return loss, and crosstalk with the product interface budget across frequency, not at one convenient marker. A passive coupon should satisfy |S11|^2 + |S21|^2 <= 1 for excitation at port 1; a violation points to calibration, normalization, or data-quality problems. A single loss value cannot separate dielectric, copper, radiation, and connector losses.
P: Port-1 passivity check: |S11|^2 + |S21|^2 <= 1.
H2: Worked Example 20.3
P: Problem. A channel shows S21 = −18 dB and S11 = −12 dB at 26.6 GHz. Find the fractions of power transmitted and reflected, and the fraction lost in the channel.
P: Solution. Transmitted: 10^(−1.8) = 0.016; reflected: 10^(−1.2) = 0.063. Their sum is 0.079, so 92 % of the incident power is dissipated or radiated in the channel. The −12 dB return loss is poor; a reflection coefficient of 0.25 suggests a via or connector discontinuity that the TDR of Section 20.1 can locate.
H2: 20.6 Dynamic range, IF bandwidth, and sweep time
P: Book 9 Chapter 25.3 covers VNA noise floor, dynamic range, IF bandwidth, and sweep time. For PCB acceptance, choose settings so the noise floor sits below the smallest coupling or insertion-loss feature that controls the pass/fail decision. Record source power, IF bandwidth, point count, averaging, and calibration with each coupon result; hold them constant across lot comparisons or document why they changed.
P: For noise-floor and sweep-time estimates, see Book 9 Chapter 25.3; Example 20.4 applies the trade-off to a coupling limit.
H2: Worked Example 20.4
P: Problem. A coupling limit is -60 dB with a 0 dBm test source. At 1 kHz IF bandwidth the analyzer noise floor is -73 dBm. What is the largest IF bandwidth that preserves 10 dB of margin below the limit? Estimate the sweep time for 1601 points at 1 kHz and at the selected setting.
P: Solution. A 10 dB margin requires a floor no higher than -70 dBm. The present -73 dBm floor has 13 dB margin, so bandwidth may double: 2 kHz raises the floor by 3 dB to -70 dBm. The approximate sweep time is 1601/1000 = 1.60 s at 1 kHz and 1601/2000 = 0.80 s at 2 kHz. Confirm the actual analyzer setting and noise floor on the coupon fixture.

H2: Practice
P: 1. A 30 mm coupon section on epsilon_r = 3.55 has TDR edges separated by 377 ps. Confirm its length from the round-trip delay and decide whether a 35 ps system edge can resolve it.
P: 2. A 50 ohm board coupon produces a calibrated TDR reflection coefficient of +0.06. Find the local impedance and decide whether it is within a +/-5% target around 50 ohms.
P: 3. Two stripline lanes on the same stack-up have a one-way delay mismatch of 24 ps. Estimate their electrical-length mismatch for epsilon_r = 3.55.
P: 4. On 100 mm and 150 mm coupons, calibrated S21 is -1.1 dB and -1.5 dB at the same frequency. Estimate trace loss per centimeter after the common launches cancel.
P: 5. The product channel budget allows at most 1.0 dB interconnect loss at 26.6 GHz; a 100 mm production coupon measures -1.8 dB. By how much does it miss the budget, and which stack-up loss inputs should be rechecked against the Chapter 14 model?
P: 6. The two TRL lines from Worked Example 20.2 were designed using epsilon_r = 3.55, but a fabricated coupon measures epsilon_r = 3.80. Estimate the shifted 90-degree frequencies and the combined usable bands. Do they still cover 2 to 16 GHz?
P: 7. A passive two-port coupon reports S11 = -2 dB and S21 = -1 dB at one frequency. Does it satisfy the port-1 passivity check |S11|^2 + |S21|^2 <= 1? What should you inspect if not?
P: 8. An approved coupon shows 1.2 dB insertion loss. A new lot reads 1.6 dB, but the measured thru standard shifted by 0.6 dB during the same session. Can you attribute the 0.4 dB difference to the board stack-up? What should be repeated before deciding?
