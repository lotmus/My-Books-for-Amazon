Author: Lothar J. Musiol
Subtitle: From Bits to Waveforms: Sampling, Modulation, Fading, OFDM, MIMO, and the 2026 Band Plan

## Description (KDP HTML)

<p><b>How a modem turns bits into a waveform and back: Shannon's limit, sampling, pulse shaping, fading, OFDM, MIMO, synchronization, and the software-defined receiver, with the numbers a wireless engineer actually works with.</b></p>
<p>Why does a hundred times more bandwidth buy a deep-space probe only 44 % more capacity? Why do two Wi-Fi radios that both meet their crystal specification disagree by 232 kHz, and why does every packet still decode? Why does a Wi-Fi link that sends one packet at a time move 57 Mbit/s, and 913 Mbit/s when it sends 64? <i>Communications, Wireless, and SDR</i> answers those questions in 25 chapters:</p>
<ul>
<li><b>The link:</b> Shannon's limit, sampling and complex baseband, pulse shaping and the matched filter, constellations and EVM, multipath and diversity, OFDM, coding and MIMO, the 2026 band plan for Wi-Fi 7 and 5G, and a complete FM receiver in software.</li>
<li><b>Inside the modem:</b> decimating and CIC filters, carrier and timing synchronization, equalization, FSK, spread spectrum and LoRa chirps, pilots and channel estimation, multiple access and scheduling, link adaptation, HARQ and real throughput.</li>
<li><b>Systems:</b> ranging and positioning, interleaving and CRCs, massive MIMO, cellular SINR, satellites and non-terrestrial networks, and battery-powered IoT radios.</li>
<li><b>Silicon, sensing, light, and your own network:</b> the modem in FPGA and ASIC logic, radar detection, microphone arrays and sensor fusion, fiber and data-center optics, infrared and optical wireless, and a LoRa mesh, BLE positioning, and a network of ESP32 nodes you can build.</li>
</ul>
<p>Every chapter ends with <b>What You'll Actually Use</b>, an honest list that separates the everyday working tools from the theory you mainly need for exams or deeper study. <b>From the Bench</b> notes add the practical layer: chasing aliases, reading eye diagrams and constellations, protecting an SDR from overload, debugging Wi-Fi throughput, and measuring sleep currents.</p>
<p>Inside: 34 diagrams, including capacity curves, eye diagrams, error-rate curves, OFDM spectra, LoRa chirps, coin-cell lifetimes, range-Doppler maps, and fiber link budgets, all computed from the worked examples; 256 practice problems, each worked in full with a check and the usual mistake; On the Job notes on who uses each topic; and a linked source list. Written for engineering students and working engineers moving into wireless and SDR. Book 5 of the nine-book Electrical Engineering Series; builds on <i>RF, Microwave, and Transceivers</i> (Book 4).</p>


<p><b>Reader level:</b> Recommended for ages 18 and up: engineering students and adults comfortable with algebra, complex numbers, probability, and basic signals. Prior RF and circuit foundations from Books 1 and 4 make the progression smoother. Age is a guide; preparation matters more.</p>
