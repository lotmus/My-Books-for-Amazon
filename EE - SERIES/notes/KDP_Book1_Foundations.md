# KDP listing: Foundations of Electronics (Electrical Engineering Series, Book 1)

Author: Lothar J. Musiol

## Description (KDP HTML)

<p><b>The physics, the math and the circuit theory every electronics engineer actually uses, explained by an engineer who uses it.</b></p>
<p>Why does a bare LED die on a 5 V supply? Why do your headlights dim when the starter cranks? Why does a 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi signal care how long a circuit-board trace is? <i>Foundations of Electronics</i> answers questions like these from first principles, in 38 short chapters and three parts:</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Part I, the physics:</b> charge, fields, Gauss's law, voltage, capacitance, current, magnetism, induction and Maxwell's equations.</li>
<li><b>Part II, the math:</b> SI units, engineering notation, uncertainty, algebra, linear systems, decibels, trigonometry, complex numbers, phasors and just enough calculus.</li>
<li><b>Part III, the circuits:</b> Kirchhoff's laws, dividers, nodal and mesh analysis, Thevenin and Norton, transients, AC analysis, power factor, resonance, transformers, and a practical first look at the Smith chart and impedance matching.</li>
</ul>
<p>Every chapter ends with a section called <b>What You'll Actually Use</b>, an honest list that separates the everyday working tools from the theory you mainly need for exams or deeper study. <b>From the Bench</b> notes add what textbooks tend to leave out: probing and scope-ground pitfalls, capacitor DC-bias derating, resistor derating, trace sizing, and the rules of thumb engineers use to sanity-check a design.</p>
<p>You also build things. A ten-part <b>reader lab</b> (an ESP32-S3 board, an OLED display, ultrasonic, motion, weather and GPS sensors, a LoRa radio, a servo, an 18650 cell and a camera) is introduced here and used through the whole series, always analog first: a thermistor and a Wheatstone bridge make a thermostat with no code before twenty lines on the ESP32 do the same job. Three projects run through the book: a Smart Weather Station, a Home Energy Dashboard and a Smart Refrigerator Monitor.</p>
<p>Inside: 56 diagrams, more than 70 worked examples, 144 practice problems plus 12 review questions, with every solution worked in full, On the Job notes from working engineers' roles, a formula reference, a glossary and a circuit-analysis checklist.</p>
<p>This is a textbook, not a popular-science tour: it uses equations and expects you to work problems. If you want a readable, rigorous foundation that tells you what matters in practice, for a first EE course, a career change or a refresher, start here. Book 1 of the nine-book Electrical Engineering Series.</p>


<p><b>Reader level:</b> Recommended for ages 16 and up, including advanced high-school students and adults. Algebra and trigonometry are helpful; calculus is introduced gently and is not required in every chapter. Age is a guide; readiness for the stated math matters more.</p>
