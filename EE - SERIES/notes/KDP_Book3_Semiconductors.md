# KDP listing: Semiconductor Physics and Devices (Electrical Engineering Series, Book 3)

Author: Lothar J. Musiol

## Description (KDP HTML)

<p><b>From the silicon crystal to the chip: how diodes, transistors, memories and power switches really work, with the numbers an engineer uses.</b></p>
<p>Why does a diode "turn on" at 0.7 V when it is already conducting at 0.5 V? Why does a chip six times larger cost nearly twelve times as much? Why did high-voltage switching move to silicon carbide and gallium nitride? <i>Semiconductor Physics and Devices</i> answers those questions in 28 chapters:</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Carriers and junctions:</b> bands, doping, drift and diffusion, the built-in voltage, the diode law and the diode you can buy, plus GaAs, GaN and InP for RF, heterojunctions and the HBT.</li>
<li><b>Transistors:</b> the bipolar transistor, bias and the hybrid-π model, the MOSFET from the capacitor up, and the FinFET and nanosheet.</li>
<li><b>Inside the amplifier:</b> current mirrors, matching and Pelgrom's law, the two-stage op-amp from its transistors, and the noise devices make.</li>
<li><b>CMOS as a chip:</b> inverter delay and power, SRAM, DRAM and flash, and what breaks a chip: ESD, latch-up, hot carriers and oxide wear-out.</li>
<li><b>Light, power and manufacturing:</b> LEDs, photodiodes and solar cells, power MOSFETs, IGBTs, SiC and GaN, how a transistor is made, piezoelectric materials and acoustic resonators, and the compact models and corners of a process design kit.</li>
<li><b>From datasheet to model:</b> how a SPICE model is fitted from a data sheet for real diodes, BJTs, MOSFETs and JFETs, why every model is a fit and not the device, and how to tell where a fit stops being true.</li>
<li><b>Materials, light, sensing and the chip:</b> SOI, SiGe, InP, GaAs, GaN, gallium oxide, diamond and AlN compared by figure of merit; lasers, VCSELs, APDs, SPADs, image and event pixels; MEMS, Hall, gas and quantum sensors; and the ASIC-versus-FPGA decision with an NRE break-even.</li>
</ul>
<p>Every chapter ends with <b>What You'll Actually Use</b>, an honest list that separates the everyday working tools from the theory you mainly need for exams or deeper study. <b>From the Bench</b> notes add the practical layer: forced beta, bias that ignores β, ESD at the connector, flash wear, hot on-resistance and running corners early.</p>
<p>Inside: 33 diagrams, worked examples with declared values, practice problems with every solution worked in full, parameter lists for datasheet-to-SPICE work, and a linked source list. Written for engineering students and working engineers who want the device physics behind their circuits. Book 3 of the nine-book Electrical Engineering Series; builds on <i>Foundations of Electronics</i> and <i>Circuits, Components, and Control</i>.</p>
