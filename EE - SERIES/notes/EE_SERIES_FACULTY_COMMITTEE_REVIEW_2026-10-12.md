## Committee standard

Scores reflect technical rigor (30%), teaching and problem design (25%), structure and scope (15%), source/reproducibility support (15%), and clarity (15%). A score of 8 means strong and useful, with identifiable improvements; 9 means unusually strong and close to a polished course text. Scores are editorial judgments, not peer-review certification. Because the series spans distinct specialties, a single “as good as a professor” comparison would be misleading; we judge whether each volume teaches its declared subject to a demanding reader.
