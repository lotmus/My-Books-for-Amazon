## Reader, voice, method

Students, working engineers moving into embedded work, and serious hobbyists. Didactic, bench-near, modern. One repeatable method runs through the whole book and is applied in the worked examples (the "Method" thread):

1. Write a short spec (purpose, hardware, pins, interfaces, timing, power, error handling, acceptance tests).
2. Break it into small modules.
3. Write or prompt one module at a time, with the spec attached.
4. Review the code against a checklist.
5. Build and unit-test on the PC.
6. Test on hardware with the scope or logic analyzer.
7. Commit.
8. Iterate with precise bug reports.
9. Document.

The spec template and the review checklist are given once (Chapter 1, boxed lists, plus Figure 1.2) and reused in the worked examples throughout and in Chapters 24 and 25; Appendix A repeats them.

Threads: "On the Job" (firmware-engineer reality, at least once per part), "From the Bench" (every chapter), "AI Pair-Programming Note" (in every chapter; full treatment in Chapter 25), "What You'll Actually Use" (every chapter).

Hardware: the series reader lab (ESP32-S3, OLED, ultrasonic sensor, PIR, BME280, GPS, LoRa radio, servo, 18650 module, camera) and the CONTROLLERS boards (ESP32-S3, Pico/RP2040, STM32, Pi 5, nRF52840, Teensy 4.1). Prices quoted as "typically" or "about".

One-home rule: circuits and logic levels (Books 1-2), sensor physics (3), RF front ends (4), modulation, LoRa airtime math and protocols (5), batteries and regulators (6), layout, EMC and test equipment (7), ASIC/fab economics (3). Book 8 links to them and does not re-derive.

<p>Why does a button that works on the desk fire three times in the field? Why does a 115200-baud link fail at one board and not another? Why does an ESP32-S3 node that should last a year on an 18650 cell die in nine days? <i>Firmware and Software for Electronics</i> answers with code you can run and numbers you can check, in 26 chapters:</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Tools and method:</b> ESP-IDF, Arduino, PlatformIO, the Pico SDK and STM32CubeIDE; C for microcontrollers; MicroPython and Python on the PC; C# with .NET, nanoFramework and Visual Studio.</li>
