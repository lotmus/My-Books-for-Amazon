# KDP listing: Circuits, Components, and Control (Electrical Engineering Series, Book 2)

Author: Lothar J. Musiol

## Description (KDP HTML)

<p><b>Real parts, real op-amps, real control loops: the working core of electronics design, from an engineer who builds with it.</b></p>
<p>Why does a "10 µF" capacitor measure 4 µF on your board? Why does a follower that works on the bench oscillate once you add a cable? Why does a simulation that matches the datasheet perfectly still fail on real hardware? <i>Circuits, Components, and Control</i> answers those questions in 28 chapters:</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Analog and digital basics:</b> bias points and headroom, logic levels and noise margins, Boolean algebra, flip-flops, counters and timing with metastability, 555, 4000 and 7400 builds, and a first look at FPGAs.</li>
<li><b>Op-amps with real numbers:</b> feedback, followers and capacitive loads, gain-bandwidth and slew rate, noise, DC error budgets, comparators, instrumentation, transimpedance and charge amplifiers, sample-and-hold circuits and voltage references.</li>
<li><b>Datasheets and SPICE:</b> how to read an op-amp or comparator datasheet line by line, and how to turn it into a SPICE macromodel checked against the datasheet, with a clear warning that a model is a fit, valid only over the range it was fitted to.</li>
<li><b>Real components:</b> resistors, capacitors and inductors as you buy them, with derating, drift, pulses, current sensing, decoupling, DC-bias loss and inductor datasheets, plus LED drivers, optocouplers, relays and flyback diodes.</li>
<li><b>Filters:</b> from first-order RC to Sallen-Key and multiple-feedback designs with standard parts.</li>
<li><b>Control:</b> a smart thermostat as the running example, PID with anti-windup, PWM and PID on a Raspberry Pi Pico or Teensy, lead and lag, digital control and sampling delay, state feedback and observers, H-bridge, servo and stepper drives, root locus, stability margins and oscillators.</li>
</ul>
<p>Every chapter ends with <b>What You'll Actually Use</b>, an honest list that separates the everyday working tools from the theory you mainly need for exams or deeper study. <b>From the Bench</b> notes add the practical layer: Kelvin connections, isolation resistors, dead time, synchronizers, error budgets and how to measure a loop on real hardware. <b>On the Job</b> notes show where this material is used at work.</p>
<p>You also build things, analog first, with the series reader lab: circuits that work with no controller at all, robotics and RC builds, and three running projects (a Smart Weather Station, a Home Energy Dashboard and a Smart Refrigerator Monitor).</p>
<p>Inside: 59 diagrams, 108 worked examples, 215 practice problems with every solution worked in full, a formula reference and a glossary. A textbook that expects you to work problems; it builds on Book 1, <i>Foundations of Electronics</i>. Book 2 of the seven-book Electrical Engineering Series.</p>
