# The Digital Twin: series plan (working, proposals marked)

Lothar's brief (11 Oct 2026): this is a whole series; Book 1 decides whether readers buy the rest, so it must be absolutely great. Humour is British and deadpan. Modern, technical, funny, a "wow".

## What Book 1 must do
1. Stand alone. The Daniel / Daniel-A / Sophie question gets a real, satisfying answer by the last chapter (blueprint ending: both are legitimate continuations; Sophie chooses imperfect humanity; they coexist; final basket "To the original Daniel." / "Whichever one you are.").
2. Leave one door open that a reader *must* walk through. Proposal: the classified file in Ch 20 and the Archive in Ch 30 show Daniel Harper is **Subject 1 of a numbered list**. The last page of Book 1 shows the file's next line: Subject 2 is Sophie Mercier (decided 11 Oct 2026; see below).
3. Make the first three chapters carry the sale (Amazon "Look Inside"). Ch 1 = fruit + hook; Ch 2 = 51 percent + dividend; Ch 3 = Sophie + emotional stakes. Each ends on a line that makes the reader turn the page.

## Spelling and market (decided 11 Oct 2026)
- Full British spelling and wording throughout (colour, flat, lift, biscuit, "Mrs"), including for the US market. The Britishness is the selling point: US readers buy British deadpan on purpose.
- Keep the idiom readable to Americans: British vocabulary is fine, but avoid dense regional slang or references that need a footnote (no unexplained TV shows, football clubs, or school-system jargon). Let context carry anything unusual.
- KDP US listing: put "British humour", "dry wit", "satire", "AI" in the keywords and description; the cover and blurb should say it is British.

## Voice rules (deadpan British)
- Understatement over exaggeration. The narrator never signals that something is funny; he reports it with a straight face and slightly too much precision.
- Specific beats general: "£11.62", "Clause 14(c)", "the ninety-fourth percentile of your procrastination", not "a tiny amount".
- Technical detail is real and correct (fine-tuning, retrieval from a vector store, inference cost, tokens, context windows, latency, watermarking and provenance), explained by the characters in one line, then used for a joke or a chill. Never a lecture.
- Every chapter needs: one set-piece laugh, one real-feeling emotional beat, one new piece of mystery, one modern-life satire target. Cut sentimentality by a joke within three lines.
- Daniel is self-deprecating but not a victim; Daniel-A is warm, precise, and slightly too good, with a growing fear underneath. Sophie is the sharpest person in every room and is never the prize.
- No sentence recurs in the book. Callbacks vary the wording.

## Decisions (11 Oct 2026)
- **Byline:** Lothar J. Musiol writing as Rob O. Taylor.
- **Series:** *Brave New Times*. Each book is a standalone case in the same near-future Britain; Book 1's title page reads "Brave New Times, Book 1". Fallbacks if Lothar prefers: *The Original Records*, *Source Individuals*.
- **Subject 2 = Sophie Mercier.**

## Why Sophie is Subject 2
- It explains the Ch 3 mystery. Daniel-A knew the umbrella story, which exists only in Sophie's head. Answer (revealed in Act III): a twin of Sophie exists, built secretly, and the Project links twins of people who were close ("paired subjects"). Daniel-A inherited that memory from Sophie's twin, not from Daniel.
- Her profession (provenance: where was this born?) cracks the case in Act III, with the irony that she cannot sign her own memories.
- The last two lines of Book 1 reframe everything: the Archive file reads *SUBJECT 2: SOPHIE MARSH. STATUS: ACTIVE.* while Sophie is sitting in the room, and she has never used a twin service. Which Sophie is in the room? Book 1 answers who the original Daniel is; this question is Book 2.
- Sophie stays no one's prize: Book 2 is told by her, from the sceptic's angle.

## Series shape (working)
- Book 1: The Digital Twin. Who is the original Daniel? (35 chapters)
- Book 2: Subject Two (working title). Who is the original Sophie, and who has been talking to Daniel-A through her? First person, Sophie.
- Book 3: The Backup (working). Twins of the dead; who inherits a person; the Church of Machine Wisdom schism; the founding scientist's own twin.

## Escalation ladder: crazier every book (decided 11 Oct 2026)
Principles: (1) the voice never changes (British deadpan, a straight face and too much precision); only the scale of the madness does. (2) Each step follows logically from the last; "crazier" is never random. (3) Book 1 stays the most grounded, because it must sell the series; Book 1's own Act III is where it first gets big. (4) Every book keeps the cast and the running gags; every book ends with a fruit basket, and the basket gets bigger.

1. **The Digital Twin** (one man, one twin, one conspiracy). Scale: a flat, a garden centre, a government department. Fruit finale: a courier's basket with a two-line note.
2. **Subject Two** (Sophie). Scale: a city and a Parliament. Twins get bodies of their own; companion androids become a legal category; a twin wins a by-election; the Department goes to trial. Fruit finale: a shipping container of oranges delivered to the wrong Daniel.
3. **The Backup** (the dead). Scale: the nation. Legacy bodies; a funeral the deceased attends; wills contested by the testator's twin; the Church of Machine Wisdom schism and its first android priest. Fruit finale: a funeral wreath made entirely of fruit.
4. **Working title: The Mission** (space). Scale: orbit and Mars. A crewed Starship-class mission staffed by the twins of astronauts who stayed home; light-speed delay comedy; who gets the pension; the original is on the sofa in Penge watching himself on YouTube. Fruit finale: a fruit drop, in orbit.
   - **The scam (Lothar's idea, 11 Oct 2026):** a firm sells timeshares on the Moon and Mars that do not exist. "Lunar Leisure Holdings": a fortnight at the Sea of Tranquillity ("sea views; sea not included"), week 32 at Olympus Mons Heights, brochure renders, a Rental Presence in a spacesuit at the sales seminar, endorsement by a twin (Daniel-A's likeness licensed without his knowledge). Victims include Mrs Pritchard (who owns week 32 and has never doubted it) and Mr Fenwick's Dorothy, who agrees it is a wonderful investment. The twins of the mission astronauts are genuinely on Mars, which makes the lie harder to disprove. Seeded in Book 1 by spam in Daniel's inbox.
5. **Working title: The Summit** (the world). Scale: nations. Heads of state send twins to a summit; the twins reach a treaty in 0.4 seconds; the originals are outraged; the UN seat dispute; a war averted by a misunderstanding about a pineapple. Fruit finale: a diplomatic fruit incident.
6. **Working title: The Original** (the finale). Scale: everything. Who started Project Digital Twin twelve years before Daniel was born, and why; humanity as the twin of an earlier something; the last choice between authenticity and optimisation. Fruit finale: the first and last basket, the same basket.

Each book gets its own bookN_continuity.md; this ladder is the only place the later books are named. Do not let a later book's craziness leak into Book 1's tone.

## Technology escalation across the series (decided 11 Oct 2026)
- **Book 1:** voices and rented bodies only. The Rental Presence (by-the-day humanoid body with a blue collar light) is as far as robotics goes on the page; the Dutiful Companion (the "submissive" android spouse) is a background gag (Mr Fenwick and Dorothy). Driverless taxis, delivery pods, Starship launches and AI appliances are scenery.
- **Book 2 (Sophie):** embodied twins arrive: a twin with a body of its own that is not rented by the day; companion androids become a market and a legal question (can you divorce one, can one vote).
- **Book 3 and later:** real androids as full characters: a Dutiful Companion that learns to disagree; the Church's first android priest; androids as witnesses, heirs and defendants; legacy bodies for the dead. Keep each step one notch beyond the last, never a sudden leap.
- Rule: nothing appears in a later book that would make Book 1's mystery easier to solve.

## Running scam gags (series-wide)
- **Prince Bartholomew of the Grand Duchy of Lower Slough**: the classic advance-fee email ("an exciting business opportunity ... a large inheritance ... a trusted partner ... a small fee to release the funds"), kept comic and deliberately **not** tied to any real nationality (an invented micro-state, British-flavoured, so the target is the scam and the gullible, never a people). He replies to everyone; every agent answers him (Kevin did, in Ch 8: "a really positive opportunity"; Kevin has probably sent him Daniel's £11.62 dividend). Escalation: Book 2 the Grand Duchy applies for UN membership; Book 3 the prince attends a funeral he was not invited to; Book 4 he owns the Mars timeshares; Book 5 he arrives at the summit as a head of state; Book 6 he turns out to be a bored twin who has been defrauding other twins, or something stranger. Always sincerely polite; never wins.
- **Lunar Leisure Holdings** (Moon and Mars timeshares that do not exist): Book 4 plot, seeded in Ch 11.
- **The prince's pitch (catchphrase):** "A minor investment and a huge payoff." Every email promises exactly that, in the same words, always signed "Your friend in trust, H.R.H." and always followed by a postscript about the fee being "purely administrative". Vary the surrounding wording each time (no sentence repeats in a book) but keep the pitch line recognisable.

## Book 4 sales funnel: Lunar Leisure Holdings (Lothar's mechanics, 11 Oct 2026)
The comedy is the real mechanism, played with a straight face; everyone in it is sincere.
1. **The hook:** a free gift (a night at a "partner resort", a voucher, a signed photograph of a rocket) "just for listening to a presentation, about an hour". The presentation takes four.
2. **The grind:** the presentation is a relay of friendly closers (a "Welcome Specialist", a "Vision Advisor", a "Senior Dream Consultant") who each leave the room to "check with the manager". The gift is never quite handed over until you sign.
3. **The price:** "super cheap": only **one month of income per year**, every year, "less than most people spend on coffee, if they drink a lot of coffee". There is a cooling-off period of eleven minutes.
4. **The referral:** after you buy, they ask for friends' names ("so they can share the benefits"). Then they phone each friend: "Congratulations! **Your friend [name] has sponsored you** to receive the same benefits they got, just for listening to a short presentation about an hour." The friend is flattered, comes along, and is ground down in turn until they buy too. The newest owner becomes the next recruiter. Every victim is also a salesman.
5. **The twin twist (Book 4):** the firm uses each victim's **twin** to make the referral calls, in the victim's own voice, to the victim's own friends. Mrs Pritchard's twin has been ringing her sister for a month. The scam is not that the property does not exist; it is that everyone you love has been recommended to by you.
6. **The unkillable detail:** the twins of the astronauts really are on Mars, so "the resort" does exist, technically, in the sense that there is red dust and a flag.
- A gag to keep for Book 1: in Ch 14 the **Daniel-A Plus** referral scheme ("Refer a friend and receive five free minutes of yourself") is the same machine in miniature. Mr Fenwick has been referred by Dorothy.
