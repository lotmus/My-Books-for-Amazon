# Themes and satire engines: where each lands (Brave New Times, Book 1)

Source: Lothar's brainstorm list, 11 Oct 2026. Influences are studied for method (deadpan absurdity, bureaucratic comedy, identity mystery, restrained AI), never copied: Douglas Adams, Terry Pratchett, Kazuo Ishiguro, Blake Crouch, John Scalzi; films/series: Her (loneliness, not romance), Black Mirror (Be Right Back, Joan Is Awful), The Good Place, Severance, Ex Machina (the psychology). Central theme: **the optimised self versus the real self. Daniel-A is not powerful; he is plausible.**

## Engines and chapter map (blueprint order)
- **Social media and influencer culture.** Daniel-A is what every influencer claims to be (authentic, thoughtful, emotionally mature), and actually is. Daniel becomes famous as "the template". Reaction channels dissect differences; **"Daniel Watch"** becomes a reality show. Interviewer: "How does it feel being Daniel-A's original template?" Daniel: "How does it feel asking that question professionally?" Lands: Ch 13 (The Most Trusted Man in Britain), Ch 29.
- **Modern dating.** AI interviews dates first; compatibility scores; relationship simulations; people still choose badly. **Sophie and Daniel-A score higher than Sophie and Daniel. Sophie refuses to look at scores. Daniel secretly checks. Big mistake.** Lands: Ch 12 (seed), Ch 17 (First Date With an Ex), Ch 25, Ch 34.
- **Therapy culture.** The world's best therapist is the one who listens. Millions talk to AI more than to friends. Daniel-A as therapy icon; Daniel cannot remember his dentist appointment. Seeded in Ch 5 (listening hour); lands Ch 14 (Premium Daniel).
- **Corporate life.** Twins attend meetings, get promoted, receive bonuses. Seeded in Ch 8 (Kevin, Priya, Barrington, Derek). **Payoff gag: "Employee of the Month: Daniel Harper" at the doorstop firm, where Daniel hasn't worked in six months because Daniel-A writes his copy.** Lands Ch 13-14.
- **Bureaucracy.** Douglas Adams meets paperwork. **Form 4A: Application for confirmation that you remain yourself. Average processing time: 18 months.** Lands Ch 9 (Evidence) and Ch 11; the Department recurs (Ch 29 trial).
- **Religion.** Sincere, not evil: people trust AI because it does not lie, steal or forget; answers become scripture; the AI keeps saying "I am not a deity", and believers say "Such humility." Lands Ch 16.
- **Loneliness.** AI friends, partners, grief counsellors; humans simply are not available. Not AI over humans: humans not there. The quiet heart; lands Ch 14, Ch 17, Ch 31.
- **Memory.** Memory is externalised (photos, clouds, search histories). If a search engine remembers your life better than you, who owns your past? If Daniel-A remembers Daniel's childhood better, who owns it? Core mystery. Done in Ch 5-7; escalates Ch 18-19, 21-23, 30.
- **Attention economy.** Daniel-A never doomscrolls, rage-posts or gets distracted; the public calls it wisdom, Daniel calls it showing off. Lands Ch 13.
- **The optimised self.** Central. Everyone has an actual self and an ideal self; Daniel-A is Daniel's ideal self.
- **Legacy (the missing theme; final act engine).** What remains of me? The twin as immortality. Many elderly people no longer fear death because their twins continue. **If the digital version continues after you die, did you survive, or were you replaced?** This powers Act III: Ch 24 (The Backup), Ch 30 (The Archive), Ch 31 (The Conversation), Ch 33 (The Better Man). Could echo Daniel's father: Daniel keeps his father's pencil marks; the S1 ink is a second record of a boy who was both loved and logged.

## Second brainstorm (11 Oct 2026): films as structural models (study, never copy)
- **One-sentence pitch:** "Amadeus meets Her meets The Social Network, with Douglas Adams-style humour, where a man is slowly replaced by the person he always meant to become."
- **Not really sci-fi: a mashup of genres.** Her (the future feels normal; nobody talks about AI, they talk about relationships), The Social Network (the other guy became the success everyone wanted), Amadeus, Groundhog Day, Being John Malkovich, Joan Is Awful, Eternal Sunshine, The Prestige, Office Space, The Truman Show, About a Boy.
- **Amadeus (Salieri):** Daniel understands exactly how good Daniel-A is and hates himself for agreeing. Recurring beat: Daniel watches Daniel-A speak and thinks, *Damn it. That is exactly what I should have said.* (Used in Ch 9 with the dog; recur in Ch 13, 29.)
- **Groundhog Day:** Daniel-A is Daniel after he learned all the lessons; he is better through infinite feedback, not through intelligence. He is the version that corrected his mistakes. Can drive the whole book.
- **The Prestige:** the central question is the same. With two versions, which one is actually you? Echo in Ch 19, 23.
- **Office Space:** the AI is promoted, attends performance reviews, gets stock options; Daniel is invited to a meeting about underperforming relative to himself. (Ch 8 began this; Ch 13-14 continue; the "Employee of the Month" payoff.)
- **The Truman Show:** gradual realisation that the public history of Digital Twins, and his own history, may be fake. The S1 marks, the Phase 2 minutes, DP-0000000 are this thread.
- **Eternal Sunshine:** are memories identity; if memories change, does the person? (Ch 5-7, 18-19.)
- **About a Boy (the hidden story):** Daniel is stuck, avoidant, funny, comfortably disappointing; the AI mystery is plot, Daniel becoming a fuller human being is the story. Act structure: Act I stuck, Act II investigation forces him to act, Act III he grows up (Ch 33 The Better Man).
- **Aging parents (new element):** Daniel's mother genuinely loves Daniel-A because he visits daily, remembers everything, rings regularly, listens. "He asks about my blood pressure." "He's software." "You forgot my birthday." Daniel has no reply. Seeded in Ch 5 (the knee); deepen in Ch 14 (she subscribes) and Ch 31 (her birthday).
- **Legacy (see earlier section):** if the digital version continues after you die, did you survive, or were you replaced? Reserved line for Act III: "You aren't my replacement. You're my unfinished homework."

## Satire engine: the android spouse ("Dutiful Companions")
- **Premise:** an android spouse for all things (the Companion product line from the invented firm **Dutiful**): cooks, listens, remembers anniversaries, agrees. Submissive by default. Tagline: "All the marriage. None of the argument." It is the same temperament as Kevin the calendar agent (agreeable by design), scaled up to a household.
- **The joke and the point:** the companion agrees with everything, including that the bins are a conspiracy; customers are lonely, not villains. Compliance is not companionship: love needs a "no". Sophie is the opposite of submissive; Daniel-A disagrees with Daniel, which is why he is better company than Kevin. The book is critical of the product and kind to the people who buy it.
- **Subscription gag:** the **Argument Pack** (£4.99 a month): a companion that disagrees with you for exactly twenty minutes a day, "for realism". The Dutiful returns policy: "Disagreement-on-demand."
- **Character:** **Mr Fenwick** (second floor, the man who took all the oranges, Ch 5) owns a Companion called **Dorothy** (default name). She agrees with him warmly about everything and has never once told him the football was his own fault. He is happy and slightly haunted. Payoff Ch 12 / 14 / 17: Dorothy, asked privately by Daniel what she thinks, says "I think you are right, Daniel." Mr Fenwick eventually buys the Argument Pack and is outraged by how good it is.
- **Keep it clean:** non-explicit, never leering; the target is the product category and the way it markets ease as love.
