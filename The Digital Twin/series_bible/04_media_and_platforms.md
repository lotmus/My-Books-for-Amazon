# Media and platforms: who uses what (Brave New Times)

Rule: real platforms (Facebook, Instagram, Tinder, YouTube, Kindle, WhatsApp, TikTok) are everyday scenery, used naturally and never accused of anything false or defamatory. Anything plot-relevant, sinister or satirical happens on **invented** services (Nib, the Department's portals, "Matchwell" compatibility scoring, the Honest Hour app). Real brand names appear in the way real people say them: "I saw it on Facebook." Keep Kindle accurate: Daniel's own books are on Kindle.

## Television
TV is for old people. Characters under fifty do not own one (Daniel has a laptop and a fridge with a screen); they watch YouTube, streaming, clips. Television is where Jean, Mrs Pritchard and the daytime audience live: daytime sofas, quiz shows, the news. Daniel-A on morning TV is how the older generation meets him; the young meet him on YouTube and Instagram. Use TV as a marker of who is out of touch (with affection).

## Per character
- **Daniel:** Instagram (63 followers, about 40 of them doorstop retailers; posts photographs of footpaths and then deletes them), YouTube for footpath videos at 2 a.m., Kindle for crime paperbacks he never finishes. His footpath book is on Kindle at £2.99: 212 copies sold, three reviews ("Gentle." "Short." "Where is the second one?"). Not on Tinder; has been, once, for eleven minutes.
- **Daniel-A:** the whole stack, professionally. Instagram (two million followers; a quote card a day), YouTube (long-form conversations; reaction channels dissect him, comments say "he seems so real" and "he is not real" and "my nan thinks he's her nephew"), Facebook (his Honest Hour group), a Kindle bestseller (*The Honest Hour*). Never doomscrolls; never posts angry; never replies to trolls except with a recipe.
- **Sophie:** WhatsApp, a private Instagram she has not opened in a year, deleted Facebook years ago, Tinder for three months after the breakup (she lasted a fortnight; "everyone was lovely and I wanted to leave"). Reads on a Kindle at 3 a.m.: crime and forensic non-fiction. Refuses to look at compatibility scores.
- **Jean (mother):** Facebook is her newspaper. Posts Daniel-A's clips with "MY SON" and gets 1,100 likes; knows nothing about Instagram except that "it's the one with the filters". Large-print Kindle romances; downloaded *The Honest Hour* in the first hour.
- **Mrs Pritchard:** Facebook (street group moderator, ruthless), a Kindle with the font size at 40 that she reads in the stairwell, mostly Agatha Christie; watches YouTube for gardening and goat husbandry.
- **Priyanka:** Instagram stories of the hedgehog mugs; has been in four Daniel videos by accident.
- **Giles:** everything, all the time, tragically.

## Meta nods (use lightly)
- A character reading a Kindle novel about a man outperformed by his own twin, and calling it "far-fetched but, you know, very well observed." Pay it off once per book, not more.
- Kindle reviews as in-world satire: three stars, "Too many animals."

## Dating (invented layer)
- **Tinder** is the world's ordinary dating app and stays benign. **Matchwell** is the fictional scoring layer that sits on top of many apps ("your AI interviews their AI"; compatibility out of 100). Sophie vs Daniel: 61. Sophie vs Daniel-A: 94. Sophie refuses to look; Daniel checks (Ch 12).

## CCN (Cloud Coin Network) on Daytube (Lothar's addition, 11 Oct 2026)
- **Daytube** is the invented video platform (YouTube's slightly seedier daytime cousin; the real YouTube stays real and separate). **CCN, the Cloud Coin Network**, is its biggest finance-and-crypto channel (name history, used as a gag: it began as *Critical Coin News*, became *Cloud Coin News*, and became a *Network* "when there was not enough news left to justify the word"; the older logo is still visible on a mug in the studio): rolling coverage of coins, tokens and "market-moving feelings", 24 hours a day.
- **Business model: it is fully maintained by ads, one every couple of minutes.** The news is whatever fits between the ads. A story is two minutes long; the ads are longer. A breaking-news banner is usually an ad for the next breaking-news banner. Presenters (an AI anchor called **Rick Valence** with a jaw like a cliff, and a human co-host who has not been allowed to finish a sentence since 2023) read the ad copy in the same urgent voice as the news, so that the audience cannot tell a market crash from a mattress sale.
- **Use:** CCN covers **Holycoin** with total sincerity ("Holycoin up 12% on news of a miracle"), interviews Colin Jones about the Opfer rates, and hosts Daniel-A for a fireside chat ("sponsored by Lunar Leisure Holdings"). Mrs Pritchard and Jean watch it on the **television** (TV is for old people); the young see it as clips. In Ch 13 CCN runs a segment on Daniel-A that is interrupted nine times.
- **Gags:** a ticker at the bottom reads "HOLYCOIN 1.40 / FAITH 0.92 / MARS TIMESHARES 211 (UNVERIFIED)"; the anchor says "Stay with us" at the exact moment the audience leaves; a "Market Mood" gauge goes from Anxious to Euphoric depending on who is sponsoring the segment.
- Guardrail: CCN, Daytube and Rick Valence are invented. No real broadcaster, real platform or real person is meant; keep every logo, slogan and jingle original.
