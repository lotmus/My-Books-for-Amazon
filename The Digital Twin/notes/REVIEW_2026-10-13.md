## 1. Target reader (8.6)

What works:
- **The voice.** Daniel's deadpan, the specifics (£11.62, 2 h 11 m, 41 times), and the dry understatement hold for 108,000 words.
- **The first three chapters** carry the sale: fruit and a goat; the 51 percent chart and the £11.62 dividend; Sophie and the umbrella with the "I don't know why I said that" tape.
- **Running gags that pay off:** the fruit baskets (first and last), Kevin ("I saw opportunities"), the goat eating paperwork, Clause choosing, the Salieri line ("Damn it. That is exactly what I should have said", reversed in Ch 28), the stile, Priyanka's cup, "Right".
- **Act III payoffs that earn their tears:** Ned in the cellar; "Dan's me"; Jean ("Both of them were mine"); the hat as the whole philosophy (and Peter's badger rhyme); the stile with "HE WROTE."
- **The ending hook** (S2 line, incoming call from Sophie while Sophie is in the room) is clean and not a cheat.

What a reader will feel less:
- Chapters 11 to 16 are episodic (records, Matchwell, the bakery and Daytube, Plus Day, the cat, the church). Each has a laugh and a clue, but the spine slackens for about 18,000 words.
- Sophie is off the page from Ch 25 to Ch 28 and only appears at the trial's end. Her absence is motivated; it still costs warmth.
- Chapters 21 to 24 are four long conversations in kitchens and cellars. They are the book's heart and also its stillest stretch.

## 2. Harsh critic (7.9)

1. **Simile and tic density.** About 91 "a man / a woman who..." similes, 171 "like a..." similes and 75 "I should say / on the record" signposts, across 108,000 words (roughly one of the first two types every 600 words). The voice is the selling point, but in Acts II and III the move "in the manner of a man who has..." is a crutch. A line-edit pass cutting a third of them would lift prose by about half a point.
2. **Tidy resolutions.** Pike withdraws on principle; Sir Rupert is moved to mend a stile; Pym rules from the heart; Giles's sabotage works. For a satire of institutions this is too kind. One sharper loss would help (see "Open items").
3. **Antagonist.** The Programme is a dead man's hand (Menzies), a filing system (Veitch) and a platform (Nib). That suits the theme, but Act III has no face that wants to win. Sir Rupert is the nearest and he folds in one scene.
4. **Exposition.** Calder explains the Programme three times (Ch 20, 21, 32). Ch 32's testimony recaps what the reader knows; it earns its place only for Ned's entrance and the bell.
5. **Convenience.** The whole household gathers in Daniel's flat for Chapters 27, 33 and 35. It is the book's warm centre, but it is used three times.
6. **Legal logic** is deliberately thin: a Deputy Registrar rules against the Crown's agent with a self-aware "I will be overruled". That is acknowledged on the page; it passes as satire.
7. **Sentiment.** Acts II and III end more chapters on tears or a tender line than the voice supports. The jokes cut it within three lines; in some places they arrive late.
8. **Length of the arc.** The mystery (what was done to Daniel) resolves by Ch 21, and the last 14 chapters are consequences. Ch 25 to 29 keep tension by deadline (the Friday rewrite), which works; Ch 30 and 31 are the soft spot.

## 3. Fact-checker / subject expert (8.8)

Checked and fine:
- **AI detail:** LLM adapters, retrieval and "grounding", temperature 0.7, attribution logs, hallucination defined as unsupported confident statement, inference cost, signed capture and provenance. All in line with current practice and used for jokes, not lectures.
- **Genetics:** blastomere separation (embryo splitting) is a real technique; monozygotic twins are not told apart by standard DNA profiling; milk-tooth pulp can yield DNA; "presumed singleton" is the fictional hinge. Plausible for a satire.
- **Places and things:** Royal Berkshire Hospital (Reading), Tilehurst, Elizabeth line to Reading, Pitlochry on the Highland sleeper, Euston, Royal Bolton, Croydon, Slough, Penge, Canary Wharf, Starship catch, DHSS in 1986, the Millennium Dome as a hole in the ground when the PC was built. No real person is named. Real brands (Tesco, Quality Street, Swan Vestas, Weetabix, Kindle, YouTube, Tinder) appear only as everyday scenery.
- **Arithmetic:** body rate £3,490 a day = about £0.121 a second over the 8 included hours, extras at £0.349 a second (about 2.9x); virtual £349 a month with 12 hours a week = about £6.70 an hour, extras £34.90 (about 5x); Ch 10's £7,726.86 = 22,140 s at £0.349. Dates: 14 March 1988 was a Monday; 14 March 2028 is a Tuesday; 22 October 2026 is a Thursday; Sun 20 Dec is the Sunday before Christmas; 11,769 days from 31 Aug 1994 to 20 Nov 2026.

## Open items (what stands between 8.3 and 9)

1. **Line edit for tics** (point 1 above): cut about a third of "man who / manner of / I should say" in Chapters 9 to 35.
2. **One real loss in Act III.** Something the victory cannot fix: for example, Nib's lawyers get Daniel-A's *Honest Hour* royalties frozen for good, or Calder is prosecuted despite the ruling, or Ned's tower is taken into Crown custody for a week with no guarantee. A cost would make the kindness of the ending land harder.
3. **Give Sophie a scene in Ch 26 to 28** (a phone call from Bolton, a text, or Gucci arriving by post) so that her absence is felt as choice and not as subtraction.
4. **Trim Ch 20, 21 and 32 exposition by about 5 percent**; move Calder's three accounts to two.
5. **Smooth Ch 11 to 16** with one thread: a recurring deadline or a short chapter-opening ticker of days left in the Evidence Period.
6. **A Lothar read-through**, then two or three outside readers.
