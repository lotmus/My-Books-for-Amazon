# PART IV — Who Stays

---

## 13. Who Stays

She hits the airlock and thinks, for a second, of the kitchen she left: a real one, with a clock that lied about Andromeda and told the truth about casseroles. Most of the species is still in that kitchen. They did not buy a ticket. They will not buy a ticket. A camp on a dead world is a staffing choice for a few thousand worldlines, maybe tens of thousands if the posters have a lucky decade. It is not a migration. It is a field trip that forgot to end.

So the question people ask — *once we leave, will Earth recover for those who stayed?* — has a rude first word. **We.** Most of us stay.

![Figure 13. A full Earth, bright, large in frame. A tiny mark for a camp. The staff is the disk.](fig13.png)

Hot: most humans will stay on Earth for any near-term leaving. Launch capacity and tickets are not a species-move. The number of people who have ever been in orbit is a village. The number who have been as far as the Moon is a dinner party, last century. The number who will live a season on Mars in the next two decades, if the lucky decade arrives, is a campus, not a nation. The other eight billion — the number moves, the order of magnitude does not — will still be in the casserole kitchen, opening a real oven, arguing about a real clock.

A ticket is not only money, though money is a filter with a mean streak. A ticket is a body that can take the g, a year that can take the radiation, a nation that will let you go, a program that has a seat, a window that is open, a family that can stand the delay. Most bodies, most years, most nations, most families: stay. That is not a moral. It is a headcount.

---

Who is “we,” when the slide says *we must become a multiplanetary species*?

Sometimes it means a company. Sometimes it means a flag. Sometimes it means a species that has been promoted, without a vote, to a passenger list it will never see. The promotion is the rude part. A person in a floodplain, a person in a heat wave, a person whose clinic closed, a person who will never be offered a physical for a stack, is being asked to cheer for a basket that will not hold them. They may still like the photograph. Liking a photograph is allowed. Being drafted into a *we* that leaves is not a measurement.

Mara’s sister, in the real kitchen, uses *we* the way the slide uses it. She means: our kind, our cleverness, our backup. She does not mean: I have a boarding time. Mara has a boarding time that is a window home, if the tin still holds. She does not have a species in her pocket.

Rohan’s mother lives in a city that floods in the ugly years. She is invented. The flood is not a metaphor. She asked him, before he left, whether the camp would make the water recede. He told her the truth, which is Chapter 15’s title, and she nodded the way people nod when the truth is not the comfort they paid for. She stays. She is the recovery question. Not the basil. Not the slide. Her street.

---

A few thousand settlers, even a few tens of thousands, are a town on Earth and a rounding error in a species. They are a large outpost if they exist. They are not a departure of *us*. The recovery of the kitchen — air, ice, plastics, the dead — is a question about the people who still cook in it. Chapters 14 through 16 are that question. This chapter is the refusal to let the question put on a spacesuit and leave the room.

Look first. The world that already has a staff is this one. The staff is not a remnant. The staff is the species.

Count the people who have already left the well, so “we” has to sit still.

A few hundred people have been to orbit. Twelve walked on the Moon, all of them in a short stretch of the last century, all of them home in days. Nobody has walked on Mars. The village that has been above the air is not a nation, and it is not growing into one by a slide. A heavy stack that someday flies often could move the village toward a campus. A campus is still not eight billion. Eight billion is the order of the staff. The number moves. The order does not.

A ticket is also a body. Radiation, bone, fluid, a heart that can take the g, a psyche that can take the monologue. Most bodies will not be offered the physical. Most of those who are offered it will not pass, or will not go, or will go once and come home. Coming home is the sortie. Staying gone, with children who do not need the next ship, is the settlement. We do not have the second. We have a dinner party’s worth of the first, historically, and a warm argument about a larger dinner.

Cadence, so a campus has to show its trucks.

A person in a ship is not a person plus a suitcase. A person for a year away from Earth is food, water, air, a spare pump, a doctor’s kit, a pressure shell, and the propellant that moved the shell. The low-Earth station already teaches the ratio: a handful of people, and tonnes of cargo a year, and a ride home that is a matter of hours if the politics hold. Mars is the same ratio with a worse well and a window that opens every twenty-six months or so. You do not “send a thousand settlers” the way you send a thousand emails. You fly a great many tonnes, most of them not people, and you do it again because the first delivery is a construction site that still eats Earth.

Order of magnitude, so the slide cannot choose a prettier one. A heavy stack that actually flies a hundred tonnes of useful cargo — a claim, warm until the cadence is boring — would need on the order of a hundred such flights to move ten thousand tonnes. Ten thousand tonnes is a modest industrial park, not a nation, and a lot of it will be tanks, steel, and spares, not citizens. Windows, failures, and the propellant you spent to move the propellant will eat the brochure number. A village at the other end, if the lucky decade arrives, is still a village. The staff on the bright disk is still measured in billions.

Tickets filter harder than mass. Radiation over a cruise of months. Bone and fluid in a gravity that is not quite none and not quite home. A heart. A psyche that can take the monologue and the delay. A nation that will spend the seat on you and not on a sensor. A family that can stand a year of light-time measured in minutes, which is not a phone call. Most of that filter says stay, before money has spoken. Money then says stay again.

Rohan’s mother does not fail this filter. She is not a reject. She is the species. The slide that drafts her into a passenger list is using her street as a backdrop for a camp she will not see. She can like the photograph. The recovery of her street is not in the photograph. It is in the furnaces that are still on her side of the well.

## 14. The Clocks of Recovery

Claim, hot: different damages keep different clocks. They do not share a kitchen timer.

![Figure 14. Four pots on a stove, four large labels: air, ice, plastics, extinct. Different flames.](fig14.png)

**Air.** The fastest pot that still pretends to be slow. Carbon dioxide is a well-mixed gas with manners we have measured. A large share of an extra pulse is taken up by oceans and land in decades to a few centuries. A tail stays. The deep ocean and the rocks are ruder; they think in millennia. If you turned every engine off tomorrow, the casserole would not leap back to 1800 by Friday. It would coast. The needle would droop, then linger. A century of burning is not a weekend of weather.

where a pulse of CO₂ is a slug added to the air — and “taken up” means moved into water and wood and soil, not uninvented.

The air also has a faster, ruder guest: methane, which is a stronger blanket per molecule and a shorter career, and aerosols, which are a parasol. The parasol is Chapter 15’s business. Here, keep the pot. Air is the one people mean when they say *recover*. They mean the needle. The needle is real. It is not the only pot.

**Ice.** Ice sheets have millennial manners. A melt you earned in a century does not file a resignation because a launch window opened. Greenland and the Antarctic south are not ice cubes in a glass. They are continents of frozen time. They respond to the heat you already put in the well. They can be committed to a loss that arrives after the slide has been forgotten. Mountain glaciers are faster and still not a weekend. Sea ice is faster still, and its death is not the same sentence as a sheet’s death: one is a white lid on a dark ocean, a mirror; the other is a pile that raises the water when it leaves the land. Different clocks. Different invoices.

**Plastics.** Plastics are not a gas. They are a junk drawer the size of a coast. They do not unmake because the clever apes learned a new hobby. They break into smaller rudenesses. They sit in a gyre, in a bird, in a sediment. You can stop making them. You cannot recall the ones you already threw. A recovery of *production* is a policy. A recovery of *the drawer* is a fantasy with a broom. The broom is worth pushing. The drawer will still be there for a career measured in centuries, and fragments after that.

**Extinct species.** The coldest clock of all: forever, on any clock a person can own. You can grow a forest. You cannot grow a grandmother of a bird you finished. A genome in a freezer is a library, not a grandmother. A library can, in lucky cases, help a cousin. It cannot un-kill a lineage whose last worldline has ended. Chapter 26 says a library is not a seed unless you look first. Here the sentence is ruder: the dead do not file a return because a camp departed.

---

Soils, reefs, rivers, a chemical you banned and a chemical you did not. Each has a pot. A soil you mined for a century is not a weekend of cover crop, though the cover crop is worth planting. A reef that cooked is not a weekend of cooler water, though the cooler water is the only kindness left. Persistence is not a sermon. Persistence is a clock. If you want “Earth recovers,” you must say *which pot* and *on whose calendar*.

Nadia Okonkwo runs a tide gauge and a patience. She is invented. The gauge is not. She has a plot that does not care about a landing year. She would like the air pot to be less rude for the sister on the coast. She does not need a ship to want that. She needs a furnace policy, a farm policy, a forest that is not a press release. The ship is a different book of invoices.

Mara logs the pH of a tray that is not a biosphere. On the delay, someone asks whether the pots at home will reset when the ambitious have gone. She writes the only sentence that is allowed to be hot about clocks: they do not share a kitchen timer, and they do not care about the ship.

A fifth pot, because people will ask: *the water*.

Rivers you straightened, aquifers you drank, a wetland you paved. Some of that is a policy you can reverse in a career: a dam you open, a well you stop mining, a floodplain you stop building on. Some of it is a clock with millennial manners: a drained peat that will not be a peat again because you want it to be. Recovery is not one noun. “The environment” is a drawer of pots. If a slide says Earth will recover when we leave, ask it to pick a pot and a calendar. If it cannot, it is selling a garden.

A sixth, ruder pot: *the heat already in the well*. The ocean has swallowed a share of the extra warmth and will give it back on its own clock. You can stop adding. You cannot un-add Friday. That is why a departure of a camp is not a thermostat even if you pretend the camp *was* the furnaces, which it is not.

Nadia’s gauge does not argue with any of this. It rises or it does not. It has no opinion about a lander.

Numbers, so a pot cannot borrow another pot’s calendar.

Air. A large share of a carbon pulse is still in the atmosphere and the upper ocean for centuries. A stubborn share thinks in thousands of years, because the chemistry that turns carbon dioxide into stone is slow. If the furnaces stopped, the needle would droop on a human career and still not be a preindustrial morning in that same career. “Recover” for air, said honestly, is *less rude than it would have been*, on a clock your grandchildren can partly see. It is not a rewind.

Ice. Melt all of Greenland and you have on the order of seven meters of sea, delivered over centuries to millennia, not on a launch-day. The great Antarctic pile is several times that, and most of it is not poised to fall on Friday; parts of it can still be committed by heat you have already spent. Mountain glaciers answer in decades. Sea ice answers in seasons and changes the mirror of the ocean when it goes. Nadia’s gauge is the sum of these clocks plus the heat already in the water. A camp departing does not reset the sum.

Plastics. A bottle can take decades to centuries to become crumbs, and the crumbs are still the polymer. Stopping the tap is a policy you can do in a career. Emptying the drawer is not. A bird that died of the drawer stays dead. A sediment that archived the crumbs will outlast the press release.

Extinction. One-way on any calendar a ministry owns. A freezer genome is a library card. A library card does not hatch a flock. Chapter 26’s rule — look first, seed later — applies here as a refusal: you do not get the grandmother back because you were ambitious somewhere else.

Soils and reefs, one line each, because the slide will try to fold them into “nature.” A peat you drained and burned is carbon already in the air and a ground that will not be that peat again because you miss it. A reef that bleached past a point is a structure that took centuries and a chemistry you warmed. Cooler water is the kindness. It is not a time machine.

Appendix A14 writes the pulse remaining, the ice-sheet manners, the plastic persistence, and extinction as a one-way mark. Here, keep the four pots — and the two extras if you need them. Different flames.

## 15. Leaving Is Not a Cleanup

Claim, hot: a few thousand settlers do almost nothing to the air. Carbon dioxide does not care that a talented engineer now grows basil under an LED. The thermostat is furnaces, farms, forests, and the habit of turning old sunlight into motion. Remove a village’s worth of passengers and the needle does not bow.

Claim, cold: leaving is a cleanup. It is not. The kitchen you abandoned is still the kitchen. Calling a dead world a spare Earth is a way to stop washing the one you have.

![Figure 15. A needle that does not move when a tiny group steps off a scale. The label: headcount is not a thermostat.](fig15.png)

Do the arithmetic once, so it can sit still.

A person is not a tonne of carbon. A life is a pattern of energy: a house, a plate, a road, a grid, a state that pours concrete. The pattern varies by zip code more than by virtue. A rich-country life can be on the order of ten-and-more tonnes of CO₂ a year; a poorer life, less; a camp life on a dead world, at first, *more*, because rockets and life support and printed spare parts are not a monastery. Take ten thousand settlers. Give them, generously, a rich-country footprint and then some. You have a town’s emissions. Global emissions are measured in tens of billions of tonnes a year. The town is a rounding error that might even have the wrong sign if the camp is a construction site.

where the thermostat is industry and land use, not headcount on a ship.

The slide that says *if we leave, Earth can heal* is doing a swap. It swaps a species-move — which is not on offer — for a camp, and then swaps the camp for a shutdown of the furnaces. The furnaces are not on the ship. The furnaces are in the kitchen that still has the staff.

---

Claim, warm: if *industry* collapsed — not a camp departing, but the furnaces going dark — emissions would fall fast. Aerosols would fall too, the bright soot and sulfate that were pretending to be a parasol. The mix is not a simple spring morning. You can lose the parasol before you lose the heat. The casserole can feel, for a while, ruder. Ecosystems would start a different career, not rewind the tape. Collapse is not a cleanup method. It is a different story with its own dead.

This is the paragraph people skip when they want leaving to be a virtue. A virtue that requires a collapse is not a cleanup. It is a famine with a press kit. The dead in that story are not a greenhouse. They are Rohan’s mother’s street, and Nadia’s gauge, and the sister who still says *we*.

A quieter, hotter fact: you can lower emissions without a collapse and without a camp. That work has a name and a bill and a politics. It is not this book’s manual. This book’s job is to stop the camp from stealing the work’s costume.

---

Earth “bounces back” as a garden once the clever apes go: cold. The apes are not going. The few who leave do not take the furnaces. The species that stayed would still be the thermostat, or the collapse would be the thermostat, and either way the extinct stay extinct, the plastics stay a drawer, the ice keeps millennial manners, the air coasts. A garden is a staffed thing. A staffed thing is a policy, not a departure.

Mara’s sister asked, in a message that took the long way, whether the planet would be nicer when the ambitious had gone. Mara did not vote. She wrote the only sentence that is allowed to be hot: **most of us are still there, and the clocks do not care about the ship.**

Put the rounding error in tonnes, once.

Order of magnitude, not a sermon. Global CO₂ from human industry and land is on the order of forty billion tonnes a year. A rich-country life can be ten to twenty tonnes a year, counting the house and the plate and the road, not only the breath. Ten thousand such lives are a tenth to a fifth of a million tonnes — a town. A town against forty billion is a few parts in a hundred thousand. The needle does not bow. If the camp is still being built, the sign can flip: rockets, steel, and life support can spend more carbon per person than the kitchen they left, for a while. Leaving then makes the air slightly worse and the slide slightly prouder. Pride is not a measurement.

The other pots do not follow the air. A species that is gone stays gone whether or not a thousand engineers grow basil. Plastics already made do not resign because a launch window opened. An ice sheet does not read the passenger list. Chapter 14 keeps those clocks apart. This chapter only refuses the swap that says the passenger list *is* the thermostat.

A city, so the camp cannot hide behind a smaller noun.

A million rich-country lives, at ten to twenty tonnes each, are on the order of ten to twenty million tonnes a year. That is a real city on Earth. Set beside forty billion tonnes, it is a fraction of a percent. A million people living on Mars would be the largest headline in the history of leaving and still a small city on the thermostat. We do not have the million. We have a warm argument about a campus. The campus is smaller than the city that already fails to move the needle by headcount alone. Furnaces, farms, and forests move it. They stay in the kitchen.

The wrong-sign season deserves a second look, because pride likes to skip it. Building the camp spends steel, methane or kerosene, electricity for oxygen, concrete for pads, and flights that throw most of their mass away as the price of the well. Per person, for a while, the leaver can be a larger emitter than the sister who stayed in a small apartment. That is not a reason to call a launch a crime. Chapter 16 keeps that costume off the pad. It is a reason the sentence *we left so Earth could heal* is false in the only decade in which leaving is even a camp. The heal, if it comes, will be a change in the kitchen’s furnaces. The camp is a passenger on that change, or a small debit against it.

Aerosols, once more, because the collapse story steals them. Sulfate and soot from the same furnaces that warm the world also shade it a little. Turn the furnaces off in a crash and the shade drops in weeks to years while the carbon dioxide stays for centuries. The heat can step forward before it steps back. That is a warm caution about *how* a shutdown feels. It is not an argument for keeping the soot. It is not an argument for leaving. It is a warning that a press kit which wants a simple spring morning is not reading the pots.

Mara does not run a furnace. She runs a tray. Her sister runs a kitchen that is downstream of furnaces. The message that asks whether the ambitious will save the kitchen by departing has the staff and the camp swapped. Most of the ambitious, by headcount, are still in the kitchen. They are the thermostat, if anyone is.

Appendix A15 writes the settler-versus-emissions arithmetic and the aerosol unmasking as a warm caution, not as a wish for collapse. Here, keep the needle. Headcount on a ship is not a thermostat.

## 16. The Bill of Going

There is a ruder invoice. The infrastructure of *going* — the energy, the metals, the cement, the propaganda that this planet is already a write-off — can worsen the bill for the people in the casserole kitchen. Rockets are not a carbon offset. A speech about “backup worlds” is not a climate policy. Look first. The world that already has a staff is this one. Seed later is a rule for a dark sea. It is also a rule for a biosphere you have not finished killing.

![Figure 16. A pad, a power line, a mine, a slide that says spare. The caption: the bill of going is paid at home.](fig16.png)

Warm, not a law: spaceflight is not a crime. A rocket is a small line on a global carbon ledger compared with a fleet of coal plants, and a person who sneers at a launch while idling a convoy is running a costume. The honest sentence is smaller and ruder. The *industry of leaving*, if it scales toward the mass a city would take, is energy, tankers, mines, foundries, pads, a political story that the kitchen is already lost. That story is the warm harm even when the rocket’s own plume is a rounding error. People who believe the kitchen is lost do not vote for the broom. They vote for the dome. The dome is cold. The broom is the work.

Metals and cement are the unphotogenic cousins. A camp that wants to be an outpost wants landing pads, power, pressure vessels, solar or a reactor, a lot of dirt moved. If the dirt is lunar or Martian, that is ISRU and a maybe. If the dirt is Earth’s, that is a mine and a kiln and a ship. Early camps will eat Earth. That is not a scandal. It is an invoice. An invoice that is sold as a carbon offset is a scandal.

Propaganda is a temperature because it moves a vote. *Disposable Earth* is a phrase that can be a joke and can be a policy. As a policy it is a write-off of Nadia’s gauge and Rohan’s mother’s street. This book will not pretend a joke is harmless once it has a budget.

---

Honest near term.

A crew around the Moon this decade: warm as a class. A landing attempt in the 2030s: warm. A sustained outpost in the 2030s–2040s: warm-to-cold. A city this decade: cold. Most humans stay: hot. Leaving is not a cleanup: cold. The bill of going can worsen the kitchen: warm. Official dates will slip: hot as a pattern. The orbits will not.

What a finished reader is allowed to keep:

A flyby is not a town. A town is not a cleanup. Most of us stay. The clocks do not share a kitchen timer. A vehicle is not a city. Routine is an unpaid invoice. Three nouns, unfused. Look first on the world that already has a staff.

If you wanted a downtown on Mars by Friday, you should have your money’s worth as hygiene, not as a refund-shaped insult. The basil still has to live. The airlock still sticks. The radio will carry another year. She will not vote for it. She will hit the drawer, and log the pH, and wait on a window that is a fact about two orbits, not a mood.

The universe still does not have an opinion. The dirt does. The dirt at home is the one with a staff. Wash it.

A worked pad, so the rounding error has a face.

A large methane-class launcher burns a few hundred to a few thousand tonnes of propellant on the way up, depending on the stack and the day. Carbon from the methane, and from the grid that made the oxygen and the steel and the concrete, is a number you can write. Write it. Then write a coal plant’s year, or a nation’s year, or a rich city’s year. The pad loses as a villain if the comparison is honest. The pad wins as a villain if the comparison is a sneer at a photograph. Neither comparison is the warm claim. The warm claim is the *industry* if it scales toward a city: many pads, many tanks, many mines, a politics of write-off. Scale is the word. A science program is not that scale. A downtown-as-product is a request for that scale plus a story that the kitchen is already lost.

Look first, then, has a domestic job. Do not let a camp steal the broom. Do not let a broom steal a camp that is honest about being a camp. Both can be staffed. Both have invoices. Only one of them has eight billion worldlines in it.

Nadia’s gauge, Rohan’s mother’s street, Mara’s sister’s oven: those are the staff. The pad is a tool they may choose to pay for. The slide that says *spare* is a tool that spends their vote without asking.

What the bill looks like when you itemize it.

A pad. Concrete, steel, a flame trench, a road, a workforce, a grid hookup. One pad is a factory. A city-in-space story is many pads, because cadence is the product. The carbon of one science flight is a rounding error next to a coal fleet. The carbon of an industry that flies every week, for decades, with the ground equipment to match, is a line item a treasury can see. Still not the whole thermostat. Large enough to count. Large enough that calling it an offset is a lie you can audit.

A mine. Aluminum, steel, copper, the awkward metals in electronics, cement for the civil works. Early camps eat Earth, as this chapter already said. The eating is tonnes, not a vibe. A pressure shell is tonnes. A power system that can hold a lunar night is tonnes. Solar that would run a town is a field, and the field is mined somewhere. If a later decade really does bake bricks out of lunar dirt or Martian air, that decade has paid an ISRU pole. It has not been paid in the decade of the attempt. The attempt’s metal comes from the staffed world.

A story. *This planet is the practice round.* The sentence can be a joke at a bar. At a budget hearing it is a permission slip to skip the broom. Nadia does not need the pad banned. She needs the hearing to notice that her gauge is not a backdrop. Rohan’s mother does not need a lecture against orbit. She needs the flood wall funded in the same decade as the flame trench, because both are invoices and only one of them is being called destiny.

A last split, so a reader can leave with both tools.

Pay for a camp that tells the truth: sortie, then maybe outpost, city not this decade, most of us stay. That camp can be worth the pad. Pay for a broom that tells the truth: furnaces, farms, forests, the four pots on their own clocks. That broom is the staff’s work. Refuse the third product, which uses the camp as a reason to drop the broom, and the broom as a reason to sneer at every honest truck. The third product is the slide that says *spare*. It spends a vote it did not earn.

Appendix A16 writes rockets on a carbon ledger, mines, and why a write-off is a vote. Here, keep the pad and the slide that says *spare*. The bill is paid at home.

A later book, if you want it, will keep her hands and refuse helium. You do not need it. You can stop here. The seal will still need the heel of a glove tomorrow.
