## Sell copy

A capsule arrives on the same delay as the orbiter’s voice. The label is optimistic. Her hands are not.

*A Longer Life Is Not a New Body* is a popular book about healthspan, the brain you already use, and why ten thousand years is not a straight line. Lifespan is not healthspan. Aging is many clocks, not one fuse. A gene fix is a local repair — this instruction, this tissue, this invoice — not a fountain. The sentence that we only use ten percent of our brain is false. You already use the organ, plus sleep, practice, tools, and other people. The carrot that makes a body chase a cue has no off-switch. A long life does not install one.

The last fifty years of real, uneven progress are stairs with walls, not a ruler you can lay across the year 12,000. A fast trip is the one real door to that year, and it is a skip forward, not a longer life: the body's own clock never speeds up. Progress is more often an S-curve than a ray. The limit is often a meeting, a ledger, a famine, a front. A file is not a worldline. Forever-mind stays cold.

Every claim wears a temperature. Hot means measured. Warm means a working account with gaps. Cold means a wish sold as a product. String theory, if named, cannot currently be tested. You do not need a medical degree. You need curiosity and a willingness to let a miracle come apart into pots.

This is Volume 3 of *Look First*. It is a complete book. You can stop here.
