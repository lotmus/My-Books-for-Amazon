# Book 2 Outline — *A Permit Is Not a City*

**Subtitle:** *Moon, Mars, and Why Leaving Does Not Clean the Kitchen*

**Promise:** Real programs, real clocks, and the planet you do not get to save by boarding a ship.

**Stands alone.** A reader who never opened Book 1 still gets temperatures, the kitchen, and the woman on a dead world. One courtesy sentence may say that a longer book already retired a cosmic “now” and treated elsewhere as a *permit*. They do not need that book.

**Queued questions that live here**

- Moon and Mars settlements planned — how soon? (Ch. 4–12)
- Once people leave Earth, will the environment recover for those who stayed? (Ch. 13–16)

**Do not steal from Book 1:** no global now as a thesis; no CMB courtroom; no wormhole bill; no second-origin zoo. Steal only the woman, the temperatures, and look-first.

**Date hygiene:** this outline is written from a 2026 kitchen. Official years will move. Write *kinds of clocks*. A slip is engineering plus politics. It is not destiny and it is not, by itself, fraud.

Target: 16 chapters, Barrow-short per idea. Sample pages for Earth recovery and Moon/Mars how-soon sit beside this file.

## Where the queued questions sit

**How soon — Moon and Mars (Ch. 4–12, sample page `05_Sample_Moon_Mars_How_Soon.md`)**

Write three nouns on the cover of the chapter, then keep them unfused: *sortie*, *outpost*, *settlement*. ISRU is a kitchen you have not built. A reusable heavy stack is a maybe-truck. A city is a maybe-civilization.

**Earth recovery if people leave (Ch. 13–16, sample page `04_Sample_Earth_Recovery.md`)**

Most people stay. Departure of a camp is not a thermostat. Extinction does not un-extinct. Plastics do not file a resignation. Ice sheets have millennial manners. The industry of *going* can raise the bill for those who stay.

## Temperature-tagged claims

### Settlement timelines

| Claim | Temp | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Governments and firms are spending real money on lunar crew systems, landers, and (on paper) surface camps | **Hot** | Programs exist. Hardware exists at various maturities. This is not a rumor. |
| Uncrewed lunar landings, orbiters, and sample returns have already happened in this century | **Hot** | Flags and robots are not settlements. |
| Official crew dates slip; slips are engineering plus politics | **Hot** as a *pattern* | Do not treat a slipped year as destiny or as proof of a con. |
| A crew around the Moon this decade (Artemis II-class) | **Warm** | The stack is the fact; the exact year is a mood. Write it as a *class* of mission. |
| A crewed lunar *landing attempt* in the 2030s (Artemis III-class + HLS) | **Warm** | Owes a lander that can leave, suits, abort modes. “Attempt” is the honest noun. |
| A sustained lunar *outpost* (weeks to months, cargo from Earth) in the 2030s–2040s | **Warm-to-cold** | Architecture fights (Gateway vs surface-first; national ledgers) can move this a decade. |
| China’s ILRS as a 2030s multinational camp, after real precursor landers | **Warm** as *intent and precursors*; **cold** as a city | Chang’e-class work is hot. A research station is still a camp. |
| Starship-class vehicles changing the *mass* you can throw if they become routine | **Warm** | Routine is the unpaid invoice: refueling, reliability, cadence. |
| Uncrewed Starship on Mars *this decade* because a founder said the year | **Cold** as a schedule | The vehicle is real and unfinished. Mars EDL, life support, and ISRU are not posters you can cash. |
| Crewed Mars *landing* in the 2030s | **Warm-to-cold** | Allowed by hopium and by a lucky engineering decade. Not selected by present demonstrated pieces. |
| A *city* on the Moon or Mars (children, farms, no cargo cult) by 2040, or “this decade” | **Cold** | Crew sorties ≠ settlement. Do not promise cities. |

### Earth recovery if humans leave

| Claim | Temp | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Most humans will stay on Earth for any near-term leaving | **Hot** | Launch capacity and tickets are not a species-move. |
| A departure of thousands of settlers does almost nothing to global CO₂ or heat | **Hot** | The thermostat is industry and land, not headcount on a ship. |
| Different damages have different clocks | **Hot** | Airborne CO₂: decades to centuries for a large share, longer tails; ice sheets: centuries–millennia; extinctions: forever on human clocks; plastics: persistent junk. |
| If *industry* collapsed, emissions would fall fast; aerosols would also fall; the mix is not a simple spring morning | **Warm** | Cooling from lost soot/sulfate can unmask warming; recovery of ecosystems is not a reset. |
| Leaving is a cleanup | **Cold** | False as a policy. The kitchen you abandoned is still the kitchen. |
| The infrastructure of leaving (energy, metals, launches, propaganda that Earth is disposable) can worsen the bill for those who stay | **Warm** | Not a law that spaceflight is a crime. A reminder that rockets are not a carbon offset. |
| Earth “bounces back” as a garden once the clever apes go | **Cold** | Extinct species do not file a return. Soils, reefs, and ice remember. |

## What this book is allowed to sell on Amazon

A finished reader who can say: *a flyby is not a town; a town is not a cleanup; most of us stay; the clocks do not share a kitchen timer.*

A reader who wanted a downtown on Mars by Friday should get their money’s worth as hygiene, not as a refund-shaped insult. The basil still has to live.
