# Sample — Moon and Mars, how soon

Book 2 voice page. ~700 words. Written from a 2026 kitchen. Years will move. Temperatures should not.

---

The radio is late, as always. On the good weeks it is four minutes. On the rude weeks it is twenty. Someone on Earth is still selling a year as if a year were a landing gear.

A program is not a poster. That is the first temperature.

Hot: people and firms are spending real money to put crews around the Moon, to land on it, to throw a heavy stack that might, if it learns manners, throw more mass than the old horses. Uncrewed landers have already kissed dust in this century. Sample capsules have already come home. China has picked up far-side rock and talked, with partners, about a camp they name like a station. The American ledger has a name too, and a habit of printing a date that later needs a new page. The hardware is not a rumor. The downtown is.

Hot as a *pattern*: dates slip. Heat shields, suits, refueling, politics, a weld, a budget. A slip is not destiny. It is not, by itself, a con. It is an invoice the slide did not want to show.

Warm: a crew around the Moon this decade — a flyby, a cabin, a look at a dead close world that is three days away, not three seasons. Call it an Artemis-II-class morning. The exact month is a mood. The class is the fact: flesh, cis-lunar, home in a week if the stack behaves. That is a *sortie*. It is not a town.

Warm: a landing *attempt* in the 2030s — a cabin that goes down and is supposed to come up, a lander that still owes a demonstration of the ugly parts: refuel in orbit, survive the last kilometer, light the engines in a gravity well with no atmosphere to help you regret. HLS, Starship-class, whatever logo wins the week. “Attempt” is the honest noun. A flag is not a greenhouse. A greenhouse is not a school district.

Warm-to-cold: a winter-over. Months. Cargo from Earth. A Gateway or a surface tin that changes floor plans because architecture is a fight, not a law. China’s ILRS, on paper, is this kind of camp in the 2030s after real precursor landers. Intent is warm. A city is still cold.

Now Mars, which is where the posters go to breed.

A ship that is large and, one day, cheap per tonne is a *vehicle*. If Starship-class flight becomes routine — cadence, refuel, a hull that does not make the news every time it lights — the mass you can throw changes. That is warm. Routine is the unpaid invoice.

Mars engineering is ruder than Mars rhetoric. You must hit a thin air at speed and live. You must make water and oxygen from ice and a six-millibar excuse for an atmosphere, or you must keep importing the kitchen. You must sit through a twenty-minute argument with Earth and not treat it as a conversation. You must wait on a launch window that is a fact about two orbits, about twenty-six months, not a mood. Book 1 already wrote the Δv. This book writes the Gantt chart’s funeral.

Cold: a city on Mars this decade. Cold: children who do not need a cargo cult. Cold: “settlement” as something you can buy by Friday because a founder named a year. Uncrewed hardware *might* be thrown in a lucky window. That is still not a town. Crewed Mars in the 2030s is warm-to-cold — allowed by a kind decade, not selected by the pieces we have already photographed working.

Three nouns, keep them unfused.

**Sortie** — you go, you work, you come home, or you die on a schedule Earth still owns.  
**Outpost** — you stay a season with a umbilical.  
**Settlement** — the umbilical can fail and the babies still eat.

ISRU is the kitchen word for the middle noun trying to become the third: melt the ice, crack the CO₂, print a spare. Papers and prototypes exist. A city does not.

She hits the airlock. The basil does not care which logo paid for the LED. She is the honest picture of how soon: not never, not downtown, a stuck drawer on a dead world, waiting on a window. The universe still does not have an opinion. The dirt does.
