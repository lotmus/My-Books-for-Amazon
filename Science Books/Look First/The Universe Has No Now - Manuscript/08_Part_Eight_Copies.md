## 37. Four Kinds of Elsewhere

“Multiple realities” is four claims in a trench coat. If you let them fuse, you can prove anything and have explained nothing. Keep them in separate rooms.

The coat is a kitchen habit. Someone hears “other worlds,” pictures a door, and starts borrowing furniture from four different houses. A repeat of this galaxy, far away, gets mixed with a bubble that froze under different constants, which gets mixed with a quantum branch that never shared a place, which gets mixed with a mathematical structure no one could inhabit because inhabiting is not what a theorem does. The mix will sell a ticket. The mix is not a theory. It is four drawers emptied onto one bench.

Open the drawers one at a time. A useful test, before the labels: ask what question the claim is answering. More space answers *how big is the dough.* Other bubbles answer *could the leftover constants have been otherwise.* Other branches answer *what the electron’s equation does with leftovers.* Other mathematics answers *what existence even is.* Those are four questions. A coat that answers all four with “other worlds” has stopped asking.

**More of this universe.** Same laws, same constants, more space. If the dough is infinite, or merely brutally large, and the ingredients are sprinkled in a statistically ordinary way, then a finite pattern — a galaxy like this, a kitchen like this, a woman hitting an airlock — repeats. Not because anyone copied it. Because there are only so many ways to arrange a closet of atoms, and an infinite warehouse will reuse the arrangements. These copies are *elsewhere* in the ordinary, spacelike sense. You cannot visit. Light will not finish the trip.

This is the cheap copy. Chapter 38 is its room. The physics, if you grant the extras, is the physics we already use: an expanding FLRW loaf, a Hubble volume as a tile, a finite list of ways to stock the tile. The copies sit on the same map as Andromeda, only ruder. They are not behind a curtain. They are behind a distance. Distance, in an accelerating cosmos, is sometimes a forever.

**Other bubbles, other rules.** If inflation never quite clocks out, and if the way it clocks out has many valleys, then other hot bangs can freeze with different effective constants, different particle menus, different numbers of large directions. This is the expensive copy machine. It is a live hypothesis stacked on a working theory stacked, often, on an untestable framework.

This is Chapter 39’s room. The burst of Chapter 13 is the working theory. Eternal inflation is the burst that may not retire. The menu of valleys is the maybe. A landscape inherited from string theory is a catalog sitting on the maybe. **String theory cannot currently be tested.** Do not let the catalog put on the cheap copy’s coat and walk around as if it had been counted in the leftover glow.

**Other branches.** The electron’s equation does not pick a single outcome. One reading says the leftovers stay: both ways persist, decohered, non-interfering. These copies are not other places. They are other terms. You are one term.

This is Chapter 40’s room, and Chapter 6 already put a chair in it. Everett’s habit is a reading of the same rule that builds chemistry. It is not extra cosmology. It does not hand you a hallway to a kinder marriage. The copies, if they persist, persist elsehow, not elsewhere. Confusing elsehow with elsewhere is how the trench coat gets buttoned.

**Other mathematics.** If existence is cheap and consistency is enough, then every coherent structure is a universe. This is a metaphysical bet. It is not physics. Barrow would have smiled and called it an idea for ideas.

This drawer is the one that should stay closed in a book that has a leftover glow to be loyal to. A consistent set of axioms is not a sky. It does not have a helium fraction. It does not have a woman, except as a symbol in a formal system that has decided to talk about women. You may find the bet beautiful. You may even find it thrifty: why should existence be stingy once consistency is satisfied? Beauty and thrift are not a temperature. They are a taste. This book will not run an experiment on a theorem and call the theorem a neighbor.

Barrow’s habit is the right one here. Walk up to the idea. Call it an idea for ideas. Do not let it complete the other three drawers by metaphysics. A far tile, if it exists, still has to obey our leftover helium. A bubble, if it exists, still has to be a solution of something. A branch, if it persists, still has to be a term in the equation we already use. Other mathematics owes none of that. That is why it feels like a completion and why it is not physics.

![Figure 37. Four large boxes in a 2-by-2. Labels: more space / other bubbles / other branches / other math.](Figures/figs/fig37.png)

Keep the 2-by-2 on the wall. The rows are not a ranking of how much you should want them. They are a ranking of how easily they fuse in a conversation. More-space copies share our laws; people treat them as travel. Bubble copies share a family of laws and then don’t; people treat them as destinations with different weather. Branch copies share a past up to a fork and then don’t share a place at all; people treat them as hops. Mathematical copies share nothing that physics can cash. People treat them as a completion of the other three. The completion is the coat.

What would a ticket even be?

To a more-space copy: a path through this loaf, same constants, long enough to reach a matching tile. Chapter 26 already spent that path. The event horizon ate it. You can name the copy the way you name a galaxy in a deep field. You cannot arrive.

To a bubble: a path into a different freeze — different fine-structure constant, different shove, maybe a different number of large directions. The wall between pockets is not a customs desk. In the usual pictures it is still-bursting dough. A crew that does not sleep cannot clock out of our chemistry and clock into another.

To a branch: a hop between terms of a sum. Chapter 40 will refuse the hop by name. There is no door in the sky labeled tails. There is a silence after decoherence.

To a theorem: boots on a page of axioms. A category error that has learned to walk. You do not visit consistency. You write it down.

Four refusals. One coat. Put the coat down before you start proving that the basil both died and was saved, that Λ is both a miracle and a selection, that a far kitchen both is and is not a destination. Those sentences can be made separately. They cannot be made as one noun.

The greenhouse workbench has four drawers. One holds spare seals, same kit, more of it, the way a longer shelf holds more jars of the same dirt. One holds a manual for a kitchen that never shared a wall: different voltage, different water, a basil that would not be basil. One holds two copies of today’s log, both true in their own terms, only one of which she can write with this pen. One holds a sheet of blank axioms, elegant, uninhabited. A visitor dumps all four into a coat and says, *other worlds.* She should put the coat back. The seals will not fix the axioms. The second log will not water the plants. The manual for the other kitchen will not open the airlock. The blank sheet will not grow potatoes.

She can feel the temptation in the wrists. The airlock sticks. The radio is late. A far drawer with more of the same dirt sounds like help. A drawer with different laws sounds like a miracle weather. A drawer with another log sounds like a second try. A drawer of axioms sounds like a reason the first three must exist. Help, miracle, second try, reason: four hungers. The coat is those hungers sewn together. Physics is allowed to be unkind to hungers. It is not allowed to be sloppy about drawers.

Dark matter is not a fifth room. Dark energy is not a sixth. They are ingredients *in this room*. Chapter 17’s extra pull is a mass budget in the leftover glow, in the Bullet Cluster, in the way galaxies hold together. Chapter 18’s shove is a term in the stretch, a faintness in empty space, a plot twist in *H(t)*. Neither is a copy of the kitchen. Neither is a bubble with other rules, except in the cold sense that some other valley might have a different shove — and that cold sentence belongs in drawer two, labeled as such, not smuggled into drawer one as if the missing mass were a missing universe. A particle we have not yet named is still a particle *here*. A vacuum energy we have not yet explained is still a vacuum energy *here*. Missing nouns are not extra loaves.

A tidal sea under ice is not another reality. It is a dark room in this one. Europa’s ocean, Enceladus’s spray, a maybe-Hycean under a hydrogen blanket: those are permits and dark pantries in the same expanding dough, the same constants, the same leftover helium. You could, in principle, send a melt-probe. You cannot, in principle, send a melt-probe to a branch, or to a bubble with a different fine-structure constant, or to a theorem. The sea is elsewhere in the ordinary sense that a basement is elsewhere. It is not a fifth kind of copy. People reach for “another world” because the ice is a lid and the Sun is a rumor. Keep the nouns sober. A lid is a lid. A copy is a claim about inventory.

The confusion is honest in the mouth and expensive in the argument. A basement you have not entered *feels* like a second house. It is still plumbed to the first. The same gravity. The same water, if water is there. The same table of nuclei. Chapter 29’s dark seas are the largest nearby lesson in this: strangeness is not a new loaf. Strangeness is a room with the lights off. If the melt-probe tastes a second origin, that origin is still *this* universe’s chemistry, this helium, this leftover glow as a ceiling the sea never saw. If it tastes a sterile bath, the sterility is also this universe’s. Neither result opens drawer two, three, or four. Both results are dirt, delay, and a fiber. That is the point of keeping the nouns sober. You can act on a basement. You cannot act on a fused coat.

Why the sobriety matters: fuse the rooms and you can “explain” anything. The basil died? A branch where it lived. The shove is small? A bubble that passed a filter. A pattern repeats? Infinite space. A pattern looks arranged? Other mathematics in which arrangement is cheap. Each of those sentences, in its own drawer, can be a responsible maybe. Stacked, they are an oil that lets a sermon slide. This part will not stack them.

Keep the nouns sober. The rest of this part takes the first three claims in order and refuses, each time, to sell you a ticket. A popular filing of the four drawers calls them levels: more volume, other vacua, other branches, other mathematics. The filing is a set of labels, not a detection. Use it if it keeps the coat unbuttoned. Do not use it as a poster of doors.

**Hot:** we have one leftover glow, one set of constants, one table of nuclei, and a sky larger than the patch we have seen. Dark matter pulls in this sky. A tidal sea, if it is wet, is wet in this sky. **Warm:** inflation, dark ingredients, and a quantum rule that does not pick a single outcome. **Cold:** the four drawers, each in its own way. The coat is colder still. It is not a fifth claim. It is a way of not having a claim. If a later chapter seems to be wearing two drawers at once, that is a mistake in the reading, not a new kind of elsewhere. Send it back to the 2-by-2.

## 38. More of This Universe

Assume, for a page, that space goes on, same laws, no edge, matter doing statistically ordinary things. Then the Hubble volume — the room we can, in principle, have seen — is one tile. Beyond it, more tiles. The number of ways to arrange the quantum states in a tile is enormous, but finite if you take the holographic bound seriously. In an infinite warehouse of tiles, every allowed arrangement happens, and happens again.

That is the cheap copy in one breath. Now unpack it, because the breath is doing a lot of work, and not all of the work is the same temperature.

An FLRW universe, the expanding dough of Chapter 7, can be infinite in space. The spatial slices — the “nows” cosmology hires from the leftover glow — can be the three-dimensional analog of an endless plane: same rules, no wall. Galaxies, on large enough averages, sprinkled the way Chapter 14’s twitches and Chapter 16’s web sprinkle them. No special place. No edge to walk off. That geometry is allowed. It is not required. Finite spaces with no edge are allowed too: a 3-sphere, a 3-torus, other closed shapes that look, locally, like the endless plane if they are large enough. The leftover glow does not hand you the global topology. It hands you a patch.

The Hubble volume is the patch. Not exactly the particle horizon, not exactly the event horizon: a room of order the Hubble length, the distance light could cover in a Hubble time, the tile people use when they want a fair comparison between “what we can see” and “what a repeat would have to match.” The particle horizon is the edge of what we can already have seen, set by the age of the stretch. The event horizon is the edge of what we can still reach with a future letter. The Hubble tile is a fair-comparison box sitting between those rudenesses. Inside one tile: a leftover glow, a web of galaxies, a greenhouse, a childhood kitchen, this sentence. The tile is finite. The question is how many tiles the loaf contains.

We already know the answer is more than one. The leftover glow is a picture of a last-scattering surface larger than the Hubble length of that era, stretched. Inflation, if you buy it, stretched a still smaller patch into something that makes our whole sky look local. “Much larger than we see” is not a mood. It is the working theory’s everyday implication, and it is why the cheap-copy argument even gets a hearing. The hearing is not a verdict. Larger is not infinite. A warehouse with a thousand rooms is already larger than the room you are standing in. It is not an infinite warehouse.

If the warehouse is infinite, and if the sprinkle is ergodic — statistically the same, no conspiracy that makes every tile a unique snowflake forever — then a finite list of arrangements, drawn infinitely often, must repeat. Not as a project. As a tautology of large numbers. A deck of cards, shuffled without end, will eventually deal a hand you have already seen. An infinite deck of Hubble volumes will eventually deal a volume that matches this one to whatever precision you asked for, then deal it again.

There is a version of you who did not open this book. There is a version of the greenhouse where the basil died in month four. There is a version, much farther, where a dead world was never visited. These are not hops. They are the cheap copies: same physics, different draws, out of reach because the particle horizon and the event horizon are rude.

The “you” in that paragraph is a pattern of atoms, or a pattern of quantum states, occupying a region. It is not a soul that jumped. It is a repeat, the way two identical copies of a recipe are two copies. The woman who did not open the book is not your understudy, waiting in the wings. She is a later or farther stretch of dough that happens, if the extras are granted, to match a stretch you occupy. She does not get your radio. You do not get her afternoon.

![Figure 38. A grid of identical rooms receding. A picture of a possibility, not a photograph of copies.](Figures/figs/fig38.png)

How finite is a tile’s menu?

If you treat a Hubble volume as an ordinary box of particles, the number of arrangements is already a Barrow shock: more than you can say out loud, less than infinity. Count the ways to place a roomful of atoms and you get a number that looks like a lie. Count the quantum states more carefully and the lie gets worse. If you take holography seriously — Chapter 23’s stubborn skin, the inventory of a region written on its boundary — the menu becomes finite in a cleaner way. A region’s distinguishable states cannot exceed what the area of its bounding surface can hold, at about one bit per few square Planck lengths. A de Sitter-like tile, bounded in practice by an event horizon, has a finite area of order 10¹²² Planck patches, and therefore a finite library. The number of possible books in that library is two to that power: a stack of exponents you cannot write as an ordinary integer. Enormous. Finite. Infinity is not hiding in the closet of one tile. It would have to hide in the number of tiles.

That finiteness is doing the cheap copy’s real work. Infinite space plus an infinite menu need not repeat; you could have a new snowflake forever. Infinite space plus a finite menu must, if the draws are independent enough, reuse the books. The holographic bound is the cleanest reason the menu is finite. It is warm-to-cold as a statement about the whole sky — our space is not the negatively curved special case where holography is a theorem — and it is stubborn as a hint. Even without holography, a finite energy in a finite box, with a quantum grain, is a finite list. The cheap copy is, at bottom, that list plus a warehouse that never stops.

Ergodic is the other extra. It means the sprinkle does not, at some scale, freeze into a crystal of unique rooms, or stop being defined, or start repeating for a boring geometric reason that is not “infinity plus a finite menu.” Inflation, if you buy it, helps: it stretches a small, mixed patch into a large, mixed patch, and the twitches are statistically ordinary. Help is not a proof that the whole spatial slice is an ergodic infinite plane. Help is a reason the cheap-copy argument is not crazy. Crazy would be assuming a unique snowflake at every distance for no reason. The cheap copy assumes the opposite, which is also an assumption.

Is space infinite? We do not know. We know it is larger than the tile, and flat to a boredom that inflation explains as a feature. Infinite is a cold extra. “Much larger than we see” is warm. The cheap copies require the stronger extra, plus a faith that the statistical sprinkle does not, at some scale, start repeating for a boring reason or stop being defined.

Flatness is the measurement people try to promote.

Planck’s leftover-glow maps find a curvature parameter, Ω_k, consistent with zero. Parallel lines, on the largest scales we photograph, stay parallel to a boredom. Inflation predicted that boredom: stretch a curved surface enough and the ant sees a plane. **Ω_k ≈ 0 does not prove the slice is infinite.** A 3-sphere with a radius far larger than the Hubble length looks flat in the patch. A 3-torus, finite and edgeless, looks like infinite FLRW until you have gone all the way around, and “all the way around” may be farther than light has traveled since the glow. Topology is sneaky. The data say: not a small, curvy room. They do not say: warehouse without end. Treating “flat enough” as “infinite” is a kitchen leap. The leap is popular. It is still a leap.

There is a search that tried to catch the sneak. If the last-scattering surface wrapped around a small enough 3-torus, the leftover glow would show matching circles: the same pattern, twice, on the microwave sky, because you would be seeing the same patch of fog in two directions. People looked. They have not found the circles. That is a warm push: whatever the global shape is, it is not a snug little room we have already walked around by looking. It is not a proof of infinity. It is a proof that “finite but huge” still has a lot of room to hide. Appendix A38 writes the curvature and the repeat. Here: **flat is not infinite. Flat is a local manners.**

So the temperatures, named:

**Hot:** the universe is larger than the part we have seen; it is close to flat on that part; galaxies recede; the glow is a baby picture of a mixed fog. **Warm:** inflation made the flatness a feature, and the region beyond the tile is more of the same dough, same laws, for a long way. **Cold:** the dough is actually infinite, the sprinkle is actually ergodic, the holographic menu is actually the right count, and therefore a copy of this kitchen sits in some far tile, then sits again. The cheap copies are a fact only if those cold extras are facts. They are a picture of a possibility. Figure 38 is honest about that. It is not a photograph of copies.

One popular inventory of this room calls it Level I: more of the same FLRW volume, repeats by distance. The name is a filing label, not a detection. The label is useful if it keeps this drawer from fusing with bubbles, branches, and theorems. It is harmful if it puts a grid of rooms on a poster and lets a reader think the grid has been counted in the microwave sky. It has not.

The distances, if you grant the extras, are a Barrow shock of a different kind. A repeat of a planet-sized pattern is already unspeakably far. A repeat of a whole Hubble volume is farther in a way that does not fit in a throat. These are not destinations. Chapter 26 already spent the event horizon: most of the sky has left. The cheap copies, if they exist, live in a region that will not take a letter, let alone a ship. Light will not finish the trip. The particle horizon is the past’s rudeness. The event horizon is the future’s. Together they make “elsewhere in this universe” into a museum, not a road.

Even if the copies exist, they do no work for the woman in the airlock. Her potatoes are not saved by a tile where potatoes lived. Grief is local. So is a project. The cheap copies are a fact, if they are a fact, about the warehouse. They are not a consolation and not a map.

Say the potatoes out loud, because consolation is the usual hire.

She logs a blight. Starch fails. The window home is still months of orbit away. Somewhere, if the warehouse is infinite and the menu is finite, a greenhouse that matches hers to a ridiculous number of decimal places has a tray that lived. That tray is not her tray. It does not share her worldline. It does not share her Tuesday except as a matching pattern, which is a compliment the loaf does not cash. She cannot eat a repeat. She cannot be consoled by a statistical tautology. The dirt in front of her either makes starch or it does not. The copy, if any, is a fact about far crumbs. Far crumbs do not water this pot.

The far greenhouse does not even share a *when* she could name without cheating. Chapter 1 already retired a cosmic now. A matching tile is spacelike-separated from her blight. It is Andromeda’s navy without a navy: real, if the extras are granted, and unavailable as a cause or an effect of this log entry. Consolation that cannot enter the past cone is not consolation. It is a poster about large numbers. Barrow liked to shock readers with billions. The cheap copy’s billions are so large they stop being a shock and become a shrug. A shrug does not save potatoes.

A project is the same refusal with a better mood. You do not grow basil because a far tile might. You grow it because this thread is the one you can work along. The cheap copies, even at their most generous, are not colleagues. They are recurrences. Recurrence is not company. It is a statement that a finite story, told often enough, is told again.

Keep the drawer shut against hops. Same laws, more space, maybe repeats: that is all this chapter owns. The next chapter will try to change the laws. That is a different coat, and it is more expensive.

## 39. Other Rooms, Other Rules

The expensive copies need a burst that does not know how to retire, and a menu of retirements.

Eternal inflation, Chapter 15, is the burst that may not retire. The menu is the maybe: different valleys, different leftover constants, different low-energy laws. In one valley the cosmological constant is a monster and galaxies never form. In one it is a little shove, and someone hits an airlock. In one there are two large dimensions and no kitchens. Weinberg’s filter needs this menu, or something like it, to turn “Λ is small” from a miracle into a selection.

Do not rerun Chapter 15 as a whole. Keep the one extra this part needs: the local burst is a working theory; the froth of other bursts is a rumor the local burst can tell if you let it drink.

In many slow-roll stories, the field that stretched the patch reaches the bottom of its hill in some places and keeps rolling, or keeps jittering, in others. The places that stop become hot bangs — pocket universes, bubbles, a noun you can hate and still need. The places that keep going keep stretching. Our leftover glow would be the inside of one stopped place. Outside, if the rumor is right, the bursting is still hiring. Different stops can freeze different effective physics if the underlying theory has more than one valley to fall into. That is the expensive machine: not more of the same tile, but other tiles whose constants were never ours.

From inside a stopped place the sky looks ordinary. A leftover glow. A stretch. Helium. Galaxies. You do not see the bursting next door. You see a patch that reheated, mixed, and froze into the kitchen you have. That is why eternal inflation is easy to oversell and hard to kill. It does not predict a new smudge in tonight’s field. It predicts that our smudge is one of many, and that the many may not share our leftover constants. The local burst of Chapter 13 can be right while the froth is a rumor. Keep the temperatures. Local inflation is warm. Eternal hiring is a live maybe. Other laws in other pockets are the maybe stacked on the maybe.

Where does the menu come from? Often, from string theory’s landscape: a reported 10⁵⁰⁰ ways to compactify extra directions and freeze a vacuum. **String theory cannot currently be tested.** A number like 10⁵⁰⁰, offered without a risky prediction that would kill the framework, is a catalog, not a census. Other frameworks offer other menus. Some offer one valley. The sky we have does not yet choose.

The number is a celebrity, so treat it like one. Extra directions, curled small. Fluxes, wrapping those directions, choosing a vacuum energy and a set of leftover constants the way a combination lock chooses a number. Count the combinations people know how to write and you get a shock with a lot of zeros. Ten to the five hundred is not a census of kitchens. It is a count of locks. Some locks may not open onto a cosmos that lasts. Some may be the same kitchen counted twice. Some may be artifacts of an approximation. The advertised number moves when the approximations move. That is allowed in a research program. It is not allowed in a result.

The shock is not a measurement. It is a count of sentences in a framework that has not yet named a signature we could miss. Compactify differently, freeze differently, and you can accommodate, after the fact, almost any low-energy kitchen. A framework that can accommodate almost any kitchen is not, so far, a theory of this kitchen. Equation (18), if you open the appendix, is a reminder of what a test would have to do: a risky number, a signature, a way for the whole idea to die. It has not been done. This book’s standing rule does not get a holiday because the number is large. **String theory cannot currently be tested.** Repeating the sentence is the point. Celebrity numbers try to hypnotize. A temperature label wakes you up.

Other menus exist on paper. Loop-ish gravities, extra-field cosmologies, a single vacuum that just happens to be ours. Some of those are also untested. Some are more modest. Modesty is not a proof. The leftover glow, the helium, the shove’s size: those are the court. The catalog is a witness who has not agreed to be cross-examined.

A modest menu can still be an ensemble. One valley is not. Two valleys begin to be, if you have a reason both are occupied. 10⁵⁰⁰ valleys are an ensemble only if they are more than a list of sentences. Until the framework risks a death, the list is a maybe about a maybe. Chapter 15 already said permission is not a postcard. This chapter says a catalog is not a census. The repetition is deliberate. Celebrity numbers come back if you do not keep saying no.

![Figure 39. Three bubbles, three different simple icons inside. Unphotographed.](Figures/figs/fig39.png)

The measure problem sits on the catalog like a cat. Infinite bubbles, infinite observers, no agreed count: “typical” becomes a fight. Until the fight ends, “the landscape predicts observers like us” is a hope. It is a better hope than a required Omega Point, because it does not contradict the shove. It is still a hope.

The cat deserves a paragraph of its own, because it is the reason the expensive copies cannot yet do the job people hire them for.

If bubble begets bubble without end, the word *most* breaks. Most by what? Volume? Then the still-inflating regions, which stretch without limit, drown the stopped ones, or the stopped ones that stopped later drown the ones that stopped earlier, depending on how you cut. Count by bubbles? Then you need a cutoff, a regulation, a rule that says when two infinities have been made finite enough to compare. Count by observers? Then you need to know what an observer is, and whether a bubble that makes a brief, dense flash of many brains outranks a bubble that makes a few, slowly, in galaxies. The youngness paradox is one of the rude children of a bad measure: some cuttings predict that typical observers live almost at the beginning of their bubble, which we do not. Other cuttings predict other rudenesses. No cutting is the one everyone else has to accept. Until one is, a sentence that begins “the landscape predicts” is a sentence that has not finished its homework.

The kitchen analog is an infinite pantry with no agreement about jars. You want to say “typical jam is strawberry.” You cannot, until you say how you count jars that never stop arriving. Count by shelf-meters and the newest, emptiest shelves win. Count by jars and you must say when you stop the delivery. Count by tasters and you must say who counts as a taster. Eternal inflation’s measure problem is that pantry. It is not a quibble. It is why the expensive copies cannot yet turn Weinberg’s number into a prediction you could miss. A filter without a count is a hope with a mesh sketched on it.

Weinberg’s filter is why anyone bothers.

In 1987 he asked what value of the cosmological constant observers should expect if the constant is drawn from a wide range. Too large a shove and galaxies never form; too small, and you would need a reason the draw sat far below the galaxy-formation edge. The measured value sits near that edge, give or take the arguments about how to draw the edge. That is interesting. It is the rare case where “looking arranged” was turned into a number. Chapter 41 will sit with the number and with the sermons people attach to it. Here, the one sentence that matters is: **the filter needs an ensemble.** Without a menu of valleys, or of tiles, or of something that plays the same role, the weak anthropic cut is a reminder that we are not on Venus. It is not an explanation of why Λ is small. With an ensemble, it becomes a mesh. You throw away the monster shoves, look at what is left, and ask whether we are a typical survivor. You still need a measure. You still need the ensemble to be more than a catalog. You do not get to say the universe wanted a greenhouse. You get to say we could not have found ourselves in the valleys that did not want anyone.

Pointer, then, not a theft of Chapter 41: expensive copies are one of the ensembles people reach for. Cheap copies are another, weaker for Λ, because cheap copies share our laws and our leftover shove — a far tile with the same potatoes blighted or saved still has the same small cosmological constant. Branches are a third, and they do not help Λ at all unless the shove itself is a quantum leftover that forks, which is a different, colder story. Other mathematics will explain anything and therefore nothing. If you want a filter rather than a purpose, you need a drawer that actually contains variation. Drawer two is the expensive candidate. It has not passed a test. It is allowed to sit on the table. It is not dinner.

Weinberg’s number is the reason to keep the candidate anyway. A small shove, sitting near a galaxy-formation edge, looks arranged. An ensemble of shoves plus a mesh turns “arranged” into “survivors.” Chapter 41 will do that work without promoting the mesh to a craftsman. This chapter’s job is narrower: name the ensemble, name the catalog, name the cat, refuse the ticket to the next bubble.

If you live in a bubble, the neighboring bubbles are not destinations. The walls, in the usual pictures, are empty and expanding. You cannot book a crew that does not sleep and arrive at a different fine-structure constant. You can, at best, infer that you are in a valley that permits you. That inference is the weak filter. It is not a tour.

The wall sentence is practical, so keep it practical. Between bubbles, in the usual eternal-inflation cartoons, inflation is still going. The domain wall — the boundary between a stopped kitchen and a still-bursting dough — expands. The space you would have to cross is not a hallway with a different weather on the far side. It is a region that stretches too well to finish. Different constants are not a climate you could pack for. They are a different low-energy physics, a different chemistry, a different number of large directions. A crew that arrived, if arrival were a word, would not be a crew. Chemistry would not be chemistry. There is no airlock to hit. There is no basil to name. Neighbor bubbles are, at best, an inference from the fact that our valley is livable. They are not a pin on a map.

A vacuum that decays is not a door either. People hear “tunnel to another vacuum” and picture a station. A true-vacuum bubble nucleating in a false vacuum is, in the usual pictures, a disaster or a rewrite of local law, not a ticket booth. You do not book it. You do not steer it. If our vacuum is metastable, the news is a lifetime, not a destination. Chapter 43 will sit with endings. Here: neighboring rooms with other rules are not ports. They are, if they are anything, an ensemble for a filter. Chapter 41 is where the filter is allowed to work. This chapter’s job is to refuse the tour.

**Hot:** we live in one expanding region with one leftover glow and one small shove. **Warm:** inflation can be eternal in many models, and a small Λ wants an explanation that might be a selection. **Cold:** the bursting never stops, the valleys number 10⁵⁰⁰, the measure will someday settle, and a bubble with two large dimensions is as real as this kitchen. Barrow’s smile still fits. An idea for ideas. A catalog. A cat on the catalog. Chapter 41 will use the mesh without pretending the catalog was a census. Until a risky signature exists, 10⁵⁰⁰ is a celebrity, not a count of neighbors, and the neighbors are not a place you could grow basil.

## 40. Every Allowed Outcome

Hugh Everett, in the 1950s, took the electron’s equation at its word. The equation offers a sum of ways. The usual habit is to throw away the ways that did not “happen” at a measurement. Everett’s habit is to keep them. The sum is the inventory. A measurement is a fork. After the fork, the branches do not talk; decoherence makes the interference go away. You are the branch that includes this sentence.

The year, if you want the stamp, is 1957. The dissertation is a relative-state reading: the global wavefunction does not collapse. A measurement correlates an apparatus with a system. Each term of the correlation is a consistent story for the observers inside it. There is no extra postulate that picks one story and burns the rest. The rest stay, decohered, as unavailable to you as a spacelike navy.

This is not extra cosmology. It is a reading of the same rule that builds chemistry. It is warm. It is not a photograph of other lives. It does not give you a hop. The carnival version — jump to the branch where the basil lived, where the sea is empty, where you did not lose who you lost — is a story. The physics, if Everett is right, says those branches are *on the inventory* and *unavailable*. They are as unavailable as Andromeda’s navy and ruder, because they are not even elsewhere. They are elsehow.

Chapter 6 already spent decoherence, the record, the leftover glow as an observer that did not need a mind, and the strange loop that later minds are. This chapter will not rent that furniture again. It needs one new kitchen, one rude moral about duplicates, and a practical fact that does not budge.

The new kitchen is a coin.

You flip it by the sink. In the equation, both faces persist. Decoherence — entanglement with air, with photons, with the grain of the table — makes the interference go away on a timescale you could, in a careful lab, assign a number to. No soul is required for the going-away. Afterward there is a heads term and a tails term. You are, in this term, the person who sees heads. There is an elsehow in which you see tails. You wash one dish. The dish you dirtied while the coin was in the air, waiting, talking, not talking. The tails-you washes a dish too, in that term: maybe the same plate elsehow, maybe a different plate if the afternoon already forked. You do not wash both. The sink in this term has one plate. The coin in this term has one face showing. The other face is not under the table. It is not in a far Hubble volume. It is a term you do not occupy. You cannot lean sideways and dry the other plate. You cannot radio the other face and ask whether the basil lived. The no-signaling theorem is the grown-up name for that refusal: local operations do not send notes between terms. The greenhouse radio, which can at least reach twenty minutes ago in *this* term, is more powerful than a branch.

Stay with the dish a moment, because the hop always tries to sneak back through the suds. You saw heads. You put the plate in the rack. Somewhere in the sum, a you who saw tails is putting a plate in a rack. If the I is a tangle, both of those stretches have an equal right to the pronoun. That equality is not a ticket. It is the ruder moral: a perfect copy of a loop is a second loop. You do not get to be both dishwashers. You get this sink, this face of the coin, this one plate. The carnival wants you to treat the other term as a spare afternoon you could still choose. Everett, if you buy him, says the choosing *was* the fork. After the fork there is no remaining chooser who stands above both terms and picks. There are two choosers, decohered, silent. You are one of them. Wash the plate you have.

That is the kitchen. Keep it. It is not Chapter 6’s ant nest and not Chapter 6’s Moon. It is a coin and a dish and a sink that does not do two jobs.

Decoherence, in this sink, is not a philosophy. It is a rate. Air molecules, photons, the roughness of the table, each take a little record of which face is up. After enough records, putting the two faces back into interference would require conspiring with every record that left. The conspiracy does not happen in a kitchen. It barely happens in a lab, and only when the lab is built to prevent records. A hop would require the conspiracy, or a door the equation does not draw. Neither is on offer beside the sink.

![Figure 40. One line that splits into two that never rejoin.](Figures/figs/fig40.png)

Why put this at the core of a book about the universe? Because the block, without it, is only all the times. With it, if you buy the reading, the block is all the times and all the allowed versions. “Everything, everywhere, all at once” is a phrase this book will not treat as a title. It will treat it as a temptation to be made precise. All the times: the loaf. All the versions: the forks. All at once: a bad kitchen phrase for “equally in the inventory.” Not a permission to walk sideways. Not a permission to hop.

Elsehow is the word to keep when elsewhere tries to steal the scene. A far Hubble volume, if Chapter 38’s extras are granted, is elsewhere: a spacelike other, a tile, a distance. A neighboring bubble, if Chapter 39’s extras are granted, is a ruder elsewhere: other rules, not a destination. A branch is not a place. After decoherence you cannot point at the sky and say “the tails are that way.” There is no that way. There is another term in a sum. The split in Figure 40 never rejoins. Rejoining would be interference, and interference is what the environment spent. The carnival hop would require a door between terms. The equation, taken at Everett’s word, does not build that door. It builds a fork and then a silence.

The practical fact does not budge. The worldline you occupy is the one you can work along. A melt-probe either tastes life or it does not. A library either arrives or it does not. The other outcomes, if they persist, persist without you. That is either a horror or a relief. It is, in any case, not a ticket counter.

Taste the probe as a discipline, not a sermon. A machine chews ice, pays out a fiber, drops into a dark sea. In this term the water has a chemistry or it does not. A second origin or a sterile bath. The equation may, if Everett is right, also contain the other tasting. You will not read both reports. The report you can act on is the one on this thread: seed later or not, look first or have failed to, write the log or lose the fiber. The elsehow tasting does not cancel a plague you poured, and it does not console a sterile sea. It is not a spare life. It is a term. Terms do not do dishes in this sink.

The discipline is the same in the greenhouse. Potatoes make starch or they do not. A launch window opens or you wait. Those are forks only in the carnival sense. In the geometry they are stretches of one worldline, with causes in a past cone and effects in a future cone. Everett does not turn them into spare lives you can cash if this stretch goes badly. If both outcomes persist, they persist as two later stretches that do not talk. The woman who logs a blight does not get to read the log where the tray lived. She gets this log. Look first is a rule for this term. It does not get cheaper because another term might have looked later.

A loop that calls itself I wants a ruder answer. If the I is a tangle — a translation that stays closed — then a perfect copy of the tangle is a second I, not a hop of the first. Two nests built from the same plan are two cities. They may share a past up to the fork. After the fork they do not share a now, because there is no now. The woman in the greenhouse, duplicated by a printer that worked too well, does not “go” into the duplicate. She acquires a twin who will swear, with equal right, that she is the original. That is not Everett. That is logistics plus a loop. Everett is ruder still: the twin is not even elsewhere. She is elsehow, and she does not get your radio.

The printer twin is a useful fool. She stands in the same room, eats the same basil, hits the same airlock, and is still a second person, because the tangle that says *I* is instantiated twice. Instantiation is not travel. Chapter 6’s strange loop was a picture of what a mind feels like from the inside: symbols that point at the pointing. Copy the tangle and you have two insides. Neither inside is a suitcase the other can climb into. Everett’s twin does not even get the shared room. She is a term. The printer at least left two bodies in one greenhouse. The branch leaves you with one body and a silence.

People who love copies as consolation are buying a translation and calling it travel. Keep the translation. Fire the ticket.

The translation is allowed to be interesting. A tangle that can be copied is a fact about what an I is: not a spark that hops, a pattern that can be instantiated. That fact belongs in a book about a loaf of events. It does not belong at a ticket counter. Instantiation elsewhere is Chapter 38’s cheap copy, if the extras hold. Instantiation elsehow is this chapter’s branch. Instantiation by a printer is logistics in one greenhouse. Three instantiations. Zero hops. The hunger that wants a hop will try to wear all three as a coat. Chapter 37 already told you what to do with the coat.

Consolation is the hire this whole part has been refusing. A far tile where the potatoes lived. A bubble where Λ was kind. A branch where the coin was tails and the person you lost is still in the kitchen. Each, in its drawer, may be a responsible maybe. None of them is a method. None of them is a spare you. The I that wants a spare is asking for an edit, and Chapter 35 already spent edits. A duplicate is a second I. A branch is an elsehow I. Neither is a continuation of this dishwashing, this blight, this fiber paying out into the dark.

Appendix A40 writes decoherence, the no-signaling theorem, and the loop. You cannot use a fork to send a note to the branch next door. Pairings can be stronger than any local recipe of hidden instructions; Chapter 6 already sat with Bell and Aspect. Stronger is not a telegram. You cannot choose what to do with the coin in a way that writes a bit on the other face’s afternoon. Relativity already denied a frame-independent “at once” in which the two ends could confer. Everett adds a ruder silence: even the terms that share a past up to the flip do not confer after the environment has spent the interference. The radio in the greenhouse is more powerful than a branch. It can, at least, reach twenty minutes ago in *this* term.

**Hot:** quantum interference is real; decoherence is a calculation; Bell pairings are real; you still cannot telegraph with them. **Warm:** Everett’s reading keeps the leftovers without a collapse postulate, and it fits a loaf that already refused a single now. **Cold:** you have photographed the other term, or hopped into it, or mailed it a dish. You have not. The coin lands both ways. You wash one plate.
