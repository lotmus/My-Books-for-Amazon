## Sell copy

There is a clock on the kitchen wall, and it is lying to you. Not by being fast or slow, but by pretending that “now” is a thing the universe shares.

*The Universe Has No Now* is a popular cosmology book that starts where the physics actually starts: with *events*, not with a solar-system tour. Two people in one kitchen can disagree about which events are simultaneous without either of them being wrong. Light is late on purpose. Andromeda’s “now,” if the word still means anything, is years of argument at walking speed. Your life is a thin thread through a four-dimensional loaf, not a bead sliding on a wire.

Once “now” has been retired as a cosmic fact, the book rebuilds the past we can actually reconstruct: a hot beginning with no center, a stretch that is speeding up, a leftover microwave glow, three minutes that forged most of the helium in your kitchen, and a cosmic inventory in which ordinary atoms are the minority. Dark matter is the extra pull. Dark energy is the shove. Neither one is a mood.

Black holes arrive as one-way facts about events, not as doors. Wormholes and warps stay on the table long enough to show the bill. Getting somewhere else, in the near term, looks like a greenhouse on a dead world, a radio that answers a quarter-hour late, and ice-covered seas that may hold a second origin — or an empty kitchen. A second trunk of life would not owe us a human face, a human-sized skull, or our four-letter script. Sample size: one.

Every claim wears a temperature. Hot means measured. Warm means the best working account, with gaps. Cold means allowed by the equations and not selected by the data. String theory, whenever it appears, cannot currently be tested. A required final mind that computes forever is not a destiny the shove will sign.

The voice is a kitchen table, not a pulpit: short chapters, invented scenes with real numbers, and an appendix for readers who want the Friedmann equation and the Bekenstein bound. You do not need a physics degree. You need curiosity and a willingness to let the clock come apart.

This is Volume 1 of *Look First*. It is a complete book. You can stop here.
