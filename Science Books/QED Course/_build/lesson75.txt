# How to use this lesson

Lesson 39 promised that the Dirac Hamiltonian's nonrelativistic reduction gives g=2. Lesson 43 computed α/(2π)≈0.001161 and said it was Schwinger's a=(g−2)/2. Lesson 64 assembled the vertex correction and stopped, because the second form factor had not been named. This lesson names it, evaluates F2(0) at one loop, and obtains a=α/(2π). The full Feynman-parameter integral is outlined rather than every index contracted in public; the steps that are new, the Gordon identity and the isolation of σ^{μν}q_ν, are done in the open. Expect eight to ten hours.

## Learning objectives

- Write the on-shell vertex as ū(p′)Γ^μ u(p)=ū(p′)[γ^μ F1(q²)+iσ^{μν}q_ν F2(q²)/(2m)]u(p), with q=p′−p.

- Identify F1(0)=1 as charge (after renormalization) and F2(0)=a=(g−2)/2 as the anomalous magnetic moment.

- Use the Gordon identity to see how a magnetic-moment term can hide inside a γ^μ vertex.

- State the one-loop result F2(0)=α/(2π), hence g=2(1+α/(2π))≈2.00232.

- Distinguish this finite number from the ultraviolet divergence in F1, which was absorbed into charge renormalization.

# 1 g=2 from the Dirac equation, recalled

Lesson 39, Section 4.2, stated that expanding h_D=α·(p̂−eA)+βm+eφ to nonrelativistic order produces −(e/2m)σ·B from the algebra of the Dirac matrices, i.e. a magnetic moment with g=2. That calculation uses only the tree-level vertex ieγ^μ. There is no adjustable parameter. Experiment, already in 1947, sat slightly above 2. This lesson computes the first correction.

### Worked example 1

If the interaction Hamiltonian of a nonrelativistic spin is −μ·B with μ=g (e/2m) S and S=σ/2, what g corresponds to the Dirac term −(e/2m)σ·B? Comparing −(e/2m)σ·B=−g(e/2m)(σ/2)·B gives g=2.

# 2 Two form factors

The most general Lorentz-covariant, parity-conserving vertex between on-shell Dirac spinors, built from p, p′, and γ^μ, can be reduced with the Dirac equation to two scalar functions of q²:

$$ ū(p′) Γ^μ u(p) = ū(p′) [ γ^μ F₁(q²) + i σ^(μν) q_ν F₂(q²) / (2m) ] u(p)

F1(0) is the electric charge in units of e, after renormalization F1(0)=1. F2(0) multiplies the magnetic moment beyond the Dirac value: a=F2(0).

The Gordon identity,

$$ ū(p′) γ^μ u(p) = ū(p′) [ (p+p′)^μ / (2m) + i σ^(μν) q_ν / (2m) ] u(p)

shows that even the tree vertex γ^μ already contains a magnetic piece, the one that produces g=2. Any extra F2 is an anomaly on top of that.

# 3 Isolating F2 from Lesson 64

Lesson 64's integral for iΛ^μ(p′,p) is a loop with three propagators. After combining denominators with a Feynman parameter (the step Lesson 66 deferred), shifting the loop momentum, and using the on-shell conditions p²=p′²=m², the coefficient of iσ^{μν}q_ν/(2m) is ultraviolet-finite: extra powers of q in the numerator improve the large-k count relative to F1. The coefficient of γ^μ is logarithmically divergent and is absorbed into F1's renormalization, i.e. into the charge.

Evaluating the finite F2 integral at q²=0 yields

$$ F₂(0) = α / (2π)

which is Schwinger's 1948 result. Then a=α/(2π) and g=2+α/π=2(1+α/(2π)).

### Worked example 2

Compute g from α=1/137.036. a=1/(2π×137.036)=0.0011614, g=2.0023228, matching Lesson 43, Exercise 13. The modern experimental electron anomaly is 0.001159652..., so this one-loop term already accounts for all but a few parts per million; the rest is higher loops and tiny hadronic pieces, Lesson 78.

# 4 Why F2(0) could not have been absorbed

Lesson 67 absorbed divergences into parameters already in the Lagrangian. There is no tree-level σ^{μν}F_μν ψ̄ψ operator in ℒ_QED. A finite F2(0) is therefore a prediction, not a quantity that can be hidden in e or m. That is why a=(g−2)/2 is a precision test rather than a renormalization condition.

# 6 Summary

1. The on-shell vertex is described by F1(q²) and F2(q²).

2. F1(0)=1 after charge renormalization; F2(0)=a=(g−2)/2.

3. Gordon's identity shows g=2 already sitting inside the tree vertex.

4. The one-loop vertex correction gives F2(0)=α/(2π), ultraviolet-finite.

5. This cannot be absorbed into ℒ_QED's existing parameters, so it is a prediction.

# Solutions

**1.** −(e/2m)σ·B versus −g(e/2m)(σ/2)·B implies g=2.

**2.** As in Section 2; F1(0)=1, F2(0)=a.

**3.** Direct reading of the Gordon identity.

**4.** Any extra structure can be reduced using p̸u=mu, ūp̸′=mū, and q=p′−p; parity excludes γ⁵ terms in QED.

**5.** F2 comes with an extra q from σ^{μν}q_ν, improving the large-k power count to a finite integral.

**6.** 1/(2×3.14159×137.036)=0.00116141.

**7.** g=2(1+0.00116141)=2.0023228.

**8.** Experiment is about 0.00115965, a relative difference of order 0.15% of a, i.e. the size of α/π corrections to a itself (two-loop).

**9.** No corresponding operator in the original ℒ_QED; F2 is generated, not parametrized.

**10.** a would become an input, not a prediction, and QED would lose this test.

**11.** The spatial distribution of the anomalous moment, a form factor in q².

**12.** Lesson 64's second matrix structure is iσ^{μν}q_ν/(2m).

**13.** g>2; the loop increases the magnetic moment slightly.

**14.** The muon mass sets a larger loop scale, so heavier virtual states are less suppressed than they are for the electron.

**15.** g=2+2a.

**16.** After combining three denominators, the k-integral is Lesson 66's logarithmically divergent template for F1 and a finite piece ∫dx x(1−x)=1/6 for F2; combined with e²/(16π²)=α/(4π) this produces α/(2π).
