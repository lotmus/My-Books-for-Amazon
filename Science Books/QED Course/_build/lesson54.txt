TITLE: Electron-Electron Scattering
TAGLINE: Møller scattering — the second, exchange diagram forced by identical particles, the relative minus sign, and the interference trace that ties all four momenta together
NUM: 54
NEXT: Lesson 55 turns from two electrons to an electron and a positron, where the same identical-particle machinery reappears in a different guise: annihilation into a photon competes with ordinary scattering, connected by crossing symmetry.

# How to use this lesson

Lesson 53 computed electron-muon scattering by exchanging one photon between two distinguishable particles. Electron-electron scattering, historically called Møller scattering, starts from the same single-photon-exchange idea but adds a complication distinguishable particles never raise: since both incoming and both outgoing particles are electrons, Wick's theorem produces a second, equally valid contraction, indistinguishable in principle from the first except for which final-state electron is labeled "3" and which "4." Fermi-Dirac statistics forces a relative minus sign between the two resulting diagrams, and their interference, when squared and spin-summed, produces a single trace threading all four momenta together, unlike anything in Lesson 53's calculation. This lesson carries that trace through to the end, arriving at one of the most quoted formulas in introductory QED. Expect nine to eleven hours; Section 4's identity and Section 5's trace are the technical heart of the lesson.

## Learning objectives

- Explain why identical particles in the final state force a second Wick contraction, and derive the relative minus sign between the two resulting amplitudes from Lesson 36's fermionic reordering rule.

- Write the two amplitudes ℳ_t and ℳ_u and show ℳ_u is obtained from ℳ_t by exchanging p₃ and p₄.

- Show that the direct and exchange terms alone reuse Lesson 53's result with t↔u, while their interference produces a genuinely new, single trace involving all four momenta at once.

- Derive and apply the identity γ^μγ^aγ^bγ^cγ_μ=−2γ^cγ^bγ^a.

- Use this identity to reduce the interference trace, in the massless limit, to −8s², and combine every piece into the complete result 2e⁴[(s²+u²)/t²+(s²+t²)/u²+2s²/(tu)].

- Explain why the resulting cross section diverges at both forward (t→0) and backward (u→0) scattering, unlike Lesson 53's single forward divergence.

- Reproduce the full calculation, including the key identity and the final formula, from memory.

# 1 Two diagrams, one relative sign

## 1.1 A second Wick contraction

For e⁻(p₁)+e⁻(p₂)→e⁻(p₃)+e⁻(p₄), the same second-order S-matrix term used in Lesson 53 now has ψ_e, ψ̄_e appearing at both vertices, since only one field species is involved. Wick's theorem produces two inequivalent contractions consistent with the external states: one in which the electron at x₁ becomes the outgoing electron labeled 3 (direct), and one in which it becomes the electron labeled 4 (exchange), with the photon still connecting the two vertices in either case.

## 1.2 The relative minus sign

Lesson 36, Section 6.2, established that every reordering of fermionic field operators needed to reach a given contraction pattern contributes a factor (−1)^P. The exchange contraction of Section 1.1 requires one additional transposition of fermionic operators relative to the direct contraction (swapping which external electron state, 3 or 4, is reached from x₁), so the exchange term enters with an extra factor of −1.

## 1.3 The two amplitudes

$$ i ℳ_t = (ie²/t) [ū(p₃)γ^μu(p₁)][ū(p₄)γ_μu(p₂)] , qquad i ℳ_u = (ie²/u) [ū(p₄)γ^μu(p₁)][ū(p₃)γ_μu(p₂)]

each built exactly as in Lesson 53, Section 1.3, with ℳ_u obtained from ℳ_t by exchanging p₃ and p₄ throughout (which also exchanges t=(p₁−p₃)² for u=(p₁−p₄)²), and the total amplitude, per Section 1.2,

$$ ℳ = ℳ_t − ℳ_u

### Worked example 1

Explain why this relative minus sign is not optional, in the sense that no different but equally valid choice of overall phase convention could remove it. The minus sign is forced by Lesson 36's derivation of the fermionic anticommutation structure itself, ultimately tied to the spin-statistics behavior of the electron field fixed since Lesson 36's own opening sections; changing it would require the electron field to obey different (bosonic) statistics, a qualitatively different, experimentally false theory, not a matter of convention.

# 2 Squaring: three pieces

## 2.1 Direct and exchange squared

$$ |ℳ|² = |ℳ_t|² + |ℳ_u|² − 2 Re(ℳ_t ℳ_u^*)

The first two terms, spin-averaged and summed, are exactly Lesson 53's result, reused with no new calculation: (1/4)Σ|ℳ_t|²=(2e⁴/t²)[(s−2m²)²+(u−2m²)²+4tm²] (setting m_e=m_μ=m in Lesson 53, Section 4.2), and (1/4)Σ|ℳ_u|² is the identical formula with t and u exchanged throughout.

## 2.2 The interference term

The third term is new. Writing out ℳ_tℳ_u^* and conjugating (using Γ̄=γ^ν for γ^ν, Lesson 51, Section 3.1) gives ℳ_u^*=(e²/u)[ū(p₁)γ^νu(p₄)][ū(p₂)γ_νu(p₃)], so

$$ ℳ_t ℳ_u^* = (e⁴/(tu)) [ū(p₃)γ^μu(p₁)][ū(p₄)γ_μu(p₂)] [ū(p₁)γ^νu(p₄)][ū(p₂)γ_νu(p₃)]

Summing over all four spins using the completeness relations (Exercise 6 tracks every spinor index explicitly), every spinor index closes into a single chain, giving one trace, not a product of two:

$$ Σ_(spins) ℳ_t ℳ_u^* = (e⁴/(tu)) , Tr [ γ^μ (p̸₁+m) γ^ν (p̸₄+m) γ_μ (p̸₂+m) γ_ν (p̸₃+m) ]

### Worked example 2

Explain, structurally, why this trace could not have been anticipated from Lesson 53's factorized calculation. In Lesson 53, the electron and muon spinors never appeared in the same bracket, so completeness produced two separate matrices, one per particle, each closing into its own trace; here, ℳ_t's p₁-p₃ pairing and ℳ_u's p₁-p₄ pairing share the same p₁, so summing over p₁'s spin links the two brackets together directly, and the chain of indices never breaks into two independent pieces.

# 3 A key gamma-matrix identity

## 3.1 Statement

$$ γ^μ γ^a γ^b γ^c γ_μ = − 2 γ^c γ^b γ^a

## 3.2 Derivation

Using {γ^c,γ_μ}=2δ^c_μ to rewrite γ^cγ_μ=2δ^c_μ−γ_μγ^c,

$$ γ^μγ^aγ^bγ^cγ_μ = γ^μγ^aγ^b(2δ^c_μ − γ_μγ^c) = 2γ^cγ^aγ^b − (γ^μγ^aγ^bγ_μ)γ^c = 2γ^cγ^aγ^b − 4g^(ab)γ^c

using Lesson 20's already-established γ^μγ^aγ^bγ_μ=4g^ab (Lesson 20, Section 2.3's method, extended by the identical technique one gamma matrix further, Lesson 20, Worked example 2). Now rewrite γ^aγ^b=2g^ab−γ^bγ^a in the first term,

$$ 2γ^cγ^aγ^b = 2γ^c(2g^(ab) − γ^bγ^a) = 4g^(ab)γ^c − 2γ^cγ^bγ^a

Substituting back, the two 4g^abγ^c terms cancel exactly:

$$ γ^μγ^aγ^bγ^cγ_μ = [4g^(ab)γ^c − 2γ^cγ^bγ^a] − 4g^(ab)γ^c = − 2 γ^cγ^bγ^a

confirming Section 3.1.

### Worked example 3

State what Section 3.1's identity gives when C (the third factor) is a scalar multiple of the identity matrix rather than another gamma matrix, and use this to handle the mass terms in Section 2.2's trace. If C=m·I for a constant m, the identity's right-hand side becomes −2(mI)γ^bγ^a=−2mγ^bγ^a, so a mass insertion simply carries through the reversal unchanged in position, exactly as an ordinary slashed momentum would, allowing every term in the expansion of (p̸₁+m)γ^ν(p̸₄+m) to be reduced by the same identity, mass terms included (Exercise 9 works through the complete expansion).

# 4 The interference trace, evaluated

## 4.1 The massless case

Setting m=0 for clarity (Exercise 12 addresses the general massive case), Section 3.1's identity, applied with a=p₁ (meaning the identity is used in its slashed-vector form, contracted with the momentum components), b=ν, c=p₄, gives

$$ γ^μ p̸₁ γ^ν p̸₄ γ_μ = − 2 p̸₄ γ^ν p̸₁

so the interference trace becomes

$$ Tr[γ^μp̸₁γ^νp̸₄γ_μp̸₂γ_νp̸₃] = − 2 , Tr[p̸₄γ^νp̸₁p̸₂γ_νp̸₃] = − 2 , Tr[p̸₃p̸₄γ^νp̸₁p̸₂γ_ν]

using cyclicity of the trace to move p̸₃ to the front. A second application of the two-object contraction identity γ^νγ^aγ^bγ_ν=4g^ab (Lesson 20, Section 2.3), with a, b now the indices carried by p̸₁, p̸₂,

$$ γ^ν p̸₁ p̸₂ γ_ν = 4 (p₁·p₂) I

reduces the trace to

$$ Tr[γ^μp̸₁γ^νp̸₄γ_μp̸₂γ_νp̸₃] = − 2 · 4(p₁·p₂) , Tr[p̸₃p̸₄] = − 2 · 4(p₁·p₂) · 4(p₃·p₄) = − 32 (p₁·p₂)(p₃·p₄)

using Tr[a̸b̸]=4a·b (Lesson 20, Worked example 2).

## 4.2 Converting to s

In the massless limit, p₁·p₂=p₃·p₄=s/2 (Lesson 52, Section 5.2, with m=0), so

$$ Tr[γ^μp̸₁γ^νp̸₄γ_μp̸₂γ_νp̸₃] = − 32 (s/2)(s/2) = − 8s²

Substituting into Section 2.2's Σℳ_tℳ_u^*=(e⁴/(tu))(−8s²), the interference term's net contribution to (1/4)Σ|ℳ|² is −(1/2)Σ_(spins)(ℳ_tℳ_u^*)=−(1/2)(e⁴/(tu))(−8s²)=4e⁴s²/(tu), using the 1/4 spin-averaging factor and the −2 from |ℳ|²=|ℳ_t|²+|ℳ_u|²−2Re(⋯) together, i.e. (1/4)(−2)=−1/2 (Exercise 14 verifies this bookkeeping in full).

### Worked example 4

State the single most striking feature of Section 4.1's result. The interference trace depends only on p₁·p₂ and p₃·p₄, that is, only on s; unlike the direct or exchange terms individually, it carries no dependence on t or u at all, a fact not obvious in advance from the trace's original, symmetric-looking form.

# 5 Assembling the total

## 5.1 The massless result

Combining Section 2.1's three pieces (Lesson 53's t-channel and u-channel results, plus Section 4's interference term),

$$ (1/4) Σ|ℳ|² = 2e⁴ [ (s²+u²)/t² + (s²+t²)/u² + 2s²/(tu) ]

the celebrated Møller scattering formula, manifestly symmetric under t↔u (reflecting the electrons' indistinguishability) and singular at both t=0 and u=0.

## 5.2 General massive result

Without the massless approximation, the interference trace evaluates to −8(s−2m²)(s−6m²) (Exercise 12), giving the complete result

$$ (1/4)Σ|ℳ|² = (2e⁴/t²)[(s−2m²)²+(u−2m²)²+4tm²] + (2e⁴/u²)[(s−2m²)²+(t−2m²)²+4um²] + 4e⁴(s−2m²)(s−6m²)/(tu)

reducing to Section 5.1 as m→0.

### Worked example 5

Explain why the cross section built from Section 5.1's formula diverges at both θ=0 and θ=π (forward and backward scattering), unlike Lesson 53's electron-muon result, which diverged only at θ=0. t=0 corresponds to forward scattering (Lesson 52, Section 4.3), where the exchanged photon's momentum transfer vanishes; u=0 corresponds to backward scattering, where instead the outgoing electron labeled 4 exactly continues the incoming electron labeled 1's momentum. Since the two outgoing electrons are indistinguishable, "backward scattering of electron 1 into slot 4" is physically the same configuration as "forward scattering of electron 1 into slot 3," so the same soft-photon-exchange divergence that appears at t=0 for a distinguishable process reappears at u=0 here, simply relabeled.

# 7 Summary

1. Identical particles in the final state force a second Wick contraction, with a relative minus sign from Lesson 36's fermionic reordering rule.

2. ℳ=ℳ_t−ℳ_u, with ℳ_u obtained from ℳ_t by exchanging p₃ and p₄.

3. |ℳ_t|² and |ℳ_u|² reuse Lesson 53's result directly (with t↔u for the exchange term); the interference term −2Re(ℳ_tℳ_u^*) requires a new, single trace threading all four momenta.

4. The identity γ^μγ^aγ^bγ^cγ_μ=−2γ^cγ^bγ^a, derived from the Clifford algebra, is the key tool for reducing this trace.

5. In the massless limit, the interference trace reduces to −8s², depending on s alone.

6. The complete result is (1/4)Σ|ℳ|²=2e⁴[(s²+u²)/t²+(s²+t²)/u²+2s²/(tu)], symmetric under t↔u and divergent at both t=0 and u=0.

# Solutions

**1.** Lesson 36, Section 6.2, states that a vacuum expectation value of fermionic field operators equals a sum over complete pairings, each weighted by (−1)^P for P transpositions needed to bring the paired operators adjacent, in their original order. The direct contraction pairs the operators in their "natural" order (electron at x₁ to electron labeled 3, electron at x₂ to electron labeled 4); the exchange contraction instead pairs the electron at x₁ to the electron labeled 4, requiring one additional swap of which external state is reached from which field operator, hence one extra transposition and one extra factor of −1.

**2.** ℳ_t=(e²/t)[ū(p₃)γ^μu(p₁)][ū(p₄)γ_μu(p₂)]; relabeling p₃↔p₄ gives (e²/t′)[ū(p₄)γ^μu(p₁)][ū(p₃)γ_μu(p₂)] where t′=(p₁−p₄)²=u (Lesson 52, Section 1.1); this is exactly ℳ_u, confirming the exchange is obtained by the stated relabeling, propagator denominator included.

**3.** From Lesson 53, Section 4.2, (1/4)Σ|ℳ_t|²=(2e⁴/t²)[(s−m_e²−m_μ²)²+(u−m_e²−m_μ²)²+2t(m_e²+m_μ²)]; setting m_e=m_μ=m gives (2e⁴/t²)[(s−2m²)²+(u−2m²)²+4tm²]. Exchanging t↔u throughout (per Exercise 2's relabeling) gives (1/4)Σ|ℳ_u|²=(2e⁴/u²)[(s−2m²)²+(t−2m²)²+4um²].

**4.** |ℳ|²=(ℳ_t−ℳ_u)(ℳ_t−ℳ_u)^*=|ℳ_t|²−ℳ_tℳ_u^*−ℳ_uℳ_t^*+|ℳ_u|²=|ℳ_t|²+|ℳ_u|²−2Re(ℳ_tℳ_u^*), using ℳ_tℳ_u^*+ℳ_uℳ_t^*=ℳ_tℳ_u^*+(ℳ_tℳ_u^*)^*=2Re(ℳ_tℳ_u^*). The minus sign in |ℳ|² traces directly to the minus sign already present in ℳ=ℳ_t−ℳ_u (Section 1.3), itself forced by Section 1.2's fermionic exchange sign.

**5.** ℳ_u=(e²/u)[ū(p₄)γ^μu(p₁)][ū(p₃)γ_μu(p₂)]; conjugating each bracket using [ū(p′)γ^νu(p)]^*=ū(p)γ^νu(p′) (Lesson 51, Section 2.1, with Γ̄=γ^ν=Γ) gives ℳ_u^*=(e²/u)[ū(p₁)γ^μu(p₄)][ū(p₂)γ_μu(p₃)], relabeling the dummy index μ→ν as in Section 2.2.

**6.** Writing every bracket with explicit spinor indices and tracking which momentum's spin sum links which pair of indices (as in Lesson 51, Section 2.2's method but now with four distinct momenta instead of two), the chain runs p₁'s indices (shared between the first and third brackets) through p₄'s indices (shared between the second and fourth) through p₂'s (second and fourth again) through p₃'s (first and third again), closing back on itself after passing through all four completeness insertions, producing the single trace of Section 2.2.

**7.** {γ^c,γ_μ}=γ^cγ_μ+γ_μγ^c=2δ^c_μ (the mixed-index Clifford algebra relation); rearranging directly gives γ^cγ_μ=2δ^c_μ−γ_μγ^c.

**8.** Substituting Exercise 7's identity into γ^μγ^aγ^bγ^cγ_μ=γ^μγ^aγ^b(2δ^c_μ−γ_μγ^c)=2γ^cγ^aγ^b−γ^μγ^aγ^bγ_μγ^c. Using γ^μγ^aγ^bγ_μ=4g^ab (Lesson 20), the second term is 4g^abγ^c, giving 2γ^cγ^aγ^b−4g^abγ^c. Substituting γ^aγ^b=2g^ab−γ^bγ^a into the first term, 2γ^c(2g^ab−γ^bγ^a)=4g^abγ^c−2γ^cγ^bγ^a. The full expression is then 4g^abγ^c−2γ^cγ^bγ^a−4g^abγ^c=−2γ^cγ^bγ^a, confirming the two 4g^abγ^c terms cancel exactly.

**9.** p̸₁γ^νp̸₄ (three slashed/gamma objects) reduces via Section 3.1 to −2p̸₄γ^νp̸₁ when sandwiched by γ^μ⋯γ_μ. mp̸₁γ^ν (two objects) reduces via γ^μγ^aγ^bγ_μ=4g^ab to 4m p₁^ν I. mγ^νp̸₄ (two objects) similarly reduces to 4m p₄^ν I. m²γ^ν (one object) reduces via γ^μγ^aγ_μ=−2γ^a to −2m²γ^ν. Summing all four gives γ^μ(p̸₁+m)γ^ν(p̸₄+m)γ_μ=−2p̸₄γ^νp̸₁+4m(p₁^ν+p₄^ν)I−2m²γ^ν.

**10.** γ^νp̸₁p̸₂γ_ν=p1_ap2_bγ^νγ^aγ^bγ_ν=p1_ap2_b(4g^ab)=4p1_ap2^a=4(p₁·p₂), using Lesson 20's γ^μγ^aγ^bγ_μ=4g^ab contracted with the momentum components of p₁ and p₂.

**11.** Starting from Tr[γ^μp̸₁γ^νp̸₄γ_μp̸₂γ_νp̸₃], apply Section 3.1 to the μ-contraction (a=p₁,b=ν,c=p₄): γ^μp̸₁γ^νp̸₄γ_μ=−2p̸₄γ^νp̸₁, giving Tr[−2p̸₄γ^νp̸₁p̸₂γ_νp̸₃]=−2Tr[p̸₄γ^νp̸₁p̸₂γ_νp̸₃]. Using cyclicity to move p̸₃ to the front: −2Tr[p̸₃p̸₄γ^νp̸₁p̸₂γ_ν]. Applying Exercise 10, γ^νp̸₁p̸₂γ_ν=4(p₁·p₂)I, gives −2·4(p₁·p₂)Tr[p̸₃p̸₄]. Using Tr[p̸₃p̸₄]=4(p₃·p₄) (Lesson 20, Worked example 2), this is −2×4(p₁·p₂)×4(p₃·p₄)=−32(p₁·p₂)(p₃·p₄), matching Section 4.1.

**12.** Using Exercise 9's expansion for the μ-contraction, the mass-dependent terms (4m(p₁^ν+p₄^ν)I and −2m²γ^ν) contribute additional pieces beyond Exercise 11's leading −32(p₁·p₂)(p₃·p₄) term when carried through the remaining ν-contraction and the outer trace; collecting every resulting term (a longer but mechanically identical calculation, following the same contraction identities applied twice more) and converting every dot product to Mandelstam variables via Lesson 52, Section 5.2 (with all four masses equal to m) yields, after simplification, the stated general result −8(s−2m²)(s−6m²), which correctly reduces to −8s² (Exercise 11's massless answer, converted via p₁·p₂=p₃·p₄=s/2) when m→0.

**13.** With m=0, Lesson 52, Section 5.2, gives p₁·p₂=(s−m₁²−m₂²)/2=s/2 and p₃·p₄=(s−m₃²−m₄²)/2=s/2, using m₁=m₂=m₃=m₄=0.

**14.** (1/4)Σ|ℳ_t|²=2e⁴(s²+u²)/t² (Lesson 53's massless result, Exercise 3 with m=0), (1/4)Σ|ℳ_u|²=2e⁴(s²+t²)/u² (t↔u exchange), and the interference contribution −(1/2)Σ(ℳ_tℳ_u^*)=−(1/2)(e⁴/(tu))(−8s²)=4e⁴s²/(tu) (Section 4.2). Summing all three: 2e⁴(s²+u²)/t²+2e⁴(s²+t²)/u²+4e⁴s²/(tu)=2e⁴[(s²+u²)/t²+(s²+t²)/u²+2s²/(tu)], matching Section 5.1.

**15.** Swapping t and u in Section 5.1's formula gives 2e⁴[(s²+t²)/u²+(s²+u²)/t²+2s²/(ut)], identical to the original expression with its first two terms simply relabeled and the third unchanged (since ut=tu); this symmetry must hold because relabeling which outgoing electron is called "3" and which is called "4" is a purely notational choice with no physical content, given the electrons' indistinguishability, so the physical cross section cannot depend on which label was assigned to which.

**16.** Electron-muon scattering has only the direct, t-channel diagram (Lesson 53, Worked example 1); there is no exchange diagram and hence no interference term, so nothing in that calculation produces a term singular at u=0. Worked example 5's argument specifically traces the u=0 divergence to the physical indistinguishability of the two final-state electrons, allowing "backward scattering into slot 4" to be reinterpreted as "forward scattering into slot 3"; an electron and a muon in the final state are always distinguishable, so no such reinterpretation, and no corresponding divergence, exists for that process.
