TITLE: Electron-Positron Annihilation
TAGLINE: Two photons from annihilation, a plus sign where Møller scattering had a minus, and a final result of striking simplicity: 2e⁴[t/u+u/t−2]
NUM: 55
NEXT: Lesson 56 turns to Compton scattering itself, the process this lesson's diagrams are the crossed, annihilation-channel partners of, and shows how much of that calculation follows immediately from this one by crossing symmetry.

# How to use this lesson

Lesson 44 first raised electron-positron annihilation into a photon as an example of a process no single vertex could complete on its own. This lesson finishes the job properly: e⁻(p₁)+e⁺(p₂)→γ(k₁)+γ(k₂), a genuine, two-vertex process with an internal electron line connecting the two photon emissions. The calculation reuses almost everything already built, the positron completeness relation from Lesson 21, the photon polarization sum from Lesson 51, and the contraction identity derived in Lesson 54, but combines them in a new configuration, with two diagrams that add with a plus sign rather than Lesson 54's minus, since it is the two photons, not the fermion line, that can be emitted in either order. The final result is remarkably compact. Expect eight to ten hours.

## Learning objectives

- Derive the two Wick contractions for e⁻e⁺→γγ and show they combine with a plus sign, in contrast with Lesson 54's fermionic minus sign.

- Write both amplitudes ℳ_A, ℳ_B explicitly, identifying their internal photon-emission momenta with the Mandelstam-like variables t=(p₁−k₁)² and u=(p₁−k₂)².

- Use Lesson 21's positron completeness relation Σv(p)v̄(p)=p̸−m and Lesson 51's covariant photon polarization sum to reduce |ℳ|² to a sum of traces.

- Apply Lesson 54's contraction identity to reduce the direct-squared trace to 8tu in the massless limit.

- Reduce the interference trace to −8tu by the same method, and assemble the complete result (1/4)Σ|ℳ|²=2e⁴[t/u+u/t−2].

- Explain why this process's two diagrams carry a relative plus sign while Lesson 54's carried a minus sign.

- Reproduce the complete calculation, and the final formula, from memory.

# 1 The process and its two diagrams

## 1.1 The Wick contraction

Following Lesson 49's method, at second order the interaction term e²[ψ̄γ^αψA_α](x₁)[ψ̄γ^βψA_β](x₂) contracts ψ(x₁) with the incoming electron (u(p₁)e^(−ip₁x₁), Lesson 48, Section 2.1), ψ̄(x₂) with the incoming positron (v̄(p₂)e^(−ip₂x₂), using ψ̄'s d-term per the corrected assignment of Lesson 48, Section 3.2), and A_α(x₁), A_β(x₂) with the two outgoing photons (ε_α(k₁)e^(ik₁x₁), ε_β(k₂)e^(ik₂x₂), Lesson 48, Section 4.2). The remaining fields, ψ̄(x₁) and ψ(x₂), contract into the internal propagator S_F.

## 1.2 A plus sign, not a minus

A second, equally valid contraction exchanges which vertex emits which photon (k₁ from x₂, k₂ from x₁ instead). Unlike Lesson 54's exchange of two identical fermions, this exchange involves the two photon fields A_α(x₁), A_β(x₂), which are bosonic; Lesson 47, Section 2.1, established that no minus sign accompanies reordering bosonic field operators, so this second diagram enters with a plain plus sign.

## 1.3 The two amplitudes

Carrying out the spacetime integrals exactly as in Lesson 49, Section 2, the two vertex delta functions fix the internal momentum of the first diagram to q_A=p₁−k₁ (with q_A²=t) and, for the second, q_B=p₁−k₂ (with q_B²=u):

$$ i ℳ_A = (ie)² , v̄(p₂) γ^β [ i(q̸_A+m)/(q_A²−m²+iε) ] γ^α u(p₁) , ε_α(k₁) ε_β(k₂)

$$ i ℳ_B = (ie)² , v̄(p₂) γ^α [ i(q̸_B+m)/(q_B²−m²+iε) ] γ^β u(p₁) , ε_α(k₁) ε_β(k₂)

$$ ℳ = ℳ_A + ℳ_B

### Worked example 1

State the general principle this lesson's plus sign and Lesson 54's minus sign both illustrate. Whether two diagrams contributing to the same process combine with a plus or a minus sign is fixed entirely by the statistics of whichever particle's role is being exchanged between the two diagrams, bosonic exchange (as here, between the two photons) giving a plus sign and fermionic exchange (as in Lesson 54, between the two final-state electrons) giving a minus sign; the number of diagrams alone never determines the sign.

# 2 Squaring and summing

## 2.1 Spin and polarization sums

Spin-averaging over the initial electron and positron and using the positron completeness relation Σ_sv_s(p)v̄_s(p)=p̸−m (Lesson 21, Section 6.1) alongside the electron's Σu_s(p)ū_s(p)=p̸+m, and summing over final photon polarizations using the covariant replacement Σε_με_ν→−g_μν (Lesson 51, Section 5.2), reduces (1/4)Σ|ℳ|² to a sum of three traces, one each for |ℳ_A|², |ℳ_B|², and the interference term 2Re(ℳ_Aℳ_B^*).

## 2.2 The three traces

$$ T_(AA) = Tr[ γ^β(q̸_A+m)γ^α(p̸₁+m)γ_α(q̸_A+m)γ_β(p̸₂−m) ]

$$ T_(BB) = Tr[ γ^α(q̸_B+m)γ^β(p̸₁+m)γ_β(q̸_B+m)γ_α(p̸₂−m) ]

$$ T_(AB) = Tr[ γ^β(q̸_A+m)γ^α(p̸₁+m)γ_α(q̸_B+m)γ_β(p̸₂−m) ]

with Σ|ℳ|²=e⁴[T_AA/(q_A²−m²)² + T_BB/(q_B²−m²)² + 2T_AB/((q_A²−m²)(q_B²−m²))], so that (1/4)Σ|ℳ|²=(e⁴/4)[T_AA/(q_A²−m²)² + T_BB/(q_B²−m²)² + 2T_AB/((q_A²−m²)(q_B²−m²))], the explicit factor of 1/4 accounting for averaging over the two unmeasured initial spins (Lesson 51, Section 1.2).

### Worked example 2

Explain why T_BB is obtained from T_AA simply by exchanging α↔β and q_A↔q_B, without a fresh calculation. ℳ_B has exactly the same structure as ℳ_A with the two vertex indices and the internal momentum relabeled (Section 1.3), so squaring and spin-summing it reproduces T_AA's calculation verbatim under this relabeling, exactly as Lesson 54, Worked example 2, noted for a different pair of amplitudes.

# 3 Reducing the direct trace

## 3.1 The α-contraction

Using γ^μγ^aγ_μ=−2γ^a and γ^μγ_μ=4 (Lesson 20),

$$ γ^α(p̸₁+m)γ_α = −2p̸₁ + 4m

reducing T_AA to a trace of (q̸_A+m)(−2p̸₁+4m)(q̸_A+m) sandwiched by γ^β⋯γ_β and multiplied by (p̸₂−m).

## 3.2 The massless result

Expanding fully (Exercise 8 carries out every term) and applying Lesson 54's identity γ^μABCγ_μ=−2CBA together with the two- and one-object contraction identities to each resulting piece, then setting m=0,

$$ T_(AA) = 32(q_A·p₁)(q_A·p₂) − 16 q_A²(p₁·p₂)

Using q_A·p₁=t/2, q_A·p₂=−t/2, q_A²=t, and p₁·p₂=s/2 (all massless relations derived from t=(p₁−k₁)²=(p₂−k₂)² and u=(p₁−k₂)²=(p₂−k₁)², Exercise 9),

$$ T_(AA) = 32(t/2)(−t/2) − 16t(s/2) = −8t² − 8ts = −8t(t+s) = 8tu

using s+t+u=0 (Lesson 52's sum rule, all four particles taken massless in this limit) to write t+s=−u.

### Worked example 3

State, by Worked example 2's relabeling, the value of T_BB. Exchanging t↔u throughout Section 3.2's derivation (since T_BB's own internal momentum is q_B, with q_B²=u playing the role q_A²=t played for T_AA) gives T_BB=8ut=8tu, the identical numerical expression, since multiplication does not care about the order t,u are written in.

# 4 The interference trace

## 4.1 Setting up

T_AB differs from T_AA only in its middle factor: q̸_A appears once and q̸_B appears once, rather than q̸_A appearing twice. Carrying out the identical steps as Section 3 (Exercise 12 works through every term), the three-object reversal identity now gives γ^βq̸_Ap̸₁q̸_Bγ_β=−2q̸_Bp̸₁q̸_A (reversing an asymmetric triple), and the analogous two-object identities apply to the remaining, mass-dependent pieces.

## 4.2 The massless result

Setting m=0, the surviving term is

$$ T_(AB) = 16 [ (q_B·p₁)(q_A·p₂) − (q_A·q_B)(p₁·p₂) + (q_B·p₂)(q_A·p₁) ]

Using q_B·p₁=u/2, q_A·p₂=−t/2, q_A·q_B=0 (Exercise 13 verifies this last, notable simplification, using k₁·k₂=s/2), q_B·p₂=−u/2, and q_A·p₁=t/2,

$$ T_(AB) = 16 [ (u/2)(−t/2) − 0 + (−u/2)(t/2) ] = 16 [ −tu/4 − tu/4 ] = −8tu

### Worked example 4

State the single most useful simplification found along the way to Section 4.2's result. q_A·q_B=0 in the massless limit; without this simplification, the middle term of the bracket would survive and complicate the final expression, but it vanishes identically once k₁·k₂=s/2 (from (k₁+k₂)²=s with both photons massless) is substituted, a clean, checkable fact worth remembering.

# 5 Assembling the result

$$ Σ|ℳ|² = e⁴ [ 8tu/t² + 8tu/u² + 2(−8tu)/(tu) ] = e⁴ [ 8u/t + 8t/u − 16 ] = 8 e⁴ [ u/t + t/u − 2 ]

Dividing by 4 for the spin average (Section 2.2),

$$ (1/4) Σ|ℳ|² = 2 e⁴ [ t/u + u/t − 2 ]

a strikingly compact result: no explicit s-dependence at all once expressed this way (s enters only implicitly, through the sum rule s+t+u=0 constraining t and u together), symmetric under t↔u, and manifestly non-negative, since t/u+u/t≥2 for any two same-signed real numbers (Exercise 15), with equality only at t=u.

### Worked example 5

Explain why (1/4)Σ|ℳ|² must be non-negative, and confirm Section 5's formula respects this on general grounds, independent of the specific algebra. A spin-averaged, spin-summed squared amplitude is, by construction, a sum of quantities each of the form |ℳ|² for a specific spin and polarization configuration, and a sum of non-negative numbers is non-negative; Section 5's formula, 2e⁴(t/u+u/t−2), is non-negative because t and u are both negative for physical scattering (Lesson 52, Section 3.2, extended to this process), making t/u a positive ratio, and any positive ratio r satisfies r+1/r≥2 (Exercise 15), so the bracket is never negative.

# 7 Summary

1. Electron-positron annihilation into two photons proceeds through two diagrams, differing in which vertex emits which photon, combining with a plus sign since the exchanged particles (photons) are bosonic.

2. The two amplitudes ℳ_A, ℳ_B have internal electron momenta q_A=p₁−k₁ (q_A²=t) and q_B=p₁−k₂ (q_B²=u).

3. Spin and polarization sums, using positron completeness and the covariant photon polarization sum, reduce |ℳ|² to three traces, T_AA, T_BB, T_AB.

4. Lesson 54's contraction identity reduces each trace, in the massless limit, to T_AA=T_BB=8tu and T_AB=−8tu.

5. The complete result is (1/4)Σ|ℳ|²=2e⁴[t/u+u/t−2], compact, symmetric under t↔u, and manifestly non-negative.

6. This same amplitude structure, related by crossing symmetry, gives Compton scattering's result in Lesson 56.

# Solutions

**1.** Lesson 48, Section 3.1 (as corrected), showed that ψ̄(x)=ψ†(x)γ⁰ contains a d_s(p) term, an annihilation operator for the positron, while ψ(x) contains only d_s†(p), a creation operator; since annihilating an incoming positron requires an annihilation operator, only ψ̄(x₂) can be contracted against it.

**2.** The two photon fields A_α(x₁) and A_β(x₂) are the only fields in the interaction term whose external assignment (to k₁ or k₂) is not already fixed by particle type (unlike the fermion fields, which are fixed by which annihilates an electron and which annihilates a positron); Wick's theorem therefore produces two distinct terms differing in which photon field is paired with which external photon momentum.

**3.** By direct substitution, q_A²=(p₁−k₁)·(p₁−k₁)=(p₁−k₁)², matching the definition t=(p₁−k₁)² given in the notation table; similarly q_B²=(p₁−k₂)²=u by definition.

**4.** Exchanging which vertex carries which photon index in ℳ_A's expression (α↔β) together with the corresponding internal momentum (q_A→q_B) gives ie·v̄(p₂)γ^α[i(q̸_B+m)/(q_B²−m²+iε)]γ^βu(p₁)ε_α(k₁)ε_β(k₂), matching Section 1.3's stated ℳ_B.

**5.** Σ_su_s(p)ū_s(p)=p̸+m (electron) and Σ_sv_s(p)v̄_s(p)=p̸−m (positron), both established in Lesson 21, Section 6.1; the positron's carries a minus sign because it was derived directly from the explicit block structure of the negative-energy (v-type) solutions to the free Dirac equation, a structural difference from the positive-energy u-type solutions, not an arbitrary convention.

**6.** Lesson 51, Section 5.3, explained that the covariant replacement Σε_με_ν→−g_μν is only justified by a partial, qualitative argument (that terms proportional to k^μ vanish against a conserved current), with the complete proof deferred to Lesson 71's Ward identity; using it here for a genuine physical calculation relies on that same provisional justification, not yet a completed proof.

**7.** γ^α(p̸₁+m)γ_α=γ^αp̸₁γ_α+mγ^αγ_α; the first term is γ^μγ^aγ_μ=−2γ^a applied with a↔p₁ (i.e. contracted with p₁'s components), giving −2p̸₁; the second uses γ^αγ_α=4 (Lesson 20), giving 4m; summing gives −2p̸₁+4m.

**8.** Expanding (q̸_A+m)(−2p̸₁+4m)(q̸_A+m) produces terms with zero, one, or two powers of m multiplying products of zero, one, two, or three slashed vectors; applying γ^β⋯γ_β to each (using the three-object reversal for the pure q̸_Ap̸₁q̸_A term, the two-object identity γ^μγ^aγ^bγ_μ=4g^ab for terms with two slashed factors, and the one-object identity for single slashed or scalar terms) reduces every term to a combination of slashed vectors and scalars ready for the final trace against (p̸₂−m), exactly the steps summarized in Section 3.2's stated massless result once m is set to zero (eliminating every term but the leading q̸_Ap̸₁q̸_A piece).

**9.** With p₁²=k₁²=0 (electron treated massless in this limit, photon exactly massless), t=(p₁−k₁)²=p₁²−2p₁·k₁+k₁²=−2p₁·k₁, so p₁·k₁=−t/2. Then q_A·p₁=(p₁−k₁)·p₁=p₁²−k₁·p₁=0−(−t/2)=t/2. Using t=(p₂−k₂)² as well (Lesson 52-style equivalent definition), p₂·k₂=−t/2 similarly, giving q_A·p₂=(p₁−k₁)·p₂=p₁·p₂−k₁·p₂; using k₁·p₂=p₂·k₁=−u/2 (from u=(p₂−k₁)²=−2p₂·k₁) and p₁·p₂=s/2, q_A·p₂=s/2−(−u/2)=(s+u)/2=−t/2 (using s+t+u=0). And q_A²=t directly from Exercise 3.

**10.** T_AA=32(t/2)(−t/2)−16t(s/2)=32(−t²/4)−8ts=−8t²−8ts=−8t(t+s). Using s+t+u=0, t+s=−u, giving T_AA=−8t(−u)=8tu.

**11.** By Worked example 3's relabeling (α↔β, q_A↔q_B, hence t↔u throughout Section 3.2's derivation), T_BB=32(q_B·p₁)(q_B·p₂)−16q_B²(p₁·p₂)=32(u/2)(−u/2)−16u(s/2)=−8u²−8us=−8u(u+s)=−8u(−t)=8ut=8tu, the identical numerical value.

**12.** Following Section 4.1's setup, the surviving massless term after applying the three-object reversal γ^βq̸_Ap̸₁q̸_Bγ_β=−2q̸_Bp̸₁q̸_A and taking the trace against (p̸₂−m) with m=0 gives, using the four-gamma trace identity (Lesson 20), Tr[q̸_Bp̸₁q̸_Ap̸₂]=4[(q_B·p₁)(q_A·p₂)−(q_B·q_A)(p₁·p₂)+(q_B·p₂)(q_A·p₁)]; multiplying by the accumulated prefactor of 4 from the −2 reversal combined with the trace's own overall 4 gives the stated 16[⋯] of Section 4.2.

**13.** q_A·q_B=(p₁−k₁)·(p₁−k₂)=p₁²−p₁·k₂−k₁·p₁+k₁·k₂=0−(−u/2)−(−t/2)+s/2=u/2+t/2+s/2=(s+t+u)/2=0, using s+t+u=0.

**14.** Σ|ℳ|²=e⁴[T_AA/t²+T_BB/u²+2T_AB/(tu)]=e⁴[8tu/t²+8tu/u²+2(−8tu)/(tu)]=e⁴[8u/t+8t/u−16]=8e⁴[u/t+t/u−2]. Dividing by 4 for the spin average over the two unmeasured initial spins (Section 2.2), (1/4)Σ|ℳ|²=2e⁴[t/u+u/t−2], matching Section 5 exactly.

**15.** For r>0, r+1/r−2=(√r−1/√r)²≥0 (a perfect square, since expanding (√r−1/√r)²=r−2+1/r), with equality only at r=1; so r+1/r≥2 for all r>0. For physical scattering, t<0 and u<0 (Lesson 52, Section 3.2, extended to this process's photon legs), so t/u=r is a ratio of two negative numbers, hence positive, and the inequality applies directly, confirming t/u+u/t−2≥0 and hence Section 5's formula is non-negative.

**16.** Crossing symmetry relates processes obtained from one another by moving a particle from the initial state to the final state (or vice versa) and reversing the sign of its four-momentum, since an outgoing particle of momentum k is treated, in the crossed process, as an incoming particle of momentum −k; comparing this lesson's process (incoming e⁻,e⁺; outgoing γ,γ) to Compton scattering (incoming e⁻,γ; outgoing e⁻,γ), moving the incoming positron to an outgoing electron and one outgoing photon to an incoming photon relates the two; the Mandelstam-like variables built from momentum differences would be expected to map into each other with some combination of sign flips and relabelings (for instance, this lesson's s, built from the two incoming momenta p₁+p₂, would be expected to correspond to a t-like or u-like variable of Compton scattering, built from a momentum difference instead of a sum, once the crossed momentum's sign is flipped), the precise details of which Lesson 56 works out explicitly.
