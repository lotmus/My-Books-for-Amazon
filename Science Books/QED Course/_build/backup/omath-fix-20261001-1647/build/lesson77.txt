TAGLINE: Lesson 68's running charge, turned into a modification of Coulomb's law at distances near the electron's Compton wavelength: the Uehling potential, its −27 MHz in hydrogen, and its dominance in muonic atoms and the muon's magnetic moment

# How to use this lesson

Lesson 68 described vacuum polarization as a running coupling, α(Q²), probed by scattering at high momentum transfer. Lesson 76 used the same effect at the opposite end of the scale, where it contributed −1/5 to hydrogen's 19/30 and −27 MHz to the 2s level. This lesson connects the two pictures. It Fourier-transforms Lesson 68's Π̂(q²) into a correction to Coulomb's law, the Uehling potential of 1935, derives its contact limit and the hydrogen shift, and then follows it to systems where it is not small: muonic hydrogen, where it is the largest part of the Lamb shift, and the muon's anomalous magnetic moment, where electron loops and hadronic loops both enter. The full radial form of the Uehling potential is quoted, and its limits are derived. Expect five to seven hours.

## Learning objectives

- Write the static potential of a point charge including vacuum polarization, Ṽ(Q) = −4πZα/[Q²(1 − Π̂(−Q²))].

- Use Π̂ ≈ (α/15π)Q²/m² to obtain the contact term δV = −(4Zα²/15m²)δ³(r), and derive the s-state shift −4α(Zα)⁴m/(15πn³).

- State the Uehling potential and its limits at distances much smaller and much larger than 1/m.

- Explain why vacuum polarization dominates the Lamb shift of muonic hydrogen, and estimate the scales involved.

- Identify the vacuum-polarization contributions to a_μ, including the electron loop and the hadronic contribution obtained from e⁺e⁻ data.

- Explain why vacuum polarization is a precision correction at long distance and a running coupling at short distance.

# 1 From Π̂ to a potential

## 1.1 The static potential

For a static nucleus of charge Ze, the exchanged photon carries no energy, so q² = −Q², with Q the magnitude of the three-momentum. The tree-level potential energy of an electron is the Fourier transform of −4πZα/Q², which is −Zα/r. Lesson 68 showed that vacuum polarization replaces α by α/(1 − Π̂). In momentum space,

$$ Ṽ[Q] = − 4πZα / (Q² [1 − Π̂[−Q²]]) ≈ − (4πZα/Q²) [1 + Π̂[−Q²]]

to first order in α.

## 1.2 The contact term

For Q ≪ m, Lesson 68, Section 4.1, gave Π̂ ≈ (α/15π)Q²/m². The correction is then

$$ δṼ[Q] = − (4πZα/Q²) (α/15π) (Q²/m²) = − 4Zα² / (15m²)

which is independent of Q. A constant in momentum space is a delta function in position space:

$$ δV[r] = − (4Zα² / 15m²) δ³[r]

This is the long-distance limit of vacuum polarization. To an atomic electron, whose wave function varies over distances much larger than 1/m, the correction looks like a point-like extra attraction at the nucleus.

## 1.3 The s-state shift

First-order perturbation theory gives ΔE = −(4Zα²/15m²)|ψ(0)|². With |ψ_ns(0)|² = (Zαm)³/(πn³),

$$ ΔE_ns = − 4α[Zα]⁴m / (15πn³)

and zero for l ≥ 1. This is exactly the −1/5 that Lesson 76 included in its 19/30, since (4α(Zα)⁴m/(3πn³)) × (−1/5) = −4α(Zα)⁴m/(15πn³).

### Worked example 1

Evaluate the shift for the 1s and 2s states of hydrogen. With α⁵m = 1.0594×10⁻⁵ eV, the 1s shift is −(4/15π) × 1.0594×10⁻⁵ eV = −8.99×10⁻⁷ eV, which is −217 MHz. The 2s shift is one-eighth of this, −27.2 MHz. Both are measured, as parts of the total Lamb shifts of about 8173 MHz and 1046 MHz for 1s and 2s. The sign is negative: vacuum polarization binds the electron more tightly, because near the nucleus the electron sees part of the unscreened charge.

# 2 The Uehling potential

## 2.1 The full form

Fourier-transforming the complete one-loop Π̂, not only its small-Q limit, gives the potential derived by Uehling in 1935:

$$ V[r] = − (Zα/r) [1 + (2α/3π) ∫₁^∞ du e^(−2mru) [1 + 1/(2u²)] (√[u² − 1])/u²]

The integral is quoted from the standard result, and its two limits are derived in Section 2.2. The variable u = √(s)/(2m) labels a virtual electron-positron pair of invariant mass √s ≥ 2m. Each pair contributes a Yukawa potential of range 1/(2mu). The lightest pairs, with mass 2m, set the range 1/(2m) ≈ 193 fm.

## 2.2 The two limits

At distances much larger than 1/m, the exponential suppresses everything except the region near u = 1, and

$$ V_U[r] ≈ − (Zα/r) (α / (4√π)) e^(−2mr) / [mr]^(3/2) qquad mr ≫ 1

At distances much smaller than 1/m, the integral grows logarithmically, and

$$ V_U[r] ≈ − (Zα/r) (2α/3π) [ln[1/(mr)] − γ_E − 5/6] qquad mr ≪ 1

with γ_E = 0.5772 Euler's constant. This short-distance logarithm is Lesson 68's running coupling seen in position space: at distance r, the effective α is larger by about (2α/3π)ln(1/(mr)), which matches (α/3π)ln(Q²/m²) with Q ≈ 1/r.

## 2.3 The picture

The two limits describe one physical object, a cloud of virtual pairs surrounding the charge with a size of about 1/(2m). Outside the cloud, the charge is fully screened and appears as α ≈ 1/137, with an exponentially small correction. Inside it, the probe sees more of the bare charge, and the effective coupling grows logarithmically. A hydrogen atom, whose size is about 5×10⁴ fm, lies far outside the cloud. That is why the contact approximation of Section 1.2 is accurate for it.

### Worked example 2

Check that the contact term reproduces the volume integral of the Uehling correction. Integrate δV over all space: ∫d³r δV(r) = δṼ(Q → 0) = −4Zα²/(15m²). For an atomic wave function that is nearly constant over the 1/m region, ⟨δV⟩ ≈ |ψ(0)|²∫d³r δV, which is the contact result. The approximation fails when the wave function varies over a distance of about 1/m, which is the situation of Section 3.

# 3 Muonic hydrogen

## 3.1 A smaller atom

In muonic hydrogen a muon replaces the electron. The reduced mass is m_r = m_μM_p/(m_μ + M_p) = 94.96 MeV, about 186 times the electron's, so the Bohr radius is 1/(αm_r) = 197.327 × 137.036/94.96 fm ≈ 285 fm. This is comparable to the vacuum-polarization range 1/(2m_e) ≈ 193 fm. The muon orbits inside the electron-positron cloud.

## 3.2 Vacuum polarization dominates

Two things change. The relevant loop is still the electron loop, because the electron is the lightest charged particle, but now the muon's momentum, about αm_r ≈ 0.7 MeV, is not small compared with m_e. The contact approximation fails, and the full Uehling potential must be used. More importantly, the effect is no longer suppressed by (Q/m)². In ordinary hydrogen it is α(Zα)⁴m_e/(15π), a small part of the Lamb shift. In muonic hydrogen it lowers 2s by about 205 meV relative to 2p, making it by far the largest QED contribution. The self-energy, which dominated ordinary hydrogen, is suppressed for the muon by its larger mass, because the muon's own Compton wavelength is small compared with the orbit.

## 3.3 The proton radius, revisited

The 2s–2p interval in muonic hydrogen is predicted as about 206.03 meV − 5.23 meV × (r_p/fm)², with smaller corrections. The measured 202.37 meV, from the Paul Scherrer Institute, gives r_p ≈ 0.841 fm. This value required the Uehling potential to be computed to a precision of a part in 10⁵, and it is the measurement behind Lesson 76's proton radius puzzle.

### Worked example 3

Estimate the ratio of the vacuum-polarization shift in muonic and electronic hydrogen, ignoring the failure of the contact approximation. The contact shift scales as |ψ(0)|²/m_e² ∝ m_r³. The ratio is (m_r,μ/m_r,e)³ ≈ 186³ ≈ 6.4×10⁶. Starting from 27.2 MHz = 1.12×10⁻⁷ eV, the estimate is 0.72 eV. The true value, 0.205 eV, is smaller by a factor of about 3.5, because the muon's wave function varies within the 193 fm cloud and does not sample the full contact strength. The estimate fails in the expected direction, and only the full Uehling potential gets the number right.

# 4 Vacuum polarization in the muon's magnetic moment

## 4.1 The electron loop

At two loops, the muon's anomaly includes the diagram in which the internal photon of Lesson 75's vertex correction carries an electron-loop insertion. The internal photon in the muon's vertex has momenta of order m_μ, at which the electron loop gives a large logarithm. The result is a coefficient of (α/π)² of about (1/3)ln(m_μ/m_e) − 25/36 ≈ 1.08, larger than all the mass-independent two-loop terms together. The complete two-loop coefficient for the muon is 0.7659, compared with −0.3285 for the electron. Its value is quoted, not derived.

## 4.2 The hadronic contribution

When the internal photon carries a loop of quarks and gluons, the hadronic vacuum polarization, perturbation theory cannot be used, for the reason given in Lesson 68, Section 6.2. Instead, a dispersion relation expresses the contribution as an integral over measured data:

$$ a_μ^had = (α²/3π²) ∫_(4m_π²)^∞ (ds/s) K[s] R[s]

where K(s) is a known function that weights low energies heavily. The result is about 7×10⁻⁸, which is more than 100 times the current experimental uncertainty in a_μ. Lattice QCD now computes the same quantity from first principles. Lesson 75, Section 5.5, described the comparison.

### Worked example 4

Estimate the electron-loop logarithm in a_μ. ln(m_μ/m_e) = ln 206.77 = 5.33, so (1/3)(5.33) − 25/36 = 1.777 − 0.694 = 1.083. Multiplied by (α/π)² = 5.40×10⁻⁶, this is 5.85×10⁻⁶, about 0.5 percent of a_μ. In a_e, the corresponding muon loop is suppressed by (m_e/m_μ)² and contributes only about 2.8×10⁻¹², because heavy loops decouple (Lesson 69, Section 4.3).

# 5 One effect, three regimes

Vacuum polarization appears in three regimes with different appearances. At distances much larger than 1/m, it is a contact term, measured in the Lamb shift of hydrogen to a fraction of a percent of its 27 MHz. At distances comparable to 1/m, it is the full Uehling potential, measured in muonic atoms, and in heavy muonic atoms through X-ray transitions that test it at the percent level. At distances much shorter than 1/m, it is the logarithmic running of α measured at colliders, Lesson 68. All three come from the same Π̂(q²), with no free parameters. That a single function fits all three is one of the strongest arguments that the loop is real.

# 6 What this lesson has not done

This lesson derived the contact term, its hydrogen shift, and the limits of the Uehling potential. It quoted the full Uehling integral, the muonic-hydrogen numbers, the two-loop coefficients of a_μ, and the dispersion relation for the hadronic contribution, with its kernel K(s). It did not compute higher-order vacuum polarization, such as the Källén–Sabry two-loop potential, or the light-by-light contributions.

# 8 Summary

1. For a static charge, vacuum polarization gives Ṽ(Q) = −4πZα/[Q²(1 − Π̂(−Q²))].

2. At small Q, Π̂ ≈ (α/15π)Q²/m² gives a contact attraction δV = −(4Zα²/15m²)δ³(r), which shifts s-states by −4α(Zα)⁴m/(15πn³): −27 MHz for 2s in hydrogen.

3. The Uehling potential describes a cloud of size about 1/(2m). Outside, the correction falls exponentially. Inside, it grows logarithmically, as Lesson 68's running α.

4. In muonic hydrogen the orbit lies inside the cloud, and vacuum polarization, about 205 meV, dominates the Lamb shift that measures the proton radius.

5. In a_μ, the electron loop gives a large logarithm, and the hadronic loop, taken from data or computed on the lattice, is the dominant theoretical uncertainty.

# Solutions

**1.** ∫d³Q/(2π)³ e^(iQ·r)(4π/Q²) = 1/r, the standard Coulomb transform, so −4πZα/Q² → −Zα/r.

**2.** ∫d³Q/(2π)³ e^(iQ·r)C = Cδ³(r).

**3.** δṼ = −(4πZα/Q²)(α/15π)(Q²/m²) = −4Zα²/(15m²), a constant. By Exercise 2, δV = −(4Zα²/15m²)δ³(r).

**4.** ΔE = −(4Zα²/15m²)(Zαm)³/(πn³) = −4α(Zα)⁴m/(15πn³). Lesson 76's prefactor is 4α(Zα)⁴m/(3πn³), and −1/5 of it is −4α(Zα)⁴m/(15πn³).

**5.** 1s: −(4/(15π)) × 1.0594×10⁻⁵ eV = −8.99×10⁻⁷ eV = −217 MHz. 2s: −27.2 MHz.

**6.** The shift scales as Z⁴. For Z = 10 it is about 10⁴ × 217 MHz ≈ 2.2 THz, and for Z = 50 about 6.25×10⁶ × 217 MHz ≈ 1.4 PHz. The Bohr radius scales as 1/Z: for Z = 50 it is about 1060 fm, still larger than 193 fm, but Zα ≈ 0.36 is no longer small. The contact approximation and the expansion in Zα both become inaccurate, and the full relativistic calculation is needed.

**7.** 1/(2m_e) = 197.327/(2 × 0.511) fm = 193 fm. The hydrogen Bohr radius is 52,900 fm, so it lies far outside. Muonic hydrogen's is 285 fm, which is comparable.

**8.** V ≈ −(Zα/r)[1 + (2α/3π)ln(1/(mr)) + const], so α_eff(r) = α[1 + (2α/3π)ln(1/(mr))]. With Q = 1/r, Lesson 68 gives α[1 + (α/3π)ln(Q²/m²)] = α[1 + (2α/3π)ln(1/(mr))]. The constants depend on how Q is matched to r.

**9.** m_r = 105.66 × 938.27/(105.66 + 938.27) MeV = 94.96 MeV. a = ħc/(αm_r) = 197.327 × 137.036/94.96 fm = 284.8 fm.

**10.** (186)³ × 1.12×10⁻⁷ eV ≈ 0.72 eV, against the true 0.205 eV. The contact approximation assumes that the wave function is constant over the vacuum-polarization cloud. In muonic hydrogen it is not, so the effective overlap is smaller.

**11.** 5.23(r/fm)² = 206.03 − 202.37 = 3.66, so (r/fm)² = 0.700 and r ≈ 0.837 fm. Smaller corrections omitted here move it to the quoted 0.841 fm.

**12.** (1/3)(5.33) − 0.694 = 1.083. Multiplied by (α/π)² = 5.40×10⁻⁶, this gives 5.85×10⁻⁶.

**13.** The kernel K(s)/s falls rapidly with s, roughly as m_μ²/s², so the integral is dominated by the lowest energies at which R(s) is large: the ρ resonance near 775 MeV, which accounts for about three-quarters of the total. That region is fully nonperturbative. It must be taken from precise measurements of e⁺e⁻ → π⁺π⁻, or from lattice QCD, and the precision of either one limits the muon g − 2 comparison.
