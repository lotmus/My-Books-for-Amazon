## Sell copy

The airlock still sticks. The radio answers minutes late. A slide on Earth is still selling a year as if a year were a landing gear.

*A Permit Is Not a City* is a popular book about Moon, Mars, and why leaving does not clean the kitchen you already staff. It starts with dirt, delay, and dates — measurements before moods — and refuses to fuse three nouns that posters love to marry: *sortie*, *outpost*, *settlement*. A flyby is not a town. A landable year is not a school district. A large cheap stack is a vehicle, and routine is the unpaid invoice.

The Moon comes first because it is close: three days, a second and a third on the radio, a classroom for landing, leaving, suits, and dust. What has already flown this century — loops, landers, sample returns, tip-overs in public — is taught before any founder’s year. Other flags and other ledgers raise the temperature of access. They do not, by themselves, raise the temperature of babies after the ships stop.

Mars is where the posters breed. Engineering keeps four drawers separate: window, EDL, ISRU, delay. Rhetoric welds them into a decade and a child. Most humans stay. Different damages keep different clocks. A few thousand settlers do almost nothing to the air. The bill of going — energy, metals, a politics of write-off — is paid at home.

Every claim wears a temperature. Hot means measured. Warm means a working program with gaps. Cold means a downtown sold as inventory. String theory, if named, cannot currently be tested; this book barely needs it. You do not need a physics degree. You need a willingness to let a downtown wait until a kitchen works.

This is Volume 2 of *Look First*. It is a complete book. You can stop here.
