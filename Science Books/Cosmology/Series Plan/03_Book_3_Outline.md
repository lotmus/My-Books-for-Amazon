# Book 3 Outline — *The Body Keeps Its Own Clock*

**Subtitle:** *Lifespan, Minds, and Why Ten Thousand Years Is Not a Straight Line*

**Promise:** What medicine and machines can actually do to us — without a fountain, an unused-brain unlock, or a 10,000-year exponential.

**Stands alone.** Temperatures are taught in Chapter 1. Book 1’s carrot (sex as shuffle, supernormal cue) may be pointed at, not assigned as homework. Forever-mind and Omega Point stay **out** and **cold**, as Book 1 already fired them.

**Queued questions that live here**

- Extrapolate last 50 years of tech / science / medicine to the next 10,000 years (Ch. 9–12, 16)
- How long will humans live with new meds and DNA fixes? Using “full brain” to adjust to new challenges? (Ch. 2–8, 13–15)

**Do not steal Book 1’s courtroom:** no CMB, no habitable-zone map, no Titan solvent paper (that stay is Ch. 30). Steal the woman, the temperatures, look-first (now: do not overwrite a childhood because a slide said enhancement).

Target: 16 chapters. Sample page: `06_Sample_Longevity_Brain.md`.

## Temperature-tagged claims

### Lifespan, meds, DNA

| Claim | Temp | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Lifespan and healthspan are different pots | **Hot** | You can add frail years. That is not the product people think they bought. |
| Aging is many clocks, not one fuse | **Hot-to-warm** | Mechanisms are a research field with real pieces; a unified “aging switch” is not in the kitchen yet. |
| Gene therapy / CRISPR as *local* fixes (a broken gene, a blood lineage, a rare enzyme) | **Hot** as a *class of win already in clinics or late trials* | Name the class, not a miracle brand. |
| DNA editing as a fountain that resets the body | **Cold** | Local is the word. The rest of the house still ages. |
| Typical human life to 150, 200, 1,000 years as a scheduled product | **Cold** | Allowed as a wish. Not selected by present levers. Mark it. |
| Modest further gains in *healthy* years this century in rich-country medicine | **Warm** | Continues a real, slowing curve if institutions hold. Not a law. |
| Everyone on Earth shares those gains | **Cold** as a default | Invoices are unequally paid. |

### Brain, “full capability,” new challenges

| Claim | Temp | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Humans already use their brains; the “10%” sentence is false | **Hot** | Imaging, lesion maps, energy budget. Do not dress the myth up as a future unlock. |
| Plasticity: practice, sleep, tools, other people change what a worldline can do | **Hot** | This is not spare unused tissue waiting for a password. |
| Pharmacological or device “enhancement” with small, messy effects | **Warm** | Stimulants, some implants, education-as-tech. Trade-offs. |
| Unlocking 90% unused mind to meet 10,000-year challenges | **Cold** | There is no tank. Do not sell one. |
| A second origin might hire a different mind (web, committee, no skull) | Courtesy only | That zoo lives in Book 1, Ch. 30. One sentence, then return to *this* ape. |

### 10,000-year tech / science / medicine

| Claim | Temp | Note |
|---|---|---|
| The last ~50 years saw huge, uneven gains (compute, sequencing, vaccines, packets) | **Hot** | Real. Not a proof of the next 200×. |
| Many gains are S-curves: fast middle, then walls | **Hot** as a *pattern* | Energy, materials, thermodynamics, attention, regulation, war. |
| Last 50 years × 200 = year 12,000’s kitchen | **Cold** | Arithmetic abuse. 10,000 / 50 = 200 is not a physical method. |
| A gadget catalog for the year 12,000 | **Cold** | We cannot name the furniture. We can name the *bills* that will still be due. |
| Energy, coordination, and biology’s many clocks still constrain whatever is invented | **Warm** | The honest extrapolation is invoices, not a skyline. |
| Progress is guaranteed if we wait | **Cold** | Institutions fail. Knowledge can be lost. The loaf does not owe a sequel. |

## Recurring scene

She names the basil and logs the pH. In this book a capsule arrives on the same delay as the orbiter’s voice. It may keep a joint useful. It will not make her immortal. She does not have ninety percent of a mind in the cupboard. She has a morning.
