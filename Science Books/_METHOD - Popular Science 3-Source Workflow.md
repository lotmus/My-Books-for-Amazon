# Popular science book workflow (3-source method)

This is the workflow to follow for new work in this folder; nothing below has been applied to the existing manuscripts yet._

---

Popular science differs from pure textbooks: voice, narrative drive, analogies, rhetorical force, and reader engagement matter far more than formal rigor or exercise sets. The 3-source method can still work, but it needs clear adjustments.
Chronological steps (AI and Manual)

Manual: Select 3 strong popular science books on the same topic (preferably with different styles or emphases).
AI: Condense each book to its core argumentative and explanatory spine (~40–60 pages). Instruct: keep the essential claims, key evidence, central analogies, and major rhetorical moves; discard secondary anecdotes and repetition; do not copy distinctive wording.
Manual + AI: Review each condensation for fidelity to the original argument and note which analogies or explanatory devices are especially powerful or distinctive.
AI: Create a unified glossary of terms and concepts across the three condensations.
AI: Rewrite the three condensations using consistent terminology.
Manual: Finalize the shared conceptual vocabulary.
AI: Merge the three equalized versions into one coherent narrative outline + draft, combining the strongest explanatory threads.
Manual: Redesign the overall architecture and narrative arc so it does not follow any single source’s chapter order or rhetorical progression.
Manual + AI: For every major section, generate alternative explanations and analogies with AI, then heavily rewrite them yourself. Replace distinctive original analogies with new ones of equal or greater clarity.
Manual: Rebuild the voice and rhetorical stance from scratch. Popular science lives or dies on personality and persuasive energy — this cannot be left to AI.
Manual: Invent or carefully redesign narrative hooks, transitions, and moments of wonder/insight so they feel original.
AI: Run a similarity audit against the three original condensations. Flag residual parallels in:
Choice and framing of analogies
Argumentative sequence and rhetorical moves
Distinctive explanatory devices
Overall narrative rhythm

Manual: Eliminate every flagged similarity through rewriting or replacement.
Manual: Add clear original contribution (new framing, updated science, different emphasis, fresh examples from recent research, personal synthesis, etc.).
Manual: Final pass focused on voice, pacing, emotional/intellectual engagement, and independence from the sources.
Manual: Professional editing and legal review before commercial release.

Key refinements versus the textbook version

Far more weight on voice and rhetorical originality (almost entirely manual).
Analogies and explanatory devices must be actively replaced, not just rephrased.
Narrative architecture and reader engagement become central manual tasks.
“Original value” is more about fresh synthesis, framing, and storytelling than about new exercises or formal completeness.
Similarity auditing focuses on pedagogical/rhetorical fingerprints rather than problem sets or formal structure.

Realistic expectation
With these refinements the method can produce a commercially defensible popular science book that is better structured and more content-rich than a pure from-scratch AI attempt. Success still depends heavily on the quality of the manual rewriting of voice, analogies, and narrative drive. The AI handles volume and consistency; the human supplies the aliveness and originality that readers actually buy.

Time-savers

Decide the voice and rhetorical stance before merging. Write a one-page voice guide (tone, attitude, level of wit, how much personal address, etc.) and force every AI generation to follow it. This prevents later massive voice rewrites.
Replace only the most distinctive or famous analogies. Serviceable but non-iconic ones can often be lightly reworked instead of fully reinvented.
Build the narrative arc at outline level first (manual, 1–2 hours). Then let AI fill sections inside that fixed arc. This stops structural thrashing.
Do one focused “aliveness” pass at the end rather than trying to make every paragraph lively during drafting. Fix engagement in a single dedicated sweep.

General Time-savers

Limit sources to 2 instead of 3 when the topic is mature and well-standardized. The third book adds diminishing returns and extra condensation/audit work.
Work in layered passes, not perfect first drafts
First AI pass = rough condensation + merge.
Second pass = nomenclature + structure.
Third pass = only the sections that failed the similarity or quality check.
Avoid polishing everything equally.
Create a reusable “style and constraints” document once (target length, tone, notation rules, forbidden close patterns). Feed it to the AI at every step so you stop re-explaining.
Batch the similarity audits
Run one strong AI audit on the full merged draft rather than section-by-section audits early on. Only do detailed manual fixes on flagged parts.
Use AI for first-pass redesign of examples/analogies, then only manually upgrade the weakest 30–40%. You do not need to hand-craft every single one.
Set hard time boxes per stage (e.g., 90 minutes max for reviewing one condensation). Perfect is the enemy of finished.

Highest-leverage overall strategy

1. AI does almost all volume work (condensation, equalization, first merge, first alternative explanations, similarity flagging).  
2. You only touch the material that affects legal safety, technical correctness, or reader experience.  
3. Everything else stays at “competent AI draft” level.

Used this way, many people cut total human hours by roughly 40–60% compared with the full careful version of the method, while still producing a commercially defensible result. The biggest remaining time costs will almost always be: (1) fixing technical precision and (2) restoring voice/engagement in popular science.
