The Wee Scallywag and the Gentle Elf
Book 1 of The Step Stories
by Otis Umberlight

Wilf came up the path with the slate under one arm and the wolf baby at his heel. The baby had decided the arrangement was permanent. Titch sat on the step, turning a hem that had begun the morning offended and was now merely precise. Sparefunk arrived wearing one pair of boots, carrying the spare, and balancing three tyres he treated as unexpected but welcome. Hopsi set her bowl of hops beside her and threaded the needle.

“Finished?” said Sparefunk.
“Yes,” said Wilf.
“Still annoying?”
“Yes. But done. The baby thinks the walk is the best part.”

The wolf baby took the crust Titch offered, inspected a hop, declined it with a small sneeze, and sat firmly on Wilf’s foot. A moonbeam crossed the roof, cancelled every wrinkle it had tried, and lay straight on the wood. No one reached. In the grass the rectangular eggs kept their corners, unconcerned.

They did the day’s small work. Wilf lifted the stone the current had nudged back into the channel. Titch made the hem lie flat and stay flat. Sparefunk stacked the tyres so they could not roll and set the spare boots where a hurry could find them. Hopsi stitched, ate a hop, stitched again. The axle bird landed, sang four clear notes, mentioned worms as an afterthought, and left before anyone could answer.

A soft voice came from the road. Sweets, it said. A quicker way. Hopsi raised her eyes, looked at the face, and shut her mouth. Wilf said, “Run.” They ran — slate, bowl, spare boots, wolf baby — only as far as the bend where the step was still visible. The stranger remained on the road. They sat in the grass, finished the crust between them, and watched the ordinary light. Behind them the channel ran clear and even.

Later they walked on. The path followed the stream. The mill grew smaller but did not disappear. Sparefunk carried the spare boots without shifting them. Hopsi offered the last hops; only she ate them. Titch folded the crust cloth. At the rise they stopped. The way back lay open behind them. The way forward waited, unhurried.

They sat while the light thinned. The wolf baby claimed Wilf’s boot and closed its eyes. No one spoke. Tomorrow the slate would be marked, the hops would be found, the tyres might increase, and both the step and the path would still be there.

The stream continued beside them, as it always had.

The End
