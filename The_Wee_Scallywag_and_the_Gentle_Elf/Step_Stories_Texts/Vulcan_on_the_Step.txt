They kept the practical logic of the step.

Sparefunk trimmed the story until it held only what was true: one mill, uneven grain, wet boots. Hopsi shut her mouth when a soft voice offered sweets; the silence was the logical act. Wilf said “Run” because running to the bend served the needs of the four and Boot. Titch set the crust out evenly. No one announced a philosophy.

The rectangular eggs kept their corners — infinite small differences, none requiring a speech. The grain stood taller where the light reached. Boot chose the foot because it was warm and reliable. The spare boots stayed ready. The channel stayed clear.

People elsewhere talked too much. On the step the needs of the moment were met with whatever lay nearest: needle, stone, shut mouth, or the short run. That was enough.

The stream continued, logically.
