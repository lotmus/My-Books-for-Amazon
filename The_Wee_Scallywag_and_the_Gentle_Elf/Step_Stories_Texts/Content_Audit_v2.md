# Content audit of v2 (story, science, characters, ending, twists)

Read as four people: a 6–9 reader (and the adult reading aloud), a harsh critic, a science fact-checker, and an Amazon browser. Scores out of 10. v3 is the fix.

## Overall: v2 scores 5.5 / 10. It is lively but not yet a story.

## 1. Story and plot (4/10)
- **Names the secret on page 1.** The narrator calls him "Sparefunk" from the first page, so "guess his true name" has no mystery. The final leaf "reads the true name" with no clue trail. The reader has been ahead of Wilf the whole way.
- **No cause between scenes.** Messengers, ghost, toad, pike, stranger arrive one after another with no chain. Swap any two chapters and nothing changes.
- **Weak stakes.** Why must the wheel turn? The king's men are never given an order. The "small thing besides" in the bargain is never paid or explained.
- **Dangling threads.** The stuck wheel is never un-stuck. The tyres, rectangular eggs and the king are never paid off.
- **The stranger is a pantomime villain.** He appears, is stopped by stones, and leaves. Why him, why now? No connection to anyone.
- **Pace.** Too much plain narration of "what they did", not enough decisions. The children never choose anything difficult.

## 2. Characters (5/10)
- No arcs. Wilf, Titch, Tabitha end exactly as they began. Tabitha arrives on day three and does nothing the others could not.
- Sparefunk is only cheeky. Why does he make bargains? Why those boots? Nothing under the surface.
- Hopsi's "speaks only what is true" is a fine rule but is never tested. A rule is not an arc until it costs her.
- Titch has a hem and a crust and no problem. Boot is a prop.
- The ghost has no name, no history, no connection to Wilf (the story calls the mill "the mill" and Wilf "Wilf Millersen" without noticing the obvious).
- Voices all sound the same in the dialogue; the bible's voices are not yet on the page.

## 3. Science (5/10; magic and fact mixed)
- **Wrong or shaky:** "He ran so fast the sun stood still" reads as real relativity. It is not; nothing a boy can do changes time. The "moonbeam can't be caught" line is poetry, not physics.
- **Tunnelling (correction, 11 Oct):** an earlier note of mine said to cut it. That was wrong. Quantum tunnelling is real and belongs in the book (the original chat summary lists a culvert scene). The risk is only the line between fact and magic: real tunnelling happens to very small things, never to a boy. A ghost passing through a wall is the magic version. v2 has the ghost go through the wall with no fact beside it, so the real physics is missing. v3 states the real fact (through Hopsi, who only says true things), ties it to why the sun shines, and uses it in the plot.
- **Correct but lost:** the leaf rhyme about variation, the eight-minute sunlight, the lever, the storm. They appear and are not explained, tested, or used to solve anything.
- **No science does any work in the plot.** In a good STEM story the science wins the fight.
- **Fix.** Make Hopsi (who can only say true things) the book's fact-checker. Every fact she states is real. Everything magic is stamped or marked as fantasy. Use the science to solve the plot (see v3), and add a "Hopsi's True Things" back page with five small experiments.

## 4. Ending (4/10)
- A feast, a settling, a closing image. Nothing is won, lost or learned. The bargain "closes" with no cost.
- v3 ending: the bargain's "small thing" turns out to be a place on the step. The wheel turns. The ghost goes home. One egg rocks.

## 5. Twists (3/10): there are none
v3 has four, each planted early:
1. The "something stuck in the wheel" is the Soft Voice's bag, put there by Sparefunk. He was the stranger's errand imp.
2. The Soft Voice's "quick way" is the shortest route and the slowest. The true path is quickest. (Light does this too: least time.)
3. The ghost is Wilf's great-great-grandfather, lost on the Soft Voice's "quick way" over the bog two hundred years ago.
4. The "one small thing" in the bargain was a place to stay. "It's in the small print" comes back at the end.

## 6. Voice, language, length (6/10)
- The deadpan is good in places and vanishes in the action chapters. Keep short sentences. Add the bible's smells and voices.
- Some repeated sentences between chapters. Cut (rule: no sentence recurs inside a book).
- About 3,000 words for ages 6–9 is fine; v3 is about 5,500 with chapter breaks short enough to read at bedtime.

## 7. Marketing and the KDP blurb
- The blurb promises "relativity, QED, genetics". v3 delivers lever, light bending, quickest path, inheritance and selection, sunlight delay, weather, and one honest line on time and speed. Update the blurb to match.

## Checks done on v3 (below)

- Hair: as in the bible.
- No Word tables; American or British? The old drafts are British ("colour", "grey"); v3 stays British.
