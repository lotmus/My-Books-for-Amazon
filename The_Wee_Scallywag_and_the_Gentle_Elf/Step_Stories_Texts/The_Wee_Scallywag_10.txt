The Wee Scallywag and the Gentle Elf
Book 1 of The Step Stories
by Otis Umberlight

Wilf came up the path with the slate under his arm and the wolf baby at his heel. The baby had firm opinions about boots and crusts and none whatever about school. Titch sat on the step turning a hem that had offended her earlier. Sparefunk wore one pair of boots, set the spare pair neatly aside, and stacked two new tyres he had found; he treated them as reasonable gifts. Hopsi ate hops from her bowl and stitched with the calm of someone who preferred thread to talk.

“Finished?” said Sparefunk.
“Yes,” said Wilf.
“Still annoying?”
“Yes. But done.”

The wolf baby took the crust Titch offered, sniffed a hop, declined it, and sat on Wilf’s foot to settle the matter. A moonbeam tried three wrinkles over the roof, cancelled them, and arrived straight on the wood. No one reached for it. The rectangular eggs in the grass kept their corners.

They worked. Wilf cleared one more stone so the channel stayed fair. Titch finished the hem. Sparefunk counted tyres and left the spare boots ready. Hopsi closed a seam and shut her mouth when a soft voice drifted from the road.

The stranger appeared — nice smile, sweets, a quicker way. Hopsi looked at his face and stayed silent. Wilf said, “Run.” They ran, wolf baby included, only as far as the bend where the step could still be seen. The stranger did not follow. They sat, shared what crust remained, and let their breathing slow. The light stayed ordinary. Behind them the cleared channel ran clean.

By evening they had walked the new path far enough to choose. The mill was smaller. The spare boots were still ready. The hops were fewer. The wolf baby claimed Wilf’s boot. No longer speech was needed. The stream continued beside them, and the way back remained open.

Tomorrow would still be possible.

The End
