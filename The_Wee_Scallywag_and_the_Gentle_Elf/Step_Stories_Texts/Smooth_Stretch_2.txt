The next morning the wolf baby was on the step before anyone else. It had claimed the place and saw no reason to renegotiate. Wilf arrived with a fresh slate. Titch came with the crust already broken. Sparefunk appeared in the worn boots and glanced at the spare pair as if checking that the furniture had not moved. Hopsi brought the bowl of hops and the needle.

They settled. The wolf baby took the crust, considered the tyres, and decided against them. Hopsi ate three hops and began on a seam that had come loose overnight. Wilf looked at the channel; the stone he had moved was still moved. Sparefunk counted the tyres. The number had not changed, which he accepted as a temporary condition.

A moonbeam, early and thin, tried one short wrinkle above the wheel and cancelled it at once. What remained lay straight across the boards. No one reached. The rectangular eggs kept their corners. The axle bird landed, sang four notes, mentioned gravel in a half-hearted way, and left before the sentence was finished.

Titch’s hem progressed. Hopsi’s seam closed. Wilf set the slate where it would not be stepped on. Sparefunk left the spare boots exactly where they stood. The wolf baby yawned, inspected Wilf’s boot, and arranged itself for later. The stream continued at the same unhurried rate it had used the day before.

No stranger came up the path. This was noticed and not discussed. The light stayed ordinary. Everyone remained quiet enough for the work that was actually present.

By evening the slate was still unmarked because school had not yet required it, the hops were fewer, the seam was finished, and the wolf baby had claimed the boot on schedule. The spare pair stood dry. The tyres waited. Tomorrow would bring whatever small difference it chose to bring, and the step would meet it in the same order.
