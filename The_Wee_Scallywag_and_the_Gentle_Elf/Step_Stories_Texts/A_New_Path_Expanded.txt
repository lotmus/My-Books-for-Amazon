A New Path
Book 2 of The Step Stories
by Otis Umberlight

They left the step at dawn. Wilf carried the slate. Titch carried the folded crust cloth. Sparefunk wore the walking boots and carried the spare pair. Hopsi carried the needle and a bowl with enough hops for the morning. Boot trotted between them, larger than the boot it once slept in.

The path followed the stream. At the first bend the mill was still visible. At the second it slipped behind the rise and reappeared only when they turned and waited. The light that lay across the path had left the sun eight minutes earlier. No one counted.

They sat. Titch set out the crust in even pieces. Boot took its share and declined a hop. Sparefunk set the spare boots down so the mud could dry at the edges. Hopsi ate two hops and stitched a small tear in the cloth. Wilf looked at the slate; it would need marking when they returned, or if they returned.

A tyre lay half-hidden in the grass. Sparefunk stacked it with the ones he already carried in his head and left it. No soft voice came from the road. The rectangular eggs were far behind.

By afternoon the ground was damper. The coast was still further. They reached a flat place and sat again. The way back had not closed. The way ahead had not demanded them. Boot drank from the stream and returned to Wilf’s foot. No speech grew long.

They stayed until the light thinned. Tomorrow the same choice would wait under light that was already travelling.

The stream continued beside them.
