Wilf came up the path with the slate under his arm and the wolf baby at his heel. The baby had decided the step was reliable and Wilf’s boot was better than the stream edge for sleeping. Titch was already sitting, turning a hem that had apparently offended her. Sparefunk wore one pair of boots and left the other standing empty, as if the empty pair were part of the furniture. Hopsi ate hops from a small bowl and stitched with the steady attention of someone who preferred thread to talk.

“Finished?” said Sparefunk.

“Yes,” said Wilf.

“Annoying?”

“Still.”

“But done.”

Wilf set the slate down. The wolf baby sniffed the place where crusts appeared. Titch put half of one there without comment. The baby took it, examined Sparefunk’s spare boots, and left them alone. Hopsi bit her thread and offered the bowl. No one wanted hops.

A moonbeam came over the roof, tried a wrinkled route, cancelled it, and settled straight on the step. No one reached. The rectangular eggs kept their corners in the grass. The axle bird sang three plain notes, mentioned worms, and forgot the rest. Sparefunk counted the tyres stacked beside the step. There was one more than yesterday. He did not ask where it had come from.

Wilf moved a stone so the channel ran clear. Titch’s hem grew shorter by a careful stitch. Hopsi folded the bit she had finished. The wolf baby lay down with its nose on Wilf’s boot. The light thinned. The spare boots stayed where they were. No one spoke more than the moment needed.

The stream continued. Tomorrow the slate would be marked again, the hops would be there, and at least one tyre would probably have arrived. The step would still be the step.
