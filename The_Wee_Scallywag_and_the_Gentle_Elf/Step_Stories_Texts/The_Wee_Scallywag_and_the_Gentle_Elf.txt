The Wee Scallywag and the Gentle Elf
Wilf, Titch, Sparefunk, Hopsi, and the Wolf Baby

Wilf came up the path with the slate under his arm and the wolf baby at his heel. The baby had decided the step was reliable and Wilf’s boot was better than the stream edge for sleeping. Titch was already sitting, turning a hem. Sparefunk wore one pair of boots and left the other standing empty. Hopsi ate hops from a small bowl and stitched.

“Finished?” said Sparefunk.
“Yes,” said Wilf.
“Annoying?”
“Still.”
“But done.”

Wilf set the slate down. The wolf baby took the crust Titch had set out. Hopsi offered hops. No one wanted them. A moonbeam tried a wrinkled route, cancelled it, and settled straight on the step. No one reached. The rectangular eggs kept their corners. The axle bird sang and forgot to advertise. Sparefunk counted the tyres. There was one more than yesterday.

Wilf cleared a stone from the channel. The teapot cloud had rained only on the shorter stalks; the next water would be fairer. Titch finished the hem. Hopsi closed the seam. The wolf baby claimed the boot. The light thinned. The spare boots stayed empty. No stranger came. No vampire was present. The moonshine ghost, being only unfinished light, cancelled with the rest.

They stayed until sleep was reasonable. People talked too much and did too little elsewhere. On the step the small true things had been done: the channel cleared, the crust shared, the boots ready, the seam mended, the mouth shut when shutting it was useful.

The stream continued. Tomorrow the slate, the hops, the tyres, and the step would still be there.

The End

Cast
Wilf Millersen — miller’s son
Titch Mitchel — neighbour’s daughter
Sparefunk — the wee scallywag
Hopsi (Elfie Didlhop) — the gentle elf
Wolf baby — domesticated, sleeps on the boot

For ages 6–9. Mostly text. Deadpan. Quiet.
