Afternoon brought a small difference. The teapot-shaped cloud appeared, precise as ever, and rained only on the shorter stalks. Wilf watched it, then cleared one more stone from the channel so the next ordinary water would not copy the cloud’s preference. The taller stalks stayed dry. He left them to it.

Titch set out the crust. The wolf baby took it and sat. Sparefunk added nothing to the tyre count and did not move the spare boots. Hopsi ate hops, finished a second seam, and offered the bowl again. The rectangular eggs kept their corners. A moonbeam arrived late, cancelled a long wrinkle behind the chimney, and settled straight. The axle bird sang, mentioned nothing useful, and departed.

No one ran. No one reached for the light. The stream continued. By the time the cloud had gone the grain looked slightly less uneven, which Wilf noted and did not announce. Titch’s hem was done. Hopsi put the needle away. Sparefunk regarded his two pairs of boots as a solved arrangement. The wolf baby claimed Wilf’s boot early.

The light thinned. Everyone was quiet enough. Tomorrow the slate would probably be marked, the hops would be replenished, and at least one tyre might have arrived without explanation. The step would still be there to meet them.
