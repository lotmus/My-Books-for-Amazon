The Wee Scallywag and the Gentle Elf
Book 1 of The Step Stories
by Otis Umberlight

The mill stood by the stream. Wilf Millersen sat on the step with his slate. Boot, the wolf baby, lay across his foot. Boot had once been small enough to sleep inside the spare boot; now it was larger and chose the foot instead. Titch Mitchel turned a hem that had started the day offended and was now merely precise. Sparefunk wore one pair of boots and left the spare pair standing empty, ready. Hopsi ate hops from her bowl and stitched.

“Finished?” said Sparefunk.
“Yes,” said Wilf.
“Still annoying?”
“Yes. But done. The walk is the part Boot likes.”

Boot took the crust Titch set out evenly, declined a hop, and settled. A moonbeam tried its wrinkles over the roof, cancelled them, and arrived straight. No one reached. In the grass the rectangular eggs kept their corners.

They did the small work. Wilf moved the stone so the water would run true; the teapot cloud had been selective, and the channel made the next flow fairer. Titch finished the hem and kept the cloth. Sparefunk stacked the tyres so they would not roll and left the spare boots where a hurry could find them. Hopsi closed the seam, offered the bowl, accepted the refusal, and put the needle where morning would find it. The axle bird sang four notes, mentioned gravel, and forgot.

A soft voice came from the road. Sweets. A quicker way. Hopsi looked at the face and shut her mouth. Wilf said, “Run.” They ran — slate, hops, spare boots, Boot — only as far as the bend where the step could still be seen. The stranger did not follow. They sat, shared what crust remained, and let the light stay ordinary. Behind them the channel ran clean.

Later they walked the path. The mill grew smaller. The eight-minute light from the sun lay across the flat place. Boot trotted and returned to the foot. No one chose yet. The way back stayed open. The wetter coast stayed further.

They sat until the brightness thinned. Sparefunk set the spare boots side by side. Hopsi put the needle through her sleeve. Titch folded the cloth. Wilf kept the slate dry. Boot slept.

People elsewhere talked too much and did too little. On the step and on the path the small true things were done. Tomorrow the slate would be marked, the hops would be found, the tyres might increase, and both directions would still be possible.

The stream continued.

The End
