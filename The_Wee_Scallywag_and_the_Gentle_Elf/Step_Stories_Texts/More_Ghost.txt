The moonshine ghost tried again at dusk. It hovered over the shorter stalks, pale and unfinished, while the proper beam had already settled straight on the step. Wilf watched the water move cleanly through the cleared channel and left the ghost to its cancelling. Titch broke the crust evenly. Sparefunk ran the field edge; the ghost parted and closed behind the boots like a curtain that had not decided. Hopsi ate two hops, shut her mouth, and stitched the last open seam. The wolf baby sniffed the air, found nothing worth chasing, and claimed the boot.

The rectangular eggs kept their corners. The spare tyres stayed stacked. No stranger came. The spirits of uneaten paths and unrained stalks moved through the grain and left no mark the children needed to answer. By the time the light was thin the ghost had gone the way of the wrinkles. What remained was the step, the four of them, the wolf baby, and the ordinary moon.

They stayed until sleep was reasonable. Tomorrow the slate, the hops, the boots, and the channel would still be ready. The stream continued, unhaunted and sufficient.
