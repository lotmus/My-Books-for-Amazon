# Message for a teacher or parent reader (ready to copy)

**Subject:** Could you read a children's book before it is published? About 20 minutes.

Hello,

I am about to publish a children's fantasy adventure, *The Wee Scallywag and the Gentle Elf* (ages 5 to 9, read aloud or read alone). It has a miller's son, a cheeky little devil called Scally, an elf who only says what is true, a ghost, a wolf pup and a stranger with sweets. Real science runs through it: levers, rainbows, genes, light in water, the Moon and the tides, and what fire needs. It also teaches safety: strangers, fire, water and roads.

I would be very grateful for an honest read from someone who knows children. Please don't be polite. I am looking for:

1. Anything too frightening for a 5 or 6 year old (the stranger, the fire, the burnt mill).
2. Any science that is wrong or confusing. The analogies are recipe cards for genes, a marble and a hill for tunnelling, a barrow wheel for light bending in water, and dough for tides.
3. Whether the safety messages are clear and right (No, Go, Yell, Tell; a family code word; "shout, get out, get a grown-up" for fire; "reach or throw" for water).
4. Whether a child could follow the story from the pictures alone (there are 67 painted pictures).
5. Anything you would cut, shorten or add.

It is about 10,600 words, roughly 70 to 80 minutes aloud, so feel free to skim chapters 1, 5, 7 and 9, which hold most of the science and the safety scenes. I have attached a one-page checklist of the claims I already checked (Teacher_Check.md) so you can tell me where you disagree.

Thank you!
