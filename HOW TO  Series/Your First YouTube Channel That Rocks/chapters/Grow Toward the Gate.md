*A working guide for creators who want a channel, not a hobby*

# Grow Toward the Gate

*The gate is arithmetic: subscribers, plus hours or Shorts views, inside a rolling window. Here is what moves those numbers, and what only looks like it does.*

*Layer: Use after publishing. Workbook: the week sheet for the counts, the decision log for each tactic you test.*

The thresholds in chapter 9 are not a mood. They are counts with windows. A channel that knows the arithmetic can tell whether it is a month from the gate or three years from it, and can decide what to change. A channel that does not know it posts, waits, and checks the Earn tab like a lottery result.

None of the tactics below costs money, and none is a formula YouTube publishes. Each one is labeled.

## I. The arithmetic of the gate

Work the numbers once, with your own average view duration from chapter 7.

> Worked example. These are planning numbers, not a forecast. 4,000 hours is 240,000 minutes. If the average long-form view on your channel lasts 4 minutes, that is 60,000 views in a year, about 165 a day, every day. A channel that applies on or after 1 February 2027 needs 8,000 hours: 120,000 views at the same 4 minutes, about 330 a day. Raise the average view to 6 minutes and the 8,000-hour bar needs 80,000 views, about 220 a day. On the Shorts route, 10 million qualified views in 90 days is about 111,000 a day, and the 2027 route of 20 million is about 222,000 a day.

Every input in that arithmetic — which hour bar applies, where the Shorts floor sits — is a number YouTube can move without warning you first.

> **Thresholds change.** Before planning around any monetization target, check the live Earn tab in YouTube Studio and YouTube’s official Partner Program eligibility page for your country. This book reflects information checked in October 2026.

Two lessons sit inside that arithmetic.

First, view duration is a lever as strong as views. Moving the average view from 4 minutes to 6 cuts the views needed by a third. That is chapter 4’s work — the opening, the pace, the hard step slowed down — and it costs no reach at all.

Second, the routes are different sizes. In the example above, the long-form route needs 165 to 330 views a day. The 2027 Shorts route needs about 222,000. That is not a rule against Shorts. It is a reason to choose the route on purpose.

## II. Pick a route, and keep the counters separate

Long-form means public videos whose hours accumulate. Shorts means public Shorts in the Shorts feed, counted as views. A channel that posts both still has to choose which number it is trying to fill, because the two counters never mix (official rule).

If the route is long-form, a Short’s job is to send a viewer to a long video: the related-video link and the spoken pointer from chapter 2. If the route is Shorts, the long videos still help: they hold the viewers who want more, and they are where the hours accumulate in case the route changes.

Shorts money also has its own floor from 1 February 2027 (chapter 11, §V). For a channel below that floor, that makes long-form the near-term income route either way.

## III. Ask for the subscription with a reason

Subscribers are half of every gate, and they are the people the subscriptions feed shows your next video to. Ask for them the way you would ask for anything else: once, with a reason, at the right moment.

- **State a reason to subscribe, tied to the next video.** “Next week is the tile line, the one most quotes leave out. Subscribe if you want it.” Creator heuristic: a reason beats a bare “subscribe.” YouTube publishes no comparison. Test it.
- **Say it once, and not in the opening.** Author recommendation: one line near the end, tied to the next video.
- **Use a subscribe link where you share the channel elsewhere.** A channel address that ends in `?sub_confirmation=1` opens a subscribe prompt. Creator heuristic: it converts better than asking a visitor to find the button. Treat it as a test.
- **Pair the end screen’s subscribe element with a video element.** A viewer who goes on to a second video has a second chance to subscribe, and adds watch time either way.

## IV. Make one video lead to the next

Ten minutes watched counts as ten minutes toward the hour bar whether it happens in one video or across three. A path between videos is the cheapest growth a channel has.

- **End screens.** [YouTube’s end screen page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6388789) allows them in the last 5 to 20 seconds of a video at least 25 seconds long. Point one at the next video in watch order, by name, not at “best for viewer” on every video.
- **Series playlists.** Chapter 2 has the setting. YouTube says a series playlist lets other videos in the series be featured when someone is watching one of them.
- **A named next video inside the video.** Chapter 7, §IV showed how few viewers reach the end screen. Say the next video’s name where the graph is still high, in a sentence that belongs in the teaching: “If your quote has no tile line at all, that is the next video.”
- **Pinned comments on long videos.** Once advanced features are on, a long-form comment is clickable. Pin a link to the next video in watch order.

## V. Premieres and live streams

A premiere publishes a video at a set time with a live chat beside it. A live stream is the chat with you in it. Both do two jobs for a growing channel: they give subscribers a reason to show up at one hour, and they rehearse the room where Super Chat and Super Stickers will one day be switched on, which chapter 12 covers.

One rule matters for the count. YouTube’s eligibility page says hours from live streams that are unlisted, deleted, or not converted to video on demand do not count. A public stream that stays up as a video keeps its hours. For a premiere or a live stream, YouTube’s recommendation page says to consider when your audience is active (chapter 2, §V). Pick the hour your viewers can attend.

## VI. What does not count

Some ways to move a number do not move the gate.

- **Paid promotion of your own videos.** [YouTube’s monetization overview](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/94522) says views and watch time from paid ad campaigns do not count toward the thresholds. Only follow-on views do: organic views from people who watch more of your videos after seeing the promoted one. An ad can introduce a channel. It cannot buy the hours.
- **Bought views, subscribers, or likes.** YouTube’s [fake engagement policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3399767) covers them. Chapter 10 explains why they are also a risk to the whole channel, not just a waste of money.
- **Sub-for-sub swaps.** Subscribers who will never watch add one count and subtract from every other: they are offered your videos and do not click, which teaches the system that your videos do not interest the people who subscribe. That last step is a creator heuristic about how recommendations respond, not a published mechanism. The spam policy does name coordinated sub-for-sub schemes as engagement manipulation (official rule).
- **Private and unlisted videos.** Their hours do not count, and neither do the hours of a video you delete. Publish the version you would stand behind, then leave it up.

## VII. Collaborations

A video made with another channel in the same subject puts you in front of viewers who already watch that kind of video. It is the one growth move that borrows trust instead of buying reach. The terms are simple: each channel should be glad of the result, the video should answer a question both audiences have, and neither channel should pay the other for it. If one does pay, that is a paid promotion, with the disclosure chapter 10 describes.

Collaborations are a creator heuristic as a growth tactic. YouTube does not publish an effect size. The arithmetic is still sound: a viewer who arrives from a related channel already watches videos like yours, and that is the viewer most likely to stay.

*In short:* Work the gate as arithmetic. Average view duration is a lever as strong as views. Ask for the subscription once, with a reason. Point every video at the next one. Promoted and bought views do not count.

## VIII. Claims that do not survive the Earn tab

- That Shorts views fill the 4,000-hour bar. YouTube’s eligibility page says watch hours from the Shorts feed do not count toward it. Shorts have their own view bar. Long videos have the hour bar.
- That ads on your own videos buy the gate. YouTube says paid ad campaign views do not count. Only follow-on organic views do.
- That more subscribers is always better. A subscriber who never watches is one count on one bar and a weaker signal everywhere else.
- That the gate is far away for everyone. It is a number of views a day at your own view duration. Work it out. Then work on the duration.
