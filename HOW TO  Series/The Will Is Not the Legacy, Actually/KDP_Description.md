## Description (HTML)

```html
<p><b>Most families assume the will decides who gets what. Often it does not.</b> This book gives detailed legal examples from California and Germany; readers elsewhere get a shorter topic-by-topic orientation and questions to take to a local professional. It also treats the stories, skills, and creative work behind a life as part of what a family can preserve and pass on.</p>
<p>A retirement form signed twenty years ago still names an ex-spouse. A house deed names one child and forgets the other two. A joint account set up "just to help with the bills" quietly passes to one sibling. A life insurance policy that says "my heirs" lands in court. These documents decide first, and the will gets what is left.</p>
<p><i>The Will Is Not the Legacy, Actually</i> follows twenty-five invented families through the rules of those two worked examples. For each one you see what the family assumed, what actually happened and why, what could have prevented it, and what to do now, including what to do if it has already happened in your family.</p>
<p><b>Inside you will find:</b></p>
<ul>
<li>A sample will annotated clause by clause, and one estate planned three different ways</li>
<li>The real alternatives to a will: beneficiary forms, payable-on-death and transfer-on-death, joint ownership, lifetime gifts, small-estate procedures, and trusts, each with the way it goes wrong</li>
<li>Powers of attorney and healthcare directives: how to choose trusted decision-makers and record your wishes if illness or injury changes what you can manage yourself</li>
<li>The Family List: one organized record of accounts, policies, property, debts, subscriptions, contacts, and where the originals are, with passwords kept out of it and reachable through a password manager's emergency access</li>
<li>Your voice on record: how to record video messages and a video diary with an ordinary phone or webcam, prompts that work, how to store clips so they survive you, and why a video is not a will in California or Germany</li>
<li>Ethical wills, letters that explain unequal gifts, and a creator handoff plan for work and hobby knowledge, how-to books, fiction, and unfinished projects—with options to preserve, update, continue, or let them rest</li>
<li>The receiver's half: the first ninety days for an executor, and digital access from the other side of the lock</li>
<li>Printable to-do lists for you (now, this month, this year) and for your family after a death, an "In Case of Emergency" binder checklist, worksheets with filled examples, and California and Germany side by side</li>
</ul>
<p>Written in plain language, with sources for the legal guidance listed in the back. Living somewhere else? A topic-by-topic chapter covers valid wills, family members you cannot leave out, what passes outside the will, powers of attorney, taxes at death, and probate in other US states, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand, with official starting points and questions to take to a local lawyer or notary.</p>
<p><b>Who is it for?</b> Adults of any age who are planning their affairs or helping a family member. Older teens (ages 16–17) may enjoy the story, interview, and recording prompts with an adult; the legal, financial, and healthcare-planning sections are written for adults and call for local professional guidance.</p>
<p><i>An educational guide, not legal advice. All families in this book are fictional.</i></p>
```
