A real 200-page book is about 55,000 to 65,000 words. The file you have is a field manual, closer to 8,000. Stretching it with definitions and repeated warnings will not get there. What has to be written is the material the short version only points at.

**Scenes and decisions, not more rules.** The current chapters say what a will does not control. A long book shows the mess that follows: the retirement form still naming an ex, the house in joint name with one child, the letter that contradicts the will, the unsigned power of attorney the week before a hospital stay. You need twenty to thirty fully worked cases, each three to five pages, with the facts, the wrong move, the document that actually governed, and what should have been signed instead. That block alone is about 70 pages, and it is the part readers remember.

**The will, drafted in public.** Chapter 3 is an element list. The long version walks a sample will clause by clause: revocation, executor powers, specific gifts, residue, what happens if a child dies first, a simple trust for a minor, a no-contest clause and when to leave it out. Then the same estate redrafted three ways: simple will, will plus beneficiary forms, funded revocable trust with a pour-over will. Annotated samples, not blank forms, run 25 to 35 pages.

**Alternatives with the failure modes filled in.** Payable-on-death, transfer-on-death deeds, joint title, lifetime gifts, intestacy, small-estate affidavits, prenups and buy-sell agreements each need their own chapter: when it works, the tax or creditor surprise, and one case where it overrode the will. Trusts need a separate treatment for revocable, testamentary, and special-needs, including what “funding the trust” means asset by asset. That is another 40 pages.

**Thoughts, at the length of a craft book.** The prompts are a list. The long book shows weak and strong answers to the same prompt, a full sample ethical will, letters for unequal gifts, timed messages, and a method for turning voice memos into a short memoir without sanding off the voice. Add a chapter on unfinished work: code, a business, a manuscript, research. About 30 pages.

**The receiver’s half.** Executors get a bad pamphlet and a courthouse. Write the first 90 days: death certificates, the original will, creditor notices, the account the will does not control, family pressure, when to hire the lawyer. Digital access gets the same treatment: legacy contacts, two-factor, what to export, what not to put in a will that may become public. About 25 pages.

**Worksheets that earn their pages.** Asset map, beneficiary audit, guardian brief, healthcare wishes in ordinary language, handoff script, permission sheet for recordings. Blank pages and filled examples. About 20 pages. A jurisdiction appendix can add 15 to 20 if it is real comparison — common law versus civil law, forced heirship, holographic wills, electronic wills — and not a fake statute dump. One honest chapter on what this book cannot know about the reader’s state is better than fifty thin state summaries.

Skip glossary padding, motivational chapters, and a history of inheritance except where a rule is otherwise inexplicable. Put the disclaimer and the “see a local lawyer” line once per part, not once per page.

Written in that order, the manuscript lands near 200 pages because each chapter has a job. If you want it produced, the practical cut is part by part: cases first, then the annotated will, then the long version of the thoughts chapters.
