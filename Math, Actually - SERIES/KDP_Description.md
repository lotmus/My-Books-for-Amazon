## Volume 1 — Math, Actually, Volume 1: From Arithmetic to Calculus

Subtitle: From Arithmetic to Calculus (Chapters 1–12)

**Mathematics is one connected subject. This is where it starts.**

Most people meet mathematics as a list of unrelated school subjects. *Math,
Actually* treats it as one connected story, fifty chapters long, told one
section at a time. Volume 1 starts with counting and ends with the integral.

Twelve chapters, 109 sections, 430 worked examples:

- Counting, number theory, and why the primes behave the way they do
- Algebra, functions and graphs, and coordinate systems
- Euclidean geometry and trigonometry
- Logarithms and powers, systems of equations, and vectors
- Limits, the derivative, and the integral, through the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

Every new idea starts from something you already know. You see where the old
idea breaks, you work through real numbers, and only then does the idea get its
name. The worked examples are meant to be tried with a pencil before you read
the solution. Each chapter ends with Check Your Understanding questions and
problems, and the full Answer Key and worked Solutions are at the back.

Written for adults coming back to mathematics, students who want the reasons
behind the rules, and anyone who was told they "weren't a math person."

Volume 1 stands on its own. Volumes 2–4 carry the story on to the research
frontier.

## Volume 2 — Math, Actually, Volume 2: From Multivariable Calculus to Set Theory & Logic

Subtitle: From Multivariable Calculus to Set Theory & Logic (Chapters 13–25)

**From one variable to many, and down to the foundations underneath it all.**

Volume 2 takes the calculus of Volume 1 into several dimensions, then builds the
tools that science and engineering run on.

Thirteen chapters, 118 sections, 435 worked examples:

- Functions of several variables, multivariable calculus, and vector calculus
  (divergence, curl, and the great integral theorems)
- Series, Fourier transforms and the FFT, and the Laplace transform
- Integral geometry
- Matrices and linear algebra, including eigenvalues and the SVD
- Analytic geometry and complex analysis
- Counting carefully, plus set theory and logic: the foundations the whole series rests on

The method is the same as Volume 1. You start from a concrete case with real
numbers, see why the familiar tool stops working, and meet the new idea only
once you need it. Every chapter has worked examples, questions and problems,
with a full Answer Key and Solutions at the back.

It works best after Volume 1 or a first calculus course.

## Volume 3 — Math, Actually, Volume 3: From Differential Equations to Abstract Algebra

Subtitle: From Differential Equations to Abstract Algebra (Chapters 26–37)

**Where mathematics stops being a toolkit and starts being a landscape.**

Volume 3 covers the chapters where most university mathematics lives: the
equations that describe change, the methods that compute their solutions, and
the abstract structures behind both.

Twelve chapters, 105 sections, 361 worked examples:

- Differential, integro-differential, and partial differential equations
  (heat, waves, and Laplace's equation)
- Optimization and the calculus of variations
- Numerical methods
- Differential geometry, topology, advanced integral geometry, and tensors and manifolds
- Functional analysis and measure theory
- Abstract algebra, from groups to Galois theory

You still build up to each idea before it is defined. Every abstract claim is
tied to an example you can check. Answers and full solutions are at the back.

It assumes the chapters before it (Volumes 1–2 or the equivalent coursework).

## Volume 4 — Math, Actually, Volume 4: From Category Theory to the Frontier

Subtitle: From Category Theory to the Frontier (Chapters 38–50)

**The last volume, and the view from the end of the road.**

The last volume moves from the most abstract mathematics there is to the places
where mathematics meets the world: signals, games, data, machines, and the laws
of physics.

Thirteen chapters, 118 sections, 359 worked examples:

- Category theory and information theory
- Wavelets and signal analysis
- Game theory and decision mathematics
- Statistics and probability, standard and advanced
- Computational geometry, control theory, and dynamical systems and chaos
- Mathematical physics
- Advanced optimization and advanced numerical methods
- Where mathematics meets the world: modeling real systems, and the open problems
  still waiting

It assumes the three volumes before it, and several sections reuse earlier
theorems without proving them again. Every chapter ends with questions and
problems, with answers and solutions at the back.
