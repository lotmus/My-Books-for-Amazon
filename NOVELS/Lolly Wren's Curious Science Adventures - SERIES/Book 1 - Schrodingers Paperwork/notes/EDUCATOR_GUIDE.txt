SCHRÖDINGER'S PAPERWORK — EDUCATOR'S GUIDE
Supplementary reading for introductory quantum mechanics / modern physics

================================================================================
WHAT THIS BOOK IS
================================================================================

Schrödinger's Paperwork is a satirical novel in which each of its 18
narrative chapters is paired with an optional, standalone physics
lecture in the Appendix. The lectures are not simplified for the joke —
they are honest introductory-level treatments of real quantum
mechanics, written to the standard "if you cannot explain it simply,
you do not understand it yet."

The novel is not required to understand the lectures, and the lectures
are not required to enjoy the novel. Students can be assigned either
independently.

================================================================================
WHY ASSIGN IT
================================================================================

- Each lecture is short (10-20 minutes' reading) and self-contained,
  making it easy to assign one per week alongside a standard syllabus.
- The book is explicit about what is settled physics versus what
  remains an open interpretation question (see especially Lecture 11,
  The Measurement Problem, and Lecture 12, Pilot-Wave Theory) — useful
  for teaching students to distinguish consensus science from ongoing
  debate.
- The novel's bureaucratic-satire framing gives students a concrete,
  memorable narrative hook for otherwise abstract ideas (measurement,
  decoherence, entanglement) — several of the book's central scenes
  are built to BE the physics concept dramatized, not just illustrate
  it.
- A curated set of interactive physics demonstrations exists for many
  of the lectures (eleven, at last count — see the book's companion
  index at lotmus.github.io/lolly-wren-companion), suitable for
  in-class use or as a pre-lecture hook.

================================================================================
SAMPLE DISCUSSION / HOMEWORK PROMPTS
================================================================================

Lecture 02 + Chapter 2 ("The Wrong Queue"):
  The Ministry's queue-sorting system forces an outcome by asking a
  question in a particular basis. Have students identify the specific
  moment reality is "measured" in the chapter, and name which basis is
  being used. Compare to a real experimental measurement basis choice
  (e.g. polarization angle in a Bell test).

Lecture 09 + Chapter 9 ("The Two Columns"):
  Directive 11-C demands simultaneous exact position AND exact
  trajectory certification. Have students derive, in their own words,
  why Δx·Δp ≥ ℏ/2 makes this specific bureaucratic demand impossible in
  principle, not just impractical.

Lecture 13 + Chapter 13 ("The Panel of Experts"):
  Assign the CHSH inequality derivation, then have students calculate
  why classical strategies cap at S = 2 while quantum strategies reach
  2√2. The book's companion index includes an interactive demonstration
  ("Beat the Classical Limit") suitable for an in-class exercise before
  the formal derivation.

Lecture 16-17 (Thermodynamics / Negentropy):
  Have students explain, using Landauer's Principle, why "the Ministry
  cannot erase a record for free" is not just a narrative device but a
  literally true thermodynamic statement.

================================================================================
CAVEATS FOR CLASSROOM USE
================================================================================

- The book is honest about unsettled physics but is still a work of
  comic fiction; treat character dialogue as narrative color, not as a
  citation-grade source. The lectures themselves are the citable
  content.
- Lecture 14 (string theory/M-theory/holography) covers speculative,
  experimentally unconfirmed physics. The lecture itself says so
  explicitly and is best used as an "advanced/optional" enrichment
  section rather than core curriculum.
- No exercises or problem sets are included in the published book;
  the prompts above are original to this guide.
