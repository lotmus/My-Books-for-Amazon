# Editorial and continuity review

Reviewed 15 October 2026. Scope: complete 36-chapter story draft, about 40,000 words, and its new Word presentation. This is an internal critical reading, not an independent reader panel, historical fact-check, plagiarism scan or legal clearance. No story revisions made in this pass.

## Verdict

A complete short novel with an intelligible mystery and an emotionally credible cost, but not publication-ready. Revise the causal mechanics and compressed action before adding length. Preserve Arthur's conversation with Ruth, Beatrice's admission, Hester's independent work, the refusal ceremony and the imperfect settlement. These provide the book's identity.

## Must fix before expanding

1. **Clock conflict, chapters 9–10.** Chapter 9 gives fifty-eight hours remaining on a seventy-two-hour clock begun Monday at 09:17: that implies Monday 23:17. Chapter 10 subsequently has Glock returning at six, apparently that same evening, followed by preparation and overnight sleep. Set chapter 9 to an earlier remaining interval and make the two time systems explicit. Chapter 4's 09:32 incoming call also needs a present-side departure time distinct from Arthur's 09:40 destination time. Build a dispatch ledger with present time, destination time, duration, witnesses and return slot for every mission.

2. **Two-person return becomes three, chapters 11–13.** Eleanor specifies a two-traveller mission, but returns Arthur, Saye and herself by letting the mass balance reset. Explain beforehand whether the limit is per transit, per window, mass, energy or total protected load. Otherwise the later hard rescue limit seems discretionary.

3. **Rescue capacity shifts, chapters 16, 19, 22 and 25–27.** Estimates of nineteen, twenty-three and twenty are later stretched to twenty-two by weighing people and abandoning possessions. This can work, but the limits currently sound like firm head counts until the plot needs kilograms. State a fixed mass/energy budget early, show provisional human estimates as estimates, and keep Jon's inclusion unambiguous. Twenty-one detainees plus Jon equals twenty-two at the site; the earlier nineteen-name list needs an explicit reconciliation showing whether Jon was included in it.

4. **Private transport reserve, chapter 27.** Glock's separate return allowance solves an ethical and logistical impasse only after it arises. Earlier portable frames establish the equipment, but not a still-valid private reservation usable despite the damaged reference network. Establish the independent reserve, its constraints and why it cannot simply evacuate more detainees before the climax.

5. **The 1815 repair needs a stronger causal explanation.** The text asserts that distributing the authentic duplicate reverses the central political branch, but the original diversion has already produced major changes. Explain which decisions remain provisional until the duplicate is certified, and why the first recipient's observed conduct can be superseded without contradicting the rule against changing observed intervals. Distinguish legal authority, historical consequences and the machine's physical constraints; none should magically stand in for the others.

6. **The future warning and corpse, chapters 2, 4 and 9.** The incoming packet precedes the detected divergence. The distinction between actual intervention, protected sequence and delayed detection exists, but needs one clean concrete chronology. Glock's current and older selves likewise need a readable arrival/departure chain. Avoid treating 'sequence' as an explanation for any inconvenient date.

7. **Cresswell's reappearance, chapter 26.** 'Other operator, or a later gap' is speculation at a crucial moment. Establish his actual extraction route in advance, or remove him from this confrontation and use an already-present agent. The eventual charge sheet does not repair the missing transit explanation.

## Narrative and character development

- **First half:** The receipt, print shop and river office give Arthur useful actions suited to his profession. Retain that specificity. Add resistance arising from conflicting interests rather than another explanatory conversation.
- **Glock:** His policy rhetoric is effective, but his access is too convenient and his guards yield too readily. Give Reed and the judge earlier, individual reasons to doubt him. Make one countermeasure materially damage the rescue instead of being solved within the scene. His late flood anecdote should be planted earlier or shortened; it arrives after his dramatic defeat.
- **Eleanor:** Her concealment of Arthur's childhood induction has real moral weight, but her technical competence repeatedly produces the necessary solution. Give an earlier, costly failed assumption a visible consequence in the climax. Show another specialist checking her calculations before the emergency.
- **Beatrice and Miriam:** Strongest sustained relationship. Preserve Miriam's refusal to remain her mother's nineteen-year-old daughter. Beatrice's restitution and disciplinary outcome are appropriately distinct from forgiveness.
- **Arthur and Ruth:** The cafe and last call are the strongest emotional scenes. Do not add a rescue loophole or later resurrection to soften them. Ruth's objection to treating capacity as weather should drive an actual institutional choice, not merely a quotation at the inquiry.
- **Hester and Daniel:** Their own-world stakes keep the past from becoming a puzzle board. Expand Daniel's decision and cost beyond injuries, and let Hester negotiate a substantive disagreement with the visitors. The printer chapter should remain independent of Arthur's viewpoint.
- **Climax:** Chapters 23–28 contain parallel action but unusually frictionless cooperation once the right paperwork is produced. Strengthen the causal link between the public hearing, Hester's distributed copies and Glock's defeat. People should change sides through choices established earlier, not because the argument is suddenly eloquent.
- **Aftermath:** Chapters 29–36 are roughly a fifth of the book after the main confrontation. The funeral, displaced residents and Hester letter deserve room; the multiple inquiry/charter/accountability discussions repeat the same conclusion. Combine repeated institutional explanations and spend the saved space on the middle. Keep the 2034 hook short and separate from the resolved main mystery.

## Prose and humour

Distinctive dry dialogue is a strength. The repeated pattern 'precise bureaucratic distinction → short corrective reply' becomes predictable. Many characters share Arthur's polished wit; vary Daniel's, Miriam's and the guards' speech. Several paragraphs explicitly explain the moral meaning of the scene just completed. Remove those when the action already carries the point. Keep jokes aimed at power and procedure; the straight treatment of grief largely works.

The title of chapter 1 promises a Tuesday happening twice, while the actual event is Monday and a duty-board anomaly. Either pay off the duplicated Tuesday or retitle the chapter. The six-member band and distant colonial consequences are colourful but weakly connected to the memorandum; reduce unsupported decorative divergence or give it a purposeful evidential role.

## Originality review

The draft's independent elements include the civil-service protagonist, childhood induction, forged receivership limitations, the living daughter and lost sister conflicts, printer-led evidence distribution, an inquiry that preserves objections, and the displaced-person settlement. No source-novel text was supplied or used for line-by-line rewriting. Source-specific Vatican machinery, dynastic restoration, sacred artefact, corresponding cast and concrete source ending are absent from this draft.

Residual high-level overlap is substantial enough to examine carefully: secret institutional time intervention, constraints based on history, and an attempted political claimant. Arthur's older corpse also invokes a familiar time-travel double motif, though here it does not produce a closed identity trap. The 1746 setting should be checked for an independent reason to exist, rather than serving as a historical echo. The final manuscript uses Northumberland, while the earlier development notes still say Scotland; the manuscript controls.

This assessment uses the supplied manual's account and the actual new draft, not a full scholarly comparison against Amery's novel. Do not market this as a knock-off, adaptation or substitute; do not claim comprehensive similarity clearance. A knowledgeable outside reader can test for structural correspondences without importing more source scenes into the rewrite.

## Scores

These are one reviewer's estimates, not market research or independent expert scores.

- Target reader: **7.0/10** — engaging premise, dialogue and emotional stakes; technical rules need firmer grounding.
- Harsh critic: **5.8/10** — recurring explanatory dialogue, easy reversals and extended thematic aftermath.
- Internal logic and factual confidence: **5.5/10** — explicit rules are promising, but transport accounting and temporal sequence need repair; historical particulars are not independently checked.
- Amazon market: **6.0/10** — clear niche and appealing title; short length, missing package and unpolished mechanisms limit readiness.
- Sellability: **6.0/10** — potential for readers of comic speculative fiction; not ready to launch.
- Overall: **6.1/10**.

## Recommended next writing pass

First settle the dispatch/capacity ledger and define exactly what makes an intervention admissible. Then rewrite the affected missions and seed Glock's private reserve and Reed's hesitation. Expand the middle through consequential scenes, compress repetitive aftermath, and perform a distinct-voices pass. Reassess length after those changes; do not pad to a target. Finally obtain an outside cold read and targeted historical verification before the publication pass.
