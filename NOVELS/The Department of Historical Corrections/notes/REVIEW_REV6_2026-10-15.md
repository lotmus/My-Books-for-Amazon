## What changed (REV5 to REV6)

- About 52,400 body words (REV5: 50,400). Below the 55k aimed for: I added seven scenes and rewrote five chapters rather than pad.
- **Chapters 16 to 20 (pacing):** explanation in dialogue cut; every chapter now runs against a stated clock (Ruth at 08:40 and a 10:14 crossing; a ten-minute recess; hearing at 11:00 with an usher announcing a 12:30 booking; "twelve ten"); each has an obstacle or set piece (the grey-armband card check in the café, the stamped file in the registration hall, the countdown to the recess, the run upstairs); chapters end on an action or turn. Chapter 17 went from 1,400 to 1,100 words; chapter 16 grew from 800 to 1,050 because it now has an obstacle.
- **Glock (menace by action):** speeches cut roughly in half in chapters 10, 17, 19 and 20. He never raises his voice. Concrete consequences: he stamps Elise Marot's card and sends her to a windowless room because Arthur wrote her name down (chapter 17, which is why she is in the lower archive on Thursday); he rescinds Captain Reed's pension record in front of Arthur (chapter 26); a timetable of duty enquiry and sealing for Hester's shop (chapter 19); a visit to Perrott's lodging at 11 p.m. with a landlady's lease as the lever (new). **Motive:** the Leeds flood is now personal: his mother waited eleven months for a pension book and died in the tenth; he wants to be "unrefusable". He is physically present in four more scenes than before: 2029 stationery cupboard, Perrott's lodging, the registration hall (rewritten), Hester's shop (rewritten).
- **Seven new scenes:** Glock alone with Arthur before the ceremony (2029); Fanshawe and Saye on the Mercy (the missing pages one to thirty-nine); Beatrice and Miriam in Records at 20:00; the three committee members as people (Tench, Dacre, Hallett); Hester and the under-secretary Lyle (22 June); Glock at Perrott's lodging; the COST column payoff (REV5).
- **Homage safeguards (Amery):** see HANDOVER.md. The gold circlet, "monarchy" and "sovereign" wording was changed to a brass chain of office and an office for life, to stay further from the king motif of the title.
- **Back matter:** Acknowledgement plus a short note on the name Glock.
