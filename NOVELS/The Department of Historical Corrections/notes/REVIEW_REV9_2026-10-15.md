# REV9 critical rescore, 15 October 2026

One reviewer's estimates (an AI assistant working for Lothar), not an independent reader panel, a humour specialist or a clearance. Basis: I read the whole REV8 manuscript (chapters 1 to 37 and back matter) before choosing where to put jokes, applied the edits by script with every anchor checked, then scanned the finished file for names, counts, tics and banned words. I did not read the finished REV9 end to end a second time, and the book has not been opened in Kindle Previewer or given to a cold reader. Humour is the hardest thing to score from the inside: treat the Humour line as a hypothesis for a cold reader to test.

## What the pass did

REV8 was already dry in chapters 1 to 9 and thinner from about chapter 21 on. REV9 adds a joke system, not a joke density: a handful of running jokes planted early and paid late, bureaucratic gags told straight, and a few funnier minor characters. Glock's scenes were left alone; his only change is one sentence ("had read the small print"). Tense scenes (the ceremony, the 2029 evacuation, Ruth's calls, the Hallett exchange, Arthur's refusal) were not touched. The three twists and their plants were not touched; no physics was added.

Ch 20, the opening of the hearing.

Before: "Two clerks kept separate minutes. A third recorded the proceedings on equipment supplied by the university. Glock had objected in writing to the duplication. Pelling had appended the objection to all three records. Before anyone spoke, Dame Judith North asked that a statement be entered."

After (inserted after the objection): "The hearing opened with apologies for absence ... a Mr Glock, who had written to say that he would attend remotely and would therefore also be absent; a Mr Durrant, who was with a patient; and Belgium, which the clerks were instructed to record as expected ... Before: "He called Beatrice, who was due in that afternoon, and asked whether she could look at a curious receipt."

## What stops a 10

1. No outside reader and no Kindle Previewer check. The Humour score in particular needs a cold reader.
2. The running jokes cluster in the last third (chapters 31 to 37 carry about a dozen payoffs). A reader may feel the book is tidying up with a joke each time; if a cold read says so, cut two or three (Garrick's letter and the Finch overtime are the most replaceable).
3. Apologies for absence and Facilities each recur four to five times. That is the point of a running gag; it is also the first thing a critic would call a tic.
4. Chapter 13 (2,268 words), 15 (1,925) and 12 (1,873) are still the longest early chapters and mostly talk.
5. Humour is thin by design in chapters 21 to 30 (the ceremony and the evacuation); that is right for the story, but it makes the book's tone uneven.
6. Historical details (convent hospital, Gazette lists, Hallett's regiment, "the clerk's dinner", the 1887 precedent) are plausible, not specialist-checked.
7. The Amery check is still against published summaries; I have not read the novel.
8. Nothing from REV10 (physics, tunnelling, appendix lessons) has been started.
