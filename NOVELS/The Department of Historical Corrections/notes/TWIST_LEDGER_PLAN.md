## 2. Candidate new twists

Each plant is a line or two added to text that already exists, at least five chapters before the reveal. None creates a loop where a person causes their own existence, and none touches R1 to R15 or REV11's physics. No candidate puts anything new into ch 26.

### T1. The nurse was Margaret Glock

- **Recasts:** the 1991 rescue (9), "Why you?" (26), Arthur's RETAIN (26), Glock's whole motive. Arthur's heart stopped in 1991 and a Leeds ward nurse kept him alive until Eleanor's window; in 2017 he signed away her eleven months. Glock found the department because his mother told him once about the night a boy came back and "the woman with a watch on each wrist" (Eleanor wears two, ch 2).
- **Plants (at least three):** ch 7: the photocopy names the hospital (Leeds General Infirmary, one clause). Ch 9: Arthur remembers a stranger's cold hands and "stay with me, pet", and Eleanor says the nurse was never named because the staff roll was lost in the 1994 merger. Ch 15: file entry 1 gains "nurse not traced (roll lost 1994)". Ch 21 or 26 (one clause, no new reveal): Glock "has the hospital" ("I know the ward", one line only if REV12 leaves the scene alone; otherwise skip).
- **Reveal chapter:** new chapter 34b, "Visiting Hours" (Arthur visits the older Glock in custody; Glock quiet and specific: "She said the doctors had stopped. The woman with a watch on each wrist had not."). Then the 1991 roll found by Ives in the university deposit.
- **Reward:** the notebook's "he was grateful enough not to ask" becomes a knife; Glock is a mirror, not a monster, and the book's moral (a signature costs someone) closes on its own protagonist. Also explains how Glock found the department without a convenient accident.
- **Risks:** (1) coincidence, but it is motivated (he went looking because of her story); (2) Glock sympathetic: keep him unrepentant, he still "invented nothing"; (3) R10: the minute was open to Eleanor, so the nurse seeing her is allowed by R3; (4) ch 9 says the nurse was brought "through from the adjacent corridor", so she did not time-travel; state that.
- **Cost:** new chapter about 1,500 words; extensions about 250 words (ch 7, 9, 15); one line in ch 37 (the first dot reads "Margaret Glock, staff nurse, Leeds").

### T2. Beatrice's dot (the inspector who was left out)

- **Recasts:** Glock's "railway accident" bargain (6, 9), "I did worse" (13), the dots in her hand (15), the 2008 register in ch 22. The neglected inspection that killed Miriam in 2007 was itself the result of a department correction: in 1988 the junior clerk Beatrice entered and initialled "retained" on a transfer schedule that left out a permanent-way inspector, Mr Alan Corley. He is one of her 53 dots; she marked it in November 2007, two weeks after the funeral, "because the pencil was in my hand", and never put the two together. Glock knew from the register and sold her her own error back as a favour.
- **Plants:** ch 6 (Beatrice: "a correction which could prevent a railway accident": add "in 1988"). Ch 9 (Glock's offer: add that he named the year and "the inspector's round"). Ch 13 end ("I did worse"; keep). Ch 15 (dots: add that the oldest dot in her own hand is dated November 2007 and she cannot recall why). Ch 22 (the 2008 register left open at one page since six).
- **Reveal chapter:** new chapter 22b, "Reason for Return" (split ch 22 at "At eight that evening"; the existing Records scene opens it): Miriam spots the November 2007 dot beside the page Beatrice has not turned; Beatrice reads out the name; "personal" on the receipt takes on a second meaning. The Miriam annotation "Corrected, 15 Oct. Not personal." now sits beside it. Then the departure.
- **Reward:** the book's two theme lines (a signature costs someone; "who keeps") get a second protagonist, mother not hero; "I'm doing it because of what I did" (22) is already there and suddenly exact; Miriam's "You made me a reason" (9) turns round.
- **Risks:** (1) Why can't the department simply correct Corley? Because the interval is closed and Glock's receiver is the only route that overrides ratified registers (R1, R7); one sentence in 22b. (2) Beatrice already admits a lot (9, 34); keep the reveal a specific name and date, not a new confession of corruption. (3) Do not claim she would have noticed: dots were habit.
- **Cost:** new chapter about 1,500 words (about 900 new on top of about 600 of existing scene); extensions about 150 words in 6, 9, 15; fallout one paragraph in 34 (Beatrice's evidence about "personal interest" gets its specific) and one line in 37 (she enters "Alan Corley").

### T3. Fanshawe's unrecorded village was Strath's moss

- **Recasts:** Fanshawe as a vain man who once did the right thing; the whole 1746 expedition (the Strath beneficiaries are the village); why Fanshawe asked for the original annex and why he trusted Hester; Eleanor in the kitchen with Tom Tully, paid fourpence for peat in April 1747 (12).
- **Plants:** ch 1 (the village that nobody could name; keep). Ch 7 (Fanshawe: "Glock said it was boilerplate. I asked for the original" gains "I have a thing about estates"). Ch 12 (the petitions mention a cow lost in a flood; Eleanor and Nan Tully's kitchen peat; add one line that the moss is "the only fuel they've got"). Ch 15 or 22 (Fanshawe: his one correction that was refused was an eighteenth-century drain).
- **Reveal chapter:** ch 31 (Thursday morning, after Arthur tells Fanshawe "It was mine" and term six): Fanshawe, asked what he has ever got right, names the drain: the Strath Moss drainage correction of 1761 that would have removed the peat and the hamlet with it. Nobody found the name; Eleanor, who has read the 1747 accounts, says it: Tullymoss. "That is why it came up when Glock said 'trust'." He is not forgiven; he is explained.
- **Reward:** a quiet one, from a character the reader has teased since page two; ties the humour (On my last mission) to the book's theme (people the record cannot name).
- **Risks:** (1) Do not make Fanshawe's earlier correction the cause of 1746: he stopped a later drain, so nothing circular; (2) R10-style limit: the village survives because of a past correction, not because of the 2026 team; (3) tone: one scene of about 350 words, no speech.
- **Cost:** about 350 words in ch 31 plus about 60 in each of ch 7, 12, 15; no new chapter.
