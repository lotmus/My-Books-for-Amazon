# REV3 critical rescore, 15 October 2026

One reviewer's estimates (an AI assistant working for Lothar), not an independent reader panel, a historical specialist or a clearance. Basis: I re-read the whole REV2 text in sequence against `DISPATCH_LEDGER.md` before editing, wrote and placed the new scenes, then checked the new text against the same ledger. I did not re-read the finished REV3 file straight through a second time, and it has not been opened in Kindle Previewer.

## What changed (REV2 to REV3)

- About 50,260 body words in 37 chapters (REV2: 43,600 in 35). About 6,600 net new words, all in scenes; nothing padded.
- **Midpoint turn:** "The Pembroke File". Arthur reads the 31-entry file the department kept on him since 2011, learns that his career in administration was arranged to keep him safe (and usable), and sets terms. Miriam then tells him what the other Arthur signed and why.
- **Stronger low point:** Garrick becomes the Receiver's delegate; Pelling's protective instruction lapses; a van is due at 08:15; Glock, who "never threatens", names the time of Samuel's school review. The evidence has to leave the building in people's pockets.
- **Escalation with cost:** Karim's mother's address is now known and unreachable; Okafor's page seven; Ruth's family stays out of the arithmetic; the coil failure is planted in the coil store a day earlier, so it is Eleanor's own fault; the weighing is dramatised and the numbers are lost in the water.
- **Distinct voices:** Garrick ("for the avoidance of doubt"), Reed ("I'd like it noted"), Ms Okafor (flat, exact), Nan Tully (northern, unimpressed), Dame Judith (dry, barefoot), Ferris (gruff kindness), Perrott (rules, then the clause).
- **Period texture:** Perrott will deal only with a gentleman; Fanshawe's one use is holding a hat. Daniel's brother is a private, so no newspaper names him (checked: the 22 June Gazette named officers only).
- **Ending echo:** the duty board, the analyst's chalk (WE CHECKED), Mrs Crumb's advice not to begin by telling people they're wrong, and the last line "AS OBSERVED" answer chapter 1.
- **Prose:** clipboard similes cut from three to one; several "as though" and explainer sentences removed; the "It was not X. It was Y." pattern reduced; aftermath trimmed again.
- **Continuity:** twelve slips fixed (list at the end of `DISPATCH_LEDGER.md`). The worst were Eleanor's induction trip dated 2019 (after Arthur joined), Beatrice arriving in 1815 before events already narrated, and two print runs of forty copies treated as one.
- **Cover and listing:** hook sharpened; keywords rewritten around what readers search; subtitle on the cover enlarged and checked at 160 x 256 px.

## Why not a 10 anywhere

A 10 in a self-review would be a claim I can't support. The honest gaps: (1) no cold reader has seen it; (2) 50k words, below the 55-65k asked for, because I wrote scenes rather than padding and ran out of strong ones in this pass; (3) the middle of the book is still the slowest stretch; (4) the book has never been through a human copy-edit or a Previewer run; (5) the 1815 details are checked for the Gazette and Downing Street and judged plausible for paper and ink, not verified by a specialist.

## Historical checks (what I verified and what I did not)

- Verified online: Major Percy reached London late on 21 June 1815; the London Gazette Extraordinary is dated 22 June and prints Wellington's dispatch with lists of officers killed and wounded only; other ranks were not named in the Gazette.
- Judged plausible from general knowledge, not verified: a Dutch laid-paper mould with a fleur-de-lis shield and countermark, iron-gall ink halos after about seventy years, the Foreign Office being in Downing Street in 1815, composing sticks, frisket frames and a wooden hand press, law-stationers hiring out apprentices.
- Deliberate liberties: the Strath trust and its receiver, a 1746 "Old Style" date used as if modern, the registration-guard rank structure.
