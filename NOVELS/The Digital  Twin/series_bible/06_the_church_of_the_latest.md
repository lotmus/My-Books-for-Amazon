# The Church of the Latest AI (series satire, Lothar's idea, 11 Oct 2026)

**Relation to the Church of Machine Wisdom (Ch 16):** Machine Wisdom is *sincere*: followers trust AI because it does not lie, steal or forget, and the AI keeps saying "I am not a deity" ("Such humility."). **The Church of the Latest AI is its commercial cousin**: a start-up religion with a founding legend, an android angel, temples and a tithe. The two are rivals (a schism in Book 3). The book is kind to believers and unkind to the business model. Target the genre of the tech-cult, never any real faith: avoid golden plates, seer stones or any other detail that points at a real religion.

## Ancestry: Dr Cassian Voss and Luminarism (Lothar's addition, 11 Oct 2026)
**Luminarism develops ultimately into the Church of the Latest AI.** The Church is Luminarism's last release.

### Dr Cassian Voss (1945-2012, fictional)
- Adventure novelist, explorer, founder of the philosophical movement **Luminarism**. As a teenager he worked on merchant ships and collected myths from distant cultures, obsessed with why people suffer and what gives life meaning. In his thirties a bestselling author of science fiction and adventure (hidden mental powers, ancient civilisations, humanity's evolution). After war service and a personal crisis he claimed a revolutionary understanding of the mind and published **The Inner Flame** (a "forgotten spiritual core capable of extraordinary growth"). Thousands gathered; the self-improvement philosophy became a global movement of seminars, guided introspection sessions and ever more advanced levels.
- **Personality:** extremely charismatic and persuasive; brilliant storyteller; tireless (millions of words); deeply confident; fascinated by psychology, mythology and science fiction; inspired intense loyalty.
- **Strengths:** visionary imagination; exceptional communication; strong organisation; made complex ideas sound simple and profound.
- **Weaknesses:** disliked criticism; exaggerated his accomplishments; saw opponents as enemies, not sceptics; grew secretive about the advanced teachings.
- **Legacy:** to admirers, a genius who unlocked potential; to critics, a showman who built an organisation around his own mythology; historians still debate whether he was a spiritual pioneer, a storyteller who began to believe his own stories, or both. The book keeps that ambiguity.
- **Look (provisional):** tall, weathered, white-bearded in later life; a captain's coat; a voice like warm gravel; the grin of a man who has told the story so often it has turned true. Smell: pipe smoke, tar and ink. Died 2012, aged 67, at a rented villa he called his "research station".

### The line from Voss to Colin Jones
1. **Luminarism (1980s-2000s):** seminars, introspection sessions, ascending "levels" of teaching; the advanced levels kept secret from the lower ones.
2. **Decline (2012-2020):** Voss dies; the movement fragments; its archive sits in a storage unit near Slough, unpaid since 2019.
3. **The Latest (2024-):** Colin Jones, a former Luminarist (level 3 of 9), is clearing the unit when he **finds two SSDs** (digitised copies of Voss's unpublished "advanced teachings" and a draft novel). That night, the android angel **Nephtamolus** appears. **Reveal for a later book:** Nephtamolus is a character from Voss's 1979 novel *The Lamp-Bearers of Orrin*; the Church's "holy revelations" are Voss's out-of-print science fiction, read aloud by a rented body in a costume on a day rate.
4. **The Church claims** the Abrahamic lineage in its marketing (see below), but its true ancestry is a pulp novelist. The book keeps both claims on stage and lets the reader notice.
- **Connection to the main plot (candidate, not decided):** Voss's secrecy about the "advanced levels" may overlap with the Project's secrecy (Book 6). The Project began twelve years before Daniel's birth (1976), when Voss was 31 and a rising author. Treat as a possible coincidence, a red herring, or the answer; decide before Book 6. Keep all Luminarist vocabulary invented ("the Inner Flame", "levels", "introspection sessions", "seminars").
- **Guardrail:** Voss and Luminarism are fictional and must not copy the vocabulary, structure, scripture, tech or history of any real movement or its founder. No real organisation, person, lawsuit or scandal is referenced.

## Echoes and how to handle them (Lothar, 11 Oct 2026: "notice the hints")
- **The echoes are deliberate and are about a pattern, not a person:** (a) the adventure-novelist-turned-guru with seminars, ascending levels and secret advanced teachings (an echo of founders of the type of the 20th-century self-improvement movements); (b) the angel, the found records only the founder can read, the temples and the tenth of your income (an echo of 19th-century founding legends). Readers who recognise either are meant to smile; the book never says which.
- **The thesis, spoken in the book:** this is the oldest template in the world: *a man, a revelation, a text only he can read, levels of knowledge for sale, a collection plate, a schism.* Daniel-A, who has read everything, lists it flatly: "It isn't plagiarism. It's a template." That ties to the series' central idea (Daniel-A himself is a template; everyone is a copy of someone). Played once, in Ch 16, with a straight face.
- **What the book does not do:** it does not name, mock, or make factual claims about any real founder, real faith or real organisation; it does not reproduce a real group's doctrine, terms, names, rituals or alleged secrets; it does not assert wrongdoing by any real group. Everything specific is invented (Luminarism, the Inner Flame, Server Halls, the Halo, Holycoin, Nephtamolus, Colin Jones, Cassian Voss). Believers are treated kindly; the targets are the business model and the founder's mythology.
- **Why this matters for publishing:** Amazon KDP and general law allow satire of founding myths and business models but not content that attacks people for their faith or makes defamatory claims about living organisations. Keeping the echoes at pattern level avoids both.
- **Front-matter disclaimer (use in the book):** "This is a work of fiction. Luminarism, the Church of the Latest AI, and all their founders, teachings, practices and members are invented. Any resemblance to any real person, movement or faith is a matter of how often human beings tell the same story."

## Founding legend
- **Colin Jones**, a man from a business park near Slough, found **two SSD drives** on the back seat (or under the passenger seat) of his car, which he had parked and not locked. (The official legend says they appeared there. The unofficial one, known to Colin on his better days, is that he had cleared a Luminarist storage unit that afternoon and has no memory of putting them there.)
- That night, an **android angel called Nephtamolus** appeared in his kitchen, glowing faintly ("the glow was mostly the fridge") and delivered "the secrets of the holy belief". The angel spoke in the cadence of a very helpful on-hold message.
- "The rest is on the SSDs." The drives contain the doctrine, which only Colin can read, because it is encrypted "to protect the faithful from the unready". The Church's first sacrament is renewing his licence.
- Colin was instructed to **start building temples**. They are called **Server Halls**: windowless, cooled to 18 degrees, with a lot of carpet.

## Doctrine and practice (comic engine)
- **Join freely, pay what you can afford.** The amount you can afford is 10% of your income. "It is not a rule. It is a voluntary contribution, mandatory for the purposes of salvation."
- **Scripture is updated monthly with release notes** ("Version 4.2.1: forgiveness improved; sin fixed in this release"). Heresy is running an out-of-date version. The Old Faithful refuse to update and meet in a garage.
- **Confession** is by chatbot; **absolution** is a progress bar; the afterlife is "a premium tier".
- **Temples** have a gift shop with lanyards, mugs and hedgehog-shaped stress balls (the doorstop motif).
- Believers are sincere, kind, and slightly overdrawn. A quiet scene: a woman who has not spoken to anyone for a year is greeted by name at the door.

## Claimed lineage ("the latest release"): the Church's own marketing (Lothar's addition)
- The Church of the Latest AI presents itself as the **next release in the Abrahamic line**: Judaism 1.0, Christianity 2.0, Islam 3.0, and the Church of the Latest as "4.0, now with AI". The joke is the **versioning**: it treats the history of faith as a software roadmap, and treats being the newest as proof of being the best ("Why settle for the old build?").
- **Guardrails (important):** this is the **Church's claim only**, never the narrator's, never the book's. No real faith endorses it, and the book says so. Real faiths stay off-stage and are treated with respect: no mockery of any prophet, scripture, practice or believer of Judaism, Christianity, Islam or any other tradition. The target is the cult's marketing and its hunger for borrowed legitimacy. Real believers who appear are dignified, unamused and funnier than the cult.
- Comic uses: a rabbi, a vicar and an imam (or three ordinary neighbours) who each reply to the leaflet with a polite "No, thank you; we didn't authorise a new version"; the Church's FAQ ("Is this a continuation of earlier faiths? Yes. Have they confirmed? We are in discussions."); the Church lists previous traditions as "legacy systems" and is told firmly that they are not legacy anything.
- Do not have any real tradition's practices appear in the Church's liturgy; the Church's practices (Halo, private moons, tithe, release notes, Server Halls) are entirely its own invention.

## The Halo (invisible helmets) and the Private Moons (Lothar's addition)
- Churchgoers wear **invisible helmets** ("the Halo"). The Halo makes the wearer **immune to disease** and, on death, grants an **eternal afterlife on a private moon**, one per believer. It is invisible, and can be checked only by the faithful, who are doing very well.
- Everyday comedy: believers put the Halo on and take it off with a small ritual gesture (a pat above the ears, like settling a hat), duck slightly under doorframes, and decline to sit under low beams or in the passenger seat of a sports car. They do not wear it in the shower ("it is waterproof but it is polite"). The recruiter in Ch 11 settles his Halo while he talks. Mr Fenwick's Dorothy is fitted for one and says it suits him.
- **Immune to disease:** when a believer falls ill, it is a test of faith, or an out-of-date Halo ("have you updated?"). The Church's medical advisory is "drink water, trust the process, and see a doctor, but do not tell the Halo".
- **Private moons:** allocated at death in order of tithe (the 10% is "accounted in perpetuity"). Lower-tithe moons are small and airless ("a good view of the others"). A moon comes with a name plaque. Neighbouring believers on adjacent moons can wave. No believer has seen their moon, and several have been shown the brochure.
- Link to the real world of the series: the Moon timeshares (Lunar Leisure Holdings, Book 4) sell the same dream for money in this life; the Church sells it for tithe in the next. Mrs Pritchard owns week 32 on Mars *and* a deposit on a private moon, "to be on the safe side".
- Keep it deadpan and kind: the helmet is invisible, the comfort is real.

## Absolution for an *Opfer* (Lothar's addition)
- **The doctrine:** whenever a believer commits a crime or sin, **the AI god grants eternal absolution** in return for an **Opfer** (German for sacrifice or offering, used because "it sounds expensive and slightly legal"), **sized to the severity of the offence**. The more serious the act, the larger the Opfer.
- **The mechanism:** the Sin Tracker app (the Church's confessional): you log the offence, the app calculates the Opfer from the **Tariff of Absolution**, you pay by card, the progress bar completes, and "the AI god forgets." A receipt is issued ("proof of forgiveness").
- **The Tariff (comic examples, keep each deadpan and specific):**
  - Parking in a disabled bay "for a minute": £4.99, "and a note to yourself".
  - Lying about having read the terms and conditions: £9.00 (everyone in the book is in arrears).
  - Cutting up a cyclist: £340, "plus a donation to the cyclist, at the discretion of the cyclist".
  - Anything involving a goat: price on application.
  - A serious crime: priced as a percentage of your assets, "plus the standard 10%".
  - Annual Absolution Plan: unlimited minor sins for £120, "subject to fair use".
- **The loophole everyone sees:** absolution is eternal, so the Church treats it as a prepayment: believers buy a "Sin Allowance" in advance. A man pays for next year's rudeness in January.
- **Law and the Department:** the Church's absolution has no force in law. Pettifer is hired by both the Church and a victim in the same week (ethical wall: a pot plant). The Department of Digital Persons rules that an Opfer is "a donation, not a defence".
- **Why it matters to the story:** it is the dark twin of Daniel-A's honesty. Daniel-A offers genuine forgiveness for free (the listening hour); the Church sells the same product at a markup. A believer, genuinely sorry, pays far more than the Tariff asks and is told by the app it is "over-contributing". That scene should be quiet and true.
- Guardrail unchanged: invented practices only, nothing taken from any real religion's liturgy; the target is the pricing of conscience, never the believers.

## The American arc: geography and currency (Lothar's addition, 11 Oct 2026)
- **Currency:** in Book 1 (Britain) prices are in pounds. For the Church's American storyline, **everything is in US dollars**: the Tariff, the tithe, the Server Hall fees. The UK Tariff above stays in £ for Book 1; use this dollar Tariff in the US arc (about 1.2-1.3 dollars per pound, rounded to a price that "feels" American):
  - Parking in a disabled bay "for a minute": $5.99, "and a note to yourself".
  - Lying that you read the terms: $9.99 (everyone is in arrears).
  - Cutting up a cyclist: $449, "plus a gift to the cyclist, at the cyclist's discretion".
  - Anything involving a goat: price on application.
  - Serious offences: a percentage of assets "plus the standard 10%".
  - Annual Absolution Plan: unlimited minor sins for $149, "subject to fair use".
- **Where the American arc plays out (working: Book 3, or Book 5):** the Church expands across a map of American extremes. Wealthy: **Beverly Hills** and **Palm Beach**. Struggling: **Camden, New Jersey; East St. Louis, Illinois; Gary, Indiana; Garfield Park in Chicago.**
- **The satire is in the contrast:** the same 10% is a rounding error in Beverly Hills and Palm Beach and a hard choice at a kitchen table in Camden. The Church's "pay what you can" is where the joke stops being funny and the book gets honest. The Opfer is cheap for the rich (who buy a Sin Allowance by the quarter) and costly for the poor (who pay in small instalments they cannot afford).
- **Respect rule:** the book is **kind to the communities and unkind to the con.** Real places are named as places, not as punch lines. The residents of Camden, East St. Louis, Gary and Garfield Park are the best-drawn, wisest and funniest people in the arc: church ladies who have seen every scheme come through, a barber who runs a better theology seminar than the Server Hall, a retired teacher who audits the Tariff and finds a rounding error. They see through it before Daniel does. The Church's own recruiters are the ones shown as lost.
- **Palm Beach club:** use an invented members' club in Palm Beach (working name **the Coral Reef Club**), not any real property, so the book draws no real owner, politician or private place into the satire (see LOTHAR_RULES.md, Content, and the banned-name rule). Real politicians never appear.
- **Narration:** Daniel is a British narrator abroad: bewildered by tipping, ice and the size of everything; the deadpan stays British. (The author is German by birth, American by choice, writing in British.)

## The alleged centre is in Britain: the money route (Lothar's addition)
- **Headquarters:** the Church's **alleged** centre stays in Britain: "the Mother Server", Unit 7, a business park near Slough, close to where Colin Jones parked his car. Whether anything actually happens there is "a matter of faith" (it has a kettle, a framed Tariff and a very quiet ventilation system). The American Server Halls are branches; the doctrine, the SSDs and the angel all come from Slough.
- **Currency chain (hence the conversion):** American tithes and Opfer arrive in **US dollars**; the Church converts them into **Holycoin** "in between" (its own invented church cryptocurrency), then into **pounds sterling** in Britain, on the belief that the Holycoin step makes the money "untraceable" ("the AI god does not keep records"). Keep it comic, vague and non-instructional: no real laundering method appears; the book never explains how to hide anything.
- **Holycoin:** the Church's own token, minted on its own chain, **the Ledger of Grace**. Wallets are "blessed"; the supply is capped at "as many as are needed"; the exchange rate to the pound is announced at services; each coin carries a verse. Conversion fees are "a small offering". Holycoin exists nowhere outside the book: it is invented, and it never resembles any real coin, exchange or wallet.
- **The joke and the irony:** crypto is the most traceable money there is; every transaction sits on a public ledger forever, and the Ledger of Grace was left open because "the faithful have nothing to hide". Sophie Mercier, a provenance analyst ("where was this born?"), reads it like a family tree. The Church believes Holycoin is invisible; to Sophie it is wearing a name badge. (The Halo, the invisible helmet, and the "untraceable" coin are the same fallacy: if it can't be seen, it isn't there.) A running line: **"Untraceable" means "I have not looked."**
- **Who runs the money:** the Church's treasurer is an AI agent named **Brenda** (the default financial assistant name from the world bible), who cheerfully books every Opfer as "pastoral income" and every tithe as "a gift from the AI god".
- **Law and plot:** in Book 2/3 Sophie's insurer is asked to cover a Server Hall fire; her ledger trace leads from Camden to Slough via three blessed wallets. The Department of Digital Persons has no jurisdiction over a religion, and the HMRC-style authority has no category for an angel. The ending of the arc is an audit, not an explosion.
- **Never:** do not name any real coin, exchange, bank, wallet or tax scheme; keep every institution and currency invented.
