# How to use this lesson

Lesson 49 derived the Feynman rules from the Dyson series and Wick's theorem for operators. This lesson derives them again, from Z[J, η̄, η] of Lesson 80, and gets the same rules. The agreement is the point, because the path integral is then a full replacement for the operator method. Three things come out more cleanly. The signs of fermion loops and of exchanged identical fermions follow from reordering Grassmann variables, with no operator algebra. Lorentz covariance is manifest at every step. And derivative couplings, such as those of a charged scalar, give their contact vertices directly, where the canonical method has to work to get them. The lesson ends with the complete rule table in this course's conventions. Expect six to eight hours.

## Learning objectives

- Write Z = exp[iS_int(−iδ/δJ, −iδ/δη̄, iδ/δη)]Z₀, and explain why this generates perturbation theory.

- Derive the vertex ieγ^μ, the propagators, and the cancellation of 1/n! from the expansion of e^{iS_int}.

- Derive momentum conservation at each vertex and one ∫d⁴k/(2π)⁴ per loop, with L = I − V + 1.

- Obtain the −1 for a closed fermion loop and the relative sign of exchange diagrams by reordering Grassmann variables.

- Compute the electron–muon scattering amplitude and recover the Coulomb potential from it.

- Derive the scalar-QED vertices, including the seagull 2iq²g^{μν}.

# 1 The master formula

## 1.1 The interaction as an operator

Inside Z, every field can be produced by a derivative with respect to its source. Lesson 80 gave the rules: −iδ/δJ_μ brings down A^μ, −iδ/δη̄ brings down ψ, and iδ/δη brings down ψ̄. Any function of the fields in the integrand can therefore be pulled outside the integral as the same function of derivatives. Applying this to e^{iS_int}:

$$ Z[J, η̄, η] = exp[ i S_int[ −i (δ)/(δJ), −i (δ)/(δη̄), i (δ)/(δη) ] ] Z_0[J, η̄, η]

Everything that remains is the free Gaussian, which Lesson 80 evaluated exactly. Expanding the exponential in powers of e gives perturbation theory. Each derivative pulls down one source-propagator factor from Z₀, and setting the sources to zero at the end pairs every field with another. That pairing is Wick's theorem, derived in Lesson 80, Section 1.2, as the expansion of an exponential of a quadratic.

## 1.2 Correlators

A correlator with external fields is the same derivative structure with extra derivatives for those fields, divided by Z[0]. By Lesson 79, Section 7.1, it is

$$ ⟨Ω| T ψ[x_1] ψ̄[x_2] A_μ[x_3] |Ω⟩ = (⟨ψ[x_1] ψ̄[x_2] A_μ[x_3] e^(i S_int)⟩_0)/(⟨e^(i S_int)⟩_0)

The denominator removes vacuum bubbles, and every surviving pairing is connected to an external point.

### Worked example 1

Find the order-e term of the correlator above.

One power of iS_int brings down i∫d⁴z eψ̄(z)γ^νψ(z)A_ν(z). Pair ψ(x₁) with ψ̄(z), ψ(z) with ψ̄(x₂), and A_μ(x₃) with A_ν(z). Moving ψ̄(x₂) to the right past the pair ψ̄(z)γψ(z) takes two Grassmann swaps, so no sign is introduced. The free averages are S_F(x₁ − z), S_F(z − x₂), and D_{μν}(x₃ − z), so

$$ ∫ d⁴z S_F[x_1 − z] [i e γ^ν] S_F[z − x_2] D_(νμ)[z − x_3]

Reading from the outgoing electron at x₁ back to the incoming one at x₂, this is a propagator, the vertex ieγ^ν, another propagator, and a photon propagator attached at the vertex. The vertex factor is i times the coefficient of ψ̄γ^νψA_ν in ℒ, which is Lesson 44's ieγ^μ. No other source of i or sign entered.

# 2 Position-space rules and the 1/n!

At order eⁿ the expansion gives (1/n!)(i∫eψ̄A̸ψ)ⁿ, with n vertices at points z₁, …, z_n integrated over spacetime. A given diagram is a pattern of pairings. Relabeling the n vertex points gives the same value, because the points are integrated, and in QED every relabeling gives a different pairing of the same shape. So each distinct diagram appears n! times, which cancels the 1/n!. In QED the photon has no self-coupling and every electron line has a direction, so a diagram has no leftover symmetry factor except for rare vacuum graphs. In a theory with identical fields at a vertex, such as φ⁴, an automorphism of the diagram leaves a factor such as the ½ or ⅛ of Lesson 79's zero-dimensional model.

# 3 Momentum space

## 3.1 Conservation at each vertex

Write every propagator as a Fourier integral, for example S_F(x − z) = ∫d⁴p/(2π)⁴ e^{−ip(x−z)}S_F(p). A vertex at z collects one exponential from each line attached to it. Its integral is

$$ ∫ d⁴z e^(i [p_1 − p_2 − k] · z) = (2π)⁴ δ⁴[p_1 − p_2 − k]

so the momenta at every vertex sum to zero. Each internal line carries a ∫d⁴p/(2π)⁴. Each vertex delta function removes one of them, and one delta function survives as overall momentum conservation.

## 3.2 Counting loops

With I internal lines and V vertices, the number of free momentum integrals is I − (V − 1) = I − V + 1 = L, the number of loops. Each loop has one undetermined momentum, integrated with d⁴k/(2π)⁴. Lesson 81 showed that each loop also costs one power of ħ. A tree diagram has L = 0 and no integral at all. Every loop integral in Parts IX and X entered here.

# 4 Signs from Grassmann variables

## 4.1 Closed loops

Two vertices, ψ̄(x)γ^μψ(x) and ψ̄(y)γ^νψ(y), with ψ(x) paired with ψ̄(y) and ψ(y) with ψ̄(x), make a closed electron loop. To bring the pairs next to each other, move ψ(y) from the far right to the far left. It passes ψ̄(y), ψ(x), and ψ̄(x), three Grassmann variables, so it picks up a factor −1:

$$ ψ̄_x γ^μ ψ_x ψ̄_y γ^ν ψ_y = − ψ_y ψ̄_x γ^μ ψ_x ψ̄_y γ^ν → − tr[S_F[y − x] γ^μ S_F[x − y] γ^ν]

The spinor indices close into a trace, and the reordering gives the minus sign. Every closed fermion loop, with any number of vertices, needs an odd number of swaps to close, and so gives −1 and a trace.

### Worked example 2

Write the one-loop vacuum polarization in momentum space with every factor.

From Section 4.1, with a vertex ieγ at each end and loop momentum k,

$$ i Π^(μν)[q] = − ∫ (d⁴k)/((2π)⁴) tr[ [i e γ^μ] S_F[k] [i e γ^ν] S_F[k + q] ]

This is Lesson 62's expression. Two vertices come with 1/2!, and the two assignments of z₁ and z₂ to the two ends cancel it. Reversing the direction of the loop is the same assignment exchange, not a new diagram, so there is no further ½. The minus sign, the trace, and the i's all came from the Gaussian and the Grassmann algebra.

## 4.2 Identical fermions

In e⁻e⁻ → e⁻e⁻, the two outgoing electrons can be paired with the two vertices in two ways. The two pairings differ by an exchange of the two outgoing fields ψ(x₃) and ψ(x₄). That is one Grassmann swap, so the two diagrams enter with a relative minus sign. This is Lesson 54's Møller sign, now a consequence of anticommuting integration variables. In Compton scattering the two diagrams differ by exchanging two photon fields, which are ordinary numbers, so they add with a plus sign (Lesson 56).

# 5 External particles

The correlator of Section 1.2 has a full propagator on each external leg. The LSZ formula of Lesson 51 converts it into a scattering amplitude. Each external propagator is amputated, the leg is put on shell, and the leg is replaced by its wave function: u(p) for an incoming electron, ū(p) for an outgoing one, v̄ and v for incoming and outgoing positrons, and ε_μ or ε*_μ for photons. The path integral supplies the correlator, and LSZ, which is independent of how the correlator was computed, turns it into ℳ.

### Worked example 3

Derive the tree amplitude for e⁻(p₁)μ⁻(p₂) → e⁻(p₃)μ⁻(p₄), and recover the Coulomb potential from it.

At order e² there are two vertices, one on the electron line and one on the muon line. The 1/2! is cancelled by the two assignments. Both particles have charge −e, so both vertices are ieγ. With q = p₁ − p₃ and Feynman gauge,

$$ i ℳ = [ū_3 [i e γ^μ] u_1] (− i g_(μν))/(q²) [ū_4 [i e γ^ν] u_2] = i e² ([ū_3 γ^μ u_1] [ū_4 γ_μ u_2])/(q²)

There is no exchange diagram, because the electron and muon are distinguishable. In the nonrelativistic limit, only μ = 0 survives, with ū₃γ⁰u₁ → 2m_e and ū₄γ⁰u₂ → 2m_μ for unchanged spins. The momentum transfer is spacelike, q² = −|q|². So ℳ → −e²(2m_e)(2m_μ)/|q|². In this normalization, Born scattering from a potential gives ℳ = −(2m_e)(2m_μ)Ṽ(q). So Ṽ(q) = e²/|q|², whose Fourier transform is V(r) = e²/(4πr) = α/r. This is a repulsive Coulomb potential between like charges, the result Lesson 80 obtained from static sources.

# 7 Derivative couplings: scalar QED

## 7.1 Why the path integral helps here

For a charged scalar, the interaction contains time derivatives of the field. In the canonical method the conjugate momentum then depends on A, the interaction Hamiltonian is not simply −ℒ_int, and noncovariant contact terms appear that must cancel against noncovariant pieces of the time-ordered propagator of ∂φ. In the path integral none of this arises. The vertices are read from the Lagrangian, and the result is covariant term by term.

### Worked example 4

For ℒ = (D_μφ)*(D^μφ) − m²φ*φ, with D_μ = ∂_μ + iqA_μ, find the vertices.

The conjugate of the covariant derivative is (D_μφ)* = ∂_μφ* − iqA_μφ*. Multiplying out,

$$ [D_μ φ]^* D^μ φ = ∂_μ φ^* ∂^μ φ + i q A^μ [[∂_μ φ^*] φ − φ^* ∂_μ φ] + q² A_μ A^μ φ^* φ

For the cubic vertex, take an incoming scalar of momentum p, so φ ∝ e^{−ipx} and ∂_μφ → −ip_μφ, and an outgoing scalar of momentum p′, so φ* ∝ e^{ip′x} and ∂_μφ* → ip′_μφ*. The bracket becomes i(p′ + p)_μφ*φ. The vertex is i times the coefficient in ℒ, which is i × iq × i(p + p′)^μ:

$$ − i q (p + p′)^μ

The quartic term, iq²A_μA^μφ*φ, contains two identical photon fields, which can be paired with the two external photons in two ways. That gives the seagull vertex

$$ 2 i q² g^(μν)

Gauge invariance needs both vertices. In scalar Compton scattering, the seagull is exactly what makes k_μℳ^μ = 0, the scalar version of Lesson 71's check.

# 8 Gauge choice and counterterms

The photon rule carries ξ from the Faddeev–Popov average of Lesson 82. Every physical amplitude is independent of ξ, because the k_μk_ν term meets a conserved current (Lessons 71 and 72). If the Lagrangian is split into a renormalized part and counterterms, ℒ_ct = δ₂ψ̄i∂̸ψ − δ_mψ̄ψ − ¼δ₃F² + eδ₁ψ̄A̸ψ, the same reading rule gives the counterterm vertices: i(δ₂p̸ − δ_m) on an electron line, −iδ₃(k²g^{μν} − k^μk^ν) on a photon line, and ieδ₁γ^μ at a vertex. The Ward identity makes δ₁ = δ₂.

# 9 What this lesson has not done

It quoted the LSZ reduction formula from Lesson 51 rather than deriving it inside the path integral. It did not prove that vacuum graphs with residual symmetry factors are the only exceptions in QED, and it left the gauge-invariance check for scalar Compton scattering to Exercise 16. It did not derive the counterterm values, which Lessons 63 to 67 found from the divergent loops. The nonrelativistic normalization of the Born formula was used without proof.

# 11 Summary

1. Every field in e^{iS_int} can be replaced by a source derivative, so Z = exp[iS_int(δ)]Z₀, and perturbation theory is the expansion of that exponential.

2. Pairing derivatives with Z₀ gives propagators. Each vertex is i times its coefficient in ℒ, which for QED is ieγ^μ.

3. The 1/n! cancels against the relabelings of n vertex points. QED diagrams have essentially no leftover symmetry factors.

4. Vertex integrals give momentum conservation, and L = I − V + 1 momenta remain to be integrated.

5. Closed fermion loops give −1 and a trace, and exchanged identical fermions give a relative −1, both from Grassmann reordering.

6. LSZ turns correlators into amplitudes with u, v, and ε. The e–μ amplitude reproduces the Coulomb potential.

7. Derivative couplings give covariant vertices directly. For scalar QED they are −iq(p + p′)^μ and the seagull 2iq²g^{μν}.

# Solutions

**1.** δ/δη̄ of i∫η̄ψ is iψ, so −iδ/δη̄ gives ψ. For ψ̄η, move η to the left, ψ̄η = −ηψ̄, so δ/δη gives −iψ̄ and iδ/δη gives ψ̄.

**2.** The exponential is 1 at e = 0, leaving Z₀.

**3.** The order is ψ₁ψ̄₂ ψ̄_zψ_z A A. Moving ψ̄₂ to the right past ψ̄_z and ψ_z takes two swaps, giving (+1). Then ψ₁ψ̄_z and ψ_zψ̄₂ are adjacent pairs.

**4.** The two vertex points z₁ and z₂ can be assigned to the two distinct positions in the diagram in 2! ways, each giving the same integral.

**5.** ∫d⁴z e^{i(p₁ − p₂ − k)·z} = (2π)⁴δ⁴(p₁ − p₂ − k). The momentum entering the vertex equals the momentum leaving.

**6.** Vertex correction: I = 3, V = 3, so L = 1. Two-loop self-energy, such as the rainbow: I = 5, V = 4, so L = 2.

**7.** Bring ψ_z₃ to the front past five Grassmann variables, an odd number. The −1 is the same for any number of vertices.

**8.** Reversing the orientation conjugates the trace with the charge-conjugation matrix, giving (−1)³. The two orientations cancel.

**9.** The two diagrams pair ψ(x₃) with vertex 1 and ψ(x₄) with vertex 2, or the reverse. Going from one to the other exchanges ψ(x₃) and ψ(x₄), a single swap.

**10.** The two diagrams differ by exchanging which photon field attaches where. Photon fields commute, so there is no sign.

**11.** In the Dirac representation at rest, u = √(2m)(ξ, 0), and ū₃γ⁰u₁ = u₃†u₁ = 2mξ₃†ξ₁ = 2mδ_{s₃s₁}.

**12.** ∫d³q e^{iq·r}/[(2π)³|q|²] = 1/(4πr), so V = e²/(4πr) = α/r, using α = e²/4π.

**13.** The cross terms are (∂_μφ*)(iqA^μφ) + (−iqA_μφ*)(∂^μφ) = iqA^μ[(∂_μφ*)φ − φ*∂_μφ]. With ∂φ* → ip′ and ∂φ → −ip, the bracket is i(p′ + p)φ*φ. Multiplying by i for the vertex gives i·iq·i(p + p′) = −iq(p + p′).

**14.** A_μA^μ contains two photon fields. Two external photons can be attached to them in 2 ways.

**15.** −¼δ₃F² = ½δ₃A_μ(∂²g^{μν} − ∂^μ∂^ν)A_ν. In momentum space this is ½δ₃A(−k²g + kk)A. The two identical fields give a factor 2, so i times the coefficient is −iδ₃(k²g − kk).

**16.** Take γ(k₁, ε₁) + φ(p) → γ(k₂, ε₂) + φ(p′). The s-channel diagram is [−iq(2p + k₁)·ε₁][i/((p + k₁)² − m²)][−iq(2p′ + k₂)·ε₂*]. The u-channel diagram is [−iq(2p′ − k₁)·ε₁][i/((p − k₂)² − m²)][−iq(2p − k₂)·ε₂*]. The seagull is 2iq²ε₁·ε₂*. Replace ε₁ by k₁. In the s-channel, (2p + k₁)·k₁ = 2p·k₁ = (p + k₁)² − m², which cancels the propagator and leaves −iq²(2p′ + k₂)·ε₂*. In the u-channel, (2p′ − k₁)·k₁ = 2p′·k₁ = 2p·k₂ = −[(p − k₂)² − m²], which leaves +iq²(2p − k₂)·ε₂*. Using p′ − p + k₂ = k₁, the two sum to −2iq²k₁·ε₂*. The seagull gives +2iq²k₁·ε₂*, and the total vanishes. Without the seagull, the amplitude would not be gauge invariant.
