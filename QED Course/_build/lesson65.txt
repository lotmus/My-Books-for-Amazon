TITLE: Ultraviolet Divergences
TAGLINE: Turning three separate power-counting exercises into one formula, and discovering that only a short, finite list of QED diagrams can ever diverge at all
NUM: 65
NEXT: Lesson 66 takes this lesson's list of divergent diagrams and makes each one mathematically well defined, without changing any finite, checkable prediction — the technique called regularization.

# How to use this lesson

Three diagrams, three separate counting exercises, three different answers: Λ², Λ, ln Λ. Doing this once per diagram is fine for three diagrams. It stops being fine the moment a fourth or fifth one-loop diagram needs checking, and QED has far more than five. This lesson turns Lessons 62 through 64's case-by-case counting into a single formula, checks that formula against all three known answers before trusting it for anything new, and then asks the formula a question none of the three individual calculations could have answered on their own: how many kinds of divergent diagram does QED actually have. The answer is short enough to write on one hand, and that shortness is the reason the whole theory can be rescued at all. Expect five to seven hours.

## Learning objectives

- State the degree of divergence D found for each of Lessons 62, 63, and 64's diagrams, and notice the pattern connecting them.

- Derive D=4L−2P_γ−P_e by counting how many powers of the loop momentum each loop integral, each internal photon propagator, and each internal electron propagator contributes at large momentum.

- Verify this formula against all three previously computed diagrams before using it for anything new.

- Derive the equivalent, external-leg-only formula D=4−(3/2)E_e−E_γ, using the fact that every QED vertex joins exactly two fermion lines and one photon line.

- List every combination of external electron and photon legs for which D≥0, and identify which of Lessons 62 through 64's diagrams each one corresponds to.

- Explain, in one sentence, why this short list is the reason QED can be fixed at all, previewing Lesson 67.

- Reproduce both forms of the formula, and the resulting list of divergent diagrams, from memory.

# 1 Three answers, one question

Lesson 62 found vacuum polarization's integral growing like Λ². Lesson 63 found electron self-energy growing like Λ. Lesson 64 found the vertex correction growing only like ln Λ. Each of these numbers came from the same kind of exercise: find how the numerator grows at large loop momentum, find how the denominator grows, subtract, and see what power of k survives after the four-dimensional measure is folded in. Doing that exercise from scratch for the fourth, fifth, or fiftieth diagram in QED would work, but it would also mean re-deriving the same reasoning over and over. This lesson asks whether the reasoning itself, not just its three outputs, can be written down once.

# 2 Counting powers without picking a diagram first

## 2.1 What a loop contributes

Every independent loop momentum k is integrated over all four of its components, d⁴k. Written in the same spherical form used in Lessons 62 through 64, d⁴k∼k³dk, so before any propagator is even considered, a single loop integral already carries three explicit powers of k in its measure, plus one more from the thickness dk of each radial shell — four powers in total, matching the four components actually being integrated. With L independent loop momenta, this contributes 4L powers of k before anything else is counted.

## 2.2 What a propagator contributes

A photon propagator, −ig_μν/(k²+iε), falls off like 1/k² at large k: two powers of k removed from wherever it appears. An electron propagator, i(k̸+m)/(k²−m²+iε), falls off like k/k²=1/k at large k, since its numerator itself carries one explicit power of the momentum: only one power of k removed, not two. Every internal photon line in a diagram therefore contributes −2 to the running count, and every internal electron line contributes −1.

## 2.3 The formula

Adding Section 2.1's contribution from every loop to Section 2.2's contribution from every internal line,

$$ D = 4L − 2 P_γ − P_e

with P_γ the number of internal photon lines and P_e the number of internal electron lines in the diagram, and D the net power of k surviving at large momentum: a positive D means the integral grows like Λ^D, D=0 means it grows like ln Λ, and a negative D, not yet seen in this course, would mean the integral converges outright.

### Worked example 1

Explain why an internal electron line contributes only −1 to D, half of what an internal photon line contributes, even though both are "one internal line." A photon propagator's fall-off, 1/k², comes entirely from its denominator; an electron propagator's numerator, k̸+m, itself grows like k at large momentum, partly canceling the 1/k² of its own k²−m² denominator and leaving only a net 1/k. The counting in Section 2.2 tracks net power, not merely how many propagators are present, and an electron line's own numerator is what makes it a gentler suppression than a photon line's.

# 3 Checking the formula against what is already known

## 3.1 Vacuum polarization

Lesson 62's diagram has L=1, P_γ=0 (both photon lines are external, none internal), P_e=2 (the two internal electron lines forming the loop). Section 2.3 gives D=4(1)−2(0)−2=4−0−2=2, matching Lesson 62, Section 3.2, exactly.

## 3.2 Electron self-energy

Lesson 63's diagram has L=1, P_γ=1 (one internal photon line), P_e=1 (one internal electron line). D=4(1)−2(1)−1=4−2−1=1, matching Lesson 63, Section 4.2.

## 3.3 Vertex correction

Lesson 64's diagram has L=1, P_γ=1, P_e=2 (two internal electron lines). D=4(1)−2(1)−2=4−2−2=0, matching Lesson 64, Section 4.2.

### Worked example 2

State why this three-way check matters, given that the formula was derived, not guessed. A derived formula can still contain an arithmetic slip invisible in the derivation itself; checking it against three independent, already-verified answers, obtained by an entirely different method (direct power-counting on each specific diagram), is exactly the kind of cross-check this course has used throughout, and passing all three checks at once is strong evidence the general reasoning of Section 2 was carried out correctly.

# 4 A formula that does not need internal lines at all

## 4.1 What a QED vertex fixes

Every QED vertex, ψ̄γ^μψA_μ, joins exactly two fermion-line-ends and one photon-line-end, regardless of which specific diagram it sits inside. Across a diagram with V vertices, this fixes the total count of fermion-line-ends at 2V, and the total count of photon-line-ends at V.

## 4.2 Where those ends go

Every internal line has both its ends attached to vertices; every external line has only one end attached to a vertex, with its other end left free. Counting fermion-line-ends two ways, once as 2V from Section 4.1 and once by adding up where they actually are,

$$ 2V = 2 P_e + E_e

since each of the P_e internal electron lines supplies two vertex-ends and each of the E_e external electron lines supplies one. The identical reasoning for photon lines gives

$$ V = 2 P_γ + E_γ

## 4.3 Substituting away the internal-line counts

Solving Section 4.2's two relations, P_e=V−E_e/2 and P_γ=(V−E_γ)/2. Lesson 61, Section 4.1, gives L=P_e+P_γ−V+1 (writing I=P_e+P_γ for the total internal lines); substituting,

$$ L = (V−E_e/2) + (V−E_γ)/2 − V + 1 = V/2 − E_e/2 − E_γ/2 + 1

Substituting this, together with Section 4.2's expressions for P_e and P_γ, into Section 2.3's D=4L−2P_γ−P_e (Exercise 10 carries out every term of this substitution),

$$ D = 4 − (3/2) E_e − E_γ

a formula built entirely from the number of external legs, with every trace of V, L, P_e, and P_γ canceled away.

### Worked example 3

Verify Section 4.3's formula against vacuum polarization directly, using only its external legs. Vacuum polarization has two external photon legs and no external electron legs, E_e=0, E_γ=2, giving D=4−(3/2)(0)−2=4−2=2, matching Section 3.1 without any reference to how many internal lines or vertices the diagram happens to have.

# 5 What the formula predicts

## 5.1 A short list

D≥0 is required for a diagram to diverge at all (D<0 means the integral already converges, however it might still be handled). Section 4.3's formula, D=4−(3/2)E_e−E_γ, forces (3/2)E_e+E_γ≤4, and since E_e must be even for any QED diagram (electron lines cannot simply end; Lesson 48's fermion-flow arrows must run continuously from an incoming leg to an outgoing one), the only possibilities are:

| E_e | E_γ | D | Matches |
| 0 | 2 | 2 | Vacuum polarization (Lesson 62) |
| 2 | 0 | 1 | Electron self-energy (Lesson 63) |
| 2 | 1 | 0 | Vertex correction (Lesson 64) |
| 0 | 4 | 0 | Photon-photon scattering (not built in this course) |

Every other combination of external electron and photon legs gives D<0, an integral that converges outright with no divergence to handle at all.

## 5.2 Why a short list is good news

This is the single most important fact this lesson establishes: there are only finitely many kinds of diagram in all of QED whose divergence needs to be dealt with, no matter how many loops or how many vertices a more complicated diagram might have piled on top of one of these basic shapes. Every one of the four rows in Section 5.1's table corresponds to a quantity already present, in some form, in the original Lagrangian of Lesson 41 — a photon two-point function, an electron two-point function, the electron-photon coupling — or, in the fourth row's case, a divergence that gauge invariance removes entirely despite the naive count, a fact this course does not prove but reports honestly as known. Lesson 67 uses exactly this shortness to argue that fixing these few divergent pieces is enough to fix the entire theory, at any order, a property called renormalizability.

### Worked example 4

Explain briefly why the fourth row of Section 5.1's table, photon-photon scattering, is not built in this course even though it appears on the list. Its degree of divergence, D=0, suggests a logarithmic divergence exactly like the vertex correction's, but the actual diagram (built from a closed loop of four internal electron propagators and four external photons) turns out to be finite once its full tensor structure and gauge invariance are accounted for, a more delicate cancellation than this course's three worked examples needed and one this course reports rather than derives.

# 7 Summary

1. Counting powers of the loop momentum in general, rather than diagram by diagram, gives D=4L−2P_γ−P_e, with +4 per independent loop and −2 or −1 per internal photon or electron line respectively.

2. This formula reproduces all three of Lessons 62 through 64's results exactly, a meaningful cross-check on the general derivation.

3. Using the fact that every QED vertex joins two fermion-line-ends and one photon-line-end, the internal-line counts can be eliminated entirely, giving D=4−(3/2)E_e−E_γ in terms of external legs alone.

4. Only four combinations of external legs give D≥0: two photons, two electrons, two electrons and one photon, and four photons — exactly the three diagrams already built plus one this course does not construct.

5. This short, finite list is why QED can be repaired at all: fixing a handful of divergent quantities, already present in the Lagrangian's own parameters, is enough for the entire theory, the subject of Lesson 67.

# Solutions

**1.** Vacuum polarization: D=2. Electron self-energy: D=1. Vertex correction: D=0. Each is exactly one less than the diagram before it, 2 then 1 then 0.

**2.** A photon propagator's numerator is a fixed number (the metric g_μν or, in the Coulomb-gauge form, the polarization sum), carrying no explicit k-dependence, so its full 1/k² denominator behavior survives unmodified; an electron propagator's numerator, k̸+m, grows like k itself at large momentum, canceling one of the two powers of k in its k²−m² denominator and leaving a net fall-off of only 1/k.

**3.** D=4(1)−2(0)−2=4−0−2=2.

**4.** D=4(1)−2(1)−1=4−2−1=1.

**5.** D=4(1)−2(1)−2=4−2−2=0.

**6.** The formula was derived from general reasoning about how loops and propagators behave at large momentum, a different route from the specific, direct power-counting used in Lessons 62 through 64 for each individual diagram; agreement between a general derivation and three independently obtained specific results, all three at once, is strong evidence that no error crept into either the general derivation or the three specific calculations it is being checked against.

**7.** A single vertex supplies two fermion-line-ends and one photon-line-end; the interaction term ψ̄γ^μψA_μ contains exactly two Dirac fields (ψ̄ and ψ) and exactly one photon field (A_μ), and each field appearing in the term corresponds to exactly one line meeting the vertex in a diagram.

**8.** Every vertex contributes 2 fermion-line-ends (Exercise 7), so V vertices contribute 2V in total. Separately, each of the P_e internal electron lines has both its ends attached to vertices, contributing 2 line-ends per line, or 2P_e in total; each of the E_e external electron lines has only one end attached to a vertex (the other end is free), contributing 1 line-end per line, or E_e in total. Since both countings describe the same set of line-ends, 2V=2P_e+E_e.

**9.** By the identical method, each vertex contributes 1 photon-line-end (Exercise 7), giving V in total from all vertices; each of the P_γ internal photon lines contributes 2 line-ends, and each of the E_γ external photon lines contributes 1, giving V=2P_γ+E_γ.

**10.** From Exercise 8, P_e=V−E_e/2. From Exercise 9, P_γ=(V−E_γ)/2. Substituting into L=P_e+P_γ−V+1: L=(V−E_e/2)+(V−E_γ)/2−V+1=V/2−E_e/2−E_γ/2+1. Substituting all three (P_e, P_γ, and L) into D=4L−2P_γ−P_e: D=4[V/2−E_e/2−E_γ/2+1]−2[(V−E_γ)/2]−[V−E_e/2]=[2V−2E_e−2E_γ+4]−[V−E_γ]−[V−E_e/2]=2V−2E_e−2E_γ+4−V+E_γ−V+E_e/2. Collecting the V terms: 2V−V−V=0. Collecting the E_e terms: −2E_e+E_e/2=−(3/2)E_e. Collecting the E_γ terms: −2E_γ+E_γ=−E_γ. Total: D=4−(3/2)E_e−E_γ.

**11.** D=4−(3/2)(2)−0=4−3=1, matching Lesson 63's known result.

**12.** D=4−(3/2)(2)−1=4−3−1=0, matching Lesson 64's known result.

**13.** Lesson 48's fermion-flow arrows run continuously along an electron line from an incoming leg (or an outgoing antiparticle leg) to an outgoing leg (or an incoming antiparticle leg); a fermion line can enter and leave a diagram, but it cannot simply terminate partway, so external fermion legs always come in pairs, one where a fermion-flow line enters the diagram and one where it leaves, forcing E_e to be even.

**14.** With E_e even, the possible values are E_e=0,2,4,…. For E_e=0: (3/2)(0)+E_γ≤4 gives E_γ≤4, so E_γ=0,1,2,3,4 are candidates, but only E_γ=2 (D=2) and E_γ=4 (D=0) are shown in Section 5.1 (E_γ=0 gives D=4, a diagram with no external legs at all and not a physical process; E_γ=1 and E_γ=3 involve an odd number of photons attached to a single closed fermion loop, which vanishes by a symmetry this course does not derive, and so are not included). For E_e=2: (3/2)(2)+E_γ≤4 gives E_γ≤1, so E_γ=0 (D=1) and E_γ=1 (D=0), both shown in Section 5.1. For E_e=4: (3/2)(4)=6>4 already, so no value of E_γ≥0 gives D≥0. This confirms Section 5.1's table lists every combination with D≥0 that corresponds to an actual, physically sensible diagram.

**15.** A diagram with D<0 converges on its own once written down as an ordinary integral, with no need for the regularization-and-renormalization machinery Lessons 66 and 67 develop for the D≥0 cases; it still needs to be evaluated to get a numerical prediction, but evaluating a convergent integral is a much more ordinary task than making sense of a divergent one.

**16.** D=4−(3/2)(4)−0=4−6=−2, a negative degree of divergence; such a diagram is expected to converge on its own, with no divergence needing to be handled, consistent with Exercise 14's finding that E_e=4 admits no combination with D≥0.
