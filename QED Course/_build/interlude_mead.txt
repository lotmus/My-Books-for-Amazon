# How to use this interlude

This interlude is later on purpose. Prologues 1–4 started with Feynman's photons and arrows. Prologue 5 put Maxwell's E and B beside that picture. Lessons 8 and 38–40 then derived the potentials and the covariant derivative. Carver Mead's Collective Electrodynamics (2000) reads those results in the opposite order from nineteenth-century pedagogy: the primitive object is the vector potential as a standard of quantum phase for charged matter; E and B are derived. The interlude states that view, connects it to Aharonov–Bohm and to Lesson 38, and does not replace Feynman diagrams or Maxwell's equations. Expect four to six hours.

## Learning objectives

- State Mead's inversion: A is primary because it is what appears in the phase of a charge; E=−∇φ−∂A/∂t and B=∇×A are derived.

- Connect ∮A·dl to a measurable phase shift even in a region with B=0 (Aharonov–Bohm).

- Identify Lesson 38's local U(1) as the same insistence: a phase convention for ψ is meaningless without A_μ.

- Place inductance and collective currents as many-charge, many-photon limits of that phase coupling, not as a third electrodynamics.

- State what Mead's view does not cancel: Maxwell's equations, Feynman's three actions, or the loop calculations of Parts IX–X.

# 1 Why this is later

Mead's book is unreadable as a first chapter of this course: it assumes quantum phase, a vector potential, and the failure of E and B to explain an interference experiment where B=0 along the electron's path. Those objects did not exist in Prologue 1. They exist now. Lesson 8 showed that Maxwell can be rewritten in potentials. Lesson 38 showed that a local phase rotation on ψ forces A_μ. Lesson 40 showed [D_μ,D_ν]=iq F_μν: the field strength is already inside the covariant derivative. Mead's view is those three lessons read as a single claim about what is primitive.

# 2 Phase is what a charge notices

An electron's wave function has a phase. Transporting the electron around a loop that encloses a solenoid, with B=0 along the path and B≠0 inside the solenoid, still shifts the interference pattern. The shift is e∮A·dl, the flux, even though the electron never entered the B field. That is the Aharonov–Bohm effect. It is unintelligible if E and B are the only real electromagnetic quantities, and natural if A is the quantity that sets phase. Lesson 11's wave function and Lesson 8's A were waiting to be multiplied.

### Worked example 1

Does Aharonov–Bohm mean Maxwell's B=∇×A is wrong? No. ∇×A is still B, and Faraday's law still holds. It means that two potentials differing by a gradient can be physically distinguished by a loop that cannot be contracted without crossing flux, i.e. that A is not a disposable convenience. Gauge invariance (Lesson 38) is the remaining, correctly local, ambiguity, not the right to discard A.

# 3 Collective electrodynamics in one paragraph

A wire full of electrons shares a phase standard through A. Pushing a current is changing that collective phase in time; the inertia of the current is inductance, the energy stored in A. Mead develops circuits in that language. This course does not redo circuit theory. It records the moral: the same A_μ that appears in D_μ=∂_μ+iqA_μ is the object a laboratory inductor is using. The "collective" is a large-occupation, many-charge limit, the cousin of Prologue 5's many-photon Maxwell limit.

# 4 What is not being replaced

- Maxwell's equations remain the field equations for F_μν (Lesson 7, Lesson 42).
- Feynman's three actions remain the Feynman rules (Prologue 3, Lesson 49).
- Loops, running, g−2 remain computations in that theory (Parts IX–X).

Mead's view is a choice of primitive vocabulary: start from phase and A, derive E and B. This course started from photons and arrows, included Maxwell as the many-photon alternative, and arrived at A by gauge invariance. The three vocabularies are one theory. Anyone who uses Mead to discard diagrams, or Feynman to discard potentials, has left this book.

# Summary

1. This interlude waits until A_μ and D_μ exist.

2. Charges couple to A through phase; E and B are derivatives.

3. Aharonov–Bohm is the experiment that makes that claim sharp.

4. Inductance is the collective, many-charge limit of the same coupling.

5. Maxwell, Feynman, and Mead are one QED in three reading orders.

# Solutions

**1.** No quantum phase and no A_μ had been defined.

**2.** B=∇×A, E=−∇φ−∂A/∂t.

**3.** Δθ=e∮A·dl (ℏ=1), equal to e times enclosed flux.

**4.** A local phase rotation on ψ spoils ∂_μψ; D_μ=∂_μ+iqA_μ restores homogeneous transformation, which introduces A_μ.

**5.** [D_μ,D_ν]=iq F_μν.

**6.** No; F_μν still obeys them. They become identities-plus-equations for a derived F.

**7.** No; the three actions still generate the S-matrix. A is the field whose quantum is the photon.

**8.** ∮A·dl around a loop encircling the solenoid is the enclosed flux, a gauge invariant. The local value of A can be changed; the loop integral cannot, if the loop is kept.
