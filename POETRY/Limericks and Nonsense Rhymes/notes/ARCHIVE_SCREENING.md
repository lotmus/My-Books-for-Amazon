# Archive screening: four-line rhymes

## Scope and outcome

The book-local `bak` folder contains 40 TXT files. The 20 nominal 500-poem batches contain exactly 10,000 four-line stanzas: 19 files have `500` in the filename, and `engineers physicists.txt` is the misnamed twentieth batch.

Every one of those 10,000 stanzas was parsed and checked for its four line endings, exact duplicate text, and ABAB end-rhyme using the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary. The rhyme check found exact dictionary matches for 8,910 stanzas; 41 contained an ending word absent from the dictionary. This is a pronunciation-based screen, not a quality score: many broken stanzas rhyme by sound. There are 6,634 unique stanza texts and 3,366 exact duplicate copies across the batch files.

The files rely heavily on repeated noun-and-rhyme substitutions inside a small number of sentence frames, including “and called it pure [word],” “and kept on going at [word],” and “then became another [word].” That made many lines awkward, but the absurd images and topics are useful seeds. The rhyme screen is a candidate finder, not a final quality verdict. Two salvage passes rebuilt 80 archive ideas as grammatical, rhyming quatrains with clearer comic turns and room for readers to find their own meanings. No source stanza was copied verbatim. Continue reviewing the remaining rhyme candidates for images and situations that can be repaired into publishable poems.

The **Four-Line Nonsense Rhymes** section contains 381 poems: two selections from the separate `ai_generated_nonsense_limericks_1000.txt`, 299 earlier new pieces, and 80 archive-inspired rewrites. The raw TXT files remain unchanged in `bak`.
