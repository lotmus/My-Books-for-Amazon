# Book 3 outline v2

**Scally and the Hill with the Hanging Socks** is Book 3 of *The Step Stories*, by Anneliese Tauber. This outline records the v9 manuscript as built; the earlier planning draft remains in `Book3_Outline_v1.md`.

## Book at a glance

Eleven chapters follow the village from the first November frost to the first warm evening of spring. Hal, Wen, Titch, Tabitha, Pip, Scally, Hopsi and Gucci investigate the clicking from Hanger Hill, where bats hibernate. The Mayor plans to turn their roost into a cheese cellar. A winter blizzard also traps Dr. Liang away from home just as Titch's mother goes into labor.

The manuscript has about 23,600 words and 119 inline illustrations. It is written for independent readers around ages 7–10 and works as a read-aloud from about age 6.

## Chapters

1. **The First Frost.** Wen and Dr. Liang arrive in the village. Wen repairs the mill clock before speaking. Scally sells “frost insurance”; Wen points out that frost cannot be insured away.
2. **The Doctor's Bag.** Dr. Liang explains how a stethoscope works and teaches the children that medicine is not candy. Pip gets his head stuck in a churn and is safely helped out. The children welcome Wen.
3. **Ice on the Pond.** Wen explains that ice thickness varies and one measurement cannot establish safety. Pip falls through; the adults and children use a rope to reach him. The story models calling for help and reaching or throwing rather than going onto the ice.
4. **The Hill That Clicks.** The group watches the cave from behind a boundary rope in daylight. They do not enter or shine a light into the winter roost. A few bats are visible near the opening, and the children leave them undisturbed.
5. **The Mayor's Cheese Cellar.** The Mayor announces plans to seal the roost. Scally admits that he invented a rumor about bats to sell paper hats. The Mayor makes a bargain about learning what bats do for the village.
6. **Wen's Listener.** Wen builds a storybook sound listener from a funnel, spring and bell. The children use it from a distance to hear the bats' clicking.
7. **The Picture of the Baby.** Dr. Liang demonstrates a storybook ultrasound scanner and later shares Hal's mother's pregnancy news privately with her. Hal gradually understands that his family is changing.
8. **The Sledding Day.** The children race sleds and make a snowball. Adults keep them away from roads and water and stop them from tying sleds together.
9. **The Night of the Big Snow.** Dr. Liang is returning from a call at a hill farm when a blizzard traps her away from the village. Titch's mother begins labor. The children stay inside while adults keep to an anchored rope between the houses. Wen rings the mill bell; Gucci follows the sound and returns with Dr. Liang.
10. **The Baby.** Mrs. Crumb helps deliver Titch and Pip's baby brother. The family welcomes him, and Scally holds him.
11. **The First Warm Evening.** The villagers watch the bats emerge after hibernation. Hal presents a clearly qualified estimate of the colony's insect consumption. The Mayor admits that the bats deserve a protected roost whether or not their economic value can be proved.

## Science and safety notes

The story introduces echolocation, hibernation, ultrasound, heartbeat, water expansion, ice variability, and estimation. Hal's bat arithmetic is explicitly approximate: about 400 bats times an assumed 500 small insects each per night, over 100 nights, gives roughly 20 million.

Ice thickness guidance says four inches is a general guideline for new, clear ice for one person on foot, never a guarantee. It directs families to local guidance and says children should stay off ice until an informed adult has checked it in several places and approved it. The bat scene models observing a winter roost from a distance without entry or light. The blizzard scene keeps the children indoors and limits outdoor movement to adults attached to a rope.

The science and safety still need an outside review by an appropriate teacher, physician and wildlife expert before publication; the listing checklist records this as pending.
