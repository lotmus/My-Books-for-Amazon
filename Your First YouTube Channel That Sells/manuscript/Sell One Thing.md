*A working guide for creators who want a channel, not a hobby*

# Sell One Thing

*A channel that sells has one thing a stranger can buy. Views are not that thing.*

The title of this book is a job description. A channel that sells does not mean a channel with a lot of views, a round subscriber number, or a monetization badge you hope will arrive later. It means a stranger can watch a video, understand one offer, and pay for it. Everything else in this book — the click, the cheap production, the platform thresholds — exists to make that sale more likely and less expensive to attempt.

If you cannot name the offer in one sentence, you do not have a channel that sells yet. You have a place to post. Posting is allowed. It is not the goal.

## I. Name the buyer before the topic

A topic is not a buyer. “Fitness,” “personal finance,” and “home repair” describe shelves. A buyer is a person with a situation and a reason to spend money on it.

Finish this sentence before you design a banner: “This channel helps [specific person] who wants [specific result] and is willing to pay for [specific offer].” If the last bracket is “my videos,” rewrite it. The video is how they find the offer. It is not the offer, unless you are selling a course and the video is the sample of that course.

Write a second sentence: “This channel is a poor fit for people who want [different result].” A retirement-planning channel that also tries to serve day traders will teach neither of them well enough to earn the trust a purchase requires. Narrowing is not a branding exercise. It is how you stop making videos for people who will never buy.

## II. One offer, on purpose

Pick one of these for the first ninety days. You may add a second later. You may not start with all three and call it a business.

- **A thing you made.** A short paid guide, a template pack, a recorded class, a physical product you can ship. You keep most of the money. You also own the support.
- **A service you do.** An audit, a setup, a consult, a done-for-you job with a written scope. One clear price or a small menu of two. “Message me for rates” is not an offer.
- **Someone else’s product, with a disclosed affiliate link.** You recommend a tool or book you would use anyway, and you are paid if the viewer buys. The link has to be allowed on the platform, and the disclosure has to sit next to the link, not in a footnote. The money chapter covers placement. This chapter only requires that you choose the product before you film the recommendation.

Ad revenue from a platform partner program is not this offer. It can become extra income after the thresholds in the money chapter. It is a slow, uncertain side effect of attention. Do not build the channel as if the badge is the product. A channel with no offer can be popular and still sell nothing you control.

## III. The promise the video has to keep

Write the offer the way you would say it out loud at the end of a video, in under twenty seconds:

“If you want [result], I made [offer]. It costs [price]. Here is the link. You do not have to buy it to use what this video already taught you.”

That last sentence matters. A useful video that withholds the actual steps until checkout trains people to distrust you. Give the teaching away. Sell the faster path, the template, the personal help, or the tool that saves the work. The scam version is a video that promises a method and delivers only a pitch. Do not make that video.

Check the promise against the product. If the guide says it includes a checklist, the file includes a checklist. If the service says “one revision,” the page says what a revision is. Disappointment after payment is more expensive than a slow start, because the same viewers tell other people.

## IV. A price you can explain

You do not need a perfect price. You need a price you can say without flinching, and a reason you would change it.

Use arithmetic, not a feeling. These figures are hypothetical planning numbers, not typical results.

Suppose the offer earns you $30 after fees on each sale. Suppose, as a planning guess you will replace with your own count, that 2 of every 100 people who watch the video where you make the offer go on to buy. You need about 50 views of that video for one sale, and about $30 of earnings. If those 50 views cost you more than $30 in ads or in time you cannot spare, the offer is not carrying the channel yet. Change the offer, the video, or the price. Do not answer that result by posting more videos with no offer in them.

A service priced at $150 with the same 2-in-100 guess needs only the same 50 views to earn $150. Fewer buyers can be a better business than many buyers of a $9 file, if you can deliver the service without drowning. Write down which one you can actually fulfill this month.

## V. Put the sale where a stranger can reach it

The sale does not happen inside the player unless the platform gives you a product shelf and you are eligible to use it. For almost every new channel, the sale happens at a link: a simple page with the name of the offer, the price, what is included, and a way to pay or book.

Before the first video, that page exists. The channel description links to it. The video where you explain the offer links to it in the first two lines of the description and in the pinned comment. One primary link. A row of six competing links is a menu, and menus are where undecided people go to leave.

If you are not ready to take money, you are not ready to call the channel a channel that sells. A waitlist with no offer is a delay. A free email list is allowed as a step only if you say what they will be offered and when. “Join my community” is not a sale.

## VI. Do this before you film

Write the buyer sentence, the unfit-viewer sentence, and the twenty-second offer. Create the page with the price on it. Put that single link in the channel description. If you cannot do those four things, the next chapters will help you get watched and will not help you get paid.

## VII. Claims that do not survive a checkout page

- That a channel sells once it has subscribers. Subscribers are people who might see the next video. They are not customers until they pay.
- That you should wait for a partner program before you sell anything. The program pays the platform’s advertisers. Your offer pays you on a sale you can see.
- That more offers convert better than one. One clear offer gives a new viewer a decision. Five offers give them an excuse to make none.
- That the best videos never mention the product. A teaching video can stand alone and still end with one honest next step. Hiding the offer is not integrity. It is a missing price tag.
- That a specific conversion rate is normal. The 2-in-100 figure above is a planning placeholder. Replace it with your own count after the offer video has real views. Until then it is arithmetic, not a promise.

A last limit. This chapter will not choose your niche for you, and it will not make a weak product sell. It will refuse the version of a channel that posts for months and still has nothing a stranger can buy.
