*A working guide for creators who want a channel, not a hobby*

# When Nothing Sells

*Find the earliest break in the path. A new video is not a diagnosis.*

When a channel does not sell, the tempting explanation is “the algorithm” or “the niche.” Sometimes the videos are simply hard to find. More often the path from a stranger to a payment is broken in a specific place, and another video will not repair a missing price. Start at the top of this list. Fix the first item that is true. Do not fix item six while item two is still true.

## I. You cannot say what you sell

If the twenty-second offer is still unfinished, stop. The offer chapter is the whole diagnosis. No title, thumbnail, or posting schedule sells an unnamed thing. Write the sentence. Put a price on a page. Then come back.

If the video asks for two payments — the file and the call, or the file and a sponsor — you still cannot say what you sell. Pick one for the next ninety days. Take the other line off the page.

## II. People cannot find the price

The videos exist. The page exists. Viewers still do not arrive there. Look at the first two lines of the description, the pinned comment, and the words you say at the end. If the link is under a paragraph about your story, move it up. If you never say the name of the offer, say it. If you can read the address and cannot tap it, the price is hidden. That includes a YouTube Short, and any other site where you have not finished the phone test. If three test viewers cannot find the price without you pointing, the packaging failed and the niche did not.

This is the same shelf as the click chapter, applied to money instead of curiosity. A title can win the click and still lose the sale if the click leads nowhere.

## III. The wrong people are watching

The offer video has views, and the viewers are not the buyer you named. Read the comments and the search phrases if the platform shows them. If they came for a different promise — entertainment, a face, a controversy — your teaching will not convert them, and it should not. Change the title and the first ten seconds so the unfit viewer leaves early. Do not rewrite the offer to chase whoever happened to arrive.

## IV. The right people watch and do not believe you

They reach the page and do not pay. Ask what is missing, of the page and of the proof video. Common true answers: the scope is vague, the price has no reason, the method video hid the hard step, the proof is a claim without a walk-through. Fix one of those. A discount is not the first fix. A discount on a vague offer teaches people to wait for a lower price on something they still do not understand.

## V. They believe you and the checkout fails

They mean to pay, and the page breaks, the booking tool confuses them, or you take three days to reply. That is not a content problem. Buy the thing yourself, on a phone, as a stranger would. Fix the step where you stalled. If you cannot deliver, stop taking orders until you can, and say so on the page.

## VI. You are spending to hide the break

Ads, boosts, and “growth” services send more people into the same path. If the path loses them, you bought a faster loss. Turn the spend off until a handful of unpaid viewers can reach the page. Then, if you spend at all, cap it the way you would cap any test: a number written down before you start, and a date when you stop even if you are curious.

Platform ad revenue, if you ever qualify, does not repair a broken offer. It can sit beside a working one. It cannot substitute for one.

## VII. The honest stop

Some offers should not continue. If, after the path is clear, the right people have seen a true demonstration and still will not pay a price that covers your time, the channel is not failing to sell. The offer is being refused. Change the buyer, the promise, or the price once. If you change all three, you started a different business, and the old sheets should be labeled as closed so you do not mix the counts.

Quitting a refused offer is more useful than another month of videos that protect you from hearing no.

## VIII. Do this once

Write which of the seven breaks is true today. Do the single fix that break requires. Set a date to count page visits and payments again. Leave every other variable alone until that date.

> **Key takeaway:** Find the earliest break. Fix that one. Leave every other variable alone until the next count.

## IX. Claims that do not survive the path

- That low views and no sales are the same problem. No views means people are not arriving. Views with no page visits means the shelf is hidden. Page visits with no payment means the offer, the proof, or the checkout. Treat them as different jobs.
- That a new niche is the fix for a hidden link. Moving the link up is the fix for a hidden link.
- That you need more subscribers before anyone will buy. You need one buyer who can find the price. Subscribers can wait.
- That harsh comments are the diagnosis. A comment that names a missing step is useful. A comment that dislikes your face is not a business plan.
- That persistence means never changing the offer. Persistence means keeping the count. Refusal is data. Ignoring refusal is how a hobby becomes expensive.

A last limit. This chapter will not promise that every clear offer eventually sells. It will make the reason it does not sell visible, which is the only way the next month is more useful than the last one.
