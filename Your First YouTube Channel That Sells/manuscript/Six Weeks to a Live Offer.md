*A working guide for creators who want a channel, not a hobby*

# Six Weeks to a Live Offer

*A calendar for getting one offer in front of strangers. Move the dates. Do not skip the offer.*

Use this as a frame, the way you would use a launch calendar for a book. If a step is not true yet — the page does not take payment, the example in the video is fake, the license on the footage is unread — move the date. A channel that goes live with nothing to buy has followed a posting schedule and missed the title of this book.

Hours below are a modest pace for one person using the free tools in the production chapter. If you have less time, stretch a week into two. Do not delete the offer to save an evening.

## I. Week one: the offer on a page

Write the buyer sentence, the unfit-viewer sentence, and the twenty-second offer. Pick one shape from the offer chapter and put that shape’s sentence on the page, with the price. Read it aloud. If you stumble, the viewer will leave.

The eight jobs in the videos chapter are the only set of videos this calendar films. The click chapter uses that same set. Week one does not add a second quota.

Set up the channel only as far as the offer needs: a name a buyer understands, a description whose first lines say who it helps and contain the link, and a banner you can read on a phone. Skip the logo exploration. The page is the product. The banner is a sign. On your phone, open that link and finish a test payment or a test booking before you film anything. If you will also post the video on another site, open that site’s profile link the same day and tap it. A caption you cannot tap is not the shelf.

## II. Week two: two videos, not eight

Film “who it is for” and the offer video. Package them with the click chapter’s limits: keyword first in the title, benefit in the first lines of the description, one link, pinned comment with the same link. Review the captions before you publish. A misheard price in the automatic captions is a broken offer.

Publish both in the same week if they are ready. Tell three people who match the buyer sentence, not three people who like you, and ask them to try to find the price without your help. If they cannot, fix the description before you make video three.

## III. Week three: their words, then the method

Film the problem video in the buyer’s own phrase, then the method video. Show the steps you would be embarrassed to hide. Export both with the settings in the production chapter. Publish them with the same single link, and say once, near the end of the method video, what the paid version adds.

The problem video is the one most people replace with a clever title. Use the words the buyer would type. The method video is the one most people skip, because giving the method away feels like losing the sale. It is the opposite. The viewer who can finish a small version alone is the viewer who believes you can help with the larger one.

## IV. Week four: proof and one objection

Film proof you actually have. If the proof is small, say it is small. Film one objection video: the reason a sensible buyer hesitates, answered without insulting them.

Check the offer page again. Does it match what these videos now promise? Update the page before you update the thumbnails. A sharper thumbnail sending people to a stale page is a faster way to disappoint them.

## V. Week five: comparison, the path, then the count

Film the comparison: your offer versus the usual alternative, including doing nothing. Then film the short video whose only job is to name which of the other seven to watch next, in order, by title. Publish both. Then stop making new videos for several days and read only these counts, written down with the date:

- Views on the offer video.
- Clicks from your videos to the offer page, if your page or the platform shows them. If you cannot see clicks, count visits to the page another honest way, or ask the three buyers from week two what they clicked.
- Payments, including zero.
- The question people actually asked, in their words.

Do not change the price, the title, and the offer in the same week. You would not know which change mattered.

## VI. Week six: one decision

Choose one of these, and write the reason in a sentence:

- **Keep the offer and fill any gap in the eight,** because someone who is not a friend reached the page or paid. If all eight are already live, do not invent a ninth topic. Make the path between them clearer.
- **Rewrite the offer video and the first lines of the description,** because people watched and did not reach the page.
- **Change the offer,** because people reached the page and the same objection showed up twice. Change the price, the scope, or the buyer. Do not change all three.
- **Stop spending money to push it,** if you bought ads or a boost and the page still got no visits from strangers. Fix the path before you buy more attention.

Then schedule the next four weeks as any of the eight you still have not published, not as a new niche. If the eight are live, the next four weeks are repeats and fixes of that set. A second niche in week six is how a channel that might have sold becomes two hobbies.

## VII. Do this on paper first

Copy the six weeks onto one page with your real dates. Put the offer-page link at the top. If a week has no task that touches the offer, that week is optional. The filming is not optional forever. The offer is not optional at all.

> **Key takeaway:** Six weeks to a live price. Move a date if a step is not true. Do not skip the offer. Weeks two through five are the eight videos, including the problem in the buyer’s words and the video that points to the next one.

## VIII. Claims that do not survive week six

- That six weeks is a guarantee of income. It is a deadline for learning whether a stranger can find the price. Income is the later count.
- That you should wait to publish until all eight videos are perfect. Two honest videos and a live price teach you more than eight private drafts.
- That a quiet week means the topic is dead. Quiet means you do not have evidence yet. Evidence is page visits and payments, not a feeling about the niche.
- That ads can replace weeks two through five. Ads send strangers to whatever you already published. If the path is missing, you paid to show them a gap.
- That changing everything in week six is decisive. One change, then another quiet measurement, is decisive. A new banner, a new niche, and a new price is a new project.

A last limit. This calendar will not negotiate with a missing price. If the page cannot take money or a booking by the end of week one, week two does not start.
