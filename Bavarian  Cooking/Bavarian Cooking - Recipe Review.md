# Bavarian Cooking Made Simple – Recipe Review

Method: I read all ~100 recipes in the Word file and compared quantities and methods against published German recipes (Lecker, Küchengötter, Der Standard, ORF, infranken, Rewe, Ökotest, King Arthur etc.). Items marked ✔ were confirmed by search; the rest rest on standard culinary ratios. Nothing in your Word file or the EPUB has been changed yet.

## B. Smaller corrections

- **Wine soup**: sugar low (3 Tbsp); chives odd on a sweet soup – use croutons/whipped cream only.
- **Leberspätzle soup**: step cut off mid-sentence ("cook until"); cooked spätzle never quantified.
- **Onion soup**: "4 large onions (about 2 cups)" inconsistent (~6–8 cups); 1 Tbsp butter + 1 Tbsp oil is too little; "Swiss" vs "Gruyère"; nutmeg ½ tsp is too much (¼ tsp).
- **Goulash soup**: stray "bell pepper, diced"; typos ("hoür", "pot.simmer"); add 1 more onion, simmer 1½ h.
- **Brotzeit**: Apple-crackling lard in instructions but not ingredients; rolls listed twice.
- **Wurstsalat**: add ~100 ml broth, 1 tsp mustard, 2 gherkins (used in method, not listed) – otherwise dry.
- **Radish salad**: cucumber in method, not in ingredients.
- **Kassler**: only 6 oz sauerkraut for 4 chops → 1 lb; caraway mentioned but not listed.
- **Beef Tartare**: baguette, olive oil, garlic not listed; add raw-beef safety note.
- **Crispy Duck**: onion/apple quantities missing in the Blaukraut list; breast 8 + 12 min is well-done (8 + 6–8 for medium); "plain cookies" vs "Spekulatius".
- **Jägerschnitzel**: 2–3 Tbsp oil too little to shallow-fry 4 cutlets (~½ cm / clarified butter).
- **Steckerlfisch**: 10–15 min is short for whole 300 g fish; ✔ sources 15–20 min in oven at 180 °C or ~8–10 min/side on the grill.
- **Pork chops with pears**: 5 min/side then another 2–3 min/side overcooks (7 oz chops): sear 3 min/side.
- **Pork liver**: 4 min/side is too long (rubbery); 1½–2 min/side, optional milk soak.
- **Schupfnudeln**: 6 oz flour per 18 oz potatoes is on the heavy side (~4½ oz).
- **Pretzels**: milk listed but never added in the method.
- **Dampfnudeln**: no second rise; add 1 Tbsp sugar to cooking liquid for the caramel base.
- **Plum cake (Zwetschgendatschi)**: 150 ml milk for 14 oz flour stiff → 200 ml.
- **Bavarian Cream**: 1 packet (7 g) gelatin is light for ~800 ml; use 6 sheets/10 g.
- **Germknödel**: topping needs ~1.7 oz powdered sugar mixed into the poppy seeds; use ~1 Tbsp Powidl per dumpling.
- **Rhubarb Schmand cake**: 2 packets pudding powder is heavy → 1; stray header "Elisen Lebkuchen" in the method.
- **Apfel-Schneemus-Torte**: dissolve gelatin separately before whipping.
- **Blackcurrant ice cream**: sugar used twice – split ~3 oz / 3 oz.
- **Glühwein**: 1–2 Tbsp sugar low for a bottle (✔ 2–4 Tbsp, lecker 50 g per litre); don't hold >30 min.
- **Spaghetti with beef & parsnips**: sauce too short for 10 oz pasta – reserve pasta water.
- **Scheiterhaufen**: milk quantity used twice (soaking and custard) – split it.
- **Reiberdatschi**: traditional version adds 1 small grated onion.
- **Typos**: "ish", "mage", "Hoiwa", "Cordon Blue", "Tellerfleisch … Meerretti", "Kapress".

## D. "Tastes like Bavaria" – authenticity notes
Many dishes are international (Schaschlik, Döner, Spareribs, Saltimbocca, tartare, spaghetti, shrimp-paprika chicken, Savoy lasagne, deviled eggs, Tarte flambée is Alsatian, Germknödel/Jägertee are Tyrolean). They're fine as "Southern German" cooking but the title "Bavarian" and "authentic" claims should be softened in the blurb. Signature Bavarian flavours missing from several recipes: marjoram, caraway, nutmeg, lemon zest in the meat dishes, Butterschmalz for frying, wheat beer in Obazda, savory (Bohnenkraut) in the bean salad.
