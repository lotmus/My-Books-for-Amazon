# Gravitation, the short version
A small popular book in the spirit of Misner, Thorne, and Wheeler. Not a guide to their book. Not a course.

Aim: a reader who liked a good magazine piece on black holes can finish this in a few evenings and come away with the real idea, plus what we have actually seen since 2015.

Length: about 140–160 pages. Eight chapters. No equations in the main text. A diagram only when a sentence would be longer than the picture.

## The idea, in one line

Falling is the straight path. What we call gravity is spacetime not lining up from place to place. Mass, pressure, and light bend that lining-up. Since 2015 we have watched the bends move.

## Voice

Talk as if to a smart friend on a walk. One idea per page. Name the everyday wrong picture, then replace it. Use a number only when it makes the scale felt: the Sun’s horizon would be 3 kilometers across; LIGO saw a stretch of a proton’s width across 4 kilometers.

Do not say fabric, rubber sheet, or “time slows down” without saying for whom. Do not explain tensors, metrics, or coordinates. If a chapter needs a symbol, the chapter is wrong.

## Contents

1. The apple that did not fall
   Let go of an apple. In a falling elevator it stays put. Gravity is not a tug. It is what you feel when the ground stops you from following the straight path. The Earth is in the way.

2. Straight, but not in space
   Light sets the speed limit. Moving clocks disagree with resting clocks, and both are right. The straightest path is through space and time together. A satellite is not “pulled into orbit.” It is going straight in a geometry that bends.

3. Tides are the real gravity
   In a small falling room, gravity disappears. Stretch the room and it does not: your head and your feet want different paths. That leftover is curvature. It is also why the Moon pulls the oceans into bulges, and why you would be torn apart near a black hole long before any movie horizon.

4. What bends it
   More mass, deeper bend. Pressure and energy bend it too; light does. The rule Einstein found in 1915 is the one that matches Newton in the solar system and then does not, farther in or faster. One box: why GPS has to correct for this or it would miss the street.

5. Stars with a last size
   Squeeze a star and quantum pressure pushes back, until it cannot. White dwarfs have a maximum mass. Neutron stars have one too. Past that, nothing we know holds the surface up. Pulsars were the clue that neutron stars are real.

6. The universe is not flying apart through space
   Galaxies are not racing out into empty darkness. The space between them is stretching. The glow from the hot early universe is still here, cooled to microwaves. The stretch is speeding up. We call the cause dark energy and do not know what it is. Dark matter is the other blank: extra gravity, seen, not identified.

7. The place you do not come back from
   A black hole is not a pit with a drain. It is a region where the outward path is not there anymore. From outside, a falling clock seems to freeze and fade at the edge. The person falling does not feel that edge. We have weighed stellar black holes by the orbits of their partners, watched stars whip around the one at the center of the Milky Way, and in 2019 and 2022 made images of the dark patch and the ring of light around M87* and our own galaxy’s center. Spin drags nearby space around with it.

8. The bends can travel
   A change in the geometry can ripple outward at the speed of light. In 2015 detectors in Louisiana and Washington felt two black holes merge by the way the arms of the instruments stretched and squeezed, by less than the width of a proton. In 2017 a neutron-star merger was seen in light as well as in ripples, and the ripples did not beat the light. That is the theory, checked, not just written down.

Close, four pages
   What is still open: the inside of the horizon, whether information comes back out, why empty space weighs so little, and what gravity is when quantum rules cannot be ignored. Stop there. Do not tour rival theories.

## What “current” means here

Mention only things that have been seen or cleanly measured.

- GPS clock corrections, in daily use.
- Pulsar clocks and the slow spiral-in of the Hulse–Taylor pair.
- The microwave sky, mapped.
- Accelerating expansion, from 1998 supernovae, checked since.
- LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA mergers, starting with 14 September 2015.
- A neutron-star merger seen in ripples and in light, August 2017.
- Horizon-scale images of M87* (2019) and Sgr A* (2022).

Leave out: string theory, most quantum-gravity brand names, wormhole engineering, anything that only exists as a solution on paper.
