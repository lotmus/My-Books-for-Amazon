## Description (HTML)

```html
<p><b>Let go of an apple and nothing pulls it down.</b> It is already on the straightest path there is. The floor is what gets in the way.</p>
<p>That one idea, Einstein's, explains why astronauts float in a station where gravity is still 89 percent as strong as on the ground. It explains why your phone's map would drift about 11 kilometers a day if nobody corrected the satellite clocks, why the ocean has two high tides and not one, and why a black hole's edge is a door and not a wall.</p>
<p><i>Gravitation, Actually</i> tells the modern picture of gravity in ordinary words and brings it right up to what has actually been measured:</p>
<ul>
<li>Clocks in orbit that run fast by 38 microseconds a day, and the GPS correction that keeps them honest</li>
<li>Tides as the real fingerprint of curved spacetime, and why a small black hole tears you apart outside its horizon</li>
<li>Stars with a last size: white dwarfs, neutron stars, and what happens past the limit</li>
<li>The first images of black hole shadows, M87* in 2019 and Sgr A* in 2022</li>
<li>Ripples in spacetime caught in 2015 that stretched a 4-kilometer arm by a few thousandths of a proton's width</li>
</ul>
<p><b>Two equations, said in words. No tensors.</b> The book borrows the best teaching pictures in the subject and retells them for a general reader: the ants on the apple, the two surveyors who disagree on north but agree on distance, the pole in the barn, the tunnel through the Earth, the shell observers around a black hole, and a map on which the singularity turns out to be a moment, not a place.</p>
<p><b>Built for real understanding:</b></p>
<ul>
<li>Every section labelled Core or Extension, so you can take the short road or the long one</li>
<li>An opener and a recap box for each of the eight chapters</li>
<li>68 numeric exercises with worked solutions, each showing the usual mistake</li>
<li>Diagrams drawn for the book, plus real images from NASA and the Event Horizon Telescope</li>
<li>A glossary, review questions with answers, and a reading list with the papers behind each claim</li>
</ul>
<p>For curious readers who want the real idea, not a slogan.</p>
<p><i>Part of the Actually books by Lothar J. Musiol.</i></p>
```
