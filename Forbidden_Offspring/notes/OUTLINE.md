## Settled 12 Oct 2026 (applied below)
- Stone stays alive after the stroke, in care, to the end of the book.
- The main offspring is a physician (MD) with a biology and chemistry background. He decides to look for a stroke cure.
- Career timeline: school and biology/chemistry degree by about 2021; medical school to the MD by about 2026 (private, before the reality line); research and clinical work after 2026 (future).
- Central tension: a stroke cure would give the creator back his mind and his index. The man the offspring has decided not to follow could become able to restart the century plan. He must decide what to do with a cure he has found.

## PART ONE: STONE (1999 to 2012)

Voice: first person, past tense, dry, self-justifying, precise. Stone writes this in the notebook he starts at the end of ch 5; the reader learns it is his account only at the end of Part One.

1. **Four Minutes** (spring 1999) **[drafted]**. The window, the cover story, the part he will not set down. Hint of the hundred-year schedule.
2. **The Transfer** (1999 to Feb 2000) **[drafted]**. The Pruitts, the transfer, the birth on 1 January 2000, the six-week visit, the first sign, Stone's first fear.
3. **Test Time** (2000 to 2008) **[drafted]**. Stone withdraws, watches from afar, accepts rare consented tests, asks for bench time to "refine methods". The cohort begins. "Danny was not alone."
4. **The Second Listener** (2008) **[drafted]**. The swim meet: the second dolphin carrier, the professor and engineer parents. First tremor.
5. **The Singapore Paper** (2010 to 2011) **[drafted, v2]**. The transient episode, the confidential manuscript, the rejection recommendation, the notebook begins.
6. **The Ledger** (2000 to 2011) **[new]**. Stone describes the plan in his own project-plan tone: the century schedule, the staging (heterozygous first, dominant later), why the index lives in one place. No method, no numbers. Ends with the vault.
7. **The Cohort** (2001 to 2010) **[new]**. A catalogue of the other children and families, as Stone's field notes: low-education homes with the most friction (and their dry, warm resolutions), easy professor homes, a boy who counts everything, a girl who never needs to ask. The future dead ends, the future laureate as a quiet child, the future senator as a charming one whom Stone finds easy to like and never notes as a risk. Sets up the bad-luck theme.
8. **The Pruitts at Dinner** (2008 to 2011) **[new]**. The one time Stone hears about Danny from inside the family: a clinic fundraiser where Ray and Connie are guests. Odd moments from their side (the flat piano, the bakery total, a teacher conference), told with warmth. Stone, who has never felt guilt, notices that he is listening for a punishment that does not come.
9. **The Apartment** (autumn 2011) **[trial drafted; revise]**. The confrontation: Stone arrives, the boy out-hears him, "I've read it. All of it." The vault was a courtesy. Mrs. Aldana knocks.
10. **The Bargain** (late 2011) **[new]**. Stone tries to negotiate: stop looking, let him finish. The boy's terms: no more children without telling him; no contact with the parents. Stone agrees and does not mean it. The boy keeps copies of the index.
11. **The Last Good Week** (spring 2012) **[new]**. Stone makes one more embryo batch against his word and calls it the last improvement. Vision failing; he writes more of the notebook. Hubris at full height.
12. **The Stroke** (2012) **[new]**. A quiet, brief chapter: the clinic, the floor, the word that will not come. The master index, which lived in his head and a few hand-kept pages only he could read, is gone. Stone's narration ends in a fragment. Part One closes.

## PART TWO: THE OFFSPRING (2012 to about 2037)

Voice: first person, past tense, deadpan, precise, with sound and timing detail (see `drafts/scene_apartment_boy_POV_v1.md`). Older and drier than the boy of the trial; he looks back with irony. Chapters can open with an excerpt from Stone's notebook and the narrator's correction of it.

13. **The Notebook** (2016, age 16) **[new]**. After years of Stone in care (the Pruitts know only that "Dr. Stone had a stroke"), the boy finds the notebook. He reads it through, notes what Stone left out, and decides to say nothing to his parents. Sets rules for himself.
14. **Visiting Hours** (2016 to 2018) **[new]**. He visits Stone in the care home. Stone cannot speak well. A one-sided, strange, sometimes funny scene; Stone responds to sound and little else. Whether he understands is left open. The visit is what turns the boy toward medicine: he wants to know what happened in Stone's brain, and whether it can be undone. First mention of a cure.
15. **Odd Moments** (2012 to 2018) **[new]**. The boy's own family years, told with comedy: the neighbor's argument through the wall, answering before being asked, the school that wants him skipped three grades, Connie's courage and Ray's pride. Friction with ordinary working parents handled with respect. All resolved. His decision to pass as ordinary.
16. **The Search** (2018 to 2019) **[new]**. Using Stone's notes, clinic records and his own hearing, he traces the cohort one by one. Detective chapter. The ethics of tracing people who do not know. His rule: no one is approached by surprise.
17. **The Phonetician** (2019) **[new]**. First contact: the linguist, then a student, because she is the only other person who hears as he does. A university phonetics lab. They test each other, then talk for six hours. She has felt "misplaced" all her life. He tells her carefully. She asks one question: "Who else?"
18. **The Engineer** (2019 to 2020) **[new]**. Her partner, who has also felt different. He takes it with a long silence and a practical question. Their decision to stay under cover and decline testing. The first of the quiet majority.
19. **The Laureate** (2021 to 2024) **[new]**. A shy physicist, still unknown. A conversation in a hallway after a talk. He would rather not have known, then asks for the notebook pages about his own insert. Seeds of his later denial.
20. **The Senator** (2022) **[new]**. A charismatic student speaker who reads a room perfectly. The main offspring hears a pulse and nothing behind it. He decides not to tell him yet. A chilling, courteous conversation in which neither names it. The senator will work it out himself.
21. **Under Cover** (2023 to 2026) **[new]**. The quiet majority, one by one: a florist, a surveyor, a violinist, two women who live together, a man who built a boat. The few LGBT carriers and their dead-end lines, treated with respect. Most say: do not make us visible. The club forms loosely as a mutual-help network and as an argument about what the gifted owe the rest. "Change the world for the better" is voiced fiercely by the laureate and drily by the linguist. The first ethical quarrel.
22. **Munich** (2027) **[new]**. The linguist and the engineer move to Munich for work (invented employer). A quiet farewell chapter, German bureaucracy, the first sign that she is pregnant. The pair decline clinical genetic testing. The main offspring helps them move.
23. **The Unpublished** (2027 to 2028) **[new]**. The laureate's breakthrough is published. The world goes briefly mad. He gives three interviews and says he was lucky. The senator congratulates him in public and privately sends one line that shows he knows.
24. **The Hearing Room** (2028 to 2030) **[new]**. The senator works it out. A conversation in a room with good acoustics, where the main offspring can hear every motive. The senator does not threaten; he asks for help. The main offspring refuses and understands that refusing changes nothing. The senator takes a Senate seat at 30 (by appointment or special election in 2030, otherwise January 2031).
25. **The Prize** (October 2029) **[new]**. The laureate wins the Nobel Prize in Physics at 29. Stockholm, from the audience. The press asks for the first time whether anyone this young can be merely ordinary. His first denial.
26. **The Child** (2028 to 2031) **[new]**. Munich: the main offspring visits the linguist, the engineer and their first child (born 2028). The child hears the cuckoo clock in the next flat before it chimes. The lineage is not a plan; it is a toddler. Quiet, funny, warm. He looks at the child and decides.
27. **Restart** (2031) **[new]**. The decision chapter. The case for restarting Stone's work, told fairly (the club, the fragile cohort, the senator proves nothing is safe), against the case not to (consent, loneliness, Stone's hubris, irreversibility). He keeps the copies locked away and tells no one where. He decides not to restart. The linguist's reply: "Good. Now stop deciding for all of us."
28. **More Than Enough** (2031 to 2034) **[new]**. The world in which the genomes are out and the carriers have many children, as a montage in his dry voice: children in Germany, Brazil, Ohio; dead ends and detours. Stone's century plan seems to proceed without him, and the book does not say whether that is the same as his plan. Stone is still alive in care. The main offspring, now a physician, makes progress in stroke research; each step brings Stone's recovery nearer.
29. **The Leak** (2035) **[new]**. A slow convergence: the rejected manuscript is published elsewhere, a clinic audit finds the after-hours bench time, and a journalist assembles a story with no names. Testing becomes the social weapon: employers, schools, insurers, campaigns. The laureate and the senator are the two most visible people it could be about.
30. **Denial** (2035 to 2036) **[new]**. Hearings, experts on both sides, comfortable explanations. The laureate testifies that he was lucky, the right research at the right time: a small lie that costs him something. The quiet majority learns to be glad no one knows. The senator is running for president and leading.
31. **Duck and Rabbit** (spring 2036) **[new]**. The assassination, offstage and reported (news, a hearing on the rifle's purchase, a neighbor's quote). A heavily armed environmental activist; the paperwork was in order; the stated purpose was hunting ducks and rabbits. The main offspring hears it on a radio in a kitchen and feels relief and guilt together.
32. **Staying Low** (2036 to 2037) **[new]**. Aftermath. Suspicion drifts to another scandal. The cohort meets once, in Munich, around a long table, to decide nothing. The club continues as mutual help. The main offspring may tell some parents, one at a time (optional; at least Ray).
33. **Epilogue: The Four Minutes** (undated) **[new]**. The narrator re-reads the first page of Stone's notebook and answers it. Stone is alive, and the stroke cure works, but he is old and fragile (about 86), with gaps in his memory; the index and the plan do not come back. The narrator gives him the cure (or decides to) and sits with a man who half-remembers. The book ends on a sound: Stone's returning voice, uncertain, asking something small. A last short scene with the child in Munich may follow.

## Encounters: why this order

- **The phonetician first (2019):** she is the only person who hears as he does, so recognition is immediate and low-risk. She gives him someone honest to talk to before the others.
- **The engineer second:** he arrives through her, and his practical reaction is a relief after her intensity.
- **The laureate third:** unknown at the time, so the reveal happens before fame complicates it. His later denial is a choice made with full knowledge.
- **The senator fourth, and not told:** the offspring suspects, withholds, and is outplayed by someone who works it out himself. That keeps the offspring responsible for what he did and did not do.
- **The quiet majority between and after:** shows that most carriers choose the quiet life and prepares for the leak's effect on them.
- **The Munich visit after the Nobel (2031):** the warm center of Part Two, placed just before the restart decision because meeting the child is what settles it.
- **Stone alive to the end:** the creator remains a living problem, and the stroke cure ties the offspring's profession to his central choice.

## Threads to track

- **Families:** odd moments resolved (ch 15); friction pattern (ch 7, 8, 15); parents not told in childhood; optional adult disclosure (ch 32).
