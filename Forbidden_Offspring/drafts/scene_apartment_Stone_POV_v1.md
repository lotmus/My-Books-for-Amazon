# Forbidden Offspring: apartment scene, Stone POV, trial draft v1

*Part One voice. Stone, first person, past tense, American English. Trial only; not final text.*

---

I should say at the outset that I knew about the doorman. I took the stairs from the lobby to the second floor because a man who is about to be difficult with a child should arrive having already done something difficult. I would not call that vanity. I would call it preparation, which is the opposite, although I have been told by people with less to prepare for that the two look alike from outside.

The boy opened the door before I knocked.

He was eleven that autumn. I will not pretend he looked like anything other than an eleven-year-old, because that was the point of the work: the changes were not on the surface. He had a gray sweater with a hole at one cuff and he held a book he was not reading, and he stood to one side of the doorway at an angle that I understood later was geometry. He was keeping me where the room would tell him what I did.

"You took the stairs," he said.

I had not said a word. "I like stairs."

"Your pulse is a hundred and eight."

I laughed. It was a poor laugh, and the left side of my mouth did not join in. The doctors had a name for what that side was doing, and a schedule for it, and I had thrown the schedule away. "It's a hundred and eight because I'm sixty-one."

"No," he said. "That's the reason for the limp."

I sat down. The chair was a cheap one and it faced the window, which I realize now was also arranged. He took the other chair, the one that faced me, and put the book on his knee as if the book would say something.

I had come with a speech. I had practiced it in the car: that the Singapore paper was a matter of months, that a competent reviewer would find the fragment in a week, that there were people who would put a man in prison for what I had done and put what I had made somewhere worse. It was a good speech. I had written it the way I write everything, with the conclusion first.

"Don't," the boy said, before I got to the second sentence. "You'll do better if you let me say it."

"Say what?"

"What you did. I've read it. All of it."

I want to be exact here, because later I would be asked, by people with power, how long I had known. The answer is that I knew in that moment. Not before. I had locked the indexed notes in a place I considered a vault, and in nine seconds a child told me, without raising his voice, that the vault had been a courtesy.

"Where did you find it?"

He did not answer. He tilted his head very slightly toward the wall behind me, listening to something I could not hear, and then back. "Mrs. Aldana in 4C is going to knock in about forty seconds to ask whether I want soup. I'd like it if you were already gone."

She knocked in forty-one.

I do not think he intended it as a joke.
