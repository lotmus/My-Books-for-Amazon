# Forbidden Offspring: apartment scene, boy POV, trial draft v1

*First person, past tense, American English. Trial of the voice only; not final text.*

---

I heard him before the elevator opened.

Frank Stone walks with a drag on the left, so there are two sounds per step: the shoe, then the shoe being apologized for. He had been in the building for four minutes and eleven seconds. He had spent the first three of them in the lobby, talking himself into it. The lobby has a marble floor and a ceiling that returns everything a half second late, so I got all of it. Mostly he was breathing.

I did not get up. The door has a lock that takes 1.2 seconds to turn, and I like to know what a person does with the first of those seconds. He did nothing with it. He stood there. Then the lock turned, and he came in carrying a bag from a pharmacy as if it were a reason.

"You've grown," he said.

"Four inches since March. You are standing where the floor boards are loose. The second one will talk."

It talked. He looked down at it, then at me. People like to look at the speaker, even when the sound came from somewhere else. I have stopped pointing this out.

"You can hear that from there."

"I can hear your pulse from here. It is a hundred and eight. You took the stairs from the lobby to the second floor, then the elevator the rest of the way, because you did not want the doorman to see you wait. That was a mistake. The doorman has been writing it down since Tuesday."

He sat. He did it carefully, the way people do once they have decided a chair is a negotiation.

Eleven years ago, I am told, he had four minutes in a room with no supervisor. I have read his notes, or the part of them that I could find, and I can say that four minutes is not long. A person can boil an egg in it. A person can also do whatever he did to me.

"I came to talk about the Singapore paper," he said.

"They sequenced a cohort of three hundred and found a fragment that is not human. They don't know yet what it is. They think it is contamination." I turned the page I was not reading. "It is not contamination. It is yours. It is also mine."

The room was quiet in the way that rooms are never quiet for me. The refrigerator hummed at a steady pitch. A pipe in the wall ticked. Down the hall, a woman in 4C put down a glass. I could tell the glass was empty, because of how little it said.

Stone said, "I need you to stop."

"Stop what?"

"Looking for the others."

"I found the others."

I let him have that for a while. I wanted to see where it landed on his face, and it landed late, the way sound does across water. He is sixty-one and has the kind of face that was handsome in a way he relied on. The left side no longer agrees with the right.

"How many," he said.

"Eleven. One of them can hear what I hear, and she has the sense not to talk about it."

"They shouldn't be finding each other."

"No," I said. "That part you got wrong."

He opened the pharmacy bag and took out nothing. He just held it open. I think he wanted something to look at.

"I designed you," he said, "to be careful."

"You designed me to be fast. Careful I did myself."

Down the hall, the glass in 4C was filled again, and I listened to that instead of to him, because it was the more honest sound in the building.
