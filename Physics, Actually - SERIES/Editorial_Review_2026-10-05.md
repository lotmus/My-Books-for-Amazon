# Editorial Review — Physics, Actually, Volumes 1–3 (2026-10-05)

Full audit of all three volumes across three dimensions: **content** (completeness/coverage), **didactic simplicity** (is it genuinely easy for a lay reader with no physics background to follow), and **correctness** (physics/factual accuracy, including redoing every checkable calculation). Scope and trigger: the series was restructured, renamed, and rebranded directly on `main` since the last review (`Volume 3/Editorial_Review_2026-09-27.md`, now stale — it describes the pre-restructure ~22k-word Volume 3 and should not be used as a reference). The author asked for a full audit of all three volumes and for every finding to be fixed.

Method: every chapter of every volume was read in full, in document order, directly (not sampled) — Volume 3 cover to cover, Volume 2 cover to cover, and Volume 1's front/back matter and cross-references (the deep chapter-by-chapter content audit of Volume 1's 17 chapters was delegated to a background process; see "Volume 1" below for status). Numerical claims were independently recomputed rather than taken on faith. A separate mechanical script scanned every volume for verbatim-repeated sentences within the same book, per the author's explicit request.

**Status: every finding below has been applied** to both the `.docx` and (where one exists) the `manuscript.md`, and verified with `zipfile.testzip()` plus paragraph-count and substring checks after every edit.

## Cross-volume finding: a mismatched "Life, Actually" link

In the front-matter "Also in This Series" list, the list entry displaying **"Life, Actually — From the First Cell to the Edited Genome..."** was hyperlinked to an Amazon search for a *different* book. In Volume 1 (both the KDP UPLOAD and KINDLE EBOOK copies) it searched for `Science for Everyone The Quantum World Lothar Musiol` — the series' old, pre-rebrand name. In Volume 2 and Volume 3 it searched for `The Quantum World Lothar Musiol` — current branding, but still the wrong book. All four files had the same underlying bug: the link target was never updated when that list entry's visible text became "Life, Actually." Fixed in all four `.docx` files by repointing the `rId10` hyperlink relationship to an Amazon search for "Life, Actually Lothar Musiol." This was found by inspecting the OOXML hyperlink relationships directly — it would not show up in a plain-text read, since the mismatch is invisible until the link is actually clicked.

## Cross-volume finding: repeated sentences

A mechanical scan (sentence-split, normalized, grouped by exact text, within each book only) flagged every verbatim-repeated sentence of seven words or longer in each volume, then every genuine hit was read in context to decide whether it was an unwanted duplicate or a deliberate callback.

**Fixed (genuine duplicates):**
- **Volume 1**, electromagnetism chapter: "A changing magnetic field can produce an electric field" was stated once introducing Faraday's discovery, then repeated verbatim four paragraphs later inside the Maxwell section. Reworded the Maxwell-section instance to reference Faraday's result explicitly instead of restating it ("the mirror image of the effect Faraday had already found running the other way"), in both `.docx` copies.
- **Volume 1**, mechanics chapter: "This is not merely a mathematical curiosity" opened both the wave-interference section and, much later, the least-action section. Reworded the later instance to "This is not just elegant mathematics," in both `.docx` copies.
- **Volume 2**, Chapter 3/Chapter 5: "The expansion of the universe is accelerating" was used as a Chapter 3 preview of a fact Chapter 5 ("Dark Energy") properly covers, then repeated verbatim as Chapter 5's own topic sentence. Reworded the Chapter 3 preview to "Instead of slowing down, the expansion is speeding up," in both the `.docx` and `manuscript.md`.

**Left as-is (deliberate, not errors):** shorter/generic hits our book's own voice relies on as a device — e.g. Volume 3 Ch.14's "Ask what could kill it" stated once as a general principle and then explicitly re-applied ("That is the warning sign. Ask what could kill it.") to the simulation-hypothesis case twenty lines later in the *same* chapter; Volume 2's "In Einstein's theory, pressure itself gravitates," used once in Chapter 1 and explicitly recalled by name in Chapter 10 ("the one we met with the ball of coffee grounds in Chapter 1"); Volume 2's "Nature gets the final vote," used as a thematic refrain closing two different frontier-physics chapters. Also left alone: "Volume 2 — Gravity, Cosmology, and the Limits of Spacetime" appearing in both Volume 1's series list and its closing recommendation — one is a plain list entry, the other a hyperlinked callback; both correctly display the same real title.

## Volume 3 — full re-audit

The book has grown substantially since the last review: 17 chapters across 4 Parts (previously ~13), including three entirely new chapters (Colliders/Detectors, the Strong Force, the Weak Force) and a new Ch.13 consolidating four older speculative chapters. Read cover to cover: Prologue, all 17 chapters, both Epilogues/the closing material, the Appendix, Further Reading, and the Bibliography.

**Findings — regressions of earlier fixes.** The restructure was evidently built from a manuscript snapshot that predated the 2026-09-27 review's fixes, so several specific corrections made in that review had silently reverted:

- **Ch.1**: the `E = mc²` passage no longer glossed `c` as the speed of light. Fixed: added "Here m is the mass and c is the speed of light — about 300,000 kilometers per second" immediately after the equation.
- **Ch.3**, "Why three forces fit together": back to overclaiming that all three Standard Model forces (electromagnetic, weak, strong) "turn out to be manifestations of a common mathematical structure at high energy." Only the electromagnetic and weak forces are confirmed-unified (electroweak theory); full unification with the strong force is the unconfirmed grand-unification hypothesis the book itself discusses as speculative in Part III. Fixed: reworded to state what's actually confirmed vs. still proposed, and retitled the section "How the Forces Are Related" (the overclaim-free title it had before).
- **Ch.3**: "hadrons," "elementary fermions," and "coupling constants" were each back to being used at their first appearance without the plain-language gloss the book gives every other piece of jargon. Fixed: added an inline gloss at each term's first use.

**No other findings.** The remainder of the book — all of Part I (Ch.1–8), Part II (entropy, chaos, the vacuum), Part III (string theory, the strand-model case study, how to judge a speculative idea), and Part IV (emergence, analogy, self-reference) — was read in full and extensively fact-checked: Boltzmann's tombstone equation and the coin-toss/gas-box probability arithmetic, Landauer's principle and the 2012 Lyon bead experiment, black-hole/CMB/Penrose entropy figures (10¹⁰⁴, 10⁸⁹, 10¹²³), Lyapunov-time doubling arithmetic and the solar-system chaos timescale, the Casimir-force numbers at 1 μm and 10 nm, the Lamb shift and Bethe's train-ride calculation, dark-energy's "four hydrogen atoms per cubic meter" and "half a joule per cubic kilometer," the cosmological-constant-problem figure (consistent with Volume 2's independent statement of the same number), the critical-point numbers for water (374 °C, 218 atm) and the shared 0.33 critical exponent, Gödel-numbering arithmetic, the Planck-to-LHC energy gap, and the full bibliography's dates/attributions. No arithmetic or attribution errors found anywhere in this pass.

## Volume 2 — full re-audit

Read cover to cover this round: Chapters 1–9, 12–13, the Epilogue, Appendix, Glossary, "The Equations, Decoded," "Try It Yourself," "Solutions," Further Reading, Bibliography, and the back-matter lists. (Chapters 10–11 were read and confirmed clean in an earlier session of this same review.)

**No content or correctness errors found.** Extensively fact-checked, including: the GPS 38 μs/day figure and the apple-pair tidal-convergence arithmetic (independently recomputed to ≈15.7 μm, matching the book's "roughly sixteen micrometers"); Mercury's 43″/century precession; the GW150914 mass bookkeeping (36 + 29 − 62 = 3 solar masses radiated); the c⁵/G gravitational-wave power scale (3.6×10⁵² W) and Earth–Sun radiated power (200 W); the Hulse–Taylor orbital-decay rate (76 μs/year) and GW170817's light/gravity race (1.7 s over 130 million years); the Hubble-tension figures (67 vs. 73 km/s/Mpc) and the 68/27/5 percent cosmic budget; Vera Rubin's and Zwicky's dates; the deuterium/CMB cross-check on ordinary-matter density; neutrino counting from helium abundance and from the Z-boson width (both giving three); Kelvin's 30-million-year Sun estimate versus the Sun's actual 4.6-billion-year age; the SN 1987A neutrino count (~24 across three detectors in 13 seconds); the NIST/JILA clock-height experiments and the Pound–Rebka tower shift (2.5×10⁻¹⁵, matching its own restatement in the "Solutions" section); the Shapiro-delay numbers; the Hawking-temperature figure for a solar-mass black hole (60 nanokelvin); and the Planck length/time values. The "Try It Yourself" section's twelve problems were each independently recomputed against their "Solutions" answers — all twelve are correct.

The one fix applied here is the Chapter 3/5 sentence repetition recorded above.
